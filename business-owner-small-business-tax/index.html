Business Owner Tax Advisory and Implementation

Start with the decision that matters.

Who this is for

Established owners whose business complexity requires a coordinated planning and implementation process, particularly when several advisers or entities are involved.

When to begin

The business has outgrown a filing-only relationship, recommendations are not being implemented, or no one owns the handoff between the plan and the return.

The question we help you work through

What advisory scope fits the business, which deliverables matter, and how will implementation be tracked without gaps or duplicate work?

From evidence to action

A disciplined review with a practical handoff.

01

Define the decision and engagement

Identify the owner’s priorities, deadlines, entities, and existing advisers. Distinguish immediate filing needs from prospective planning and separately scoped implementation work.

02

Establish record readiness

Review whether books, payroll, ownership records, and prior filings are complete enough for analysis. Explain the cleanup needed before a financial comparison can be trusted.

03

Prioritize recommendations

Compare impact, evidence, cost, timing, and ongoing administration. Sequence the work so a dependent election or contribution is not recommended before its prerequisites are confirmed.

04

Make responsibilities explicit

Assign document requests, technical review, payroll changes, legal work, and return reporting. A written recommendation should have an owner and a completion record.

05

Create a review rhythm

Agree on the facts that require a new conversation, such as an acquisition, compensation change, or stronger earnings. The engagement should make clear how questions and additional scope are handled.

A productive first conversation

Bring the context. We will define the next step.

Start with a short description of the decision, the people and entities involved, and the date by which you need an answer. These records help establish whether the work is ready for analysis:

  • Recent returns and current business financial statements
  • Entity ownership and election records
  • Payroll, distributions, retirement plans, and existing adviser responsibilities
  • A short list of priority decisions and the dates they must be resolved

Do not send tax returns, account numbers, or other sensitive records through a general inquiry. Arrange approved client-portal access before providing documents.

Clarity before commitment

Agree on the scope, deliverables, and responsibilities.

Scope the work

The consultation should establish the entities and periods covered, the analysis required, available records, proposed deliverables, and coordination with your existing advisers. The signed engagement determines what AE will provide.

Define the handoff

Confirm who implements each recommendation and who reports it on the return. Legal drafting, valuation, engineering, payroll processing, and filing work should be expressly included or assigned separately.

Understand fees and timing

Review AE’s published pricing as a starting point, then confirm the applicable fee and any separate work in writing. Delivery timing depends on the agreed scope, usable records, and review requirements.

Evaluate the result honestly

A review may identify a useful action, a documentation gap, or a reason to retain the current approach. Any financial comparison needs stated assumptions and a clear distinction between deductions, tax deferral, and realized savings.

Questions before you engage.

Is return preparation automatically included?

Confirm that in the signed engagement. Planning, bookkeeping, payroll, studies, and return preparation can have separate scopes and fees.

What should I bring to the first conversation?

Describe the decision, the entities involved, the deadline, and the records currently available. Use the approved client portal for sensitive documents after access is arranged.
