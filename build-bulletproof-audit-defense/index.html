Build a Bulletproof Audit Defense

AE Tax Advisors · Tax Planning & Strategy

A bulletproof audit defense starts before the IRS ever audits you. It's built into the structure of your return from the beginning through compliant tax positions, thorough documentation, and strategic planning.

Prevention: The Best Defense

The best audit defense is a return that can withstand scrutiny from day one. This means: Taking tax positions that are fully compliant with the IRC and supported by tax court cases or IRS guidance. Substantiating all deductions with adequate documentation. Disclosing aggressive positions on Form 8275 to demonstrate transparency. Maintaining detailed records supporting all income and deductions.

Structuring for Defense

When we recommend a tax strategy, we build in audit defense from the beginning. For example, if we recommend a cost segregation study, we choose a CPA firm that's familiar to the IRS and prepares detailed reports supporting the depreciation. If we recommend an S-corp election, we ensure the W-2 salary is reasonable and documented with industry benchmarks.

Documentation is Key

In an audit, you're not trying to prove your tax position is correct (the burden is often on the IRS). You're trying to show you substantiated your deductions with adequate documentation. If you can produce receipts, business purpose documentation, and records supporting all deductions, the IRS often closes the audit with minimal adjustments.

Professional Representation

Having a CPA or Enrolled Agent represent you in an audit is invaluable. The IRS respects professionals more than taxpayers representing themselves. A professional can negotiate, appeal unfavorable findings, and navigate the audit process skillfully.

Building Your Defense Now

Start today: maintain contemporaneous documentation for all deductions, maintain detailed business records, track mileage carefully, document business purpose for meals and entertainment, keep receipts for all business expenses, maintain separate business bank accounts, and work with a CPA to review your return before filing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I represent myself in an audit?

It is rarely advisable beyond the simplest correspondence matters. Statements made during an examination cannot be retracted, and the scope of an audit frequently expands based on what the examiner learns. Representation by a CPA, enrolled agent, or attorney limits direct contact and keeps the scope defined.

What triggers an IRS examination?

Common triggers include large deductions relative to reported income, S-Corp officer compensation that appears low, real estate professional status claims, and information return mismatches. Selection is also partly random through the National Research Program.

How long can the IRS go back?

Generally three years from the filing date. That extends to six years where more than 25% of gross income was omitted, and there is no limit for a false or fraudulent return or a failure to file. Employment tax and certain international matters have their own rules.

What are my options if I owe more than I can pay?

Installment agreements, currently not collectible status, penalty abatement for reasonable cause or first-time abatement, and in appropriate cases an offer in compromise. Which applies depends on your financial position, and the analysis should happen before enforcement action begins.
