My S-Corp Paid My Health Insurance but Left It Off My W-2. How Do I Fix It After Filing?

Start with what the corporation actually paid and which returns were already filed. The correction may involve payroll, the S-corp return, and the owner's personal return.

Book a Return Review Call

The direct answer

If an S corporation paid or reimbursed health premiums for a more-than-2% shareholder in a prior year but omitted them from the filed W-2, do not simply enter a self-employed health insurance deduction on the owner's Form 1040. Reconcile the payments first, then evaluate a Form W-2c, the affected employment tax return and Form 941-X, the filed Form 1120-S and any changed K-1, and the owner's Form 1040-X. The exact package depends on what was reported originally; not every form is amended in every case.

The IRS Notice 2008-1 and current S-corporation medical-insurance guidance require both corporate payment or reimbursement and wage reporting for the more-than-2% shareholder's above-the-line deduction. A payment made only from the owner's personal account, without corporate reimbursement in the year at issue, is a different problem: a late bookkeeping entry or W-2c alone cannot establish a payment that did not occur.

Health premiums for a more-than-2% owner are generally included in W-2 Box 1. Under the IRS conditions for the plan, they can be excluded from Social Security and Medicare wage boxes 3 and 5. Box 14 can identify the amount but is not a substitute for Box 1. The wage and payroll analysis is separate from whether the owner qualifies for the personal deduction.

Sequence the corrections before filing anything

  1. Prove payment and ownership. Identify who purchased the policy, who paid each monthly premium, whether the S corporation reimbursed the shareholder, the dates of reimbursement, and whether the shareholder exceeded 2% ownership on any day during the year. Reconcile bank activity and payroll records to the general ledger.
  2. Reconstruct the W-2. Compare the premium total with the original W-2 Boxes 1, 3, 5, and 14. The employer generally files Form W-2c and W-3c with the Social Security Administration and gives the corrected statement to the shareholder. The current W-2 instructions say to file corrections as soon as possible after finding an error.
  3. Reconcile employment returns. Compare the corrected wage total with each Form 941 or other applicable employment return. Form 941-X instructions explain when previously reported wages require correction. Prior-year federal withholding is generally not rewritten merely because wages were misclassified; review actual withholding and the administrative-error exception. Do not mechanically change Social Security or Medicare wages without testing the applicable plan conditions.
  4. Reconcile Form 1120-S. Determine whether the corporation already deducted the payment as officer compensation, health insurance, another expense, or incorrectly as a distribution. If the filed income, deductions, or shareholder information changes, follow the amended Form 1120-S instructions, including a corrected K-1 when required. An unchanged corporate total does not automatically mean the classification and shareholder basis are correct.
  5. Recompute the owner's return. Use Form 7206 instructions when applicable to test the self-employed health insurance deduction, including earned-income and subsidized-plan limits. If the owner already filed, evaluate Form 1040-X with the corrected W-2 and revised schedules. If the personal return has not been filed, use the corrected wage data in the original filing.
  6. Check state and later-year effects. A corrected wage statement may change state wage reporting, withholding reconciliation, individual AGI, credits, or later carryovers. Confirm actual filing deadlines and refund-claim periods for each return; do not assume all years remain open.

A payroll provider can produce forms, but it may not know whether the corporation actually reimbursed the shareholder, whether the deduction is usable, or whether the corporate return's K-1 changed. Coordinate the tax and payroll review before submitting partial corrections.

Worked example: $18,000 paid but omitted from a filed W-2

Assume a calendar-year S corporation paid $1,500 each month directly to an insurer for its 100% shareholder's family coverage in 2025. Its bank statements prove $18,000 of 2025 payments. Payroll reported $120,000 in W-2 Box 1 but omitted the premiums, and the 2025 Form 1120-S and owner Form 1040 were already filed. Assume the plan meets the conditions for exclusion from Social Security and Medicare wages.

The preliminary corrected Box 1 wage amount is $138,000; Boxes 3 and 5 do not increase merely because the premiums are added to Box 1. The employer and payroll provider reconcile the affected quarters' Form 941 wage totals and prepare a W-2c and any required Forms 941-X. The corporate preparer determines whether the $18,000 was already deducted as compensation or improperly posted as a distribution, then assesses whether Form 1120-S or the shareholder's K-1 needs amendment.

For the personal return, the shareholder does not assume an $18,000 net refund. The corrected W-2 adds $18,000 to wage income; an eligible Section 162(l) deduction may offset that addition on Schedule 1, subject to Form 7206 and the subsidized-plan, earned-income, and other applicable limits. A Form 1040-X can still matter to make the reporting correct, and AGI-sensitive items may change. The tax result depends on the actual filed returns, premiums, plan, and state treatment. By contrast, if the shareholder personally paid all $18,000 and the S corporation never reimbursed it during 2025, the facts do not support the same correction path.

Bring these records to a return review

  • Original W-2 and W-3, all Forms 941 or 944, payroll registers, and any already-filed correction forms.
  • Insurance invoices, policy documents, premium-payment confirmations, corporate bank records, and reimbursement requests or approvals.
  • Filed Form 1120-S, K-1, owner Form 1040, and state returns for the affected year.
  • General-ledger detail for wages, insurance, shareholder distributions, loans, and reimbursements.
  • Ownership records and information about any subsidized employer coverage available to the shareholder or spouse by month.
  • Prior Form 7206 or Schedule 1 calculation, premium tax credit forms if relevant, notices, and current-year payroll setup.

Common mistakes that delay the correction

  • Claiming the personal deduction from an uncorrected W-2. Notice 2008-1 ties the deduction to corporate payment or reimbursement and wage reporting.
  • Fixing Box 14 only. Informational Box 14 reporting does not replace required Box 1 wages.
  • Automatically increasing FICA boxes. Qualifying premiums can be excluded from Boxes 3 and 5; the plan and payment facts control.
  • Backdating a reimbursement. The actual year of corporate payment matters. A journal entry cannot replace evidence of payment.
  • Ignoring the corporation's filed return. A distribution classification can affect officer compensation, deductions, and shareholder basis.
  • Correcting withholding that was never withheld. Prior-year Form 941-X rules distinguish wage corrections from changes to actual income-tax withholding.
