Tax Planning for Owners of Multiple Businesses and LLCs

More entities should mean clearer responsibilities—not more disconnected advice. Start by showing who owns what, how money moves and which returns need to agree.

Review My Multiple-Business Tax Plan

Build the ownership map first

List every legal entity, tax classification, ownership percentage, state, tax year, preparer and operating purpose. An LLC name does not establish its federal tax treatment. Record elections and effective dates before comparing a change. Include property-holding entities and household returns where the tax effects ultimately appear.

Reconcile what moves between the businesses

ItemWhat to reconcile
Owner pay and distributionsPayroll, services performed, shareholder records and cash actually received.
Loans and capitalAgreements, balances, interest, repayments and owner basis records.
Rent and shared costsActual property use, leases, invoices and defensible allocation methods.
Retirement plansRelated ownership, employee coverage and funding obligations.
K-1s and state returnsEntity amounts, owner reporting, carryovers and state-specific treatment.

Do not treat every loss as interchangeable

A loss reported by one entity does not automatically offset profit from another. Owner basis, at-risk rules and passive-activity limitations can apply in sequence. Grouping must follow the rules and facts; common ownership is not enough. IRS Publication 925 explains activity-level restrictions.

Compare changes against a baseline

Ask for a forecast under the current structure and each proposed alternative. Include payroll costs, administration, state obligations, distributions and a potential sale—not just the first-year deduction. A management company needs a genuine business purpose and supported transactions; moving an invoice does not itself reduce household tax.

One action list, named implementation owners

  • Identify the decision, assumptions and unresolved records.
  • Set dates for elections, payroll adjustments, bookkeeping and return preparation.
  • Assign each step to a named person or firm.
  • Reconcile entity and owner returns before filing.
  • Revisit the map after an acquisition, ownership change or new state activity.

If property is part of the structure, use business-and-property planning. For a specific disputed recommendation, request a tax planning second opinion.

Common questions

Does opening another LLC lower my tax?

Not by itself. Federal classification, ownership, activity and the actual transactions matter. Compare the proposed structure with the current one before adding costs.

Can one advisor coordinate several preparers?

A written scope can assign a coordinating reviewer while existing preparers retain their filings. Name the responsibility for each reconciliation and deadline.
