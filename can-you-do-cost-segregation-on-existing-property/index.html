Can You Do Cost Segregation on an Existing Property?

AE Tax Advisors Team · Published 2026-10-03

The Short Answer

Yes, you can perform a cost segregation study on property you already own, even years after purchase. Use Form 3115 to claim catch-up depreciation through a Section 481(a) adjustment. You get all missed depreciation in a single tax year with no amended returns.

But the full picture is more nuanced than a one-paragraph answer. The right approach depends on your specific income level, business structure, state of residence, and long-term financial goals. Below, we break down everything you need to know.

How Cost Segregation Works

Cost segregation is an engineering-based tax strategy that reclassifies components of commercial and rental real estate from the standard 27.5-year (residential) or 39-year (commercial) depreciation schedule to accelerated 5-year, 7-year, and 15-year MACRS recovery periods. The IRS has explicitly approved this approach through the Cost Segregation Audit Techniques Guide (ATG).

Under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA) signed into law in 2025, 100% bonus depreciation is available for qualifying property placed in service in 2026 and beyond. This means all reclassified components from a cost segregation study can be fully expensed in year one, making the tax savings immediate and substantial.

Typical Cost Segregation Results by Property Type

The percentage of a property's cost that can be reclassified varies by property type. Restaurants and hotels see 30-45% reclassification. Medical and dental offices typically see 25-35%. Retail spaces average 20-30%. Office buildings see 15-25%. Multi-family residential properties typically see 20-35%. The higher the reclassification percentage, the larger the first-year tax benefit.

For a $1,000,000 commercial property with 30% reclassification, the accelerated depreciation is $300,000. At a 37% marginal tax rate, the first-year tax savings are $111,000. Factor in state income taxes and the savings can exceed $130,000 on a single property.

Who Should Consider Cost Segregation

Cost segregation is most valuable for property owners who have significant income to offset, own property valued at $300,000 or more, plan to hold the property for at least 3-5 years, and are in a high marginal tax bracket (32% or above). Real estate professionals who qualify for REPS status benefit the most, as they can use accelerated depreciation to offset active income without passive activity limitations.
