Real Estate Portfolio Tax Planning

Start with the decision that matters.

Who this is for

Investors with multiple rentals, mixed short- and long-term use, ownership partnerships, or material acquisition, renovation, and sale decisions.

When to begin

The portfolio has grown faster than its records, a new property is closing, or a planned sale could release gains and previously suspended losses.

The question we help you work through

Which deductions are usable, which require better evidence, and how do current choices affect the portfolio’s later tax and liquidity position?

From evidence to action

A disciplined review with a practical handoff.

01

Reconcile the portfolio

Match each property to its legal owner, tax return, purchase allocation, depreciation schedule, and debt. Separate land, improvements, furnishing purchases, and operating costs.

02

Review activity and participation

Examine rental use, services, ownership, and participation records. Short stays or an ownership title alone do not establish how losses will be treated on an individual return.

03

Evaluate depreciation and improvements

Review placed-in-service support, existing depreciation, renovations, and cost-segregation feasibility. Classification and deduction utilization should be assessed together.

04

Model the planned holding period

Compare current deductions with later sale consequences, refinancing plans, and cash needs. A larger first-year deduction does not by itself establish a better lifetime result.

05

Coordinate portfolio implementation

Create a property-by-property record request and assign return, study, bookkeeping, and legal tasks. Confirm who will maintain basis and suspended-loss schedules after the review.

A productive first conversation

Bring the context. We will define the next step.

Start with a short description of the decision, the people and entities involved, and the date by which you need an answer. These records help establish whether the work is ready for analysis:

  • Property roster with ownership percentages and acquisition dates
  • Closing statements, depreciation schedules, and improvement invoices
  • Rental-use and participation records with existing loss carryforwards
  • Debt schedules, expected renovations, and proposed sale or exchange plans

Do not send tax returns, account numbers, or other sensitive records through a general inquiry. Arrange approved client-portal access before providing documents.

Clarity before commitment

Agree on the scope, deliverables, and responsibilities.

Scope the work

The consultation should establish the entities and periods covered, the analysis required, available records, proposed deliverables, and coordination with your existing advisers. The signed engagement determines what AE will provide.

Define the handoff

Confirm who implements each recommendation and who reports it on the return. Legal drafting, valuation, engineering, payroll processing, and filing work should be expressly included or assigned separately.

Understand fees and timing

Review AE’s published pricing as a starting point, then confirm the applicable fee and any separate work in writing. Delivery timing depends on the agreed scope, usable records, and review requirements.

Evaluate the result honestly

A review may identify a useful action, a documentation gap, or a reason to retain the current approach. Any financial comparison needs stated assumptions and a clear distinction between deductions, tax deferral, and realized savings.

Questions before you engage.

Can rental losses offset my business or salary income?

That depends on the applicable activity, participation, basis, at-risk, and other loss rules. A property-level deduction is not proof that the owner can currently use it.

Should every property have a cost segregation study?

No. Basis, eligible components, expected holding period, fees, and the owner’s ability to use deductions all affect feasibility.
