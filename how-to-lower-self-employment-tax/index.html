How to Lower Self-Employment Tax: What Actually Changes It

By AE Tax Advisors

Updated November 8, 2026.

To lower self-employment tax, first calculate the correct net earnings from your business and claim legitimate business expenses. Then compare whether an S corporation would reduce total tax and administrative costs. An entity election does not erase tax on the work you perform, and a retirement contribution does not automatically reduce self-employment tax.

Start with the tax you are trying to reduce

Self-employment tax funds Social Security and Medicare. The standard calculation generally applies to 92.35% of net self-employment earnings. For 2026, Social Security has a $184,500 combined wage and self-employment earnings ceiling; Medicare does not have that ceiling. Additional Medicare Tax can apply at higher income levels. Your wages from another job matter, so applying a flat 15.3% to every dollar of profit can overstate the benefit of a strategy.

Business expenses and personal deductions have different effects

An ordinary, necessary and properly substantiated Schedule C expense generally reduces the business profit used in the self-employment calculation. Personal spending does not become deductible because it is paid from a business account. Keep invoices, payment records and business-purpose support, and separate personal use of vehicles or equipment.

Your own deductible SEP or qualified retirement-plan contribution is generally an adjustment on your individual return rather than a Schedule C expense. It can reduce income tax without reducing the self-employment earnings on which that contribution was calculated. The deduction for one-half of self-employment tax likewise does not refund half of the tax. Employee retirement deferrals generally remain subject to Social Security and Medicare taxes.

When an S corporation deserves a comparison

An eligible business can elect S corporation taxation and pay an owner who provides services reasonable wages through payroll. Genuine remaining business profit can pass through without self-employment tax, but those wages carry employment taxes. The wage amount depends on duties, time and market compensation; a fixed salary-to-distribution percentage is not a substitute for that analysis.

Compare payroll administration, a separate business return, state taxes, unemployment obligations, retirement-plan effects and the QBI deduction. A small or unstable profit margin may not support the extra cost. For the election process, see our LLC and S corporation decision guide.

A practical review before year end

  1. Reconcile revenue and deductible expenses to current books.
  2. Add outside wages and project the Social Security wage-base interaction.
  3. Document the owner's services and a supportable wage range.
  4. Model total federal and state tax, fees and retirement contributions under each structure.
  5. Check election and payroll deadlines before transferring money.

Hiring a relative or adding a management company requires real work, reasonable payment and applicable employment-tax treatment. Neither step creates automatic savings. Request a business-owner tax review with your current books and prior returns.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does contributing to a SEP IRA reduce self-employment tax?

Your own deductible SEP contribution generally reduces income tax, not the net earnings used to calculate self-employment tax.

Does an S corporation save 15.3% of all distributions?

There is no universal percentage. Reasonable wages, the Social Security wage base, other earnings, state taxes and administration all affect the comparison.
