S-Corp Owner Took Distributions Before Payroll: What to Review Before Form 1120-S

AE Tax Advisors · Tax Planning & Strategy

If you elected S-corporation treatment, worked in the business, and took cash out before setting up payroll, do not file Form 1120-S by simply labeling every transfer a distribution. First reconcile actual payment dates, services performed, any wages already paid, employment-tax filings, and shareholder basis. The IRS says a shareholder-employee must receive reasonable compensation for services before non-wage distributions; it may reclassify distributions or other payments as wages to that extent. The required correction depends on the facts and what has already been filed—not on a universal salary percentage. IRS: S-corporation compensation.

Bring the election effective date, owner transfers, payroll reports, and draft return to a review before the entity return is submitted. AE can identify the wage/distribution mismatch, the filings it affects, and the steps to coordinate with your payroll provider. If you are still deciding whether owner payroll applies at all, start with that guide; this page focuses on reconciling an existing S-corp year.

Which filing stage are you in?

  • The year is still open. Set up real payroll prospectively, document a supportable wage for the services performed, and review earlier transfers with the actual dates and records. A year-end catch-up decision must reflect real payments and applicable deposit deadlines; changing a journal entry does not create a payment that did not occur.
  • The year ended, but wage and entity returns are not complete. Reconstruct the payroll history before issuing Form W-2 or filing Form 1120-S. Determine whether wages were actually paid, whether Forms 941 (or an IRS-designated Form 944) and state reports are due or late, and whether a prior distribution classification is supportable.
  • Forms W-2, 941, or 1120-S were already filed. Compare filed amounts with the underlying payments. A true reporting error may require Form 941-X for affected quarters, Form W-2c, a corrected entity return and shareholder Schedule K-1, an amended individual return, or state corrections. Do not assume all of these forms are necessary in every case. IRS Form 941-X instructions require coordination with corrected wage statements when applicable.

Reconcile wages, distributions, and the return—not just the bank balance

Build a transaction-level schedule for every owner payment: date, amount, bank account, bookkeeping label, business purpose, and any contemporaneous payroll record. Separate actual wages from distributions, documented loans, expense reimbursements, and other transfers. A payroll system such as ADP, Gusto, QuickBooks Payroll, or Paychex can calculate and transmit payroll filings, but its reports still need to agree with the company's books and tax return. In-house processing can work too if someone owns the deposits, state accounts, filings, and reconciliations.

Worked review example: An owner performed full-time services, received $120,000 of transfers during the year, and the books label all $120,000 as distributions. The payroll register and filed wage statements show $20,000 of wages. A compensation analysis based on duties and comparable roles may indicate that $20,000 needs closer examination; it does not automatically make a fixed share of the remaining $100,000 wages. Trace when each transfer occurred, whether any was a documented loan or reimbursement, what the owner actually did, and what the corporation reported. Only then determine the defensible wage amount, employment-tax period, distribution balance, basis effect, and correction sequence. This is a review model, not a proposed salary safe harbor.

How the payroll filings connect

Quarterly returns and deposits: An employer that pays wages generally files Form 941 unless the IRS has directed it to file annual Form 944 or another exception applies. After the first Form 941, quarterly filing generally continues even in a zero-wage quarter unless an exception applies. Filing the return and depositing withheld income tax and Social Security/Medicare tax are separate duties. The monthly versus semiweekly deposit schedule is determined primarily by the lookback-period tax liability, not by a guess about current annual wages; special deposit rules and exceptions also apply. Review the current Form 941 instructions and IRS deposit rules for the actual year.

Year-end wage reporting: Furnish the owner-employee Form W-2 and file wage information with the Social Security Administration by the applicable due date. Reconcile the W-2 boxes to the quarterly returns, allowing for legitimate adjustments and different wage definitions; do not assume every box equals the sum of one Form 941 line. If the wage statement is wrong, Form W-2c may be needed. Also test whether federal unemployment tax and Form 940 apply rather than assuming every corporation files it. See the W-2/W-3 instructions and Form 940 instructions.

Entity and owner returns: Officer compensation belongs on the appropriate Form 1120-S wage line; shareholder distributions are reported separately and affect stock basis and, in some cases, taxability. The Schedule K-1 reports pass-through items and distribution information; it is not a substitute for Form W-2. The shareholder's Form 1040 must incorporate both the W-2 and K-1. The Form 1120-S instructions explicitly warn that payments to an officer are wages to the extent they are reasonable compensation for services. Related retirement-plan contributions, more-than-2% shareholder health insurance, qualified business income, and state payroll or income tax may change when wages change.

What to gather for a return review

  • Form 2553 or election confirmation and the effective date; ownership and officer roles.
  • Bank statements, general ledger, shareholder loan agreements, reimbursement records, and an owner-transfer schedule with dates.
  • Payroll registers, deposit confirmations, Forms 941 or 944, Forms W-2/W-3, any Form 940, and state wage/unemployment filings.
  • Prior Forms 1120-S, Schedules K-1, shareholder basis schedules, Forms 1040, and the current draft returns.
  • A description of the owner's duties, hours, comparable compensation evidence, and any contemporaneous salary decision.

The IRS says to keep employment-tax records for at least four years after filing the fourth-quarter return for the year; other corporate, basis, and return records may need longer retention. IRS employment-tax recordkeeping guidance explains the federal payroll minimum.

Failure points to catch before filing

  • Using a 60/40 formula or an earnings threshold. The IRS applies a facts-and-circumstances compensation analysis; no fixed profit split or $300,000 income trigger makes wages reasonable.
  • Calling every distribution “tax-free.” Employment-tax treatment and income-tax treatment are different. Basis, earnings and profits, and distribution ordering can matter.
  • Backdating a catch-up paycheck. The payment date, withholding, deposits, and reporting period must be supported by actual records; an after-the-fact label alone does not cure a missed payroll obligation.
  • Using a flat 15.3% estimate on high wages. The Social Security wage base, Medicare rules, Additional Medicare Tax, employer/employee shares, and other facts affect the calculation. Model total taxes and compliance costs instead of promising a savings amount.
  • Fixing only Form 1120-S. A wage change can affect employment-tax returns, wage statements, state filings, K-1/basis schedules, and the owner's Form 1040. A payroll provider's automated report does not reconcile all of them by itself.

This is a federal decision framework, not a determination that a particular transfer was wages or a promise of tax savings. State law, benefit plans, prior-year filings, and the timing of the S election can change the result. Frequently Asked Questions

Can I simply reclassify last year's owner draws as salary?

No. A bookkeeping entry cannot create a wage payment on an invented date. Review the actual transfers, services, payroll records, and filing periods with a tax and payroll professional before deciding whether employment-tax returns and wage statements need correction.

Is there a fixed percentage of S-corp profit that must be salary?

No. The amount depends on the shareholder's actual services and the business facts. A salary-to-distribution ratio does not replace a supportable compensation analysis.

Do I need Form 941 if the owner is the only employee?

Generally, an S corporation that pays owner wages reports employment taxes on Form 941 unless the IRS has notified it to file Form 944 or another exception applies. After the first Form 941, quarterly filing usually continues even for a zero-wage quarter unless an exception applies.

What if Forms W-2 and 1120-S were already filed?

Reconcile the wage payment dates and amounts first. If reporting was wrong, the review may involve Form 941-X, Form W-2c, a corrected Form 1120-S and K-1, the shareholder return, and state filings. The actual correction sequence depends on what was filed and when.
