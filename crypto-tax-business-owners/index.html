Crypto Tax for Business Owners: Payments, Basis and Reporting

By AE Tax Advisors · Published 2026-10-03

Updated November 8, 2026.

Digital-asset tax work begins with what happened to each asset and who owned it. A customer payment, investment sale, mining reward, wallet transfer and corporate distribution are different events. Classifying cryptocurrency as property does not make every transaction a capital gain.

Separate business receipts from investment disposals

Crypto received for services or goods can produce business income measured in dollars under the applicable accounting rules. The amount included in income generally establishes basis for a later disposition. Assets held as capital assets can produce capital gain or loss; inventory and other noncapital property require different treatment.

Buying with dollars or transferring assets between wallets you own is not automatically a taxable sale. Spending or exchanging an appreciated asset can create a disposition. Reconcile network fees and the actual transaction instead of treating every wallet entry identically.

Match proceeds with supported basis

Keep acquisition dates, quantities, dollar values, fees, wallet addresses and transfer records. Missing transfer history can make software incorrectly treat transferred assets as new income or zero-basis sales.

Apply the identification rules for the relevant year and custody arrangement. Specific identification has timing and record requirements; a label such as LIFO in software does not establish compliance. Review the IRS digital-asset FAQs and applicable transitional relief before selecting lots or relying on a broker's figure.

Reconcile Form 1099-DA

Broker proceeds reporting began for covered reporting transactions in 2025, with statements generally furnished in 2026. Basis reporting for 2026 and later is not universal: covered-security rules and acquisition and custody facts matter.

Compare broker forms with the complete ledger. No form does not mean no taxable event, and gross proceeds are not the same as gain. Use the correct entity return and disposition schedules for the transaction's character.

Identify rewards and operating costs separately

Mining and staking can produce income before the asset is sold. Revenue Ruling 2023-14 addresses staking rewards for a cash-method taxpayer when the taxpayer obtains dominion and control. Later sales require a separate basis calculation.

An operating mining business must separately support business status, equipment basis, service dates, electricity and other costs. Equipment does not automatically qualify for a full immediate deduction. Entity and owner-level limits still apply.

Do not choose a corporation from one tax rate

Personal ownership, an S corporation and a C corporation can produce different reporting, distribution and loss consequences. A C corporation's 21% federal rate does not include shareholder-level distribution tax or state tax. Appreciated property distributed by a corporation can itself trigger gain.

S corporation loss basis and compensation rules require review, but holding investments does not automatically convert investment returns into wages. Model actual services, asset character and owner transactions.

Review unusual transactions and foreign accounts

Liquidity pools, lending, wrapped tokens, NFTs and protocol restructurings need transaction-specific analysis. Do not assume a real-estate like-kind exchange rule applies to token swaps or that every NFT has the same collectible treatment.

Foreign-account reporting is a separate question. FinCEN Notice 2020-2 states the virtual-currency-only account position under the FBAR rules and notes its scope. Other assets in the account, later guidance and separate Form 8938 requirements need their own review; there is no single automatic filing answer for every foreign exchange wallet.

Build a filing package the preparer can reconcile

  • Complete exchange exports and wallet addresses, including closed accounts.
  • Business invoices, reward records, dollar valuations and fees.
  • Acquisition lots, identification instructions and transfer reconciliation.
  • Broker forms, prior-year basis workpapers and entity ownership records.
  • Unresolved protocol events, foreign-account facts and the position taken.

Request a digital-asset reporting review before filing or amending. Confirm the covered entities, years, transaction volume and reconstruction work in the engagement scope.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is every crypto transaction a capital gain?

No. Character depends on the asset and transaction, and some transfers are not sales.

Does Form 1099-DA always include all basis?

No. Basis reporting depends on the year and covered-security rules; reconcile your own records.

Does receiving staking rewards wait for taxation until sale?

Not necessarily. The IRS ruling addresses income when a cash-method taxpayer obtains dominion and control; sale is a separate event.

Does a foreign crypto wallet automatically require an FBAR?

Do not assume that. Review current FinCEN rules, account contents and separate foreign-asset reporting requirements.
