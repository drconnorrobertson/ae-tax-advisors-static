Business Owner Tax Calculator

If you are a business owner earning $100,000 or more annually, there is a strong chance you are paying significantly more in federal and state taxes than necessary. The U.S. tax code is expansive, spanning thousands of pages, and it contains dozens of provisions specifically designed to incentivize business investment, job creation, and capital deployment. The challenge is that most business owners never learn about these provisions until years after they could have benefited from them. Our Business Owner Tax Calculator is designed to change that by giving you a fast, no-obligation estimate of your current tax burden and the savings you may be leaving on the table.

This calculator evaluates four of the most impactful tax reduction strategies available to business owners in 2026. First, it examines whether your current entity structure is costing you unnecessary self-employment taxes. Many sole proprietors and single-member LLC owners pay 15.3% in combined Social Security and Medicare taxes on every dollar of net business income, but an S-Corp election can reduce that burden dramatically by allowing you to split income between salary and distributions. Second, the calculator looks at retirement plan optimization. Most business owners contribute to a simple SEP-IRA or traditional 401(k), but advanced vehicles like cash balance pension plans, defined benefit plans, and mega backdoor Roth strategies can shelter far more income from taxes each year.

Third, for business owners who also own real estate (whether commercial property, rental homes, or short-term rentals), the calculator estimates potential savings from a cost segregation study. Cost segregation is an IRS-approved engineering analysis that reclassifies building components into shorter depreciation categories, accelerating your deductions and putting real dollars back in your pocket in year one. Under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act of 2025, bonus depreciation has been restored to 100% permanently, making cost segregation more valuable than ever. Fourth, the calculator factors in your state tax environment, since state income tax rates vary widely and play a critical role in total tax liability.

To use the calculator, simply enter your gross business income, select your current entity type, choose your filing status, indicate your state, provide the number of employees you have (this affects retirement plan options), and check the box if you own investment real estate. Click the calculate button and you will see a side-by-side breakdown of your estimated current tax liability alongside the potential savings from each strategy. The results include a total estimated annual savings figure that represents the combined benefit of implementing all applicable strategies.

Please keep in mind that this calculator provides estimates for educational purposes only. Actual tax savings depend on numerous factors specific to your financial situation, including deductions, credits, prior-year losses, passive activity rules, and state-specific regulations. The figures generated here are based on 2026 federal tax brackets and general assumptions about each strategy. They should not be treated as tax advice. For a precise, personalized analysis, we encourage you to schedule a free discovery call with our team. We will review your actual tax returns, business financials, and real estate holdings to build a custom tax plan with exact dollar figures and a clear implementation roadmap.

Understanding the Tax Strategies Behind the Calculator

The Business Owner Tax Calculator draws on four core strategies that represent the highest-impact planning opportunities available to most business owners. Below, we break down each strategy in detail so you understand how the numbers are generated and why these approaches work.

1. S-Corporation Election and Self-Employment Tax Reduction

When you operate as a sole proprietor or a single-member LLC, the IRS treats all of your net business income as self-employment income. That means you owe the full 15.3% self-employment tax (12.4% for Social Security up to the wage base, plus 2.9% for Medicare) on every dollar of profit. For a business owner earning $300,000 in net income, that translates to roughly $30,000 or more in self-employment taxes alone, on top of your regular income tax.

An S-Corporation election changes this dynamic significantly. When your business is taxed as an S-Corp, you pay yourself a reasonable salary, and you owe payroll taxes only on that salary amount. The remaining profit flows through to you as a distribution, which is not subject to Social Security or Medicare taxes. The IRS requires that your salary be "reasonable" for the type of work you do, and there is substantial case law defining what qualifies. For most business owners, a reasonable salary falls between 40% and 60% of net income, depending on the industry and role. The difference between your total income and your salary is the distribution, and the tax savings on that distribution is where the value lies.

Our calculator estimates S-Corp savings by modeling a reasonable salary at approximately 40% of net business income (capped at the Social Security wage base where appropriate) and calculating the self-employment tax you would avoid on the distribution portion. For owners already operating as an S-Corp, the calculator recognizes that and does not double-count this benefit. Keep in mind that S-Corp election comes with additional compliance requirements, including payroll processing, quarterly filings, and a separate corporate tax return (Form 1120-S). These costs are modest relative to the savings for most businesses earning above $80,000.

2. Retirement Plan Optimization

Most business owners know about traditional 401(k) plans and SEP-IRAs, but few realize the full range of retirement vehicles available to them. A standard 401(k) allows employee deferrals of up to $23,500 in 2026 (plus a $7,500 catch-up contribution for those aged 50 and older). A SEP-IRA allows contributions of up to 25% of compensation. While these are valuable, they barely scratch the surface for high-income business owners looking to shelter significant income from taxes.

Cash balance pension plans are the most powerful tool in this category. A cash balance plan is a type of defined benefit plan that allows dramatically higher contributions, often $100,000 to $300,000 or more per year depending on the owner's age. The contribution is tax-deductible to the business, reducing taxable income dollar for dollar. When combined with a 401(k) profit-sharing plan, a business owner aged 55 could potentially contribute over $350,000 per year into tax-advantaged retirement accounts. For someone in the 37% federal bracket, that translates to over $100,000 in annual tax savings from this strategy alone.

Our calculator estimates retirement plan savings by comparing your current likely contribution level (based on entity type and income) to the maximum available through an optimized plan stack. It factors in your income level and applies the relevant marginal tax rate to estimate the additional deduction value. Retirement plan design requires actuarial analysis and must comply with ERISA and IRS nondiscrimination requirements, so the calculator's output is a starting point, not a final figure.

3. Cost Segregation and Accelerated Depreciation

Cost segregation is an IRS-approved tax strategy that applies to business owners who own depreciable real property. Under standard depreciation rules, commercial buildings are depreciated over 39 years and residential rental properties over 27.5 years. Cost segregation studies use engineering analysis to reclassify specific building components (such as electrical systems dedicated to equipment, decorative finishes, site improvements, and specialty plumbing) into shorter depreciation categories of 5, 7, or 15 years.

With the One Big Beautiful Bill Act of 2025 restoring 100% bonus depreciation permanently, qualifying components identified through a cost segregation study can be fully deducted in the year the property is placed in service or the year the study is completed (for properties already in service). This creates an enormous first-year deduction that can offset other income, including business income, W-2 income, and investment income in certain cases.

A typical cost segregation study reclassifies 25% to 40% of a building's cost basis into shorter-lived categories. For a property with a $500,000 cost basis, that could mean $125,000 to $200,000 in accelerated deductions. At a combined federal and state marginal rate of 40%, that produces $50,000 to $80,000 in year-one tax savings. Our calculator models cost segregation savings using a conservative 30% reclassification rate applied to an assumed property value based on your income level, then applies your marginal tax rate. The actual benefit depends on property type, condition, and the engineering findings of a formal study.

4. State Tax Planning and Residency Optimization

State income taxes represent one of the most variable factors in a business owner's total tax burden. Rates range from 0% in states like Florida, Texas, and Wyoming to over 13% in California. For a business owner earning $500,000, the difference between operating in a zero-income-tax state and a high-tax state can exceed $50,000 per year. While relocating is not always practical, understanding your state tax exposure is critical to evaluating your overall position. Some states also offer unique credits, deductions, or pass-through entity tax elections that can further reduce your burden. Our calculator incorporates your selected state's approximate top marginal rate to provide a more accurate picture of your combined federal and state liability.

Frequently Asked Questions

How accurate is the Business Owner Tax Calculator?

The calculator provides directional estimates based on 2026 federal tax brackets and general strategy parameters. It does not account for every deduction, credit, or state-specific nuance. For a precise analysis tailored to your situation, schedule a consultation with AE Tax Advisors.

What is the benefit of electing S-Corp status for my business?

An S-Corp election allows you to split business income between a reasonable salary and distributions. While your salary is subject to self-employment taxes (Social Security and Medicare), the distribution portion is not. For business owners earning above $60,000, this can result in annual savings of $5,000 to $30,000 or more depending on income level.

Who qualifies for cost segregation tax savings?

Any business owner who owns commercial or residential rental real estate with a cost basis of $200,000 or more is a candidate for a cost segregation study. The study reclassifies building components into shorter depreciation periods (5, 7, and 15 years instead of 27.5 or 39 years), accelerating deductions and reducing current-year taxable income.

Can I use multiple tax strategies at the same time?

Yes. Most business owners benefit from layering several strategies together. For example, you might elect S-Corp status to reduce self-employment taxes, implement a cash balance retirement plan for large pre-tax contributions, and run a cost segregation study on your investment properties. The combined effect is often significantly greater than any single strategy alone.
