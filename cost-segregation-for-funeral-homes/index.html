Cost Segregation for Funeral Homes

AE Tax Advisors Team · Published 2026-10-03

Why Funeral Home Owners Should Consider Cost Segregation

Funeral Home Owners who own their commercial real estate are sitting on significant untapped tax savings. A cost segregation study reclassifies building components from the standard 27.5-year (residential) or 39-year (commercial) depreciation schedule to accelerated 5-year, 7-year, and 15-year recovery periods. For properties valued at $500K-$3M property, this typically generates $40,000-$200,000 in first-year accelerated depreciation deductions.

Under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA), 100% bonus depreciation is available for qualifying property placed in service in 2026 and beyond. This means all reclassified components from a cost segregation study can be fully expensed in year one, dramatically increasing the tax benefit.

What Components Get Reclassified in a Cost Segregation Study

An engineering-based cost segregation study identifies specific building components that qualify for accelerated depreciation. Common reclassifications for funeral home and mortuary property owners properties include electrical systems serving specific equipment (5-year property), specialized HVAC and plumbing (7-year property), site improvements like parking lots, landscaping, and signage (15-year property), and decorative finishes tied to the business rather than the building structure.

Typically, 20-40% of a commercial building's cost basis can be reclassified to shorter recovery periods. For a $1,000,000 property, that means $200,000 to $400,000 in deductions accelerated from 39 years to the current tax year. At a 37% marginal rate, the immediate tax savings range from $74,000 to $148,000.

Form 3115 Catch-Up Depreciation for Existing Properties

If you purchased your property years ago and never performed a cost segregation study, you can still capture the benefit. File Form 3115 (Application for Change in Accounting Method) to claim all missed accelerated depreciation through a Section 481(a) adjustment. This catch-up depreciation is taken entirely in the current tax year with no need to amend prior returns.

For funeral home and mortuary property owners who have owned their property for 5 to 15 years, the catch-up depreciation through Form 3115 can generate a one-time deduction of $100,000 to $500,000+, depending on property value and components eligible for reclassification.

Cost Segregation ROI for Funeral Home Owners

Funeral Home Owners with properties valued at $500K-$3M property can expect first-year accelerated depreciation deductions of $40,000-$200,000 through a properly executed cost segregation study. These deductions reduce both federal and state income tax liability, improve cash flow, and increase after-tax returns on your real estate investment.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much can funeral home owners save with proactive tax planning?

Funeral Home Owners earning $500K-$3M property typically save $40,000-$200,000 annually through entity optimization, depreciation planning, retirement plan design, and strategic timing of income and deductions.

What is the best entity structure for funeral home owners?

Most funeral home owners benefit from S-Corporation election for active business income, with separate LLCs for real estate holdings and investment activities. The optimal structure depends on income level, state taxation, number of owners, and whether the Section 199A QBI deduction applies.

When should funeral home owners start tax planning?

The ideal time to begin proactive tax planning is January of the current tax year, giving you a full 12 months to implement strategies. Mid-year planning still captures 50-75% of available savings. Year-end planning is better than no planning, but limits your options significantly.
