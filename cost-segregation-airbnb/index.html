Cost Segregation for Short-Term Rentals

AE Tax Advisors · Updated September 24, 2026

Should you order a cost segregation study for an Airbnb?

Order the study only when the property has supportable shorter-life assets, the resulting deduction is usable, and the after-tax timing benefit exceeds the study, implementation, and exit costs. Being listed on Airbnb or having an average stay under seven days does not prove any of those points.

A cost segregation study allocates depreciable building basis among assets with different recovery periods. For a furnished short-term rental, the strongest candidates often include separately purchased furniture and appliances, removable floor coverings, qualifying dedicated systems, and land improvements. The building structure remains long-life property. Land is never depreciable.

The two STR rules people incorrectly combine

The average-period-of-customer-use test belongs to the passive-activity rules. Treasury Regulation 1.469-1T says an activity with an average customer use of seven days or less is not treated as a rental activity for that regulation. The owner must still satisfy a material-participation test before the activity can be nonpassive.

The building's 27.5- or 39-year recovery period is a separate Section 168 question. The statute generally treats a building as residential rental property when at least 80% of its gross rental income is from dwelling units. A dwelling unit does not include a unit in a hotel, motel, or other establishment when more than half of the units are used on a transient basis. A seven-day average stay does not automatically produce a 39-year life.

Which short-term-rental assets can have shorter lives?

Classification is property-specific. The label “Airbnb asset” does not decide recovery life, and a study should not reclassify an item solely because it can be named separately.

Bonus depreciation is an eligibility test, not a blanket result

Current federal law generally provides a 100% special depreciation allowance for qualifying property acquired and placed in service after January 19, 2025. Qualified property generally includes eligible MACRS property with a recovery period of 20 years or less, but the acquisition date, placed-in-service date, eligible used-property rules, related-party rules, business use, ADS requirements, elections, and same-year disposition can change the result. Property acquired before January 20, 2025 can fall under different percentages.

States do not all follow the federal result. A federal first-year deduction can be recovered over several years on the state return. Review bonus depreciation for rental property and maintain separate state schedules where required.

Worked example: deduction, usable loss, and cash tax are different

Assume an owner purchases a furnished vacation home for $900,000, supports $180,000 of land, and has $720,000 of depreciable acquisition basis. A property-specific study identifies $150,000 of eligible shorter-life building components. Separately documented furniture acquired with the property adds $35,000 of eligible personal-property basis.

If all $185,000 qualifies for 100% federal bonus depreciation, that is the accelerated deduction before remaining building depreciation. It is not automatically $185,000 of current tax savings. If basis and at-risk limits are satisfied but the owner does not materially participate, the loss may be passive and suspended. If the owner materially participates and the activity is nonpassive, the deduction may offset other nonpassive income, subject to the excess-business-loss and other return-level rules.

At a 32% marginal federal rate, a fully usable $185,000 deduction could represent $59,200 of federal tax timing benefit before state effects. If the loss is suspended, current cash savings may be zero. If the property is sold soon, recapture and reduced basis can materially reduce the present value. This is why the review must model the return and expected exit, not multiply a study estimate by a tax rate.

Five gates before treating the loss as usable

  1. Basis. Confirm the owner's tax basis in the activity and the property's depreciable basis.
  2. At risk. Determine which cash, debt, and guarantees create amount at risk.
  3. Activity classification. Compute average customer use and review significant personal services and other regulatory exceptions.
  4. Material participation. Select a test that the contemporaneous facts and records can support; do not rely on a slogan about 100 hours.
  5. Return-level limits. Apply passive-loss, excess-business-loss, business-use, state, and other applicable limitations.

Personal use creates another branch. Vacation-home limits, converted-property basis, mixed use, and days used by owners or related parties can change both the deduction and the records required.

New property, one prior return, or adopted method?

The filing path depends on what has already been reported:

  • Current-year property: establish the correct asset schedule and report current depreciation on Form 4562 and the owning return.
  • One prior return: determine whether the current automatic-change procedure permits Form 3115 for one-year property or whether an amended return, AAR, or another correction path applies.
  • Two or more returns using an impermissible method: an accounting method may have been adopted. A qualifying automatic Form 3115 change can use a Section 481(a) adjustment, but scope limitations, designated change number, filing timing, attachments, and duplicate-copy procedure must be checked.
  • Sold property or examination: special eligibility and timing rules apply; do not assume a standard look-back filing.

Use the dedicated Form 3115 cost-segregation guide for the current filing map. Property age by itself never proves that Form 3115 is available or that amendment is prohibited.

Documents to gather before ordering the study

  • Closing statement, purchase agreement, appraisal, land support, and any allocation to furnishings
  • Placed-in-service evidence, booking history, average-stay data, personal-use days, and property-use timeline
  • Furniture inventory, seller schedules, photographs, inspection report, floor plan, and available construction records
  • Renovation invoices, contracts, change orders, abandoned-component records, and dates improvements became available for rent
  • Prior federal and state returns, depreciation schedules, Forms 4562, Form 8582, K-1s, and basis and at-risk workpapers
  • Participation logs, manager and cleaner hours, owner tasks, access records, and contemporaneous calendars
  • Expected hold period, sale assumptions, state of filing, and the provider's written study, implementation, and audit-support scope

Common failure points in STR studies and returns

  • Using purchase price instead of depreciable basis after removing land and reconciling capitalized costs
  • Assuming a fixed percentage of every STR can be reclassified
  • Automatically putting the structural remainder on 39 years because average stay is seven days or less
  • Classifying cabinets, lighting, plumbing fixtures, hardwood floors, or decks without analyzing function and permanence
  • Claiming 100% bonus without checking acquisition date, service date, ADS, business use, related parties, or the state return
  • Counting a deduction as tax savings before applying basis, at-risk, passive, and excess-business-loss limits
  • Filing Form 3115 because the property is “old” without determining whether a method was adopted and which procedure applies
  • Failing to update the fixed-asset ledger, owner basis, state schedules, and later-year depreciation
  • Ignoring Section 1245 recapture, unrecaptured Section 1250 gain, suspended losses, and the expected holding period

What should the study and implementation proposal include?

AE's published study price is $1 per square foot with a $2,000 minimum. Before comparing providers, confirm what the quoted fee actually includes: inspection method, asset-level schedules, basis reconciliation, legal support, source-cost documentation, report revisions, depreciation exports, Form 3115 work, federal and state return implementation, and examination support. Those items are not interchangeable, and tax work outside the study should be scoped separately.

A quality report should explain the approach used, identify the preparer and relevant expertise, reconcile allocated costs to total depreciable basis, document estimating sources and adjustments, classify assets with supporting law, and address related accounting-method issues. The IRS Audit Techniques Guide describes six approaches; it does not say every valid study must use one branded method or that a remote study is automatically invalid.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a short-term rental qualify for cost segregation?

A short-term rental can qualify when the owner has depreciable building basis and the property contains assets that properly belong in shorter recovery classes. The booking platform or average stay does not create cost-segregation eligibility by itself. Land, personal use, service date, property use, records, and the economics of the study must also be reviewed.

Is a short-term rental depreciated over 27.5 or 39 years?

The seven-day passive-activity rule does not decide the building's recovery period. Section 168 separately tests whether 80% or more of gross rental income is from dwelling units and excludes a unit in a hotel, motel, or other establishment when more than half of its units are used on a transient basis. Apply those facts before choosing 27.5 or 39 years.

Can short-term-rental cost segregation losses offset W-2 income?

They may, but only after basis, at-risk, passive-activity, excess-business-loss, and other applicable limits. An activity with average customer use of seven days or less is not treated as a rental activity for the cited Section 469 regulation, but the owner must still materially participate for the activity to be nonpassive.

Does every asset identified in an STR study receive 100% bonus depreciation?

No. Current federal law generally provides 100% bonus for qualifying property acquired and placed in service after January 19, 2025, but asset class, acquisition date, service date, eligible used-property rules, business use, related-party rules, ADS, elections, and state conformity can change the result.

Can I use Form 3115 for an older Airbnb cost segregation study?

Sometimes. Form 3115 can correct an adopted impermissible depreciation method through a Section 481(a) adjustment when the taxpayer and property satisfy the current procedure. A current-year correction, amended return, AAR, or another path may apply instead. Property age alone does not make Form 3115 automatic.

Which Airbnb components can be reclassified?

Furniture, appliances, removable floor coverings, certain dedicated electrical or plumbing serving qualifying equipment, and qualifying land improvements may have shorter lives. Cabinets, general lighting, building plumbing, structural decks, and permanent finishes are not automatically short-life property. Function, attachment, design, and legal precedent control.

How do I decide whether an STR cost segregation study is worth it?

Compare depreciable basis, property-specific reclassification support, bonus eligibility, immediate loss usability, state conformity, study and implementation fees, expected holding period, sale and recapture effects, and the value of timing. A projected deduction is not the same as current tax savings.

Will cost segregation automatically trigger an IRS audit?

The IRS does not publish a rule that a cost segregation study automatically triggers an examination. If examined, the taxpayer must support basis, asset classifications, recovery periods, methodology, and return implementation. The IRS Audit Techniques Guide describes the attributes examiners use to evaluate a study.
