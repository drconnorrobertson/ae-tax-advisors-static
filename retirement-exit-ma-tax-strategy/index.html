Business Exit and Retirement Tax Planning

Start with the decision that matters.

Who this is for

Owners considering a sale, partner buyout, recapitalization, or retirement transition who need tax analysis alongside their legal and financial advisers.

When to begin

A buyer has approached, a letter of intent is being discussed, or ownership needs to transition to a partner, family member, or management team.

The question we help you work through

Which transaction terms affect after-tax proceeds, when must decisions be made, and what evidence is needed before a structure is accepted?

From evidence to action

A disciplined review with a practical handoff.

01

Establish the ownership and basis file

Reconcile entity classification, stock or interest basis, asset basis, liabilities, and prior transactions. Missing basis support can undermine an otherwise detailed proceeds model.

02

Compare transaction structures

Evaluate the proposed asset or equity sale and identify the treatment of consideration allocated to different assets. Ordinary income, capital gain, depreciation-related consequences, and state treatment can differ.

03

Examine payment and rollover terms

Review installment proposals, contingent payments, retained equity, and debt payoff with counsel. Payment over time does not automatically mean every component receives the same tax timing.

04

Coordinate retirement and liquidity

Connect the transaction forecast with estimated payments, retirement funding, and cash needed outside the deal. Avoid a plan that uses the same proceeds twice or assumes unavailable deductions.

05

Prepare a closing and filing handoff

Identify allocation documentation, reporting responsibilities, payment dates, and post-closing adjustments. Retain the signed transaction documents and the final financial model together.

A productive first conversation

Bring the context. We will define the next step.

Start with a short description of the decision, the people and entities involved, and the date by which you need an answer. These records help establish whether the work is ready for analysis:

  • Ownership, basis, depreciation, and debt schedules
  • Recent returns, current financial statements, and transaction forecasts
  • Draft letter of intent, proposed allocation, and payment terms
  • Adviser contacts, retirement objectives, and expected closing timeline

Do not send tax returns, account numbers, or other sensitive records through a general inquiry. Arrange approved client-portal access before providing documents.

Clarity before commitment

Agree on the scope, deliverables, and responsibilities.

Scope the work

The consultation should establish the entities and periods covered, the analysis required, available records, proposed deliverables, and coordination with your existing advisers. The signed engagement determines what AE will provide.

Define the handoff

Confirm who implements each recommendation and who reports it on the return. Legal drafting, valuation, engineering, payroll processing, and filing work should be expressly included or assigned separately.

Understand fees and timing

Review AE’s published pricing as a starting point, then confirm the applicable fee and any separate work in writing. Delivery timing depends on the agreed scope, usable records, and review requirements.

Evaluate the result honestly

A review may identify a useful action, a documentation gap, or a reason to retain the current approach. Any financial comparison needs stated assumptions and a clear distinction between deductions, tax deferral, and realized savings.

Questions before you engage.

Should planning wait until an offer is signed?

Early analysis is generally more useful because ownership and deal terms may be harder to change after commitments are made.

Does AE provide legal deal documentation?

Tax coordination does not replace transaction counsel. Legal documents, valuation work, and investment advice need the appropriately engaged professionals.
