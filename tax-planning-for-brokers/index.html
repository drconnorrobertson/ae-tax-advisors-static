Tax Advisory for Brokerage Owners: Commissions, Acquisitions & Property

By AE Tax Advisors Team · Published October 3, 2026 · Updated November 6, 2026

A brokerage owner may be coordinating agents, commission splits, renewal income, employees and separately owned property. A useful advisory engagement should identify the actual revenue streams and ownership first, then define the decisions and implementation work. This page addresses insurance, mortgage and real estate brokerage owners; the word “broker” alone does not determine federal tax treatment.

For an individual real estate agent with personal rentals, start with agent-and-property advisory scope. For technical background, read the real estate agents and brokers guide.

Choose the decision you need help with

  • Acquiring an agency, customer book, another brokerage or a partner's interest.
  • Adding team members, compensation arrangements or another owned entity.
  • Buying the office premises instead of leasing, or coordinating an existing property entity.
  • Comparing owner compensation and retirement funding against uneven commission cash flow.
  • Reconciling a plan from another adviser with the preparer's books, schedules and filings.

Map the brokerage's actual income and obligations

Distinguish new commissions, renewal or trailing commissions, fees, referral arrangements and other services. Reconcile gross reporting with splits, chargebacks, deferred items and the governing contracts. Identify the taxpayer earning each item and any related entities. Confirm the relevant accounting method rather than moving income based only on cash needs.

Staff and producer classifications require review under the applicable rules. The statutory-nonemployee rules for licensed real estate agents do not automatically apply to mortgage staff or every insurance salesperson. IRS Publication 15-A describes employee, statutory-employee and statutory-nonemployee categories.

Buying a book of business is not an equipment purchase

An asset acquisition may include customer relationships, goodwill, contracts, receivables, equipment and other assets with different treatment. Acquired qualifying Section 197 intangibles are generally amortized over 15 years; their cost is not automatically a current operating expense or bonus-depreciation deduction. A stock or partnership-interest acquisition creates different basis questions.

Ask for an allocation and opening-records review before signing, including contingent payments, liabilities, ownership and future reporting duties. The IRS intangibles overview and Form 8594 instructions explain the general framework. Counsel should separately address licensing and contract-transfer restrictions.

QBI depends on services, not just the broker label

The brokerage-services SSTB definition focuses on arranging securities transactions and expressly excludes real estate agents/brokers and insurance agents/brokers from that particular definition. That does not exempt every activity in a mixed business from all SSTB rules or establish the owner's deduction.

Financial advice, mortgage-related services, other activities, taxable income and applicable limitations need their own analysis. Use Treasury Regulation 1.199A-5 and request a documented classification of the services actually performed. Do not treat a change of business name or a second entity as a QBI solution.

Office property and employee plans need coordinated scope

If you own the office through a separate entity, review the lease, ownership, participation, basis and depreciation. Self-rental rules can make net rental income nonpassive while losses remain passive. A study should be evaluated with those limits and future disposition consequences, rather than presented as an automatic deduction against commissions.

For retirement planning, provide a complete employee census and related-business ownership chart. Owner-only eligibility cannot be assumed at a staffed brokerage. Compare administrator costs, employee contributions and sustainable funding before selecting a cash balance or other plan. See Publication 560 and Publication 925.

For the first call, describe your brokerage type, ownership, team, property interests and decision deadline. Send sensitive records only through approved secure channels after intake.

Frequently asked questions

Are all brokers specified service businesses for QBI?

No single answer follows from the title. The securities-brokerage definition excludes real estate and insurance brokerage from that category, but other activities and QBI limitations still require review.

Can I immediately deduct the cost of buying a customer book?

Not automatically. Qualifying acquired Section 197 intangibles generally use 15-year amortization. Transaction form, asset allocation and applicable exceptions matter.

Can a staffed brokerage use an owner-only 401(k)?

Do not assume so. Employee eligibility and related-employer rules must be reviewed using the full census and ownership map.

Will the office building deduction offset my commissions?

Not necessarily. Ownership, self-rental, participation, basis, at-risk and other limitations determine how a deduction is used.
