Business Asset Disposal and Replacement: Basis, Recapture and Reporting

By AE Tax Advisors · Published 2023-08-10 · Updated November 8, 2026.

Replacing an asset creates two tax questions: the disposition of the old asset and the treatment of the new one. A purchase price minus trade-in allowance is not necessarily the new asset's tax basis or the gain from the old asset.

Reconcile adjusted basis first

Start with original basis, capital additions and depreciation allowed or allowable. Separate land, buildings and equipment where relevant. A truck that cost $500,000 and sells for $200,000 does not necessarily create a $300,000 loss; prior depreciation may have reduced basis below the proceeds.

Determine amount realized

Include cash and other consideration, and evaluate liabilities transferred or relieved. Allocate an overall business-sale price to the correct assets. Transaction costs and contingent amounts need their own treatment.

Classify the gain or loss

Depreciation recapture can turn gain into ordinary income. Section 1231 treatment may apply to qualifying business property, with holding-period and lookback rules. Not every business-asset loss is a capital loss, and not every building gain is ordinary recapture.

For equipment, Section 1245 can recapture depreciation to the extent of gain. Building and land-improvement treatment requires a separate Section 1250 and unrecaptured-gain analysis. Do not assign one tax rate to all proceeds.

Review installment and exchange limits

An installment agreement may defer eligible gain as payments are received, but depreciation recapture is generally recognized in the year of sale. Interest and debt effects also matter.

Section 1031 applies only to qualifying real property and has detailed transaction requirements. A business-equipment trade-in does not automatically receive like-kind exchange treatment.

Record the replacement separately

Establish the replacement's cost, tax ownership, business use and service date. Evaluate depreciation, Section 179 and bonus eligibility under the applicable year rules. A replacement deduction does not erase the requirement to report the old asset's disposition.

Maintain the closing file

Keep sale and purchase agreements, trade-in values, debt payoff statements, depreciation schedules and allocation workpapers. Consider state differences and the owner's ability to use any deduction before relying on a net tax projection.

Request an asset-disposal review before signing a sale or replacement agreement.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is loss measured from the original purchase price?

No. Compare amount realized with adjusted basis, including relevant depreciation.

Can installment payments defer all depreciation recapture?

Generally no. Depreciation recapture is usually reported in the sale year.
