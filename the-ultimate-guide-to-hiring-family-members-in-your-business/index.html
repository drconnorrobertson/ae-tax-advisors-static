Hiring Family Members: Comprehensive Tax Guide

AE Tax Advisors · Tax Planning & Strategy

Reviewed November 6, 2026 for 2026 planning.

Family employment is worth evaluating when a relative can perform useful work at reasonable pay. The financial question is the change in total family tax and operating cost, not the size of the business wage deduction. This guide separates spouse payroll, child earnings, and retirement contributions so a proposed hire can be compared with the current arrangement.

Build the Net-Benefit Comparison

  1. Business side: start with a supportable wage deduction, employer payroll taxes, benefits, and administration costs.
  2. Worker side: calculate income tax, employee payroll taxes, other earnings, and any credits or benefits affected.
  3. Owner side: recompute self-employment tax where relevant, QBI, state taxes, and retirement contributions.
  4. Whole family: compare the before-and-after totals using the same year, filing status, and work assumptions. Separate a current tax deduction from money committed to retirement.

Why $100,000 of Spouse Wages Is Not $15,300 of Savings

A sole proprietor's deductible wages can reduce business profit, but the spouse-employee wages generally incur Social Security and Medicare taxes on both sides. The owner's actual self-employment tax calculation, including the net-earnings adjustment, other earnings, and Social Security wage base, must be recomputed. Additional Medicare Tax and the income-tax deduction for part of self-employment tax can also change the comparison.

Illustrative payroll cost: If $100,000 of spouse wages is entirely subject to ordinary Social Security and Medicare taxes, 7.65% is $7,650 for the employee and another $7,650 for the employer, totaling $15,300 before any other payroll costs. That is a payroll tax cost in this illustration, not an automatic net tax saving. The actual owner and spouse wage-base positions can produce a different overall result.

Spouses filing jointly report combined income and allowable deductions. A spouse does not have an independent lower bracket on the joint return. Also distinguish a spouse-employee from a co-owner: partners are not employees of their partnership, and cannot simply substitute a W-2 for their partnership distributions or guaranteed payments.

Child Wages Depend on the Employer and Age

In a parent's sole proprietorship or a partnership in which every partner is a parent of the child, qualifying business wages are exempt from Social Security and Medicare before age 18 and from FUTA before age 21. At ages 18 through 20, FICA generally applies while the child-specific FUTA exemption can continue. Corporate employers and partnerships with a nonparent partner do not qualify for these child-specific exceptions.

A single-member LLC disregarded for income tax generally reports employment taxes under its own name and EIN. The family-employment regulations nevertheless look to its owner for the specific FICA and FUTA family exceptions, if all requirements are met. An LLC electing S-corporation or C-corporation taxation is different. Confirm the actual employer, owner, election, and services; an LLC label or a new management company does not create an exemption. See the IRS LLC reporting guidance and the FICA and FUTA family-employment regulations.

Federal income-tax withholding is a separate question from FICA or FUTA. Business wages paid to a child are generally subject to withholding regardless of age. An employee claiming exemption on Form W-4 generally must have had no federal income-tax liability in the prior year and expect none in the current year. Being young, dependent, or paid below a particular amount does not by itself establish that exemption. Retain a valid W-4, renew an exempt certificate as required, and check state rules separately.

2026 Earned Income and Kiddie Tax

For 2026, a dependent's basic standard deduction is generally the greater of $1,350 or earned income plus $450, capped at the applicable basic standard deduction, ordinarily $16,100 for a single filer. Other income, filing status, age or blindness, and eligibility to use the standard deduction can change the result. A zero federal income-tax calculation does not remove payroll reporting or applicable state tax obligations.

Wages for real work are earned income, not the unearned income targeted by the kiddie tax. In 2026, unearned income over $2,700 may require Form 8615 and parental-rate treatment of net unearned income when the age, support, filing, and other conditions apply. This includes certain age-18 children and full-time students ages 19 through 23. Hiring a child does not exempt their investment income. See IRS Topic 553.

Retirement Contributions Are Limited, Not Automatically Tax-Free

For 2026, the combined traditional and Roth IRA contribution limit for someone under 50 is $7,500, limited further by eligible compensation and applicable rules. An 18-year-old with $20,000 of eligible wages might contribute up to $7,500 if otherwise eligible. Roth eligibility depends on income; a traditional IRA deduction can be limited. Roth contributions are after-tax, qualified Roth distributions may be tax-free, and pre-tax retirement contributions generally defer rather than eliminate tax. See the IRS 2026 limits.

401(k) participation depends on the plan's terms and eligibility rules, not family status. The ordinary 2026 employee deferral limit is $24,500, also limited by compensation and shared with the person's other applicable plans. SEP employer contributions are not ordinary employee salary deferrals. Evaluate coverage, nondiscrimination, controlled-group and affiliated-service-group rules before assuming only family members can benefit.

Social Security credits arise from covered earnings. A child's wages excluded from Social Security under the family exception do not build credits through those excluded wages. The income-tax result, IRA compensation rules, and Social Security coverage are separate tests.

S-Corporation Wages and QBI

S-corporation shareholder-employees must receive reasonable compensation for actual services before using nonwage distributions as a planning tool. Paying relatives does not replace the owner's own reasonable-compensation analysis. Wages can reduce business QBI, while qualifying W-2 wages can affect a wage limitation when it applies. Wages are not themselves the recipient's QBI, and paying more payroll is not a dollar-for-dollar increase in the deduction. See the IRS QBI overview.

Records and Decisions Before Payroll Starts

Document duties, actual hours, work samples, market pay comparisons, approvals, and payment records. Reconcile payroll filings with the books. Review federal and state child-labor rules, state unemployment and workers' compensation, and the correct W-4, W-2, deposit, and return requirements. Family ownership is not a substitute for employee classification or evidence of actual work.

Ask your advisor for a before-and-after family tax comparison, a payroll treatment decision for each worker, and a retirement eligibility review. Bring the entity classification, owner and employee roles, wage history, proposed duties, and plan documents. Use a secure document channel for personal records. Start with the family payroll implementation guide or the child-employment checklist.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does paying my spouse $100,000 automatically save $15,300?

No. Recompute the owner's self-employment tax and include employer and employee payroll taxes, wage bases, other earnings, QBI, state taxes, and costs. Spouses filing jointly combine income, so the wages do not receive a separate lower spouse bracket.

Does hiring a child remove kiddie tax on investments?

No. Legitimate wages are earned income, but the child's investment and other unearned income still need separate review. For 2026, unearned income over $2,700 may trigger Form 8615 when the age, support, filing, and other conditions apply.

How much can a child contribute to an IRA in 2026?

For someone under age 50, combined traditional and Roth IRA contributions are generally limited to the lesser of $7,500 or eligible compensation for 2026. Roth income eligibility, traditional IRA deductibility, and other applicable rules still matter.

Do exempt child wages build Social Security credits?

Wages excluded from Social Security under the qualifying family-employment exception do not build credits through those excluded wages. Covered earnings are treated differently. IRA compensation eligibility and income-tax treatment are separate questions.
