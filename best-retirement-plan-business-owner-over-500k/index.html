Retirement Planning Above $500K: Funding Decisions Before Choosing a Plan

AE Tax Advisors · Tax Planning & Strategy · Updated November 6, 2026

Earning more than $500,000 does not establish which retirement plan is best or how much you can deduct. Start by identifying whether that figure means business revenue, business profit, owner wages or household income. Those measures affect the analysis differently. A larger pension commitment can be inappropriate even in a strong profit year if the business needs cash for employees, debt or real estate.

Decide how much cash can stay committed

Prepare a multi-year forecast after owner compensation, tax payments, operating reserves and planned purchases. Separate recurring profit from a one-time contract, asset sale or unusually strong commission year. Compare the cash available for retirement funding in a normal year and a downturn, not only the deduction available this December.

For an owner also purchasing a building, place the down payment, improvements, loan payments and retirement obligations in the same cash forecast. Depreciation may change taxable income without supplying cash for a pension contribution. Ask how deductions interact with QBI and state taxes; do not multiply every proposed deduction by a single headline rate.

Use the 2026 limits as boundaries, not promises

The 2026 401(k) elective-deferral limit is $24,500. If the plan permits, the general age-50 catch-up is $8,000; participants attaining ages 60 through 63 have a $11,250 catch-up instead. Defined-contribution annual additions generally cannot exceed the lesser of $72,000 or 100% of compensation, excluding permitted catch-up contributions. Employee deferrals count within annual additions; they are not added again above that limit.

SEP contributions have their own employer-deduction calculation and no employee catch-up contribution. A self-employed owner's computation adjusts net earnings for the deductible part of self-employment tax and the contribution itself; it is not simply 25% of unadjusted Schedule C profit. Employer contributions for an S corporation owner depend on eligible wages, not distributions. Verify limits and calculations using IRS annual retirement-plan limits and Publication 560.

For 2026, catch-up contributions for certain higher-paid participants must be Roth, subject to the applicable prior-year wage threshold and implementation rules. A permitted contribution is therefore not automatically a current pre-tax deduction. Have payroll and the administrator confirm the participant's treatment against the IRS catch-up contribution guidance.

Test spouse and employee eligibility before sizing the benefit

A spouse working in the business may participate based on genuine services, eligible compensation and plan terms. Marriage alone does not create a second maximum contribution. Coordinate each person's deferrals across employers and verify employer contributions against the proper compensation base.

A one-participant 401(k) is not available simply because nobody else works full time. Eligible employees, including applicable long-term part-time employees, can change coverage requirements. Include all related employers in the census. Staff contributions and testing results depend on the actual design and demographics; do not assume a fixed owner share or a standard minimal employee cost.

Consider pension feasibility only after the operating forecast

A cash balance plan is a defined benefit plan, not an unlimited additional deduction. Ask the actuary for required funding, the deductible range, employee costs and downside scenarios. Combining it with a 401(k) requires coordinated deduction limits and testing; adding two advertised maximums does not establish the allowed amount.

A larger current deduction also changes the timing of taxation. Future distributions, funding risk, administrative costs and the possibility of a sale or freeze belong in the decision. Use the cash balance feasibility checklist before requesting a contribution estimate based only on age.

Put separate deadlines on the calendar

December 31 is not a universal establishment and funding deadline. Certain employer-funded plans may be adopted by the employer's return due date including extensions under applicable rules. Employee deferral elections, payroll deposits, first-year sole-proprietor exceptions, pension minimum funding and deduction timing have separate requirements. A tax-return extension does not automatically extend each obligation.

Ask the administrator to identify the employer tax year, plan year, adoption date, election deadline, deposit deadline and filing responsibilities in writing. If the desired year has ended, request a specific eligibility review rather than assuming every plan remains available retroactively.

Questions for a high-income planning review

  • How much profit is recurring, and which cash commitments must remain liquid?
  • Which compensation supports owner and spouse contributions?
  • What does the employee and related-business census change?
  • Which contributions are required, optional, deductible or Roth?
  • What happens if profits decline, employees are hired or the business is sold?
  • Who owns plan design, payroll instructions, actuarial calculations and the return deduction?

Use the retirement-plan selection review for comparing plan types and the S corporation payroll and administrator handoff for implementation. This page frames the high-income decision; those guides contain the next workstreams.

Discuss the funding decision

Describe your business, employee count, existing plans and planned cash commitments in a free discovery call. Arrange secure intake before sharing an employee census or detailed financial records. Confirm the written advisory scope and any separately priced plan-design, actuarial, administration or filing work before engaging.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does earning over $500,000 make a cash balance plan the best choice?

No. Sustainable cash flow, compensation, employee coverage, existing plans and future business decisions determine whether a pension commitment fits.

What is the 2026 401(k) annual-additions limit?

Generally the lesser of $72,000 or 100% of compensation, excluding permitted catch-up contributions. The $24,500 elective-deferral limit is part of annual additions, not an extra amount above it.

Can my spouse automatically contribute the maximum too?

No. The spouse needs genuine eligible employment or self-employment income and must satisfy plan terms. Compensation, other-plan deferrals and employer contributions require individual review.

Must every retirement plan be established and funded by December 31?

No. Adoption, employee elections, deposits, employer deductions and pension minimum funding have distinct deadlines. Confirm the rules for the specific plan, employer and tax year.
