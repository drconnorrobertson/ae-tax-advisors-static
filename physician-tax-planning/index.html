Tax Planning for Physicians and Medical Practice Owners

Make practice profits, retirement commitments and property decisions part of the same conversation. Start with how you earn your income and what you need to decide next.

Discuss My Practice and Property Tax Plan

Match the review to how you practice

Practice situationPlanning focus
Employed physician with an investment propertyEmployment income, personal use, rental activity records and loss limits.
Locum tenens or independent contractorBusiness records, estimated payments, entity suitability and benefit arrangements.
Practice owner with staffOwner compensation, retirement funding, employee obligations and operating reserves.
Practice owner buying or retaining a buildingPurchase allocation, lease terms, financing, ownership and exit planning.

Why coordinate the practice and property?

A new building, retirement contribution and business acquisition can compete for the same cash. AE’s coordinated advisory scope brings the practice and owner-level facts together before selecting a strategy. Confirm the entities, properties and tax years included, and agree how your bookkeeper, return preparer and plan administrator will participate.

Owner pay and retirement require actual practice facts

An S corporation must address reasonable compensation for shareholder services. There is no universal salary-to-distribution percentage that fits every physician. Document duties, time and the practice’s income sources. See IRS compensation guidance.

Retirement planning also requires the employee census, eligible compensation, related businesses and funding capacity. A projected owner deduction is not a complete plan-cost estimate. Coordinate the design with a qualified plan administrator and review cash balance planning.

Buying a building or adding rentals

Do not assume a rental loss offsets practice income because the owner is a physician or uses a cost segregation study. Review classification, participation and limitations. For a combined purchase, compare the practice assets and building separately, supported by the agreement and valuations. See practice-and-building allocation questions and business and rental property advisory.

Prepare the first conversation

  • Identify your employment, contractor and practice ownership income.
  • List existing properties and the purchase, refinance or sale you are considering.
  • Summarize staff, retirement plans, practice debt and expected cash needs.
  • Name your current CPA, bookkeeper and plan administrator.
  • Bring the deadline for your decision; arrange secure document delivery after scope is agreed.

Review a documented practice-owner planning roadmap. This records planning recommendations, not a guaranteed realized result.

Common questions

Can employed physicians benefit from a planning review?

The relevant questions differ from practice ownership. Start with employment income, investments, properties and the actual decision rather than assuming business-owner strategies apply.

Can I keep my existing practice CPA?

Yes, a coordinated scope can include your existing preparer. Define the record exchange and implementation responsibilities in writing.
