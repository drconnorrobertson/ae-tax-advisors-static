The Business Owners Guide To Passive Loss Rules And Real Estate Professional Status REPS

AE Tax Advisors · Tax Planning & Strategy

Start With a Current-Loss and Carryforward Review

A business owner with rentals needs two separate answers: whether this year's rental activity is nonpassive, and when older suspended passive losses can be used. Real Estate Professional Status (REPS) can help with the first question when the owner also materially participates. It does not automatically release the second category. Depreciation expense is not itself a net rental loss or a guaranteed tax saving.

How Passive Rental Losses Are Limited

Section 469 generally treats rental real estate as passive even when the owner is involved, unless an exception applies. Passive losses generally offset passive income; wages and income from a business in which the owner materially participates are not passive income. Taxable income allocated on a Schedule K-1 may be passive depending on the activity and participation. Cash distributions are not the measure of passive income, and ordinary portfolio interest, dividends, and investment gains generally are not passive activity income.

For a simplified illustration, $150,000 of otherwise allowable passive deductions against $100,000 of passive income leaves $50,000 potentially suspended, subject to other applicable rules. The special rental real estate allowance can permit up to $25,000 of eligible losses for active participants; it generally phases out between $100,000 and $150,000 of modified adjusted gross income, with separate married-filing-separately rules. This allowance is not available to every high-income investor and is not a universal cap on passive loss use.

The REPS Qualification and Participation Requirements

For the same tax year, one individual must perform more than 750 hours of services in real property trades or businesses in which that individual materially participates, and those services must exceed half of all personal services that individual performs in trades or businesses. Exactly 750 hours or exactly half does not qualify.

On a joint return, one spouse must independently meet both REPS qualification tests. Spouses cannot combine their hours to meet the more-than-750-hour or more-than-half requirements. A spouse's participation does count when testing material participation in an activity, even if the spouse has no ownership interest or the couple does not file jointly.

Real property employee services count toward the qualifying REPS hours only if the employee is a more-than-5% owner of the employer. Employment hours still count in total personal services for the more-than-half comparison even when they do not qualify for the real property numerator. Investor work, such as reviewing financial statements or monitoring an investment, generally does not count as participation unless directly involved in the activity's day-to-day management or operations. A task label alone does not establish qualifying work.

For example, 1,200 qualifying real property service hours and 800 other business hours would put real property work at 60% of 2,000 total hours and exceed 750 hours. Those arithmetic tests assume the 1,200 hours are actually qualifying services in trades or businesses in which the individual materially participates. They do not establish material participation in every separate rental property.

Material participation can be established by one of seven tests with applicable restrictions. More than 500 hours is Test 1; substantially all participation is Test 2; more than 100 hours and at least as much as every other individual is Test 3. A property manager's or contractor's participation may affect the result. Review the full tests and evidence requirements.

Grouping Is Optional and Has Continuing Consequences

Rental interests are generally separate activities. A qualifying taxpayer may elect under Regulation 1.469-9(g) to treat all interests in rental real estate as one activity, which can change the unit used to test material participation. The election is optional; REPS does not require it. It is distinct from general economic-unit grouping under Regulation 1.469-4 and does not combine every operating business and excluded-rental STR into the rental group.

Attach the required election statement to the original return for a qualifying year. The election remains binding in future qualifying years even after an intervening nonqualifying year. Revocation requires a material change in facts and circumstances and the required statement, not merely a less favorable tax result. A sale of one property within an elected activity may not be a disposition of the entire activity. Review earlier returns and election statements before making assumptions about a sale-year loss release.

Current Nonpassive Losses Are Still Limited

REPS removes the automatic rental-passive rule; only rental activities in which the taxpayer materially participates become nonpassive. It does not recharacterize all real estate income, all gains, or all depreciation automatically. Capital gain character and depreciation recapture are separate questions.

A nonpassive classification is not an unlimited deduction. Check applicable entity basis limits first, then at-risk limits and passive activity rules, followed by the excess business loss limitation under Section 461(l). Personal-use restrictions and other deduction-specific rules can also limit the result.

Former Passive Activities: What Happens to Older Losses?

When an activity becomes nonpassive, prior suspended passive losses from that activity can offset its current net income. Any remaining carryover is treated as a passive loss under the usual rules, not automatically deducted against wages merely because the owner now qualifies for REPS.

Illustration: an activity has $200,000 of suspended passive losses and later generates $40,000 of current net income when it is nonpassive. The former-passive-activity rule can permit $40,000 of the old losses against that activity's income. The remaining $160,000 is still subject to passive loss rules and may be usable against other passive income if available. These figures assume the losses have already cleared other applicable limits. A new $500,000 depreciation deduction does not make the full $200,000 carryover deductible.

Disposition, Gifts, and Death Need Separate Analysis

A fully taxable disposition of the entire interest in a passive activity to an unrelated person generally permits remaining passive losses after the required netting. Selling one property, selling to a related party, an installment sale, or a partly tax-deferred exchange may produce a different result. Check grouping, amount recognized, and remaining basis or at-risk limitations before promising a release.

For a gift, the donor's adjusted basis in the transferred interest is generally increased immediately before the transfer by suspended passive losses; the donor does not take a current deduction for those losses. The recipient's basis still follows the applicable gift-basis rules. At death, the deduction for suspended passive losses is reduced by the basis increase allowed to the recipient; only the excess may be deductible. These outcomes are not determined merely by when an heir later sells.

Coordinate the Operating Business Without Ignoring Hours

An S-corporation election for a consulting business does not establish REPS. Time devoted to that business affects the owner's more-than-half calculation, and material participation governs whether its income is passive. S-corporation compensation, basis, and employment-tax requirements remain separate. Coordinate the two businesses rather than assuming that one election unlocks all rental deductions.

Annual Evidence and a Preparer-Ready Loss Ledger

Publication 925 permits any reasonable method that establishes the services performed and approximate hours, including calendars, appointment books, or a supported narrative. Contemporaneous daily time reports are not mandatory, but timely records backed by booking messages, work orders, invoices, and emails are stronger than unsupported year-end estimates. Do not double-count overlapping time.

Maintain a separate ledger for each activity's current loss, basis-limited amounts, at-risk carryovers, passive carryovers, and any excess business loss carryforward. Keep filed elections and identify the year each loss arose. A nonqualifying year can create new passive losses; a later qualifying year does not erase their history. Recheck REPS and material participation annually.

Bring prior returns, Forms 8582 and 6198, entity basis schedules, depreciation schedules, operating-business and real-estate calendars, management contracts, and planned sales to a review. Ask for a written conclusion by activity and tax year before making an acquisition or accelerating deductions.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the requirements for real estate professional status?

For the same tax year, one individual must perform more than 750 hours of services in real property trades or businesses in which that individual materially participates, and those services must exceed half of all personal services that individual performs in trades or businesses. Exactly 750 hours or exactly half does not qualify. Qualification alone does not make every rental nonpassive; material participation must also be established for each rental activity or valid elected rental group.

Can spouses combine their hours for REPS?

On a joint return, one spouse must independently meet both REPS qualification tests. Spouses cannot combine their hours to meet the more-than-750-hour or more-than-half requirements. A spouse's participation does count when testing material participation in an activity, even if the spouse has no ownership interest or the couple does not file jointly.

What happens to suspended passive losses?

Becoming nonpassive does not automatically release prior suspended passive losses. Prior losses from a former passive activity can offset current net income from that same activity; the remaining carryover is treated as a passive loss under the usual rules. A fully taxable disposition of the entire interest to an unrelated person has separate release rules. Other loss limits still apply.

What documentation does the IRS accept for hours?

Publication 925 permits any reasonable method that establishes the services performed and approximate hours, including calendars, appointment books, or a supported narrative. Contemporaneous daily time reports are not mandatory, but timely records backed by booking messages, work orders, invoices, and emails are stronger than unsupported year-end estimates. Do not double-count overlapping time.
