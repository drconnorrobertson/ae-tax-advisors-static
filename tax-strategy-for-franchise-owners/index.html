Franchise Tax Advisory: Multi-Unit and Property Review

AE Tax Advisors Team · Published 2026-10-03 · Updated 2026-11-06

Before signing for another franchise unit or buying its premises, define a tax advisory engagement that covers the acquisition, buildout, ownership group and owner cash flow together. The aim is a documented decision before commitments are made, rather than a larger deduction discovered after opening.

When a coordinated review is useful

This scope fits an owner operating several units, adding a location, buying an existing franchise or considering ownership of the real estate. It is especially relevant when one management company supports multiple operating entities. A franchisor's financial model does not establish your tax treatment, employee-plan obligations or usable deductions.

Specify the deliverables for your next unit

  • Acquisition and buildout classification: request a schedule separating franchise rights, recurring charges, equipment, improvements, land and building costs, with unresolved allocations identified.
  • Unit and owner cash-flow comparison: request alternatives showing the timing of deductions alongside debt service, opening costs and operating reserves.
  • Ownership and employee review: include all units and related companies in the facts supplied for retirement-plan and entity analysis.
  • Implementation responsibilities: identify who owns depreciation schedules, plan administration, tax elections and return preparation, and which work needs a separate engagement.

Discuss these proposed deliverables and obtain a signed scope with fees and deadlines. Do not assume a consultation includes transaction diligence, legal review, a cost-segregation study or retirement-plan administration.

For the technical background, read the industry tax-strategy guide. This page focuses on selecting and scoping an owner-and-property advisory engagement.

Decision 1: classify the acquisition before closing

Do not apply bonus depreciation to an entire franchise acquisition price. Acquired amortizable section 197 intangibles generally use a 15-year recovery period. Asset-purchase allocations and agreement terms matter. Review the IRS Form 4562 instructions and IRS Publication 544 before classifying the acquisition.

Decision 2: establish the buildout treatment

Qualified improvement property generally involves an improvement to the interior of an existing nonresidential building after that building was first placed in service. Enlargements, elevators or escalators, and the internal structural framework are excluded. New construction is not automatically QIP. Keep construction invoices detailed enough to distinguish equipment, eligible interior improvements and building costs.

Eligible property acquired and placed in service after January 19, 2025 generally qualifies for 100% federal bonus depreciation. Acquisition rules, related-party restrictions and elections can change that result. Section 179 has separate limits, and states may not follow federal treatment. A deduction also does not reimburse the purchase cost or make an unprofitable unit worth opening. See IRS Publication 946.

Decision 3: compare owning and leasing the location

A separate property company can make the lease and ownership structure clearer, but it does not by itself create a deductible rental loss against franchise profit. Review the lease, rent, payments and ownership. Self-rental rules generally make net rent nonpassive when the owner materially participates in the tenant business; net losses are not automatically nonpassive. Depreciation deductions remain subject to basis, at-risk, passive and other loss limits. See IRS Publication 925.

Model a proposed cost-segregation study against expected holding period, available income and potential disposition taxes. Use actual depreciable basis and supported asset classifications, not a percentage of the property's advertised value. Review debt and business-interest limitations across the operating and property entities.

Scope the ownership group, not just one unit

Provide the plan administrator with ownership of every unit, management company and property company, plus the complete employee census. Controlled-group and affiliated-service-group rules can require businesses to be considered together. Moving the owner to a management company is not a reliable way to exclude unit employees from retirement coverage.

For 2026, the general annual-additions limit for defined-contribution plans is $72,000 before eligible catch-ups, with a $24,500 employee deferral limit. Employee deferrals are generally limited per person across plans, not multiplied by the number of franchises. Compensation, eligibility, testing and employer funding determine the permitted contribution. An owner-only plan is unsuitable if eligible employees must participate. See IRS 2026 retirement limits, IRS one-participant plan rules and IRS Publication 560.

Request a combined owner and exit projection

An S-corporation owner who works in the business needs reasonable compensation supported by actual responsibilities, including supervising managers and opening units. The payroll-tax difference is not a flat 15.3% of distributions. Consider wage-base limits, other earnings, state costs and benefit-plan effects. A C corporation's 21% federal rate is not the owner's final effective tax rate when distributions or a sale are considered. See IRS S-corporation compensation guidance.

For pass-through entities, QBI depends on the underlying business, eligible income and applicable wage/property limitations, not the franchise label. Deductions may reduce QBI as well as taxable income. Review each unit and any proposed aggregation using IRS QBI guidance; separately analyze rental eligibility and an eventual asset sale.

Documents for scoping a multi-unit review

  • Franchise agreements, asset-purchase allocation and closing statements.
  • Lease, landlord allowances and itemized buildout invoices.
  • Unit-level profit and loss reports, debt payments and cash reserves.
  • Ownership chart and employee census across all related businesses.
  • Existing depreciation schedules, suspended losses and retirement documents.

Frequently asked questions

What should the signed advisory engagement include?

Specify the entities and decisions covered, written deliverables, assumptions, responsible parties, fees and deadlines. Identify separately any filing, legal, valuation, cost-segregation or retirement-plan administration work rather than assuming it is included.

Can I write off the entire cost of buying a franchise?

No. Equipment, real estate, franchise rights and goodwill have different recovery rules. Amortizable section 197 intangibles generally use 15-year amortization rather than bonus depreciation.

Does every franchise remodel qualify as QIP?

No. Review the building and each improvement. Qualified improvement property excludes enlargements, elevators or escalators, and the internal structural framework.

Can each franchise LLC create a separate owner-only retirement limit?

Not automatically. Related-employer rules, employee coverage and per-person elective-deferral limits must be reviewed across the ownership group.
