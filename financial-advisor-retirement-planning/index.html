Financial Advisor Retirement Plans: Owner Benefits and Staff Funding

By AE Tax Advisors · Published 2026-05-05 · Updated November 8, 2026.

An advisory firm's recurring revenue can help forecasting, but it does not guarantee the affordability or deduction of a cash balance contribution. Model owner and employee obligations together.

Reconcile compensation and existing plans

Identify wages, partnership earnings and investment distributions separately. Review contributions through broker-dealers, affiliated firms and outside employers. Apply individual and plan limits before adding another arrangement.

Use the actual employee census

Provide ownership, age, service and compensation data for advisers, client-service staff and related businesses. Coverage and nondiscrimination testing can change the proposed allocation. A small firm's staff cost cannot be assumed from an industry percentage.

Stress-test cash balance funding

Ask the actuary to model lower revenue, changes in staffing, investment performance and the expected retirement date. The benefit formula and funding assumptions determine contributions; a stated annual benefit limit is not an owner contribution allowance.

Coordinate tax and succession consequences

Deductible retirement contributions may also affect qualified business income where that deduction is available. Review plan obligations during an internal buyout or external sale. Compare current tax deferral with future distributions, liquidity and the cost of maintaining the plan.

Request an advisory-firm plan review with the census, compensation reports, existing plan documents and succession timeline.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a fixed staff-cost percentage reliable for every firm?

No. Coverage, compensation and plan design require a firm-specific calculation.

Does predictable revenue eliminate funding risk?

No. Investment results, staffing and benefit obligations can change funding requirements.
