Section 707: Partner Payments, Loans & Disguised Sales

By AE Tax Advisors · Published 2024-01-29

Updated November 8, 2026.

A payment between a partnership and an owner is not automatically a guaranteed payment or a distribution. Section 707 requires the parties to identify the capacity in which the partner acts and the substance of the transaction. That classification controls the deduction, income character and reporting.

Distinguish three common payment types

  • Distributive share: The partner receives an allocation of partnership income or loss under the agreement and applicable allocation rules.
  • Guaranteed payment: An amount for services or the use of capital is determined without regard to partnership income under Section 707(c).
  • Separate-capacity transaction: Under Section 707(a), a partner can deal with the partnership in a capacity other than as a partner, such as a properly characterized sale, service arrangement or bona fide loan.

Interest on a genuine partner loan is not automatically a guaranteed payment merely because its amount is fixed. Review the debt instrument, creditor rights, repayment terms and actual conduct. Similarly, a fixed service payment may require distinguishing a partner-capacity guaranteed payment from a separate-capacity fee.

A guaranteed payment is not always currently deductible

The partner generally includes the payment in income under the applicable timing rules. At the partnership, Section 162 deductibility is subject to capitalization rules, including Section 263. A payment connected with creating or improving an asset may need capitalization rather than a current operating expense deduction. Self-employment and other tax treatment require their own analysis.

Reconcile the agreement, general ledger, Form 1065 and each Schedule K-1. Do not use book capital as a substitute for the partner's outside-basis schedule.

Review property contributions paired with cash or debt

A property contribution followed or preceded by money or other consideration can be treated as a disguised sale under Section 707(a)(2)(B). Transfers within two years generally carry a sale presumption unless the facts clearly establish otherwise; transfers more than two years apart generally carry the opposite presumption, subject to the rules. The timing is not a standalone safe harbor.

Liability assumptions, debt-financed distributions and qualified liabilities have detailed rules. A mortgage transfer does not automatically make the entire transaction taxable or entirely tax-deferred. Review the documents and relevant regulatory exceptions before computing the sale portion and gain.

Apply the related-party rules accurately

Section 707(b) can disallow losses on property sales between a partnership and a person owning more than 50% of capital or profits, or between qualifying commonly owned partnerships. Attribution rules matter. This is not a general rule that a loss becomes deductible after waiting two years.

Gain can be ordinary when the ownership condition is met and the property is not a capital asset in the transferee's hands. The transferee's use is important; do not assume every partner-to-partnership gain remains capital gain.

Documents to review before payment or closing

  1. Partnership agreement, ownership and related-party attribution schedule.
  2. Service, loan or purchase contracts and support for pricing.
  3. Property basis, fair market value and liability details.
  4. Dates and amounts of connected contributions and distributions.
  5. Deduction versus capitalization analysis and K-1 reporting.

For a contribution, also review Section 721. For distributions, start with Section 731. Request a coordinated partnership and owner review before treating a payment as deductible or tax-deferred.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is every fixed payment to a partner a guaranteed payment?

No. The partner’s capacity and transaction substance matter. A bona fide loan, separate-capacity service fee and partner-capacity guaranteed payment can have different treatment.

Are guaranteed payments always immediately deductible?

No. Deductibility is subject to capitalization and other applicable rules.

Does waiting two years automatically avoid disguised-sale treatment?

No. The regulations use rebuttable timing presumptions and detailed exceptions; the full facts still matter.
