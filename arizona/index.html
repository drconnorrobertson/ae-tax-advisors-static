Cost Segregation for Arizona Real Estate Investors

Arizona tax advisory for real estate investors and business owners | Updated August 2026

Arizona decouples from federal bonus depreciation, which sounds like bad news until you look at the rate. At a flat 2.5%, Arizona has the lowest individual income tax rate of any state that imposes one. The state cost of the addback is roughly one-fifth of what the same addback costs in California.

That combination makes Arizona one of the simplest states in which to justify a cost segregation study. The federal deduction is untouched, the state drag is small enough to be nearly a rounding error in the model, and Arizona offers a pass-through entity tax at the same flat rate for owners of operating businesses.

How Arizona Income Tax Interacts With Federal Strategy

Arizona imposes a flat 2.5% individual income tax, the result of the accelerated flat-tax transition completed in recent years. It is the lowest rate among the states that tax income. There is no preferential rate for capital gains, though Arizona has historically allowed a subtraction for a portion of long-term capital gains from assets acquired after 2011.

The practical significance for cost segregation is the ratio between federal benefit and state cost. A $500,000 federal bonus deduction is worth up to $185,000 federally at the top marginal rate. The Arizona addback on that same amount costs $12,500. The federal benefit is roughly fifteen times the state cost.

For comparison, the same $500,000 addback costs a California resident up to $66,500 and a New Jersey resident up to $53,750. Arizona's rate structure is the single biggest reason the analysis here is easier than in most decoupling states.

Arizona Pass-Through Entity Tax

Arizona permits partnerships and S corporations to elect to pay Arizona income tax at the entity level. The rate is a flat 2.5%, matching the individual rate. Owners receive an Arizona credit for their share.

The election is made on a timely filed return, including extensions. Arizona has one procedural requirement that catches filers: all partners and shareholders must be notified of the election at least 60 days before the entity files. That notice requirement has no counterpart in most states and it is a genuine trap, because it cannot be cured retroactively.

At a 2.5% rate the absolute dollars recovered through the Arizona PTET are smaller than in high-tax states. It remains worth electing for profitable pass-throughs, but Arizona owners should weight their planning effort toward the federal side, where the leverage is.

Arizona Depreciation Conformity

Arizona does not conform to IRC Sec. 168(k). Arizona requires an addition to income to reverse the effect of federal bonus depreciation, and Arizona depreciation is then computed separately using the allowable federal method without the bonus allowance. That produces a separate Arizona basis for each asset.

Arizona updated its conformity date to the Internal Revenue Code as of January 1, 2026. In connection with that update, for tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2026, Arizona also decouples from the qualified production property bonus depreciation created by IRC Sec. 168(n) under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act.

Arizona does allow the shorter recovery periods identified in a cost segregation study. The reclassification into 5, 7, and 15-year property applies for Arizona purposes on regular MACRS. What Arizona withholds is the immediate write-off, not the class life.

Cost Segregation Considerations Specific to Arizona

Arizona's cost segregation profile is shaped by its property mix and its climate as much as by its tax rules.

First, the short-term rental concentration. Scottsdale, Sedona, Phoenix, and the Tucson corridor host one of the densest short-term rental markets in the country, and short-term rentals are where cost segregation produces its highest reclassification percentages. Furnished properties routinely reach 25% to 40% of purchase price in 5, 7, and 15-year property because the furniture, appliances, window treatments, and finish work are all short-life assets. Arizona investors are disproportionately in exactly the property class where studies pay best.

Second, the IRC Sec. 469 analysis is where Arizona investors should spend their attention. Whether a large first-year loss is usable against non-passive income turns on the seven-day average stay test and material participation, not on anything Arizona does. Arizona conforms to the federal passive activity rules, so a suspended federal loss is suspended for Arizona as well. Given how small the Arizona rate is, the entire economic question is federal.

Third, site improvements are unusually valuable in Arizona. Desert landscaping, hardscape, pool and spa installations, exterior lighting, irrigation, and paving are 15-year land improvements. Arizona properties carry more of this per dollar of purchase price than properties in most climates, and generic studies that focus on interior finish systematically undercount it. Ask specifically how the study handled exterior and site work.

Fourth, pool and outdoor living infrastructure deserves separate mention because it is frequently misclassified as part of the building shell. On an Arizona short-term rental where the pool is a primary amenity, that misclassification can move six figures of basis onto a 27.5 or 39-year life that belongs on 15.

Arizona Cost Segregation and Tax Questions

Does Arizona allow bonus depreciation?

No. Arizona decouples from IRC Sec. 168(k) and requires an addition to income to reverse the federal bonus deduction, with Arizona depreciation computed separately without the bonus. For tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2026, Arizona also decouples from the Sec. 168(n) qualified production property rules created by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act.

How much does the Arizona addback actually cost me?

Very little relative to the federal benefit. At Arizona's flat 2.5% rate, a $500,000 bonus addback costs $12,500 in Arizona tax, against a federal deduction worth up to $185,000. The same addback costs a California resident up to $66,500. Arizona has the lowest rate of any state that taxes income.

What is the Arizona PTET notice requirement?

All partners and shareholders must be notified of the entity-level election at least 60 days before the entity files its return. This requirement has no counterpart in most states and it cannot be cured after the fact, so it is the most common reason an otherwise valid Arizona election fails.

Are Arizona short-term rentals good candidates for cost segregation?

They are among the best. Furnished short-term rentals commonly reclassify 25% to 40% of purchase price into 5, 7, and 15-year property, versus 15% to 25% for a typical long-term residential rental. Arizona's Scottsdale, Sedona, Phoenix, and Tucson markets are heavily weighted toward exactly this property type.

Does Arizona landscaping and pool work count as short-life property?

Yes, and it is routinely undercounted. Desert landscaping, hardscape, pools and spas, exterior lighting, irrigation, and paving are 15-year land improvements rather than part of the 27.5 or 39-year building shell. Arizona properties carry more of this per dollar of purchase price than most climates, so ask specifically how a proposed study handles exterior and site work.
