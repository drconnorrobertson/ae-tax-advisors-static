Cost Segregation for Nevada Real Estate Investors

Nevada tax advisory for real estate investors and business owners | Updated August 2026

Nevada has no individual income tax and no corporate income tax. For a cost segregation study, that makes Nevada the simplest state in the country: there is no state addback, no decoupling form, no second depreciation schedule, and no state-level analysis to run at all. Whatever the federal number is, that is the number.

The planning in Nevada is therefore not about depreciation conformity. It is about residency, about the business taxes Nevada does impose, and about the other states you may still owe.

How Nevada Taxation Interacts With Federal Strategy

Nevada's constitution prohibits a personal income tax, and the state has no corporate income tax. There is no state tax on wages, business income, rental income, capital gains, or depreciation recapture.

What Nevada does impose is a set of business taxes that do not touch depreciation. The Commerce Tax applies to Nevada gross revenue above $4 million, at industry-specific rates ranging from 0.051% to 0.331%. Real estate businesses fall within one of the twenty-six business categories. Because the Commerce Tax is imposed on gross revenue rather than net income, depreciation of any kind, accelerated or otherwise, provides no relief against it.

The Modified Business Tax applies to wages at 1.17% for general businesses and 1.554% for financial institutions and mining, after a quarterly wage deduction. Again, unrelated to depreciation.

Nevada property is subject to real property transfer tax at closing and to property tax administered at the county level, with a statutory cap on annual increases in the tax bill.

Nevada Pass-Through Entity Tax

Nevada has no pass-through entity tax and does not need one. The purpose of a PTET is to move state income tax outside the federal individual state and local tax deduction cap. Nevada imposes no state income tax on pass-through income, so there is nothing to move.

Nevada entity owners should still consider PTET elections in other states where the entity does business or where the property is located. A Nevada LLC holding an Oregon apartment building still faces Oregon tax on Oregon source income, and the Oregon PTE-E election may be available to that entity.

This is the point most Nevada investors miss. The entity's state of formation does not determine where its income is taxed. Property location and the owner's residency do.

Nevada Depreciation Conformity

There is nothing to conform to. Nevada has no income tax, so it has no depreciation rules, no Section 168(k) position, no Section 179 limit, and no basis tracking requirement.

Federal bonus depreciation is fully effective for a Nevada taxpayer with no state offset in either direction. You maintain one depreciation schedule. When you sell, there is no state gain computation, no recapture addback, and no divergence between federal and state basis to reconcile.

The practical result is that a Nevada investor captures the entire federal benefit of a cost segregation study and pays none of the state-level cost that investors in California, Maryland, Massachusetts, or Indiana absorb. Nevada is as clean as the strategy gets.

Cost Segregation Considerations Specific to Nevada

Because the state analysis is empty, the Nevada-specific work is elsewhere.

First, residency and domicile. Nevada is a destination state for people leaving California, New York, and other high-tax jurisdictions, and the former state does not always agree that you left. California in particular applies a facts-and-circumstances domicile analysis and audits departures aggressively, looking at where you spend your days, where your family lives, where your professional licenses and vehicle registrations sit, and where your closest connections are. A cost segregation study that generates a large deduction in a year your residency is contested is exactly the kind of item that draws attention. Establish the residency cleanly before, not during.

Second, out-of-state property. If you are a Nevada resident who owns real estate in a taxing state, that state taxes the income and the eventual gain regardless of where you live and regardless of where the LLC is formed. Your cost segregation study has to be modeled against that state's conformity rules, not against Nevada's absence of them. A Nevada resident with a California rental faces the full California decoupling analysis.

Third, the reverse case. A nonresident who owns Nevada property gets Nevada's zero state tax on the rental income and the gain, which makes Nevada property unusually attractive on an after-tax basis relative to identical property in a taxing state. That differential is worth quantifying when comparing acquisition targets across states.

Fourth, the passive activity rules still apply. Nevada's lack of an income tax does nothing to help you use a federal loss. If the loss is suspended under IRC Sec. 469 because you do not qualify for short-term rental treatment or real estate professional status, it is suspended, and the study produces a suspended loss carryforward rather than a current deduction. This is the same gate as everywhere and it is where Nevada investors most often go wrong, because the state simplicity creates a false sense that the strategy is automatic.

Nevada Cost Segregation and Tax Questions

Does Nevada allow bonus depreciation on a cost segregation study?

The question does not apply. Nevada has no individual or corporate income tax, so it has no depreciation rules, no Section 168(k) position, and no Section 179 limit. Federal bonus depreciation is fully effective with no state addback, no decoupling form, and no second depreciation schedule. Nevada captures the entire federal benefit of a study with none of the state-level cost.

Does Nevada have a pass-through entity tax?

No, and it does not need one. A PTET exists to move state income tax outside the federal individual state and local tax deduction cap, and Nevada imposes no income tax on pass-through income. Nevada entity owners should still evaluate PTET elections in other states where the entity does business or where property is located, since the state of formation does not determine where income is taxed.

I am a Nevada resident with rental property in California. Does Nevada's lack of income tax help me?

Not for that property. California taxes California source rental income and the eventual gain regardless of your residency and regardless of where the LLC is formed, and California fully decouples from bonus depreciation. Your cost segregation study has to be modeled against California's rules, not Nevada's absence of them. Property location drives state taxation, not entity formation state.

Does the Nevada Commerce Tax reduce with depreciation deductions?

No. The Commerce Tax applies to Nevada gross revenue above $4 million at industry-specific rates from 0.051% to 0.331%, and it is imposed on gross revenue rather than net income. Depreciation of any kind, accelerated or otherwise, provides no relief against it. The same is true of the Modified Business Tax, which applies to wages rather than to income.

What should a former California resident watch for after moving to Nevada?

Domicile. California applies a facts-and-circumstances analysis to residency departures and audits them aggressively, examining where you spend your days, where your family lives, and where your registrations, licenses, and closest connections sit. A large cost segregation deduction claimed in a year when your residency is contested draws attention, so the residency position should be established cleanly before the study year rather than during it.
