Defined Benefit Plans: Administration, Funding and Exit Responsibilities

AE Tax Advisors · Tax Planning & Strategy

A defined benefit plan creates a retirement-benefit promise and ongoing employer responsibilities. Its administration should be clear before the first deduction is claimed: who maintains the document, calculates funding, tests employee coverage, signs filings and handles a later freeze or termination?

Understand what the legal limits measure

Section 414(j) defines a defined benefit plan; Section 414(b) concerns controlled groups, not the basic definition. Related-employer rules can affect which employees and plans must be considered together.

The Section 415(b) annual-benefit limit generally uses the lesser of the indexed dollar limit and 100% of the participant's average compensation for the highest three consecutive years, with applicable adjustments for age, service and benefit form. For 2026 the dollar limit is $290,000. This limits a retirement benefit, not an automatically deductible annual contribution. See Section 415 and the IRS annual limits.

A solo 401(k) does not permit a $185,000 annual contribution merely because the owner has high income. For 2026, defined-contribution annual additions generally cannot exceed the lesser of $72,000 or 100% of compensation, apart from permitted catch-up contributions. Deferral, employer-deduction and related-plan rules also apply. Any combined pension/401(k) design needs its own calculation.

Assign the annual work before engaging providers

Responsible roleWork to specify
Employer and plan administratorMaintain accurate census and compensation records, authorize contributions, distribute required notices and monitor provider completion.
Enrolled actuaryPerform the valuation, calculate funding requirements and applicable deduction limits, and complete required actuarial certifications.
Third-party administrator and plan counselMaintain documents and amendments, test eligibility and nondiscrimination, determine required filings and manage participant-benefit procedures within their engagement.
Tax preparerReconcile payments and deductible amounts with the employer return, related plans and carryovers; identify any correction or excise-tax filing.
Trustee, custodian and investment professionalsProvide accurate plan-asset and transaction records and perform their agreed fiduciary or administrative duties. Tax planning does not select investments.

Hiring providers does not automatically discharge the employer's responsibilities. Request a dated completion checklist and retain the documents actually filed, not just preliminary calculations.

Determine filings and PBGC coverage

The applicable Form 5500-series return and actuarial schedules depend on plan facts. A one-participant plan may have different filing rules; do not assume every plan uses the same form or that an owner-only plan is exempt from every reporting requirement. Identify required participant disclosures, funding notices, PBGC filings and any additional tax forms with the administrator. Use IRS Publication 560 as a starting point.

PBGC covers most private-sector defined benefit plans, subject to statutory exemptions. Being small does not alone establish an exemption. Review ownership, professional-service status, participant history and any other applicable exception using PBGC's coverage guidance. Covered single-employer plans have a $111 per-participant flat-rate premium for plan years beginning in 2026; variable-rate premiums may also apply, subject to exemptions and caps. Confirm the applicable year and calculation in PBGC premium rates.

Funding, vesting and administration costs

The actuarial funding obligation can continue in a low-profit year. A tax-return extension is not a blanket extension of pension funding deadlines. Calendar the plan's contribution, reporting and notice deadlines separately, and resolve missed obligations with qualified advisers rather than simply skipping a contribution.

Apply the plan's vesting terms and statutory requirements to each participant. Do not assume every defined benefit plan is immediately vested or that one generic schedule fits traditional and cash balance designs. Accrued benefits and protected benefit forms constrain amendments.

Budgeting estimates for a simple single-owner plan are $1,500 to $3,500 annually for actuarial services and $2,500 to $5,000 for total annual compliance work. The actuarial component may already be included in the total, so do not automatically add the ranges together. These are estimates requiring current written quotes. Confirm participant count, legal work, PBGC premiums, investment-related fees and special-project exclusions; a larger deduction does not make costs irrelevant.

Freeze and termination are different projects

A prospective freeze may limit future accruals, subject to required amendments, notices and protected-benefit rules. It does not eliminate funding shortfalls or annual administration for existing benefits.

A PBGC-covered standard termination requires sufficient assets to satisfy plan benefits and a formal notice, filing and distribution process. Identify the proposed termination date, participant calculations, contribution needs, permitted benefit forms and final reporting before moving assets. Noncovered plans still have applicable IRS qualification and termination requirements. Rollovers depend on distribution eligibility and elections; they are not automatic. See PBGC standard termination guidance.

Commission an administration and exit-readiness review

Bring the plan document, amendments, employee census, latest valuation, asset statement, filed returns, notices and contribution history. If a business sale, retirement or funding shortfall is approaching, include that decision date. Ask for a responsibility map, open-item list and calendar, with separately quoted corrective or termination work.

For initial funding feasibility rather than an existing-plan review, use the cash balance feasibility guide. Compare alternatives in the business-owner retirement planning guide.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the annual benefit limit an annual contribution allowance?

No. The Section 415(b) limit restricts the retirement benefit. Required funding and deductible contributions depend on actuarial calculations and applicable deduction rules.

Does every defined benefit plan pay PBGC premiums?

No. Statutory exemptions can apply. Covered single-employer plans have a 2026 flat-rate premium of $111 per participant and may owe a variable-rate premium, subject to applicable rules.

Does freezing a plan end its funding and filing duties?

No. A freeze generally concerns future accruals. Existing benefit obligations, funding and administration can continue until a properly completed termination.

Can plan assets automatically roll into the owner’s IRA at termination?

No. Termination requires the applicable funding, notice, filing and benefit-distribution procedures. Rollover availability depends on distribution eligibility and participant elections.
