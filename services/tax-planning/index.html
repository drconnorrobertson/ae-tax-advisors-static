Strategic Tax Planning for Business Owners

Start with the decision that matters.

Who this is for

Owners with growing operating businesses, several income sources, or a material change in profitability who want a coordinated review before making elections, purchases, or distributions.

When to begin

A stronger earnings year, a new entity, an acquisition, or a planned owner distribution changes the tax and cash-flow picture.

The question we help you work through

Which actions fit this year, which need more evidence, and which create future obligations that outweigh the immediate benefit?

From evidence to action

A disciplined review with a practical handoff.

01

Establish a reliable baseline

Reconcile the most recent filed returns with year-to-date books, owner payroll, investment income, and estimated payments. Identify differences between taxable income, accounting profit, and cash available to act.

02

Compare planning alternatives

Evaluate the interaction among compensation, retirement funding, reimbursements, depreciation, and entity choices. Compare realistic alternatives using the same assumptions rather than adding unrelated headline deductions.

03

Separate permanent savings from timing

Distinguish a lower lifetime tax cost from a deduction that moves between years. Model the future income, funding requirements, and disposition consequences that could change the recommendation.

04

Coordinate implementation

Assign each election, payroll adjustment, document request, and return item to an accountable person. Coordinate with the existing preparer, bookkeeper, attorney, or plan administrator where their work is required.

05

Revisit the plan when facts change

Define review points around earnings changes, transactions, and filing dates. A recommendation based on a forecast should be reconsidered when the actual results or ownership change.

A productive first conversation

Bring the context. We will define the next step.

Start with a short description of the decision, the people and entities involved, and the date by which you need an answer. These records help establish whether the work is ready for analysis:

  • Recent individual and business returns, including supporting schedules
  • Current profit-and-loss statements, balance sheets, and debt schedules
  • Ownership chart, entity elections, payroll, and owner distributions
  • Planned purchases, transactions, retirement funding, and estimated payments

Do not send tax returns, account numbers, or other sensitive records through a general inquiry. Arrange approved client-portal access before providing documents.

Clarity before commitment

Agree on the scope, deliverables, and responsibilities.

Scope the work

The consultation should establish the entities and periods covered, the analysis required, available records, proposed deliverables, and coordination with your existing advisers. The signed engagement determines what AE will provide.

Define the handoff

Confirm who implements each recommendation and who reports it on the return. Legal drafting, valuation, engineering, payroll processing, and filing work should be expressly included or assigned separately.

Understand fees and timing

Review AE’s published pricing as a starting point, then confirm the applicable fee and any separate work in writing. Delivery timing depends on the agreed scope, usable records, and review requirements.

Evaluate the result honestly

A review may identify a useful action, a documentation gap, or a reason to retain the current approach. Any financial comparison needs stated assumptions and a clear distinction between deductions, tax deferral, and realized savings.

Questions before you engage.

Will every recommendation lower my taxes?

No. A review can conclude that an attractive-sounding strategy is unsuitable, too costly, unavailable, or only a timing benefit. The relevant comparison is the supported after-tax outcome.

Can AE coordinate with my current CPA?

Discuss the intended division of responsibilities during scoping. Planning, implementation, and return preparation should each have a clearly identified owner.
