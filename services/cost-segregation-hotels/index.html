Cost Segregation for Hotels and Hospitality Properties

AE Tax Advisors Team | Updated November 3, 2026

Evaluate an acquisition, an existing hotel, or a renovation with a property-specific study and a clear tax implementation plan.

A hotel may contain guest-room furnishings, kitchen and laundry equipment, site improvements, and building systems with different depreciation treatment. Cost segregation examines the actual assets and supported costs. It does not establish a standard deduction percentage for every hotel or guarantee that an owner can use the resulting deductions immediately.

AE Tax Advisors helps owners connect the study discussion with acquisition records, existing depreciation, planned renovations and the intended holding period. Start by defining the decision: are you evaluating a purchase, correcting existing reporting, or commissioning analysis of a new project?

Start with the hotel assets, not a target percentage

Under the general depreciation system, nonresidential real property ordinarily has a 39-year recovery period. Land is not depreciable. Separately identified equipment and other qualifying property may have different recovery periods. See IRS Publication 946 for the underlying framework.

Do not assume every decorative item, lighting circuit, floor finish or exterior improvement has a shorter life. Classification depends on the asset's characteristics and use. The IRS Cost Segregation Audit Technique Guide includes hotel/casino examples and discusses study documentation. It is an examination guide, not binding law or an approval of a particular report.

A useful proposal explains the property inspected, the records reviewed, the method used to establish costs and the items outside scope. Ask whether the estimate includes furnishings that are already on a separate depreciation schedule. A room count or franchise label alone does not establish the allocation.

For a portfolio, keep a separate file for each address and owning entity. Record missing documents and unresolved allocations explicitly. Give the study team a document index so a revised invoice or updated equipment list can be traced to the relevant component.

An acquisition example that separates land and avoids double counting

Illustration only, not an expected study result: assume an acquisition has $5,000,000 of allocated purchase basis. The supported allocation assigns $1,000,000 to land, $1,000,000 to separately identified shorter-life assets, and $3,000,000 to the remaining building. These assumed amounts reconcile to $5,000,000; they are not a benchmark for hotel studies.

  • Land allocation: $1,000,000, excluded from depreciation.
  • Assumed shorter-life asset basis: $1,000,000, subject to asset eligibility, dates, elections and other applicable rules.
  • Remaining building basis: $3,000,000, requiring its own depreciation calculation.

If the entire $1,000,000 shorter-life category qualifies for 100% bonus depreciation and that treatment applies, its bonus deduction would be $1,000,000. This is not total first-year depreciation or cash tax savings. Regular depreciation on other assets, applicable conventions, deduction limitations, state treatment and fees require separate calculation. In particular, do not describe $3,000,000 divided by 39 as the first-year building deduction without applying the relevant convention.

Use the cost segregation calculator to organize an initial scenario, then replace assumptions with the supported study and return calculations.

Bonus depreciation: asset dates still matter

The IRS's Form 4562 instructions describe restored 100% bonus depreciation for certain qualified property acquired after January 19, 2025. This is not a deduction for land or the entire hotel building. Eligibility, acquisition rules, elections and the relevant service dates must be checked. A newly commissioned study does not change an older asset's history. See also Notice 2026-11 in Internal Revenue Bulletin 2026-06 and the bonus depreciation planning guide.

Existing hotels: decide the correction procedure before filing

Give the preparer the filed depreciation history, previous studies and any prior accounting-method changes. Ask for a written reconciliation of the old and proposed asset schedules. The analysis should explain what changes, which years and assets are involved, and how the adjustment was calculated.

An accounting-method change may involve Form 3115 and a section 481(a) adjustment. Automatic procedures have eligibility and filing requirements; they are not automatic approval of every correction. The IRS Form 3115 instructions explain that a negative adjustment is generally taken in the year of change, subject to applicable rules. Have the preparer confirm the current procedure and whether a method change or another correction route fits. Read amended return versus Form 3115 before choosing a filing approach.

PIP renovations: keep the new project separate

A property improvement plan is a project description, not a tax classification. Separate room furnishings, equipment, interior construction and exterior work in the invoice file. Track who owns each item, when it was completed and available for use, and whether the cost was paid by the owner, tenant or another party. Ask the preparer to assess the treatment of the actual work rather than applying one percentage to the entire renovation budget.

Maintain a removal log for replaced items, with photographs, dates and links to the old asset records where available. Ask whether any disposition treatment is available and what evidence and elections it would require. Do not assume replacing a fixture automatically produces a deduction for its remaining basis.

Compare a proposal and a complete tax implementation plan

  1. Scope: identify the addresses, entities, acquisition and renovation periods, inspection method and missing records.
  2. Deliverables: request a report, component schedules, basis reconciliation, stated assumptions and a process for preparer questions.
  3. Responsibilities: identify the study analyst and reviewer, the return preparer and the person updating the depreciation schedule.
  4. Economics: compare study and implementation fees with the modeled timing benefit, deduction usability and future sale scenario.
  5. Completion: confirm what constitutes delivery and which follow-up items or separate filing work remain.

Hotel owner questions

What percentage of a hotel can be reclassified?

There is no property-specific answer until the assets, costs, ownership and use are reviewed. Request a supported component schedule rather than relying on a standard hotel percentage. The proposal should distinguish an initial feasibility estimate from the completed study.

Does buying a hotel make the whole purchase price depreciable?

No. Land is not depreciable. A hotel acquisition may also include separately identified equipment or other assets. Reconcile the acquisition allocation and existing asset records before calculating depreciation so the same furnishings are not counted twice.

Does a new study give an older hotel the current bonus depreciation rate?

The report date does not reset the acquisition or placed-in-service history. The preparer must apply the rules relevant to the assets and their dates, including any elections and limitations. Current law does not automatically apply to an older acquisition merely because a study is completed now.

Can an existing hotel be reviewed without amending every return?

Possibly. A depreciation correction may require an accounting-method change, an amended return or another approach, depending on the facts. Form 3115 is not a universal solution. Ask the preparer to confirm the procedure, eligibility, adjustment and filing responsibilities.

What should a hotel owner bring to a consultation?

Bring the acquisition documents, allocation support, depreciation schedules, ownership details, renovation invoices and project timeline. Identify separately purchased equipment, the intended holding period, missing records, and the person preparing the return.
