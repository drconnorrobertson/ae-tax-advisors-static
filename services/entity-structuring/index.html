Multi-Entity Tax Planning and Entity Structuring

Start with the decision that matters.

Who this is for

Owners with multiple entities, related-party payments, new partners, or an acquisition who need tax coordination with their legal and accounting advisers.

When to begin

A proposed holding company, new investor, property transfer, or management arrangement adds a relationship that existing books and agreements do not yet explain.

The question we help you work through

Does the proposed structure support the commercial objective after considering tax classification, operating cost, financing, and the consequences of moving existing assets?

From evidence to action

A disciplined review with a practical handoff.

01

Map ownership and classification

Document legal owners, beneficial interests, federal tax classification, elections, and state registrations. An LLC designation alone does not establish how an entity is taxed.

02

Trace transactions between entities

Review loans, rent, reimbursements, management fees, distributions, and capital contributions. Identify the agreements and bookkeeping needed to explain each payment and its actual business purpose.

03

Review transfers before execution

Evaluate basis, liabilities, appreciation, and ownership changes before moving assets. A transfer to a commonly owned entity is not automatically tax-free, especially when corporate property is involved.

04

Model owner-level consequences

Compare the effect on compensation, distributions, loss utilization, state filings, and eventual exit. A deduction in one related entity may create income in another and should be evaluated across the owners.

05

Coordinate tax and legal implementation

Define the tax conclusions for legal counsel, the bookkeeping treatment for the accounting team, and any election or filing responsibilities. Formation documents and tax reporting must reflect the same arrangement.

A productive first conversation

Bring the context. We will define the next step.

Start with a short description of the decision, the people and entities involved, and the date by which you need an answer. These records help establish whether the work is ready for analysis:

  • Complete ownership chart and formation or operating agreements
  • Tax elections and recent entity returns with basis schedules
  • Intercompany ledgers, loans, leases, and service agreements
  • Proposed transfer terms, valuations, debt, and financing restrictions

Do not send tax returns, account numbers, or other sensitive records through a general inquiry. Arrange approved client-portal access before providing documents.

Clarity before commitment

Agree on the scope, deliverables, and responsibilities.

Scope the work

The consultation should establish the entities and periods covered, the analysis required, available records, proposed deliverables, and coordination with your existing advisers. The signed engagement determines what AE will provide.

Define the handoff

Confirm who implements each recommendation and who reports it on the return. Legal drafting, valuation, engineering, payroll processing, and filing work should be expressly included or assigned separately.

Understand fees and timing

Review AE’s published pricing as a starting point, then confirm the applicable fee and any separate work in writing. Delivery timing depends on the agreed scope, usable records, and review requirements.

Evaluate the result honestly

A review may identify a useful action, a documentation gap, or a reason to retain the current approach. Any financial comparison needs stated assumptions and a clear distinction between deductions, tax deferral, and realized savings.

Questions before you engage.

Does adding an LLC create a tax deduction?

No. The deduction depends on an actual qualifying expense and the applicable tax treatment. Additional entities can increase administration without improving the overall outcome.

Can property be moved out of an S corporation without tax?

Do not assume that. Appreciated corporate property and debt can create material consequences. Obtain transaction-specific advice before signing a transfer.
