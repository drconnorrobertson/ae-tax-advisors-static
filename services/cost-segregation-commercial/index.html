Commercial Cost Segregation Studies: Scope and Filing Handoff

Define the commercial property study before comparing estimates

For a leased office building, retail center or warehouse, a useful study must distinguish the owner's acquired assets from later improvements and tenant-owned property. The first question is which taxpayer owns which costs. The next is whether accelerating depreciation produces a deduction that taxpayer can use.

This page focuses on commissioning and implementing a commercial study. For a property occupied by your own operating business, use the owner-occupied commercial building guide. For general scope and published study pricing, see cost segregation services.

Require supported classifications, not a reclassification target

Land is not depreciable. Nonresidential real property generally uses a 39-year recovery period under GDS, but properly identified personal property and land improvements can have different recovery periods. Their function, use and governing asset classification matter. An electrical, plumbing or HVAC label alone does not establish short-life property; structural systems and equipment serving a specific business function require analysis.

No standard 25–40% reclassification range establishes the result for your building. Request the methodology, component descriptions, cost support and reconciliation to total depreciable basis. The IRS Cost Segregation Audit Technique Guide describes examination considerations; it is not IRS approval of a particular report.

Review bonus depreciation, QIP and ADS separately

Eligible property acquired and placed in service after January 19, 2025 can qualify for 100% bonus depreciation. Earlier acquisition dates, written binding contracts, used-property requirements, related-party transactions, elections and exclusions can change the treatment. A study prepared in 2026 does not reset the acquisition or service date of an older building.

Qualified improvement property generally concerns improvements made by the taxpayer to the interior of an existing nonresidential building after the building was first placed in service. Enlargements, elevators or escalators, and the internal structural framework are excluded. Purchasing an existing improved building does not, by itself, turn its interior into the purchaser's QIP.

QIP generally has a 15-year GDS recovery period, but required ADS treatment can remove bonus eligibility. An electing real property trade or business under Section 163(j), for example, must use ADS for specified real property, including QIP. Have the preparer review existing elections before using a bonus estimate. IRS Publication 946 explains depreciation eligibility, methods and conventions.

Compare incremental, usable deductions

A study estimate should compare the proposed schedule with the depreciation otherwise allowable. Nonresidential real property uses the mid-month convention: dividing basis by 39 is a full-year illustration, not a correct first-year calculation without a service-month adjustment. Keep land, remaining building depreciation and shorter-life assets in the comparison.

The report does not establish passive-loss treatment, real estate professional status, material participation, owner basis or at-risk amounts. A rental owner qualifying as a real estate professional must still establish material participation in the applicable rental activity. Excess-business-loss restrictions and state differences may also affect current use. For a lease to your own business, self-rental income recharacterization does not automatically make a rental loss nonpassive. See IRS Publication 925.

Evaluate the holding period and disposition consequences as well as current deductions. Accelerated depreciation changes future deductions and can affect gain character and recapture. A large report deduction is not a guaranteed cash-tax reduction.

Agree on the report and filing handoff

  • Study deliverables: property and taxpayer identification, reconciled depreciable basis, asset-level cost allocations, recovery classifications, methodology and supporting records.
  • Tax decisions: who approves service dates, methods, conventions, elections, federal/state differences and treatment of replaced assets.
  • Return implementation: who updates the fixed-asset ledger, prepares required forms and reconciles the final return schedules to the report.
  • Questions after delivery: who answers preparer questions, what any examination support covers and whether additional work is separately billed.

For an established impermissible depreciation method, an eligible accounting-method change may use Form 3115 and a Section 481(a) adjustment. That is not the route for every error, missed election or ownership history. An amended return or another procedure may apply. Confirm eligibility, consent procedures, filing deadlines and any election relief before promising a single-year catch-up. Review the Form 3115 instructions with the preparer. A study fee should not be assumed to include the form, return amendment or owner-level tax analysis.

Use the CPA coordination checklist to document acceptance of the schedules, not just receipt of the PDF.

Illustrative scenario: a retail building with a tenant renovation

A buyer acquires a leased retail property and later funds work for a new tenant. This is a records example, not a client result or savings projection. Keep the purchase allocation and renovation ledger separate. Reconcile the landlord allowance and tenant invoices to determine who owns and depreciates each improvement. Identify existing assets replaced by the work and document when each new asset is ready and available for use.

The study provider classifies supported costs. The return preparer determines the reporting treatment, checks prior schedules and documents any correction procedure. Before authorizing the work, ask for a written scope connecting those two jobs and specifying what remains outside the quote.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a landlord commission a study for tenant improvements?

First establish which taxpayer owns and has depreciable basis in the improvements. Review the lease, allowance agreement, invoices and existing schedules so the landlord and tenant do not claim the same costs.

How much of a commercial building can be reclassified?

There is no guaranteed percentage. The result depends on the supported costs, asset functions and applicable classifications. Land is excluded from depreciable basis.

Does a study completed in 2026 make an older building eligible for 100% bonus?

No. The study date does not reset acquisition or placed-in-service dates. The applicable year, asset eligibility, elections and required depreciation system determine bonus treatment.

Does every prior-year study require Form 3115?

No. A qualifying change to an established depreciation method may use Form 3115 and a Section 481(a) adjustment. Other errors or elections may require an amended return or another procedure. Confirm eligibility and deadlines with the preparer.

Does the study fee include tax-return implementation?

Do not assume it does. The written scope should identify who updates depreciation schedules, prepares any Form 3115 or amendment, evaluates owner-level deduction limits and provides support after delivery.
