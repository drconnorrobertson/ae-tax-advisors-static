Cost Segregation Studies and Tax Planning for Property Owners

Cost segregation identifies components with different depreciation treatment; it does not guarantee a refund or an immediate offset against other income. AE Tax Advisors helps property owners connect study feasibility with supported basis, acquisition and service dates, existing depreciation, deduction limitations, and the intended holding period.

Cost Segregation Feasibility and Study Coordination

Start with the decision that matters.

Who this is for

Owners of investment or business property considering an engineered study, including owners evaluating a previously placed-in-service property.

When to begin

A purchase or renovation is complete, a prior depreciation schedule needs review, or an estimate shows a large deduction without explaining whether it can be used.

The question we help you work through

Is a study worthwhile for this property and this taxpayer after considering fees, documentation, loss limits, and future disposition consequences?

From evidence to action

A disciplined review with a practical handoff.

01

Confirm the property facts

Establish ownership, business use, acquisition history, placed-in-service dates, and the support for building and land allocations. Separate purchases from later improvements.

02

Assess feasibility without promising results

Compare a reasoned range of potential classifications with study cost and the owner’s tax position. An estimate is a screening tool rather than a completed engineering deliverable.

03

Coordinate the study inputs

Gather settlement documents, photographs, plans, renovation details, and existing schedules. Identify discrepancies before the final report is prepared.

04

Connect the report to the return

Reconcile asset classifications, basis totals, and depreciation records with the return preparer. Existing-property corrections may require accounting-method analysis rather than a simple amendment.

05

Evaluate the holding period

Explain the distinction between accelerated deductions and permanent savings. Include later disposition effects and deduction utilization in the financial assessment.

A productive first conversation

Bring the context. We will define the next step.

Start with a short description of the decision, the people and entities involved, and the date by which you need an answer. These records help establish whether the work is ready for analysis:

  • Settlement statement and support for land and building basis
  • Acquisition, construction, improvement, and service dates
  • Existing depreciation and filed returns with loss carryforwards
  • Property photographs, renovation invoices, and expected holding period

Do not send tax returns, account numbers, or other sensitive records through a general inquiry. Arrange approved client-portal access before providing documents.

Clarity before commitment

Agree on the scope, deliverables, and responsibilities.

Scope the work

The consultation should establish the entities and periods covered, the analysis required, available records, proposed deliverables, and coordination with your existing advisers. The signed engagement determines what AE will provide.

Define the handoff

Confirm who implements each recommendation and who reports it on the return. Legal drafting, valuation, engineering, payroll processing, and filing work should be expressly included or assigned separately.

Understand fees and timing

Review AE’s published pricing as a starting point, then confirm the applicable fee and any separate work in writing. Delivery timing depends on the agreed scope, usable records, and review requirements.

Evaluate the result honestly

A review may identify a useful action, a documentation gap, or a reason to retain the current approach. Any financial comparison needs stated assumptions and a clear distinction between deductions, tax deferral, and realized savings.

Choose the next step

Which cost segregation question are you trying to answer?

The right starting point depends on what has already happened to the property. Use the routes below to prepare a specific question for AE Tax Advisors, then confirm the required analysis and deliverables in your engagement.

I am evaluating a new study

Organize the closing statement, land allocation support, property use, photographs, and improvement records. Compare the proposed report scope and fee before relying on a projected deduction. The cost segregation resource center organizes the records and decisions behind the review.

I already own and depreciate the building

Bring the complete depreciation schedule and the filed returns that show the property's treatment. Explain what you believe was missed rather than starting with a preferred filing form. Read the amended return versus Form 3115 guide and existing-property depreciation review to prepare questions for the return preparer.

I need to understand the financial tradeoff

Separate an estimated deduction from the amount usable on your return. Ask which assumptions describe your property, which are estimates, and how the intended holding period affects the comparison. Use the cost segregation calculator as an illustration, then discuss your facts with the preparer.

I am planning to sell

Give the reviewer your expected sale timing, adjusted basis records, prior depreciation, and estimated selling costs. Include the disposition in the planning conversation instead of reviewing only the first-year deduction. The rental property sale tax calculator helps organize a scenario for that discussion.

How Stratum fits the AE Tax Advisors process

Stratum Cost Segregation focuses on the property-level study. AE Tax Advisors coordinates the discovery and broader tax planning conversation. Review how Stratum and AE Tax Advisors work together for the study-to-preparer handoff, document checklist, and questions to settle before work begins.

Ask who prepares and reviews the study, who reconciles the report with your depreciation schedule, and who handles any separately engaged filing work. If your existing CPA will prepare the return, identify that person early. A study engagement, an AE advisory engagement, and tax return preparation can have separate scopes and fees.

For multiple properties, prepare a separate record set for each address and entity. That makes it easier to identify missing records, compare written proposals, and track which study and filing deliverables belong to each property.

Questions before you engage.

Is the study deduction equal to tax savings?

No. A deduction reduces taxable income, and its current use depends on the taxpayer’s circumstances. A financial estimate must also account for future consequences.

Are study work and return preparation the same engagement?

They may be separately scoped. Confirm engineering deliverables, method-change work, return integration, fees, and responsibilities before proceeding.

Choose a cost segregation engagement that fits the owner

A high-income business owner may own the operating building directly, lease it to the business through a property entity, or hold unrelated rentals. Those situations require different owner-level analysis even when the engineering process is similar. Before commissioning a study, identify the taxpayer that owns the assets and the return where the resulting depreciation belongs.

  • Owner-occupied commercial building: coordinate business use, building ownership and usable deductions.
  • Veterinary clinic or animal hospital: separate practice equipment, building components, renovations and related-party leasing.
  • Auto repair or collision property: organize shop equipment and building-system records before assigning classifications.
  • Previously depreciated property: reconcile prior schedules before selecting a correction or accounting-method procedure.

Review published study pricing and the quote checklist, then request a proposal that identifies study deliverables, required records, filing responsibilities and separate fees. A square-foot price is not an estimate of your tax benefit.
