Year-End Tax Review: 2026 Decisions and 2027 Forecast

AE Tax Advisors Team · Published January 21, 2024 · Updated November 6, 2026

Before committing year-end cash to equipment, property improvements or retirement funding, ask for one projection covering the business, rentals and household in both 2026 and 2027. This checklist is an agenda for that paid review: what must happen before year-end, what can be filed or funded later, and which assumptions change next year.

For a recurring quarterly process, use the annual tax planning checklist. For the underlying strategy choices, see year-end tax moves for profitable businesses. This page focuses on the December-to-January decision and engagement handoff.

Start with the decision, cash requirement and responsible person

Supply current business and rental financials, owner payroll, distributions, expected K-1s, household income, estimated payments, suspended-loss schedules and planned transactions. Ask the advisor to reconcile them before comparing an action in December with the same action in January. Taxable income is not bank cash, and a deduction is not a dollar-for-dollar refund.

Use 2026 limits for 2026 decisions, not the new 2027 figures

The section 179 deduction has a business-income limit and other conditions. The SUV amount is not a universal cap on total depreciation. QBI thresholds use taxable income before the QBI deduction, not revenue; SSTB and wage/property rules differ. The excess-business-loss threshold is not a blanket rental-loss allowance. Gross receipts require the applicable averaging, aggregation and eligibility rules; they are not net profit. Fiscal-year entities must use the appropriate tax year.

Retirement: distinguish election, adoption, deposit and deduction deadlines

There is no single December 31 deadline for every plan contribution. SEP establishment and funding can generally extend to the employer's return deadline, including extensions. Employee deferral elections and deposits have different rules from employer contributions; some eligible new plans have special adoption provisions. Ask the administrator for written deadlines for the specific entity and plan before relying on a later funding date.

For 2026, the general defined-contribution annual-additions ceiling is $72,000 before eligible catch-ups; the employee deferral ceiling is $24,500. Compensation, other plans and employee eligibility constrain actual contributions. Roth contributions are not current pre-tax deductions. This review does not supply an unverified 2027 retirement limit: use the separately issued IRS retirement guidance when confirmed. See IRS Publication 560 and IRS retirement limits.

Depreciation: service date matters more than the order date

Buying or paying for equipment by December 31 is insufficient if it is not ready and available for its intended use. For eligible property acquired and placed in service after January 19, 2025, federal bonus depreciation is generally 100%; it is not automatically phasing down after 2025. Acquisition rules, asset type, elections and state conformity still matter. Land and an entire ordinary building do not become bonus-eligible merely because the owner runs a business there. See IRS Publication 946.

The de minimis tangible-property safe harbor is separate from section 179. Its general invoice/item threshold is $2,500 without an applicable financial statement or $5,000 with one, subject to accounting-policy, book-treatment and election requirements. An item costing less than $5,000 is not automatically deductible. See IRS tangible-property guidance.

Property losses: test usability before spending for a deduction

Real estate professional status requires more than 750 qualifying hours and more than half of the taxpayer's personal-service time in qualifying real-property businesses. Rental material participation is a separate requirement. One spouse must independently satisfy the REPS thresholds; spouses' participation can be combined for material participation under its rules.

Becoming nonpassive does not release all earlier suspended passive losses. Former-passive-activity rules, current income from that activity and qualifying disposition rules need separate analysis. Self-rental net income can be recharacterized as nonpassive without giving the same treatment to a net loss. Apply basis, at-risk, passive and excess-business-loss limits as relevant before projecting a tax benefit. See IRS Publication 925.

Payments and elections: a December catch-up is not a universal fix

For most individuals, the estimated-tax safe harbor is based on 90% of current-year tax or 100% of prior-year tax, increased to 110% of prior-year tax when prior-year AGI exceeds $150,000 ($75,000 married filing separately). Other conditions and exceptions apply. A late estimated payment does not automatically eliminate earlier installment penalties. Withholding is generally allocated evenly unless actual timing is elected; review the specific calculation before adjusting payroll.

The normal fourth 2026 individual estimated payment is due January 15, 2027, not December 31. State and entity deadlines may differ. See IRS Publication 505.

An S election generally can be filed during the preceding tax year or within two months and 15 days after the intended effective year's start. December 31 is not the universal deadline. For an existing eligible calendar-year business seeking January 1, 2027 effectiveness, the ordinary deadline is March 15, 2027; late-election relief has conditions. Verify eligibility, shareholder consent and payroll implementation rather than assuming an amendment changes the entity retroactively. See IRS Form 2553 instructions.

Define the paid review before signing

  • Ask for a 2026 projection and 2027 comparison with assumptions, cash required and state differences identified.
  • Require an action list separating before-year-end steps from later filing or funding deadlines.
  • Identify missing records and unresolved eligibility questions instead of treating projected deductions as approved.
  • Confirm who handles payroll, elections, payments, returns, plan administration and any property study.
  • Agree written deliverables, fees, completion dates and exclusions in the signed engagement.

Discuss an owner-and-property year-end review with AE Tax Advisors. These are proposed scope items to confirm, not a representation that a consultation includes every specialist service. Existing tax, legal and retirement professionals may need to coordinate implementation.

General federal tax information reviewed November 6, 2026. No savings are guaranteed. Apply the law and guidance for the relevant year and your specific circumstances.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use the new 2027 limits on my 2026 return?

No. Apply the limits for the tax year specified in the applicable guidance. The 2027 figures are inputs for next-year planning, not replacements for 2026 amounts.

Must every retirement contribution be funded by December 31?

No. Election, adoption, employee deposit and employer funding deadlines differ by plan and entity. Obtain the applicable deadlines from the plan administrator before relying on a later contribution.

Does buying equipment in December secure a current-year deduction?

Not by itself. The property must meet the relevant eligibility rules and be ready and available for its intended use. Acquisition dates, elections, deduction limits and state rules also matter.

Does real estate professional status release all suspended losses?

No. Current rental material participation and former-passive-activity rules require separate analysis. Prior losses do not all become deductible simply because the owner qualifies this year.
