Leasing a Building to Your Business: Reconcile the Tax Result

AE Tax Advisors · Tax Planning & Strategy

Owning both an operating company and its building creates two connected decisions: who should own the property, and how should both sides report its use? A separate real estate entity can support a leasing or future sale plan, but it does not create an extra deduction simply by charging rent to your own business.

This guide focuses on reconciling the landlord and tenant before choosing a structure. For the broader property decision, see our business and real estate coordination guide; for participation and loss treatment, see the self-rental rules guide.

Reconcile both sides of a related-party lease

The operating company records qualifying business rent expense. The landlord records rental income and its own allowable property expenses and depreciation. A planning worksheet that adds the tenant’s deduction but omits the landlord’s income overstates the benefit.

Illustrative accounting model, not a client result: Assume a separately recognized landlord and tenant have the same tax year, the same owner, and $80,000 of rent that is both deductible and includible that year. The tenant’s $80,000 rent deduction is matched by $80,000 of landlord rental income. Across those two calculations, rent itself produces a net change of zero before property costs, depreciation and other tax differences. If allowable property expenses and depreciation total $60,000, the landlord has $20,000 of net rental income, while the tenant has an $80,000 rent deduction: together, the net property deduction is $60,000, not $140,000.

This model isolates the accounting; it does not promise equal tax outcomes under every ownership arrangement. Different owners, tax classifications, tax rates, payment timing and loss limitations can change the result. If both activities belong to the same federal income-tax owner through disregarded entities, a payment between them generally is not a separately recognized income-tax lease transaction. An LLC label alone does not determine tax classification.

Direct ownership does not prevent depreciation

A business can depreciate a qualifying building it owns and uses. Moving title to a separate landlord is not a prerequisite. Allocate purchase basis between land and depreciable property; land is not depreciable. Under the general depreciation system, nonresidential real property generally uses straight-line depreciation over 39 years with the mid-month convention. Residential rental property generally uses 27.5 years. Other methods or recovery periods may apply when required, including under the alternative depreciation system.

A cost segregation study can identify components with shorter recovery periods when supported by the property’s facts. Bonus depreciation eligibility depends on the asset, acquisition and placed-in-service rules; the entire building is not automatically eligible. Evaluate when the owner can use any additional deduction, the study cost, state treatment and eventual disposition. Correcting earlier depreciation may require Form 3115 and an accounting-method adjustment rather than simply amending returns; determine the applicable procedure before filing.

Choose ownership for the operating plan, not a deduction count

  • Who will keep the building? If the business may be sold while the owner retains the property, evaluate the future lease and buyer’s requirements before putting title in the operating corporation.
  • What does the lender permit? Review guarantees, collateral, due-on-transfer provisions and the intended borrower with counsel and the lender.
  • Who shares the economics? Match ownership percentages, rent, improvements and sale proceeds to the agreements. Business and property owners may not be identical.
  • What does another entity cost? Include legal work, insurance, bookkeeping, state fees and any additional returns. More layers do not automatically reduce tax.

Separate legal entities may help organize risk, but they do not guarantee that creditors cannot reach the property. Guarantees, liens, legal formalities and state law matter. Have legal counsel evaluate liability protection; do not treat a tax structure as a legal assurance.

Review rent, payment timing and rental-loss treatment together

Use a written lease and support commercially reasonable rent with relevant market evidence. Identify who pays taxes, insurance, repairs and improvements. Do not choose rent merely to move profit from one entity to another. Reconcile the tenant’s expense and payable with the landlord’s receipts and receivable, including expenses paid on the landlord’s behalf.

If an accrual-method business owes rent to a related cash-method landlord, Section 267 can defer the tenant’s deduction until the payment is includible in the landlord’s income. Related-party definitions and special S corporation rules need review; a December journal entry alone does not establish a current deduction.

The self-rental rule generally makes net rental income nonpassive when the property is used in a business in which the taxpayer materially participates. It does not automatically convert a net rental loss to nonpassive treatment. Review ownership, participation, any valid grouping and the basis, at-risk, passive-activity and other applicable loss limits before assuming depreciation offsets operating income.

Model the exit before transferring title

A sale, distribution, gift and exchange are different transactions. Moving appreciated property out of a corporation is not automatically tax-free merely because the same person owns the recipient LLC. A corporate distribution can trigger gain and shareholder consequences. Review the entity, adjusted basis, debt and transaction form before signing a deed.

For a taxable sale, distinguish ordinary depreciation recapture on applicable assets from unrecaptured Section 1250 gain and other gain. For individuals, unrecaptured Section 1250 gain can be subject to a maximum 25% federal rate; that does not mean every depreciation dollar on every building is taxed at a flat 25%. Cost-segregated components may have different recapture treatment. Model state tax and any applicable net investment income tax separately.

A Section 1031 exchange generally applies to qualifying real property held for business or investment, not inventory held primarily for sale. In a deferred exchange, identification generally must occur within 45 days and receipt by the earlier of 180 days or the applicable return due date, including extensions. Receipt of cash or other nonqualifying property, debt relief and other conditions can produce recognized gain. Reinvesting sale proceeds is not by itself an exchange; arrange the structure before closing.

A gift generally carries over basis for determining gain, with special rules, rather than providing a fair-market-value reset. Inherited-property basis rules are different. Debt and gift-tax issues also require review. Neither gifting nor a charitable contribution should be described as a universal way to eliminate depreciation-related tax.

What to bring to a coordinated review

  • Entity chart showing legal owners, percentages and federal tax classifications.
  • Deeds, closing statements, loan documents, guarantees and the lease.
  • Both sets of books, rent-payment evidence and year-end related-party balances.
  • Depreciation schedules, improvement invoices and prior cost segregation reports.
  • Participation records, suspended-loss schedules and relevant prior elections.
  • The intended business sale, property sale, refinancing or family-transfer timeline.

Ask for one reconciled model showing the tenant, landlord and owner-level consequences, along with the responsible preparer for each return. Identify legal, appraisal and lender work separately. AE can discuss the tax review and implementation scope; the facts determine whether a proposed structure is worthwhile.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does leasing a building to my own business create an extra deduction?

Not by itself. The operating business may deduct qualifying rent, but the landlord reports the corresponding rental income. Review both sides together, including property expenses, depreciation, accounting methods and owner-level limitations.

Can a business depreciate a building it owns directly?

Yes, if the property meets the depreciation requirements. Separate ownership is not required to depreciate a business-use building. Land is not depreciable, and nonresidential real property generally uses a 39-year recovery period under the general depreciation system.

Are losses from renting to my S corporation automatically nonpassive?

No. The self-rental rule generally recharacterizes net rental income as nonpassive when the property is used in a business in which you materially participate. It does not automatically make a net rental loss nonpassive. Grouping and other exceptions require a separate analysis.

Does gifting a business building eliminate its deferred tax?

Generally not. Gift basis commonly carries over for gain, subject to special rules, rather than resetting to fair market value. Debt, gift-tax reporting and the recipient’s later sale also need review. Inherited-property basis rules are different.
