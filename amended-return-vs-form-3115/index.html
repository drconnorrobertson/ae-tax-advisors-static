Can I File Form 3115 With an Amended Return?

Sometimes, but the exception, year of change, and deadline have to match. Merely attaching Form 3115 to an old amended return does not create a valid accounting-method change.

The short answer: identify the exception before amending

For an ordinary automatic accounting-method change, Form 3115 is attached to the timely filed original federal return for the year of change, with a signed duplicate filed with the IRS. An amended return is allowed in limited circumstances—not as a general way to add a missed Form 3115 to any prior year. The IRS Form 3115 instructions state the ordinary rule and point to the six-month relief provision in Revenue Procedure 2015-13, section 6.03(4)(a). A separate change-specific rule can also permit an amended return; one example is certain depreciation changes for property disposed of in the year of change under Revenue Procedure 2025-23, section 6.07.

First decide whether the issue is actually a method change. Correcting an isolated factual or computational mistake may call for an amended return without Form 3115. Correcting an adopted impermissible depreciation method may require Form 3115 and a Section 481(a) adjustment. Neither form is a universal substitute for the other.

Key takeaways

  • Timely original year-of-change return: ordinarily the normal route for an automatic Form 3115 change; the signed duplicate-copy requirement is separate.
  • Amended return within limited relief: section 6.03(4)(a) can give six months from the unextended due date if the original return was timely filed and every filing, duplicate-copy, and statement condition is met.
  • Change-specific amended-return permission: read the applicable designated-change procedure. Do not generalize the disposed-property exception to an ordinary lookback study or another change.
  • After the applicable window: test whether a later year can be the year of change; do not assume that amending the old year retroactively validates Form 3115.

Three filing paths after the original return is filed

  1. Six-month automatic-change relief. Under Revenue Procedure 2015-13, section 6.03(4)(a), the taxpayer must have timely filed the original year-of-change return, file an amended return implementing the change within six months of the return's unextended due date, attach the original Form 3115 to that amendment, send the signed duplicate by the same filing date, and include the required section 301.9100-2(b) statement. Examination, Appeals, or litigation can trigger additional-copy duties. This is a conditional filing route, not another six months added after an existing filing extension.
  2. A procedure that expressly authorizes an amendment or AAR. For certain depreciation-method changes involving disposed property, Revenue Procedure 2025-23, section 6.07(3)(b), permits a Form 3115 with an amended sale-year return or, for a BBA partnership, an AAR, subject to that procedure's limitations and collateral adjustments. AE's sale-year rental guide addresses this narrower fact pattern. A different designated change may have different requirements.
  3. A later year of change. If the original year is closed for Form 3115 and no specific exception applies, an eligible automatic change may instead be implemented on a later timely filed year-of-change return. The Section 481(a) computation then compares the old and new methods at the beginning of that later year. Eligibility, prior changes, method adoption, and the current automatic-change list still have to be checked.

Nonautomatic changes follow a different clock. The Form 3115 instructions generally require filing with the IRS National Office during the requested year of change, absent specific published relief. A routine amended return filed years later does not turn a missed nonautomatic application into a timely one.

When an amended return alone is the natural starting point

Amendments commonly address omitted income, incorrect deductions, wrong filing status, corrected tax documents, or factual errors specific to a year. If a rental property's purchase price was transcribed incorrectly, for example, the task may be to correct the basis facts and affected returns—not to change an accounting method. Entity changes can also require corrected K-1s and owner amendments.

Check the refund and assessment periods, filing method, state procedure, and effect on later years. The wrong rental basis guide helps separate the original factual error from a consistently applied depreciation method.

When Form 3115 enters the analysis

Form 3115 applies to accounting-method changes, including many depreciation-method corrections after an impermissible method has been adopted. A negative Section 481(a) adjustment may catch up previously missed deductions in the year of change, subject to the method-change terms and loss limitations.

Eligibility, designated change number, automatic-change requirements, filing copies, prior changes, and timing should be confirmed for the year filed. The Form 3115 instructions warn that the listed automatic change numbers are informational and subject to more recent revenue procedures. The signed duplicate is a real filing step even when the original form accompanies an electronically filed return.

Why depreciation corrections are often misclassified

Buying an asset but omitting it from one return may be a different issue from consistently depreciating a building under an impermissible method for several years. The number of years and pattern of treatment matter.

A cost segregation lookback frequently uses Form 3115 because the taxpayer is changing the method or recovery treatment of existing assets, but not every asset error belongs there. The deadline guide explains why a late study may point to a later change year instead of an ordinary amendment. Do not transfer the special disposed-property rule to a property that is still held.

Worked filing decision: the study arrives after filing

Assume an S corporation timely files its year-of-change return without a cost segregation study and receives the completed study four months after the unextended return due date. The preparer identifies an eligible automatic depreciation-method change and a $90,000 negative Section 481(a) adjustment. The corporation cannot simply append Form 3115 to a casual amendment. It must check whether it qualifies for the six-month procedure, file the year-of-change amendment implementing the method change within that window, attach Form 3115, send the signed duplicate no later than the amendment date, and include the required relief statement. The shareholders' K-1s and personal returns may also need coordination. This is an illustration of procedure, not a conclusion that any given study or corporation qualifies.

If the same study arrives after that relief window, the team should test a later eligible year of change and calculate its Section 481(a) opening adjustment; it should not claim that the earlier amendment deadline was extended by the ordinary return extension. If the property had already been disposed of, the separate disposed-property rule must be analyzed on its own facts, including sale-year limitations and basis/gain consequences.

Documents and dates to gather before a return review

  • The originally filed federal and state returns, proof of filing and extensions, and the exact unextended due date for the proposed year of change.
  • Fixed-asset and depreciation schedules, purchase and improvement documents, cost segregation study, prior Forms 3115, and the proposed Section 481(a) calculation.
  • Any asset disposition, sales agreement, Form 4797 computation, K-1s, BBA partnership status, examination or Appeals notice, and pending amended return or AAR.
  • The current revenue procedure for the designated change, duplicate-copy filing instructions, relief statement, and state conformity analysis.

Failure points that can invalidate the plan

  • Wrong year of change: the amended return must be for the authorized year, not simply the oldest year with missed depreciation.
  • Wrong six-month clock: section 6.03(4)(a) measures from the unextended due date, not six months after a later extension date.
  • Missing duplicate or statement: the relief path has filing conditions beyond the return attachment.
  • Wrong procedure for the item: an election or one-year factual error is not automatically a method change; a disposed asset has its own special rules.
  • Ignoring pass-through effects: entity, owner, state, and subsequent-year depreciation schedules need to reconcile.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I file Form 3115 with an amended return?

Usually an automatic Form 3115 belongs with the timely filed original return for the year of change. An amended year-of-change return can work under the limited six-month relief procedure if every condition is met, or under a change-specific rule such as the disposed-property depreciation procedure. An ordinary late amendment is not a general substitute.

Can Form 3115 replace every amended return?

No. It applies to qualifying accounting-method changes, not every factual or computational error.

Can Form 3115 recover missed depreciation?

It can in many method-change situations through a Section 481(a) adjustment, subject to the applicable procedures.

Do states follow the federal adjustment?

State conformity varies. Review the state treatment and any separate filing requirement.
