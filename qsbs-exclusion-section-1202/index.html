QSBS Exclusion: Section 1202 Limits, Holding Periods and Eligibility

Review the stock records, business activities and planned sale before relying on a Section 1202 exclusion.

Request Your Free Tax Assessment

By AE Tax Advisors Team · Billings, Montana · Updated 2026-11-06

Section 1202 can exclude part or all of eligible gain when a noncorporate taxpayer sells qualifying domestic C corporation stock. Start with the stock's acquisition and issuance dates: the 2025 law changed the holding periods, per-issuer dollar limit and gross-assets ceiling for newer stock. Entity status, original issuance, business activity, redemptions and records still determine whether the stock qualifies.

What Section 1202 Actually Does

Section 1202 excludes the qualifying percentage of eligible gain rather than deferring it. Excluded gain is not subject to net investment income tax. Stock acquired after September 27, 2010 has no Section 1202 AMT preference, but taxable gain, partial-exclusion rates and state tax still require separate analysis.

The eligible-gain ceiling is the greater of the remaining per-issuer dollar limit or 10 times the adjusted basis of that issuer's QSBS sold during the tax year, ignoring additions to basis after original issuance. The dollar limit is $10 million for stock acquired on or before July 4, 2025, or $15 million for stock acquired after July 4, 2025 (the 2026 amount, with inflation adjustments beginning after 2026). Prior eligible gain from the same issuer reduces the dollar limit; mixed old and new lots require coordination, not two independent allowances. Apply the applicable exclusion percentage to the eligible gain after this limit. A married couple filing jointly does not receive two dollar limits merely because each spouse owns shares.

State treatment is separate. Most states follow the federal exclusion, several modify it, and a small number disallow it outright. The state answer has to be checked against the state of residence at the time of sale, which is itself a planning variable when a move is already under consideration.

The Five Tests the Stock Has to Pass

All five apply. Failing any one disqualifies the entire position.

1. Domestic C corporation. The issuer must be a C corporation for substantially all of the holding period. S corporation stock never qualifies, and neither does an LLC interest or partnership interest.

2. Original issuance. The stock must be acquired directly from the corporation for money, property, or services. Stock bought from another shareholder does not qualify, though stock received by gift or inheritance can carry the original holder's status forward.

3. The gross assets test. For stock issued on or before July 4, 2025, aggregate gross assets must not exceed $50 million; for stock issued after July 4, 2025, the ceiling is $75 million in 2026. The test covers the corporation and any predecessor at all times after August 9, 1993 and before issuance, and immediately after issuance including the new proceeds. Parent-subsidiary controlled-group rules also apply. Gross assets generally mean cash plus adjusted tax basis of other property, with contributed property measured using fair market value at contribution. Enterprise valuation is not the same measure.

4. The active business test. At least 80% of assets by value must be used in one or more qualified active trades or businesses during substantially all of the holding period. This is an asset test, not an 80% gross-income or revenue test. Reasonably required working capital and qualifying investment funds expected to be used within two years for research or increased working capital can count, but after the corporation has existed for at least two years no more than 50% of its assets may qualify solely under the working-capital rule. Separate limits apply to portfolio securities and nonbusiness real estate.

5. The holding period. Stock acquired on or before July 4, 2025 must be held for more than five years. Qualifying stock acquired after September 27, 2010 and on or before July 4, 2025 can receive a 100% exclusion. For stock acquired after July 4, 2025, the exclusion is 50% after at least three years, 75% after at least four years, and 100% after at least five years. These percentages apply to eligible gain after the per-issuer limit, not automatically to all sale proceeds. Acquisition dates reflect applicable holding-period tacking rules.

What the 2025 Law Changed

Use acquisition date, including applicable tacking, for the dollar cap and holding-period tiers. Use issuance date for the corporate gross-assets test. A gift, inheritance or qualifying rollover does not automatically move older stock into the newer regime.

RuleOn or before July 4, 2025After July 4, 2025
Acquisition-date dollar limit, before reductions$10 million$15 million in 2026; indexed after 2026

Holding period and percentageMore than five years; 100% for qualifying acquisitions after September 27, 2010At least three years: 50%; at least four: 75%; at least five: 100%

Earlier acquisitions can have 50% or 75% exclusion treatment. Apply the percentage only after the eligible-gain ceiling. New shares first acquired in July 2025 cannot earn a three-year exclusion in 2026; the earliest new-stock three-year anniversary is in 2028.

Which Businesses Qualify, and Which Are Excluded by Name

Section 1202(e)(3) excludes services in health, law, engineering, architecture, accounting, actuarial science, performing arts, consulting, athletics, financial services and brokerage services, plus any business whose principal asset is the reputation or skill of employees. The named service exclusions do not depend on that additional reputation-or-skill category. Banking, insurance, financing, leasing, investing, farming, specified extraction businesses, and hotels, motels, restaurants and similar businesses are also excluded. A software or manufacturing label does not establish eligibility; review the actual activities and asset use.

These exclusions often rule out medical, legal, accounting and consulting practices. Manufacturing, software and product businesses may qualify if their actual activities and asset use meet the statute.

Mixed technology and services businesses need an analysis of actual activities, asset use and the named statutory exclusions. A product label or proprietary software does not automatically overcome consulting or another excluded service category.

The S Corporation Problem

Most profitable owner-operated businesses are S corporations, and S corporation stock is permanently outside Section 1202. This is the most common reason the exclusion is unavailable to exactly the owner who would benefit most.

Revoking an S election does not turn existing S corporation shares into QSBS and does not create a deemed new stock issuance. A genuine new original issuance by an eligible domestic C corporation must be analyzed separately. Incorporating an LLC taxed as a partnership or disregarded entity can involve qualifying original-issue C corporation stock in exchange for property, if all requirements are met. The qualifying holding period generally starts at that incorporation, and Section 1202(i) prevents pre-contribution appreciation from becoming excludable gain.

A C corporation generally pays 21% federal tax on taxable income, and taxable dividends create a second shareholder layer. Compare these costs with the potential exclusion and realistic sale structure. Our entity structuring guide covers the operating-side trade-offs.

Stacking and Packing: Multiplying the Cap

A qualifying gift or transfer at death can preserve the original issuance treatment and holding period under Section 1202(h); it does not reset old stock into the post-July 4, 2025 regime. Separate noncorporate taxpayers may have separate per-issuer limits, but a married couple filing jointly does not get two dollar limits merely by holding shares separately. Grantor trusts do not create additional income-tax taxpayers. Non-grantor trusts require review of ownership, trust aggregation, completed gifts, valuation, gift-tax reporting and assignment-of-income risk. There is no universal safe number of trusts or waiting period.

Stacking can involve genuine gifts to separate non-grantor trusts. A $60 million eligible-gain illustration using four $15 million dollar limits assumes qualifying post-July 4, 2025 acquisition dates, a future sale after at least five years, valid separate taxpayers, sufficient gain allocated to each and no reductions for prior issuer gain. It is not available merely by creating three trusts or choosing different beneficiaries.

Packing concerns the 10-times-basis alternative. Property exchanged for original-issue stock has special Section 1202 fair-market-value rules, and pre-contribution appreciation is not transformed into excludable gain. Later basis additions to existing shares are disregarded for this ceiling. A proposed contribution needs a valuation and eligibility review, not just multiplication by ten.

Gift and trust planning should be evaluated before sale commitments. Assignment-of-income, step-transaction and trust-aggregation rules turn on the facts; there is no one-year or two-year safe harbor and no safe number of trusts.

Key Takeaways

  • Excluded gain is not deferred or subject to NIIT; acquisition date determines the percentage and AMT treatment.
  • Revoking an S election does not convert existing shares into QSBS; genuine original-issue C corporation stock requires separate review.
  • Stock acquired after July 4, 2025 uses the $15 million base dollar limit and three-, four- and five-year tiers; issuance date controls the asset ceiling.
  • Health, law, accounting, consulting, financial services, and restaurants are excluded by statute.
  • Separate taxpayers may have separate limits, but spouses filing jointly and grantor trusts do not automatically add dollar caps.
  • Test issuance requirements, holding-period conditions and sale facts at the relevant times; keep a lot-by-lot record.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get QSBS treatment if my company is an LLC or S corporation?

Revoking an S election does not turn existing S corporation shares into QSBS and does not create a deemed new stock issuance. A genuine new original issuance by an eligible domestic C corporation must be analyzed separately. Incorporating an LLC taxed as a partnership or disregarded entity can involve qualifying original-issue C corporation stock in exchange for property, if all requirements are met. The qualifying holding period generally starts at that incorporation, and Section 1202(i) prevents pre-contribution appreciation from becoming excludable gain.

What happens if I sell before five years?

Stock acquired on or before July 4, 2025 must be held for more than five years. Qualifying stock acquired after September 27, 2010 and on or before July 4, 2025 can receive a 100% exclusion. For stock acquired after July 4, 2025, the exclusion is 50% after at least three years, 75% after at least four years, and 100% after at least five years. These percentages apply to eligible gain after the per-issuer limit, not automatically to all sale proceeds. Acquisition dates reflect applicable holding-period tacking rules. Section 1045 may defer gain when a noncorporate taxpayer sells QSBS held for more than six months, elects rollover treatment, and buys replacement QSBS during the 60-day period beginning on the sale date. Full deferral generally requires reinvesting the amount realized, not just the gain. Deferred gain reduces replacement-stock basis; applicable holding-period tacking must be analyzed. A rollover does not automatically secure a later Section 1202 exclusion or increase its cap.

Does the exclusion apply to an asset sale?

Section 1202 does not exclude the corporation's gain from selling its assets. A later liquidating distribution can require a separate shareholder-level QSBS analysis, but it does not erase corporate tax. Model the asset sale, distribution and stock-sale alternatives before agreeing to a structure. See how to structure your business for sale.

How many trusts can I use to stack the exclusion?

A qualifying gift or transfer at death can preserve the original issuance treatment and holding period under Section 1202(h); it does not reset old stock into the post-July 4, 2025 regime. Separate noncorporate taxpayers may have separate per-issuer limits, but a married couple filing jointly does not get two dollar limits merely by holding shares separately. Grantor trusts do not create additional income-tax taxpayers. Non-grantor trusts require review of ownership, trust aggregation, completed gifts, valuation, gift-tax reporting and assignment-of-income risk. There is no universal safe number of trusts or waiting period.

Do I need to do anything to claim the exclusion?

It is reported on the return in the year of sale, but the substantiation is built over the life of the company: the issuance documents, the gross assets calculation at each issuance, and evidence the active business test was met throughout. Companies that never assembled that file end up reconstructing it under diligence pressure, and buyers price the uncertainty.

Before You Rely on a QSBS Exclusion

Bring formation documents and tax elections, the cap table and each issuance agreement, option or convertible-instrument records, consideration and stock basis, historical balance sheets and contributed-property valuations, active-business records, redemption history, prior Section 1202 claims, trust or gift documents, state residency details, and the proposed sale timeline. Ask for a written lot-by-lot eligibility analysis and a stock-sale versus asset-sale comparison before relying on an exclusion.

A rental or leasing business does not become eligible merely by converting to a C corporation; Section 1202(e) also limits nonbusiness real estate. A useful first engagement should identify which share lots may qualify, missing evidence, the remaining issuer limit, state differences and the proposed transaction structure. Confirm the written scope, responsible tax and legal advisers, timing and fees before proceeding. Request a QSBS eligibility review.
