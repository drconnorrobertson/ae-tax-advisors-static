1031 Exchange Explained Defer Capital Gains

AE Tax Advisors · Tax Planning & Strategy

What Is a 1031 Exchange?

A 1031 exchange can defer gain on a properly structured exchange of business or investment real estate for qualifying like-kind real property. It is not an ordinary sale followed by a purchase. The amount deferred depends on adjusted basis, cash and debt movements, expenses, and recapture rules, so get the structure and tax projection in place before closing.

The Two Exchange Deadlines and Pre-Closing Setup

Identify replacement property in signed writing within 45 calendar days after the relinquished property is transferred. Receive it by the earlier of 180 calendar days or the federal return due date for the transfer year, including extensions. The periods run concurrently. For June 1, day 45 is July 16 and day 180 is November 28, subject to an earlier return deadline or applicable IRS relief. Ordinary weekends, holidays, and financing delays do not extend the periods. For a standard delayed exchange, engage the QI and restrict access to proceeds before closing.

Like-Kind Property Requirements

Section 1031 currently covers qualifying real property held for investment or business use, not ordinary equipment, securities, partnership interests, personal residences, or property held primarily for sale. Qualifying rental buildings may be like-kind to commercial buildings or raw land. U.S. real property is not like-kind to foreign real property; foreign-to-foreign exchanges may qualify if the remaining requirements are met.

Boot and Tax Consequences

Boot includes cash, non-like-kind property, and net debt relief after permitted offsets. Additional cash paid can replace debt, so matching the old loan balance is not mandatory. A larger replacement purchase or extra borrowing does not by itself erase cash retained. For a simplified $2 million exchange with $200,000 cash received, at least $200,000 realized gain, and no exchange expenses or separate recapture, $200,000 is recognized and the remaining qualifying gain is deferred. If realized gain were only $120,000, recognition under the boot rule would be $120,000. The tax rate depends on gain character and the taxpayer; there is no general 28% long-term real-estate rate.

Depreciation Recapture Tax

A qualifying exchange can defer gain attributable to depreciation, but the Section 1245 and Section 1250 recapture rules must be analyzed separately. Cost-segregated assets and non-like-kind property can require current recognition even when the real estate otherwise qualifies. For individuals, unrecaptured Section 1250 gain is subject to a maximum 25% federal rate, while ordinary-income recapture is a different category. Reconcile the depreciation schedule, Form 8824, replacement basis, and any recognized gain instead of assuming all depreciation is taxed now or all of it is deferred.

Real Application Example

A business owner sold a commercial building held 12 years. Original cost: $1.5 million. Sale price: $3.2 million. Unrealized gain: $1.7 million. Federal and state capital gains tax would total $425,000. Using 1031 exchange into three properties ($1.8M apartments, $800K retail, $600K land), the investor deferred $425,000 in taxes while redeploying capital across diversified assets.

Critical Rules That Disqualify Exchanges

Receiving or controlling all proceeds before receiving replacement property generally turns a deferred exchange into a taxable sale. Permissible cash boot in an otherwise qualifying exchange can instead produce partial recognition. A QI is the usual safe harbor, not the only possible exchange structure. Review disqualified-person rules for recent agents and specified related persons, including exceptions for prior exchange services and routine financial, title-insurance, escrow, or trust services. Related-party replacement-property purchases can fail when the related seller cashes out through a QI; simply holding your replacement for two years does not cure that structure. A direct related-party exchange also has a two-year disposition rule, subject to statutory exceptions. Missing applicable deadlines or acquiring nonqualifying property can jeopardize deferral.

State Income Tax Optimization

State residence and the location of relinquished property can matter even when the replacement is in another state. California generally requires Form FTB 3840 when California property is exchanged for out-of-state property and California-source gain is deferred, with annual reporting generally continuing until recognition. Moving exchange capital to Texas or Nevada does not by itself erase California-source deferred gain. Review current state conformity, sourcing, residency, and filing rules instead of treating geographic selection as automatic permanent tax savings.

Integration with Tax Planning

1031 exchanges coordinate with cost segregation, passive loss management, and entity optimization. If you're claiming cost segregation on replacement property or managing depreciation recapture timing, comprehensive planning addresses all tax layers simultaneously.

Next Steps

If you hold investment real estate with substantial unrealized gains, evaluate a 1031 exchange immediately. The combination of strict deadlines and potential tax savings of hundreds of thousands demands professional coordination. Schedule a consultation to structure your exchange within your complete tax strategy.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a 1031 exchange defer depreciation recapture?

A qualifying real-property exchange can defer gain attributable to depreciation, but full deferral is not automatic. Boot and the separate Section 1245 and Section 1250 recapture rules can require current recognition. Unrecaptured Section 1250 gain for individuals has a maximum 25% federal rate; it is not a flat 25% ordinary-income tax on all depreciation. Track deferred gain and carryover basis for a later taxable disposition.

What is boot in a 1031 exchange?

Boot includes cash, non-like-kind property, and net debt relief after permitted offsets. Additional cash paid can replace debt, so equal replacement borrowing is not mandatory. A more expensive replacement or extra borrowing does not by itself erase cash retained. Recognized gain is generally limited to the lesser of realized gain or boot after eligible exchange expenses, with recapture rules reviewed separately.

What are the 1031 exchange deadlines?

Identify replacement property in signed writing within 45 calendar days after transferring the relinquished property. Receive it by the earlier of 180 calendar days or the due date of the federal return for the transfer year, including extensions. Both periods run from the transfer, not consecutively. Applicable IRS relief may postpone deadlines; ordinary weekends, holidays, and financing delays do not.

What property qualifies for a 1031 exchange?

Both properties must be qualifying real property held for investment or business use. A rental house may be like-kind to a warehouse or raw land. Personal-use residences and property held primarily for sale do not qualify. U.S. real property is not like-kind to foreign real property, but foreign-to-foreign exchanges may qualify if the other requirements are met.
