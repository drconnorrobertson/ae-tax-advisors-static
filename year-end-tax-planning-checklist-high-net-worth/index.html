2026 Year-End Tax Planning for Business Owners and Investors

Start with a current projection, then separate decisions that need action before December 31 from filings and contributions with other deadlines. A year-end review should produce a dated action list, not a promised savings range.

Discuss My 2026 Year-End Tax Review

Update the full income projection

Reconcile business results, payroll, investments, K-1 estimates and rental activity. Include expected sales, bonuses and other unusual items. Compare withholding and estimated payments with the projected obligation. A return extension is not an extension to pay. Ask your preparer to identify the applicable payment and penalty rules.

Review business and property commitments together

  • Document shareholder services and reasonable compensation before adjusting payroll.
  • Check actual readiness and availability for intended use before assigning a placed-in-service date to equipment or property.
  • Reconcile existing depreciation and carried-forward losses before relying on a new study.
  • Review retirement plan adoption, election and funding deadlines with the administrator; they are not all December 31.
  • Check state obligations separately from federal deductions and elections.

A rental deduction does not automatically offset business or W-2 income. Real estate professional status does not automatically release prior suspended losses. Review material participation, former passive activities and disposition rules using IRS Publication 925.

2026 estate and gift figures: use the right tax year

For 2026, the federal basic exclusion amount is $15,000,000 per individual. The annual gift exclusion is $19,000 per recipient for qualifying gifts. These amounts are different from the 2024 figures. Gifts above the annual exclusion may require a return and use available lifetime exclusion; they do not automatically create an immediate tax bill. Portability, prior gifts, citizenship and state taxes require separate review.

Verify with the IRS estate and gift tax updates and gift tax questions.

Giving, gains and sales need transaction-specific review

For charitable gifts, confirm the recipient, transfer completion, substantiation, asset valuation and applicable deduction limits before transferring an asset. For business sales, QSBS, installment payments or Opportunity Zone investments, use the acquisition dates and current rules. An older article’s threshold or holding-period summary is not enough to establish eligibility.

If an LOI is pending, start with the pre-sale review checklist. For a property disposition, review the IRS disposition rules and the actual asset schedule.

Common questions

Must all year-end tax planning be completed by December 31?

No. Deadlines vary by action, entity, election and plan. Separate the transaction deadline from the filing or funding deadline and confirm each with the responsible professional.

Does real estate professional status release every suspended rental loss?

No. Prior losses and current activity require separate analysis under the applicable passive-activity and disposition rules.

What are the 2026 estate and annual gift exclusion amounts?

The federal basic exclusion is $15,000,000 per individual and the annual exclusion is $19,000 per recipient for qualifying gifts. Prior gifts and other facts affect the analysis.
