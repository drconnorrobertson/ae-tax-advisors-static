What Is the Best Entity Structure for a Medical Practice?

AE Tax Advisors · Business Tax Strategy · May 4, 2026

Choosing a medical practice entity requires two separate decisions: a legally permitted professional organization and its federal and state tax treatment. Compare whole-owner cash flow, retirement funding, staff costs and exit consequences. Income alone does not establish that an S-corp, C-corp or management company will save tax.

Start with professional licensing and ownership

Ask healthcare counsel which entity forms, owners and fee arrangements your state permits. A professional corporation is generally a corporation for federal tax purposes; an eligible corporation may elect S status. A professional LLC may be disregarded, a partnership or elect corporate treatment depending on its owners and elections. Neither form eliminates personal liability for a physician’s own professional conduct. Legal structure and tax classification are related but not interchangeable.

Model S-corp payroll against the actual sole-proprietor baseline

For a sole proprietor using the regular Schedule SE method, net earnings generally start at 92.35% of net business profit, not 100%. In 2026, the 12.4% Social Security component applies only up to the remaining $184,500 wage base after relevant Social Security wages; the 2.9% Medicare component has no wage-base ceiling. The 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax depends on combined covered wages and self-employment income and filing status: $250,000 married filing jointly, $125,000 married filing separately, and $200,000 for other statuses. Employer withholding above $200,000 is not the same as the household liability threshold.

An S-corp must pay reasonable compensation for services before treating payments as non-wage distributions. There is no universal defensible physician salary range. Document duties, time, specialty, local compensation evidence and the sources of business receipts. Compare employer and employee payroll taxes, other jobs, retirement contributions, QBI interactions, state taxes and compliance costs. Subtracting salary from pre-payroll profit is not the final distributable cash, because employer payroll taxes and other expenses still consume cash.

A transparent baseline example, not promised election savings

For illustration only, a single sole proprietor with $600,000 net practice profit and no other wages has $554,100 of net self-employment earnings under the regular 92.35% method. The Social Security component is $22,878 and regular Medicare is $16,068.90, totaling $38,946.90 before Additional Medicare Tax. Additional Medicare Tax on $354,100 is $3,186.90. These are not income taxes or estimated S-election savings; an S-corp comparison needs actual reasonable wages and employer costs.

C-corp benefits and retained earnings need a full exit model

The federal corporate income-tax rate is 21%, but distributed after-tax earnings may also be taxed to the shareholder. Retaining earnings does not permanently convert the owner’s tax rate to 21%, and accumulated-earnings or personal-holding-company rules may matter. Fringe-benefit deductions and employee exclusions have separate requirements, dollar limits and nondiscrimination rules. Life insurance is not universally deductible, transportation benefits are not universally employer-deductible, and medical reimbursements need an eligible plan. Compare specific benefits under IRS Publication 15-B rather than assume unlimited tax-free coverage.

Management entities and practice real estate

A management company must provide actual services at supportable prices and comply with healthcare law. A management fee is generally income to the recipient, not an extra deduction for the combined owner. Separately owned practice property raises lease, related-party timing, self-rental and exit questions. Moving appreciated property out of a corporation can trigger gain. Resolve these issues before transferring assets or signing a new lease.

Retirement capacity follows compensation and workforce facts

For 2026, employee elective deferrals are limited to $24,500. A plan that permits catch-up contributions can allow another $8,000 for an eligible participant age 50 or older, or $11,250 instead for someone attaining age 60, 61, 62 or 63 during 2026. The annual-additions ceiling is the lesser of $72,000 or 100% of applicable compensation, excluding eligible catch-up contributions. Employee deferrals, employer contributions and voluntary after-tax employee contributions share that ceiling. The corresponding dollar ceilings including catch-up are $80,000 or $83,250, not amounts everyone can contribute.

S-corp employer contributions use eligible W-2 compensation, not distributions. A cash balance plan requires actuarial design, employee coverage and funding commitments; no six-figure deduction follows automatically from a practice’s revenue. Review controlled groups and affiliated service groups when physicians own multiple entities.

Group practices, elections and a usable comparison

Partnership allocations must satisfy the applicable tax rules and agreement; they are not freely selectable deductions. Interposing an S-corp partner requires legal, compensation, benefits and tax review. Entity conversions and tax elections have deadlines and may cause gain, change basis or restrict later elections. Section 1374 generally concerns former C-corp built-in gains after an S election, not a blanket charge on every S-to-C conversion.

Ask for a side-by-side schedule covering total income and payroll taxes, state taxes, staff retirement costs, administration fees, cash retained and distributed, and a practice-sale scenario. Identify who handles legal documents, payroll, elections, returns and the implementation calendar. Do not act on a modeled deduction until the responsible professionals confirm the underlying facts.

Schedule a free 60-minute strategy session to discuss the facts needed for your entity review. Bring returns, ownership agreements, payroll and retirement documents through an agreed secure channel.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do all physicians need the same professional entity?

No. State licensing and ownership rules determine the permitted legal form. A professional corporation and a professional LLC do not necessarily have the same federal tax classification choices.

How much does an S-corp election save a physician?

There is no fixed amount. Compare reasonable compensation, employer and employee payroll taxes, other wages, state tax, retirement funding and administration against the actual current structure.

Is a C-corp’s 21% rate the owner’s final tax rate?

No. Distributions can trigger shareholder tax, and benefits, retained earnings and eventual sale or liquidation require separate analysis.

Can I change the practice’s tax classification later?

Potentially, but elections have eligibility and timing rules and conversions can trigger tax or restrict future elections. Review assets, basis, earnings history and legal requirements before acting.
