How to Plan Your QBI Deduction: Income, Wages & Limits

By AE Tax Advisors

Updated November 8, 2026.

The qualified business income deduction under Section 199A can be up to 20% of eligible business income, subject to several limits. Maximizing the deduction means calculating those limits correctly and comparing the total tax result. Spending money solely to enlarge a deduction can leave you worse off.

Start with qualified income, not gross receipts

QBI generally means qualified net income, gain, deduction and loss from an eligible U.S. trade or business. Employee wages, S corporation reasonable compensation and certain partner payments are not QBI. Allocable business deductions can reduce QBI, including certain self-employed health insurance and retirement deductions. Capital gains are generally outside QBI.

The overall deduction also faces a taxable-income limitation tied to taxable income before the deduction minus net capital gain. Carryforward losses can affect the current calculation. Prepare a business-by-business schedule rather than multiplying revenue by 20%.

Review income thresholds for the correct year

For 2026, the principal taxable-income threshold is $403,500 for married filing jointly and $201,750 for most other returns; married filing separately has a slightly different threshold. Above the applicable threshold, wage/property limits and specified service trade or business rules begin to matter. Use the current return instructions for your filing status and the expanded 2026 phase-in ranges.

Do not relabel a service business to change its result

Whether a business is an SSTB depends on its actual services and the governing rules. A new LLC name, invoice description or management entity does not by itself change classification. Separate businesses and related-party arrangements require substantive review. For eligible non-SSTBs above the phase-in range, W-2 wages and the unadjusted basis of qualified property can limit the deduction.

Model planning choices together

  • Retirement contributions: They may lower taxable income while also lowering QBI. Calculate both effects.
  • Owner compensation: S corporation wages can support a wage limit but reduce pass-through profit and create payroll tax.
  • Depreciation: A larger current deduction may reduce QBI and change future-year income.
  • Aggregation: Eligible businesses may qualify for an aggregation election, but common ownership alone is not the complete test. Maintain required disclosures and consistency.

Compare total tax before and after each action, including cash committed and the next year's result. Review our planning records and start with a business-owner review using your books, payroll reports and prior QBI schedules.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does every LLC receive a 20% deduction?

No. Eligible income, taxable income, business type, losses and wage/property limitations determine the deduction.

Can I classify my consulting company as a non-service business by changing its name?

No. SSTB classification depends on the actual services and applicable tax rules, not the company name.
