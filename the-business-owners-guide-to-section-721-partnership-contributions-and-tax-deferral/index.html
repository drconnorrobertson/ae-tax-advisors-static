Section 721 Partnership Contributions: Basis, Debt and Exceptions

By AE Tax Advisors · Published 2024-08-09

Updated November 8, 2026.

Section 721 generally permits a contribution of property for a partnership interest without current gain or loss. It preserves embedded tax consequences rather than erasing them. Review the property, liabilities, ownership and connected cash transfers before assuming a contribution is tax-deferred.

Separate value from tax basis

Illustration: property worth $2 million has a $1 million adjusted tax basis. If a qualifying contribution involves no cash, debt shifts or other exception, the $1 million built-in gain generally is not recognized merely on contribution. The partner's initial outside basis and the partnership's inside basis generally preserve the historic $1 million basis, subject to the relevant adjustments.

A later partnership sale of the property can recognize the embedded gain even if the contributor has not sold the partnership interest. A later sale of the interest or a taxable distribution can also matter. Tax deferral is not guaranteed to continue until one particular exit event.

Reconcile liabilities under Section 752

A reduction in a partner's share of liabilities generally is treated as money distributed, while an increase generally is treated as money contributed. Net the relevant changes and apply the outside-basis rules. Gain can arise when deemed or actual money distributions exceed the partner's adjusted basis; it is not correct to treat the full mortgage as automatically taxable.

The liability allocation can depend on recourse status, guarantees and other facts. Collect loan documents, pre- and post-contribution ownership and the partner's full basis schedule. Book capital and property equity do not substitute for outside tax basis.

Test connected cash transfers and special exceptions

A property contribution paired with money or other consideration can be a disguised sale under Section 707. Timing presumptions and detailed debt rules require review; waiting two years does not create an unconditional exemption. Contributions to certain investment-company partnerships can also fall outside the general nonrecognition rule.

Services are not property for this rule. A capital interest issued for services can create compensation income, while qualifying profits interests follow separate guidance. Do not apply a property-contribution example to a service partner without reviewing those distinctions.

Carry the built-in gain into the partnership records

Section 704(c) allocations generally account for differences between contributed value and tax basis. The method selected under the rules affects tax depreciation and later gain allocations; it does not automatically step every partner into fair-market-value tax basis. Retain the valuation, tax basis, allocation method and agreement provisions.

Later distributions can trigger additional rules, including Sections 704(c), 737 and 751. If the plan includes distributing another asset or returning contributed property, model that step before transferring the first asset.

Use the Section 707 guide for payments and disguised sales, and request a contribution review with the agreement, basis and loan schedules.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Section 721 eliminate built-in gain?

No. It generally defers recognition and preserves basis, subject to exceptions and later transactions.

Is the full mortgage automatically taxable?

No. The net liability change and outside-basis computation matter, alongside disguised-sale rules.

Does contributing services receive the same treatment as contributing property?

No. Services and interests issued for services require a separate compensation and partnership analysis.
