Short Term Rental Tax Strategy for Business Owners

AE Tax Advisors · Tax Planning & Strategy

Can an STR Loss Offset Wages or Business Income?

A short-term rental can generate a nonpassive loss when an exception to rental activity treatment applies and the owner materially participates. Neither a short booking nor a large depreciation deduction guarantees that result. For a high-income business owner, the practical question is whether the expected loss will be usable after all limits, not how large a deduction a study can identify.

The Seven-Day Rule Measures Customer Use, Not Personal Use

Under Regulation 1.469-1T(e)(3)(ii), an activity is not treated as a rental activity if the average period of customer use is seven days or less. Another exception applies when average customer use is 30 days or less and significant personal services are provided. Other regulatory exceptions may apply to unusual operations.

Calculate average customer use for the tax year from total days in all periods of customer use divided by the number of those periods, applying the regulation's definitions. This is not a three-year average or a seven-day owner-use allowance. For a simple one-property illustration, 210 customer-use days across 30 separate use periods gives a seven-day average; 211 days across 30 gives about 7.03 days and does not satisfy the seven-day exception. Do not split one continuous customer's stay into artificial bookings.

Material Participation Is Still Required

The rental exception removes the automatic rental-passive classification; it does not automatically make the activity nonpassive. The owner must materially participate under an applicable test. For example, Test 1 requires more than 500 hours. Test 3 requires more than 100 hours and at least as much participation as any other individual. A spouse's work counts for material participation, but a property manager's or contractor's hours can affect tests based on comparisons.

Real Estate Professional Status is not required when the STR is excluded from rental activity treatment and the owner materially participates. If the activity remains rental real estate, REPS and rental material participation may instead be relevant. Approving pricing or repairs is not automatically enough. Review STR material participation before choosing a management arrangement.

Personal Use and the Fewer-Than-15-Days Rule Are Separate

Under Section 280A, a dwelling is used as a residence when personal use exceeds the greater of 14 days or 10% of days rented at fair rental value. Rental deductions may then be limited to rental income under the vacation-home rules. Family use and below-market use can count as personal use. Record these days separately rather than relying only on platform bookings.

If a dwelling is used as a residence and rented fewer than 15 days during the tax year, Section 280A(g) generally excludes rental income and disallows rental expense deductions. It is not a route to a deductible STR loss. There is no universal 200-to-250-rental-day or 90%-to-95%-occupancy safe harbor that establishes nonpassive treatment.

Model Taxable Income, Not Gross Business Revenue

Illustration: suppose an owner has $800,000 of taxable income before an otherwise allowable $120,000 nonpassive STR loss. Simple subtraction would leave $680,000 before other tax-return adjustments. If $800,000 instead represents business revenue, this calculation is not valid without operating expenses and the rest of the return. A flat tax-rate multiplication is not a tax forecast.

A nonpassive classification is not an unlimited deduction. Check applicable entity basis limits first, then at-risk limits and passive activity rules, followed by the excess business loss limitation under Section 461(l). Personal-use restrictions and other deduction-specific rules can also limit the result.

Cost Segregation Changes Timing, Not Loss Eligibility

A cost segregation study allocates supported depreciable basis to appropriate asset classes. Land is not depreciable, and rental receipts reduce the net loss. A $2 million purchase is not automatically $2 million of building basis, nor does $500,000 allocated to shorter-lived components establish a particular first-year deduction without acquisition, placed-in-service, bonus-depreciation, and election details.

Determine the building's recovery period from its actual classification and use. Residential rental property generally uses 27.5-year GDS treatment and nonresidential real property generally uses 39 years, but the dwelling-unit and transient-use rules matter; ADS can differ. The Section 469 average-stay exception does not itself select the recovery period. Buying a second STR also does not automatically make combined losses deductible.

Guest Services and Self-Employment Tax

Nonpassive treatment under Section 469 does not by itself create self-employment tax. Substantial services primarily for the occupant's convenience, such as regular cleaning during a stay or meals, may require Schedule C reporting and self-employment tax. Ordinary rental services, such as utilities or cleaning common areas, generally differ. Review the service package under Publication 527 separately from material participation.

Real Example: Business Owner Strategy

A software company founder earning $900,000 annually acquires a $1.6 million beachfront STR property. Cost segregation identifies $400,000 in 5-year components. Year one depreciation reaches $140,000. Operating expenses (utilities, insurance, maintenance, property management) total $80,000. Total first-year loss: $220,000. Non-passive treatment allows deducting full $220,000 against business income, reducing taxable income to $680,000. Federal tax savings: $88,000. This strategy continues generating losses for multiple years through depreciation.

Before Buying or Commissioning a Study

Bring the proposed purchase allocation, expected booking periods and customer-use days, financing, expected personal use, manager's scope, each spouse's available work time, guest services, and prior loss carryovers. Ask for a pre-acquisition loss-usability review that identifies the activity classification, participation test, depreciation assumptions, and limits. Keep booking and operational evidence throughout the year so the return can be supported.

Publication 925 permits any reasonable method that establishes the services performed and approximate hours, including calendars, appointment books, or a supported narrative. Contemporaneous daily time reports are not mandatory, but timely records backed by booking messages, work orders, invoices, and emails are stronger than unsupported year-end estimates. Do not double-count overlapping time.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the short-term rental tax loophole?

The phrase commonly refers to an exception to rental activity treatment when average customer use is seven days or less, or 30 days or less with significant personal services. Meeting an exception does not automatically make losses nonpassive: material participation and other deduction limits still need review.

Can I use short-term rental losses to reduce business income?

Potentially. When an STR is excluded from rental activity treatment and the owner materially participates, an otherwise allowable loss may be nonpassive. REPS is not required for that route. Personal-use, basis, at-risk, and Section 461(l) limits can still restrict the deduction.

Does renting a home for fewer than 15 days create a deductible STR loss?

No. If a dwelling is used as a residence and rented fewer than 15 days during the year, Section 280A(g) generally excludes the rental income and does not allow rental expense deductions. This is separate from the average-customer-use exception under Section 469.

Does active participation automatically create self-employment tax?

No. Section 469 participation and self-employment tax are separate analyses. Substantial services provided primarily for guests can require Schedule C reporting and self-employment tax; ordinary rental services generally differ. Review the actual services and reporting rules rather than assuming an active or nonpassive label decides the result.
