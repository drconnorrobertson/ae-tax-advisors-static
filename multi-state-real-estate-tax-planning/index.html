Multistate Real Estate Tax Planning for Business Owners

Coordinate property states, owner residency and entity returns before choosing elections or filing.

A business owner with rentals in several states needs more than a list of returns. The review must connect where each property earns income, where its owners reside, which entities hold it and who will reconcile payments and credits. This service is designed for individuals and businesses earning $500,000 or more annually who want a coordinated plan for their business and property holdings.

Start with the property, owner and entity filing map

Property-source income and owner residency are separate questions. Rent or gain from real estate in another state can require a nonresident filing there even when the owner lives elsewhere. A resident state may also tax that income, with a credit subject to its own rules. For example, New York’s guidance distinguishes residents’ income from all sources from nonresidents’ New York-source income, including New York real property. That example is not a filing rule for every state.

An LLC’s formation state does not by itself determine all filing obligations. Map federal tax classification, legal ownership, partners or shareholders, property locations, operating activity and changes during the year. Entity income or franchise taxes, owner income taxes, nonresident withholding, sales or lodging taxes and local property taxes can have different triggers and filing rules. Do not apply a generic sales-nexus threshold or a single sales-factor formula to every rental and every tax type.

Separate withholding, composite returns and PTET decisions

  • Nonresident withholding: identify who must withhold or pay, any permitted exemption and how the payment reaches the owner’s return. Withholding is not automatically the final tax.
  • Composite filing: check eligible owners, consent, included income and whether participation actually replaces any individual filing. It may not resolve income outside the entity.
  • Pass-through entity tax: review qualifying entities and owners, the tax base, election procedure, payment dates and owner credit treatment for the specific state and year.

IRS Notice 2020-75 addresses federal deductions for specified state and local income taxes imposed on and paid by partnerships and S corporations. It does not make every state election available or guarantee a net household benefit. California, for example, has its own eligibility, annual election, payment and credit rules. Confirm the current-year requirements rather than carrying forward last year’s assumptions.

A useful comparison includes entity-level federal deduction effects, state addbacks, resident-state credits, owner eligibility and payment timing. Do not assume that paying PTET in a property state generates an equal credit in the owner’s resident state, or that it replaces all owner estimated payments. Reconcile the actual state forms and supporting payment records.

Commission a filing matrix and a named handoff

Ask the written proposal to identify the following deliverables and whether AE, your existing preparer or another specialist owns each step:

  • Filing matrix: property, entity, owner, jurisdiction, tax type, tax year, required return or unresolved question.
  • Missing-return review: prior filings, notices and possible gaps, with any reconstruction or correction work separately scoped.
  • Election and payment calendar: verified deadlines, authorized decision-maker, required records and evidence that payments were applied correctly.
  • Credit reconciliation: entity payments, withholding and owner credits tied to the relevant returns, including carryovers where applicable.
  • Preparer handoff: agreed state schedules, K-1 information, depreciation differences and a responsible professional for unresolved items.

Define the states, entities and tax years covered, when the review occurs and whether later acquisitions or residency changes require an additional engagement. A planning discussion does not establish that returns, registrations, legal work or ongoing monitoring are included.

Illustrative scope: three rentals and an operating business

An owner lives in one state, operates a business in another and holds rental properties through two LLCs in additional states. Begin with legal and tax ownership, each property’s income and sale history, and the owners’ residency. Next identify entity and owner filings, payments already made and possible credits. Only then compare a PTET election or other change. This is a review sequence, not a client outcome or a promise that each listed state requires the same forms.

Records to prepare before the consultation

  • Property and entity list, ownership percentages, acquisition and sale dates, and federal tax classifications.
  • Prior federal and state returns, state K-1s, notices, election copies and credit carryover schedules.
  • Payment confirmations, nonresident withholding statements and composite-return participation records.
  • Owner residency dates, operating locations and names of current preparers.

Share only a general description in your inquiry. Arrange secure delivery for returns and financial records. AE’s existing advisory services can be scoped around this review; agree on implementation responsibilities and timing in writing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does living in one state mean I file only there?

No. Real estate in another state can generate source income and a nonresident filing obligation. Resident-state taxation and any credit require a separate review; entity filings may also be required.

Does an LLC’s formation state decide where its rental income is taxed?

No. Review property location, entity tax classification, owner residency and activity for each tax type. Legal formation does not replace the sourcing and filing analysis.

Does a PTET election eliminate the owners’ state returns?

Not automatically. Entity eligibility, owner participation, election and payment requirements, credits and remaining individual obligations depend on the state and year.

What should the multistate engagement deliver?

Agree on a filing matrix, missing-record or return review, election and payment calendar, credit reconciliation and named preparer handoffs. Returns, corrections and ongoing monitoring are included only when expressly scoped.
