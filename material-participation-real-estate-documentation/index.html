Material Participation Real Estate Documentation

AE Tax Advisors · Tax Planning & Strategy

Build Evidence for the Test That Actually Applies

Material participation under Section 469 and Regulation 1.469-5T is an activity-level determination. It is not a label earned just by retaining decision-making authority or recording 100 hours. Identify the activity, check any grouping election, select an applicable test, and document the facts needed for that test.

For rental real estate, material participation alone generally does not overcome the automatic passive rule. The owner also needs REPS or an applicable exception. An STR excluded from rental activity treatment under the average-customer-use rules still requires a material participation analysis. Nonpassive status does not itself release prior passive losses.

The Seven Tests in Their Correct Order

  1. Test 1: More than 500 hours. Participation in the activity exceeds 500 hours during the tax year. Exactly 500 hours does not satisfy this test.
  2. Test 2: Substantially all participation. Your participation constitutes substantially all participation by all individuals, including nonowners. This is not a fixed 100-hour threshold.
  3. Test 3: More than 100 hours and no individual participates more. Your participation exceeds 100 hours and is at least as much as any other individual, including a manager or contractor. Equality is permitted; exactly 100 hours fails.
  4. Test 4: Significant participation activities. Participation exceeds 100 hours in each qualifying significant participation activity, and total participation in those activities exceeds 500 hours. These are trade or business activities in which you do not materially participate under another test; rental activities are not significant participation activities.
  5. Test 5: Five of the preceding ten years. You materially participated in the activity in any five of the ten immediately preceding tax years. The years need not be consecutive. Prior hours alone must actually establish material participation for those years.
  6. Test 6: Personal service activity in three prior years. You materially participated in a qualifying personal service activity in any three preceding tax years, whether or not consecutive. This is not a rental-specific three-of-five-years test. The regulation defines qualifying personal service fields and activities in which capital is not a material income-producing factor.
  7. Test 7: Facts and circumstances. Participation is regular, continuous, and substantial, with more than 100 hours. Management work is not counted under this test if another person is compensated for managing the activity or another individual spends more hours managing it. Facts and circumstances do not provide a simple 150-hour safe harbor.

Special rules restrict limited partners, who generally use Tests 1, 5, or 6. An LLC interest requires a facts-and-law review rather than an automatic limited-partner conclusion. Use the regulation for unusual ownership or activity structures.

Count the Right Person's Work in the Right Activity

A spouse's work counts for material participation even without ownership or a joint return. Keep the two spouses' hours separately recorded so the total can be substantiated. By contrast, one spouse must independently satisfy both REPS qualification tests: more than 750 hours and more than half of that spouse's personal services in qualifying real property trades or businesses.

Real property employee services count toward the qualifying REPS hours only if the employee is a more-than-5% owner of the employer. Employment hours still count in total personal services for the more-than-half comparison even when they do not qualify for the real property numerator. Investor work, such as reviewing financial statements or monitoring an investment, generally does not count as participation unless directly involved in the activity's day-to-day management or operations. A task label alone does not establish qualifying work.

Work not customarily performed by an owner does not count if a principal purpose is avoiding the passive loss rules. Do not inflate hours with simultaneous tasks, unperformed work, or unsupported travel estimates. A professional title or a bank transfer is not evidence of an hour of operational participation.

Evidence by Test

  • Test 1: a supported annual total greater than 500 hours in the activity, with dates, tasks, approximate time, and source records.
  • Test 2: evidence of your work and the work performed by all other individuals, including contractors. Management rights alone do not prove substantially all participation.
  • Test 3: more than 100 supported hours plus a defensible comparison with each other individual. Compare individuals, not merely the management company's combined total.
  • Test 4: an activity-by-activity schedule showing which activities qualify, more than 100 hours in each, and a total greater than 500 hours. Do not pool ordinary rental activities through this test.
  • Tests 5 and 6: prior-year records proving actual material participation in the required years, consistent activity identity, and the personal service qualification for Test 6. A tax return's assertion alone does not establish the underlying work.
  • Test 7: more than 100 supported hours and evidence of regular, continuous, substantial participation. Document paid managers and other individuals' management hours because those facts restrict the management work counted.

What Counts as a Work Log?

Publication 925 permits any reasonable method that establishes the services performed and approximate hours, including calendars, appointment books, or a supported narrative. Contemporaneous daily time reports are not mandatory, but timely records backed by booking messages, work orders, invoices, and emails are stronger than unsupported year-end estimates. Do not double-count overlapping time.

A useful entry identifies the date, property or activity, person, specific task, duration, and supporting record. Keep investor-only tasks separate. Save a manager's work description, contractor invoices, guest communications, maintenance records, and relevant calendars. Ask the preparer what corroboration is needed for the selected test before relying on a year-end reconstruction.

Two Boundary Examples

Illustration: an owner has 150 supported participation hours in one activity and the highest-participating other individual has 120 hours. Test 3 may be satisfied. If that other individual has 150 hours, equality is sufficient. If that other individual has 151 hours, Test 3 fails; another test would need to apply. Exactly 100 owner hours also fails Test 3.

Three properties with 150 owner hours each produce a 450-hour total. That total does not meet Test 1's more-than-500-hour threshold, and the properties cannot automatically be pooled. With separate activities, evaluate each separately; with a valid applicable grouping, evaluate the group. Three historical years with 100-plus hours do not establish a rental-specific three-of-five-years test, because no such test exists.

Keep Grouping and REPS Evidence Separate

The optional Regulation 1.469-9(g) election treats a qualifying taxpayer's rental real estate interests as one activity. General Regulation 1.469-4 grouping has its own appropriate-economic-unit and rental/nonrental restrictions. Neither allows arbitrary combination merely to obtain enough hours. Preserve the filed election or grouping disclosure and track continuity over time.

A nonpassive classification is not an unlimited deduction. Check applicable entity basis limits first, then at-risk limits and passive activity rules, followed by the excess business loss limitation under Section 461(l). Personal-use restrictions and other deduction-specific rules can also limit the result.

Before Claiming the Loss

Prepare an activity map, owner and spouse time records, other-worker evidence, historical participation support where relevant, and copies of elections. Identify the exact test relied on and unresolved facts. Then connect the participation conclusion to the separate rental classification, REPS, basis, at-risk, and carryforward review. Read STR material participation guidance or REPS documentation guidance for the relevant planning path.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the seven material participation tests?

The alternatives are more than 500 hours; substantially all participation; more than 100 hours and at least as much as every other individual; qualifying significant participation activities totaling more than 500 hours; material participation in five of the preceding ten years; qualifying personal service activity participation in any three preceding years; and restricted facts-and-circumstances participation. Each test has conditions. There is no rental-specific three-of-five-years test.

Can 100 or 150 hours establish material participation?

Exactly 100 hours does not satisfy Test 3 or Test 7. At 150 hours, Test 3 requires at least as much participation as every other individual. Test 7 has additional facts-and-circumstances and management restrictions. A number of hours alone does not establish either test.

Does material participation make every rental loss nonpassive?

No. Rental real estate generally remains passive unless the owner qualifies for REPS and materially participates, or another exception applies. An STR excluded from rental activity treatment still needs a material participation analysis. Other loss limits remain.

What documentation does the IRS accept for hours?

Publication 925 permits any reasonable method that establishes the services performed and approximate hours, including calendars, appointment books, or a supported narrative. Contemporaneous daily time reports are not mandatory, but timely records backed by booking messages, work orders, invoices, and emails are stronger than unsupported year-end estimates. Do not double-count overlapping time.
