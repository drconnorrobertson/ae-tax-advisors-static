Partnership AAR vs. Amended Form 1065

Determine whether a partnership correction requires an amended Form 1065 or a BBA administrative adjustment request and how partners are affected.

Book a Return Review Call

The short answer

A partnership cannot assume that every correction uses a conventional amended Form 1065. A partnership subject to the centralized partnership audit regime generally corrects partnership-related items through an administrative adjustment request, while an eligible partnership that validly elected out may follow amended-return procedures.

Book a Return Review Call

Can a Partnership File an Amended Return Instead of an AAR?

Only if the partnership is not subject to the BBA procedures for that reviewed year or specific IRS guidance authorizes an amended return. Current Instructions for Form 1065-X state that BBA partnerships must use an AAR for partnership-related items unless specific guidance permits an amendment. The election out of BBA cannot be added retroactively on Form 1065-X; it must have been validly made on the timely filed original return.

Do not infer the answer from partner count alone. The return-year election, eligible-partner rules, Schedule B answers, ownership structure, and any notice of administrative proceeding all matter. Also separate a correction filed before the original due date from one filed afterward: a timely superseding return is a different procedure from either an amended return or an AAR.

Five Checks That Decide the Filing Method

  1. Identify the reviewed year. BBA generally applies to partnership tax years beginning after 2017 unless a valid election out applies.
  2. Inspect the original return. Confirm the Schedule B election-out answers, attached election statement, partner roster, and partnership-representative designation.
  3. Test election-out eligibility. Recheck the number and types of reviewed-year partners; an ineligible partner can invalidate the election even when the partnership had 100 or fewer partners.
  4. Check timing and examination status. Form 1065-X instructions generally allow an amendment or AAR within three years after the later of the filing date or original due date, excluding extensions, and a BBA AAR must generally precede a notice of administrative proceeding for the year.
  5. Classify the change. Determine whether the item is partnership-related, whether an accounting-method procedure overlays it, and whether any narrow IRS relief actually covers the fact pattern.

The current IRS e-file guidance for amended partnership returns confirms that BBA filers use an AAR rather than an amended return, while non-BBA partnerships file a corrected Form 1065 with amended schedules and an explanation of each change.

Key Takeaways

  • Verify the partnership's BBA status for the reviewed year before choosing a form.
  • An AAR can create an imputed underpayment or push-out reporting rather than amended K-1s.
  • The partnership representative for the reviewed year controls key AAR actions.

Read the originally filed return first

Start with Schedule B, the partnership-representative designation, any election out of the BBA regime, and the ownership roster for the year being corrected. Eligibility and a timely election matter. The partners' current identities do not replace the reviewed-year facts, and a new managing member is not automatically the person authorized to act for that year.

Next classify the error. An omitted deduction, allocation change, corrected sale, basis item, credit, or depreciation adjustment can affect the partnership and partners differently. If the issue involves an accounting method, Form 3115 rules may overlay the partnership-correction procedure. Avoid choosing an AAR solely because the software offers it.

How an AAR changes the economics

A BBA AAR can result in an imputed underpayment paid at the partnership level or, when permitted and elected, adjustments pushed out to reviewed-year partners. Forms 8082, 8985, and 8986 can become part of the package. These mechanics differ from issuing a familiar amended K-1, and they can change who bears the cash cost when ownership has changed.

Model the result before filing. Compare partnership-level payment, push-out effects, interest, state conformity, partner liquidity, and the partnership agreement's tax-allocation provisions. A technically valid correction can still create an avoidable dispute if current owners pay tax attributable to former partners.

Example: a missed deduction in a sold partnership interest

Assume a partnership finds a $200,000 reviewed-year deduction after one partner has sold its interest. A non-BBA amendment might flow revised information to the reviewed-year partners. A BBA partnership may instead compute an AAR adjustment and decide between partnership-level treatment and an available push-out process. The current cap table alone does not answer who receives or funds the adjustment.

The workpaper should show BBA status, reviewed-year ownership, the corrected return lines, imputed-underpayment computation if applicable, push-out alternative, state consequences, and the economic allocation under the partnership agreement.

Example: 30 Partners Does Not Automatically Mean an Amended Return

A calendar-year partnership has 30 reviewed-year partners and discovers omitted depreciation after filing. Management assumes it can amend because the partner count is below 100. The original return, however, did not include a valid election out of BBA. The partnership is a BBA partnership for that year and generally must use an AAR; it cannot add the election out with the correction.

Change the facts: the original return made a valid election out and every reviewed-year partner was eligible. The partnership may generally use amended-return procedures, provide amended K-1s to the reviewed-year partners, and coordinate their federal and state amendments. The deduction error is identical, but the return-year procedural status changes the entire filing package.

What to Model Before Choosing AAR Payment or Push-Out

  • Partnership-level imputed underpayment, available modifications, interest, penalties, and payment timing.
  • Reviewed-year partners versus current partners and who economically bears the adjustment.
  • Forms 8985, 8986, and 8978 reporting across any pass-through partner tiers.
  • Partnership-agreement provisions for tax distributions, former partners, indemnities, and audit adjustments.
  • State conformity, state AAR procedures, composite returns, withholding, and elective PTE tax.
  • Cash-flow and communication burden when reviewed-year partners must report adjustments in a later reporting year.

Documents required for an AAR decision

  • Original Form 1065, Schedule B, and partnership-representative designation
  • Election-out statement, if one was made
  • Reviewed-year and current ownership schedules
  • Partnership agreement and transfer documents
  • Corrected trial balance, tax-basis capital, Section 704(b), and allocation workpapers
  • Prior AARs, examination notices, and correspondence for the reviewed year
  • State returns and composite, withholding, or PTE tax filings

Common Partnership-Correction Failure Points

  • Counting partners but not testing partner types. A partnership or certain other ineligible owners can defeat an election out.
  • Assuming the current partners control the reviewed year. BBA rights and burdens can turn on the reviewed-year representative and ownership.
  • Issuing amended K-1s with a BBA AAR. Pushed-out adjustments generally use Forms 8985 and 8986 instead.
  • Applying expired special relief. Revenue procedures for specific years or issues are not permanent permission to amend.
  • Missing the three-year filing window or an examination notice. Timing can close the voluntary AAR path.
  • Fixing federal reporting but not the agreement economics. Current owners can otherwise fund liabilities tied to former owners.
  • Ignoring state procedures. States may not follow the federal AAR mechanism or payment allocation.
