Tax Advisor for Real Estate Agents Who Own Rentals

By AE Tax Advisors Team · Published October 3, 2026 · Updated November 6, 2026

A high-producing real estate agent who also owns rentals has two different tax assignments: commission-business planning and property-owner planning. AE's initial conversation can help define a coordinated review of those activities, your current preparer's responsibilities and an upcoming election, purchase or filing decision.

This page describes the work to request in an engagement. For the underlying tax topics, read the real estate agent tax strategy guide. If you own a staffed brokerage or are buying a book of business, see brokerage-owner advisory scope.

When a coordinated review may fit

  • Your commissions rose sharply and you need to compare entity and compensation alternatives before changing how payments are received.
  • You own rentals and want the adviser to distinguish commission income, property results and usable deductions.
  • You lead a team, have employees or share ownership in other businesses that may affect retirement-plan design.
  • You are purchasing an office, rental or interest in another business and need a pre-closing review.
  • Your current CPA will keep preparing returns but you need a documented planning and implementation handoff.

Start with the commission and ownership records

Ask for a reconciliation of reported gross commissions, brokerage splits, referral fees, advertising, team expenses and the income shown on the return. A deposit total or Form 1099 alone may not explain the full activity. Identify who earned the income under the brokerage agreement and which taxpayer received it before discussing a new entity.

Licensed real estate agents can be statutory nonemployees for federal tax purposes when substantially all compensation is tied to sales or other output rather than hours worked, and a written contract provides that they will not be treated as employees for federal tax purposes. That is a federal classification rule, not permission to disregard state licensing, brokerage contracts or employee rules for other team members. See the IRS real estate agent guidance.

Define the entity comparison you want

Request a comparison of the current arrangement with eligible alternatives using actual profit, owner duties, other wages, benefits, payroll administration, state taxes and compliance costs. An S corporation requires reasonable compensation for shareholder services. A flat 15.3% times a proposed distribution does not calculate reliable savings because Social Security limits, Medicare rules and other factors differ.

Confirm the legal and practical ability to receive commissions through the proposed entity before implementing. A tax election does not rewrite your brokerage agreement or professional licensing requirements. Use the strong-commission-year S-election guide to prepare questions.

Own rentals? Ask for a separate participation and loss review

Working in real estate sales does not automatically make every personally owned rental loss nonpassive. Real estate professional qualification, material participation in rental activities, existing elections, ownership, basis and other limits require documentation. Professional qualification and rental participation are separate questions; an occupational title is not an election or a substitute for records.

A cost segregation proposal should identify the property owner, relevant dates and basis, expected deduction timing and current usability. It should not promise that a rental deduction will reduce commission income or self-employment tax. See IRS Publication 925 and study scope and owner-level review.

Prepare for a useful first call

Describe the commission business, whether you own rentals, your entity classification, team size, current preparer and next decision date. The first discussion can establish fit without sharing tax returns. Arrange approved secure document delivery after scope and records are agreed.

Frequently asked questions

Does being a real estate agent make my rentals nonpassive?

No. Real estate professional qualification and material participation in the rental activities are separate requirements, with additional loss limits and records to review.

Should every high-producing agent form an S corporation?

No. Compare eligibility, reasonable compensation, payroll, state rules, licensing and brokerage contracts, other wages and total costs using the actual facts.

Can I keep my current tax preparer?

Discuss that during intake. A written engagement should specify the planning, implementation and filing responsibilities of each professional.

Does the advisory fee include a property study and all returns?

Do not assume it does. Confirm the advisory deliverable and separately scoped studies, returns, amendments and specialist work before engagement.
