Section 704(b) Capital Accounts: Book, Tax and Allocation Rules

By AE Tax Advisors · Published 2023-11-27

Updated November 8, 2026.

A partnership can have several different capital and basis records for the same partner. A Section 704(b) book capital account is not the partner's outside tax basis or automatically the tax-basis capital reported on Schedule K-1. Special allocations need more than a spreadsheet that balances.

Keep the three records separate

Book capital generally reflects the economic arrangement under the applicable regulations, including permitted book values and revaluations. Tax-basis capital uses tax amounts. Outside basis tracks the partner's basis in the partnership interest and can include liability adjustments that are not part of capital.

A positive capital account does not automatically support a loss deduction or tax-free cash distribution. Reconcile contributions, income, deductions, distributions and liabilities under the rules applicable to each record.

Read the agreement before allocating items

Section 704(b) and the regulations address substantial economic effect and, when appropriate, allocation according to partners' interests in the partnership. Capital-account maintenance alone does not establish that an allocation will be respected.

Review liquidation rights, deficit obligations or other applicable safeguards, distribution priorities and the actual economic arrangement. A tax allocation cannot simply assign deductions to the partner who values them most while ignoring who bears the associated economic effect.

Contributed property introduces another layer

Property can have a book value different from its adjusted tax basis. Section 704(c) addresses pre-contribution built-in gain or loss; later permitted book-ups can create reverse Section 704(c) layers.

For example, two partners may contribute assets with the same fair value but different tax bases. Equal book capital does not make their built-in gain identical or remove the need for tax-allocation workpapers.

Do not treat revaluation as a tax-basis increase

A permitted book revaluation can change book capital without giving every partner new depreciable tax basis. Transfers and distributions may raise separate Section 743(b), Section 734(b) or other adjustment questions. Those rules are not interchangeable with a book-up.

Maintain asset-level schedules where required so the next preparer can trace the layers instead of substituting the newest appraised value for tax basis.

Apply owner deduction limits separately

Even a properly allocated loss can be limited by outside basis, at-risk, passive-activity and excess-business-loss rules. Capital-account compliance does not guarantee current deductibility.

Credits and other specially governed items can have their own allocation rules. Review the actual item rather than assuming every allocation follows the same income-and-loss clause.

Prepare an annual reconciliation

  • Agreement and amendments, including liquidation and distribution provisions.
  • Contribution values and original tax bases.
  • Book and tax income, deductions and distributions.
  • Liability allocations and partner outside-basis schedules.
  • Section 704(c) layers and any transfer or distribution adjustments.
  • K-1 reporting and suspended-loss records.

Request a partnership allocation review before a special allocation, new partner admission, buyout or distribution. The agreement, economics and tax schedules should describe the same transaction.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is K-1 tax-basis capital my outside basis?

Not necessarily. Outside basis includes additional adjustments, including relevant liability allocations.

Does a book-up create depreciation deductions for everyone?

No. A book revaluation does not automatically increase tax basis.

Can partners assign deductions to whoever has the highest rate?

Allocations must satisfy the applicable partnership-tax rules and economic arrangement, not just a tax preference.
