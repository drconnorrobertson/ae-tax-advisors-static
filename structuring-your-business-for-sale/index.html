How to Structure Your Business for Sale: Entity Changes That Save Tax

Structure determines the tax bill before any strategy is applied to it, and most of the useful changes take years to season.

Request Your Free Tax Assessment

By AE Tax Advisors Team · Billings, Montana · Updated November 6, 2026

Before agreeing to a business sale, compare the legal form, tax classification, asset and owner basis, purchase-price allocation and payment terms. An asset sale, corporate stock sale and LLC-interest transfer can produce different results even at the same headline price. Some changes need years of lead time; others can be evaluated during negotiations. The useful question is which feasible structure produces the best after-tax outcome for this seller and buyer.

Asset Sale Versus Equity Sale

In an asset acquisition, the buyer generally receives a cost basis allocated among the acquired assets, with applicable Section 197 intangibles generally amortized over 15 years. A corporate stock purchase ordinarily creates basis in the stock without changing inside asset basis, unless a qualifying election or other rule applies.

An LLC-interest transfer cannot be classified by its label alone. An interest in a disregarded single-member LLC can be treated as an asset transfer. A partnership-interest sale generally has capital treatment except for Section 751 ordinary-income items, and a Section 754 election or mandatory Section 743(b) adjustment can affect the buyer's share of inside basis. Corporate stock rules apply when the LLC is taxed as a corporation.

For an asset seller, inventory, receivables and recapture can produce ordinary income. Goodwill may generate capital or Section 1231 gain depending on the facts, but prior amortization can create ordinary Section 1245 recapture. C corporations may also face shareholder-level tax on distributed proceeds.

Model the buyer's deductions and liabilities alongside the seller's taxes, fees, debt payoff and cash at closing. A proposed basis step-up has a value to negotiate; it is not a reason to assume every seller should accept an asset sale.

The C Corporation Double Tax

A simplified C corporation model illustrates two tax levels: 21% tax on corporate gain plus an assumed 23.8% shareholder tax on the remaining 79% equals 39.802% combined federal tax. This assumes no basis recovery, losses or other adjustments and full application of NIIT. Actual asset and stock bases, distribution or liquidation treatment, state rules and transaction price can change the result.

A stock sale may avoid a separate corporate asset-sale tax, but the buyer must accept that structure. Any Section 1202 exclusion depends on the stock's eligibility, acquisition date, holding period and other requirements; a stock sale alone does not qualify.

Personal goodwill may be relevant where valuable relationships and rights genuinely belong to the individual rather than the corporation. Employment, non-compete and assignment agreements, who owns the goodwill, and the actual transaction must be reviewed by tax and legal advisors. It is not an automatic allocation or a last-minute way to avoid corporate gain.

S Corporations and the Built-In Gains Tax

A former C corporation that elects S status may owe corporate-level built-in gains tax on qualifying gain recognized during the five-year recognition period. Conversion-date value, tax basis, prior transactions and statutory limitations determine the exposure. Waiting beyond that period can change this particular tax, but does not erase owner-level tax or unrelated liabilities.

Do not convert solely to fit a planned sale date. Section 1202 has C corporation, original-issuance, acquisition-date, holding-period and other requirements, and an S election can jeopardize eligibility. Continued C status also does not guarantee exclusion. Compare those facts and the proposed buyer structure using the entity structuring guide and the Section 1202 guide linked above.

The F Reorganization With an LLC Drop-Down

This is one possible structure in middle-market S corporation sales, subject to careful execution and deal-specific analysis.

The shareholders may contribute their stock to a newly formed holding company, which elects to treat the old operating company as a qualified subchapter S subsidiary. The subsidiary may then convert to a single-member LLC. Whether the sequence qualifies as a reorganization under Section 368(a)(1)(F), and how the later sale is taxed, depend on the actual steps and elections.

A properly structured LLC-interest sale can give the buyer asset-acquisition treatment while the seller remains subject to the character of the underlying gain, including any ordinary-income items. A retained stake is not automatically tax-free: the seller must identify a valid contribution or other nonrecognition path, and the cash leg is separately taxable. See the rollover-equity closing-tax guide before relying on a 20% holdco stake in the proceeds model. An F reorganization also does not cure a historically defective S election by itself; diligence remains necessary.

In other fact patterns, a Section 338(h)(10) or Section 336(e) election may treat a stock transaction as a deemed asset sale. The seller's gain character and tax cost should be modeled and negotiated rather than assuming a fixed gross-up.

Separating Real Estate and Intellectual Property

Make two separate decisions: whether to sell the operating business, and whether to retain, sell or exchange the building. Compare buyer preferences, lease terms, financing, ownership and after-tax cash flow rather than assuming the property must travel with the company.

When real estate has been separately owned from the original acquisition, the owner may be able to sell the business while retaining the building and leasing it to the buyer at a supportable market rent. Selling the building is a separate transaction. A qualifying owner, including a corporation, may potentially use a 1031 exchange for qualifying business or investment real property. Section 1031 does not cover the sale of company stock, partnership interests, goodwill, inventory or the operating business as a whole.

Separate ownership from the start is different from extracting property that an operating corporation already owns. A distribution of appreciated property generally causes corporate gain as though sold at fair market value, subject to liabilities and other rules. In a C corporation, a shareholder distribution can also create dividend or liquidation consequences. In an S corporation, pass-through gain, shareholder basis and possible built-in gains tax need their own calculation. Doing it years earlier does not by itself remove those taxes.

A cost segregation study and existing depreciation records affect the gain and recapture model. Retaining a leased building may preserve future depreciation on remaining basis, subject to the applicable rules; it does not restore deductions already used. See holding company versus operating company for the operating structure context.

Before transferring title, provide the original acquisition records, entity elections, ownership and shareholder basis, debt, depreciation, current valuation and proposed lease or sale terms. Have tax and legal advisors compare leaving the building in place, selling it from the current owner and any proposed distribution or restructuring. Timing alone is not a tax exemption.

Sources: IRS Publication 542: gain from property distributions, IRS Publication 544: qualifying real-property exchanges, IRS Publication 541: partnership interests and basis adjustments.

Purchase Price Allocation

An applicable asset acquisition uses the Section 1060 residual allocation method and generally Form 8594. A Section 338 deemed asset acquisition uses Form 8883. Use supportable fair market values and coordinate buyer and seller reporting; do not assume every stock or membership-interest transfer uses the same form.

A Working Sequence

Ordered by how much lead time each item needs.

  • Five years out. Decide the C versus S question in light of Section 1202, and complete any conversion that starts a recognition period.
  • Three years out. Model whether real estate and intellectual property should remain in their current owner. Distinguish separate ownership from acquisition from a later taxable extraction; quantify transfer costs before changing title.
  • Two years out. Review any proposed gifting with estate and transaction counsel, including ownership, valuation, assignment-of-income, basis and exclusion requirements before a transaction becomes binding.
  • Twelve months out. Evaluate whether an F reorganization fits the deal, review the S election history, and prepare the books for diligence.
  • At the letter of intent. Model the allocation and the after-tax proceeds under each structure before agreeing to either.

Key Takeaways

  • Model asset, stock and LLC-interest transfers using the actual tax classification, basis and proposed elections.
  • A C corporation asset sale can create two tax levels. A stock sale does not by itself establish Section 1202 eligibility.
  • A C-to-S conversion can create a five-year built-in gains recognition period; compare this with all other transaction taxes.
  • An F reorganization is one possible structure, not an automatic tax-free rollover or cure for S election defects.
  • Separately owned real estate can be retained, leased or sold. A corporation can also potentially exchange qualifying real property; extracting its appreciated building can itself trigger tax.
  • Use supportable asset values and negotiate allocation, price and implementation responsibilities together.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I convert my C corporation to an S corporation before selling?

Model the proposed sale first. A former C corporation may face built-in gains tax during the five-year recognition period, and an S election can jeopardize Section 1202 eligibility. Conversion-date value, basis, the buyer structure and applicable stock-acquisition and holding-period rules matter.

Why do buyers insist on an asset sale?

An asset acquisition can give the buyer a cost basis to depreciate or amortize and allow selected liabilities to be negotiated. The benefit depends on asset eligibility, timing and price. Asset purchases do not eliminate every successor liability, and stock elections or partnership-basis adjustments may create other alternatives.

What is personal goodwill and can I actually claim it?

It is value that belongs to the individual owner rather than the company, based on actual relationships, rights and agreements. Review employment, non-compete and assignment documents, ownership and valuation with transaction counsel. A separate payment is not enough to establish personal goodwill.

Can I move my building out of the company right before I sell?

A distribution of appreciated corporate real estate generally triggers gain based on fair market value and may have shareholder-level consequences. Doing it years earlier does not by itself remove those taxes. Separate ownership from the original acquisition is different from extracting a building already owned by the company. Model the current entity, basis, debt and distribution before changing title.

How long does restructuring before a sale take?

The timing depends on the transaction. The built-in gains recognition period and applicable Section 1202 holding requirements can require years; those stock holding rules depend on acquisition date. An F reorganization and other deal steps require a tailored legal and tax schedule. Useful modeling is still possible once a buyer is identified.
