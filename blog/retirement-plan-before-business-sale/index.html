What happens to my retirement plan if I sell my company next year?

A practical owner tax decision

Book a Call

Reviewed November 6, 2026. Apply the rules and limits for the actual sale year.

The direct answer

A sale can change plan sponsorship, employee coverage, vesting and funding obligations. Reconcile eligible compensation separately from sale proceeds, then confirm who will sponsor the plan and pay each obligation before signing or closing.

A retirement plan can be part of sale-year tax planning, but a large closing payment does not create a matching contribution limit. Review the deal with your tax advisor, plan administrator, enrolled actuary if applicable, and transaction counsel while the terms can still be changed.

Reconcile sale cash with eligible compensation

Prepare two schedules: one showing the sale price, tax character and net cash available after debt, taxes and closing costs; the other showing compensation eligible under the retirement plan. They answer different questions.

  • S corporation owner: use eligible Form W-2 compensation under the plan, not shareholder distributions or the stock-sale proceeds. S corporation pass-through income does not become retirement-plan compensation merely because it is taxable to you. The IRS S corporation contribution guidance makes the distinction explicit.
  • Sole proprietor or partner: the starting point is qualifying net earnings from self-employment, with the required adjustments. Use the self-employed calculation in Publication 560, not gross receipts or the full cash received at closing.
  • Sale allocation: capital gain does not become earned compensation. Depreciation recapture and other ordinary-income sale components are not automatically plan compensation either. Have the preparer classify each item under the applicable compensation and self-employment rules.
  • Wages, bonuses or consulting after closing: separately identify the actual service provider, employer, entity, service period and plan terms. A genuine payment for services may qualify, but a purchase-price label does not establish compensation. Do not backdate payroll or reclassify sale proceeds just to increase a limit.

Reconcile payroll registers, W-2s, deferral elections, prior contributions and any other employer's plan. Eligibility, annual limits, reasonable compensation, controlled-group rules and the plan's compensation definition still apply.

Decide who sponsors the plan after closing

Equity sale: the purchased company often remains the employer and plan sponsor even though its owners change. Confirm whether the buyer requires continuation, amendment, merger or a pre-closing termination and who is authorized to sign each action.

Asset sale: the seller may retain the plan while employees move to the buyer. Specify who funds pre-closing contributions and accrued benefits, who keeps participant records, and whether any assets or liabilities transfer. Continuing to hold sale cash in the seller does not by itself establish an ongoing compensation base.

The IRS guidance on employer mergers describes several possible plan outcomes; the deal structure alone does not choose one. Review related businesses, employee transfers, coverage testing, loans, notices and possible partial termination. Do not promise employees an immediate distribution until the administrator and counsel confirm a permissible event and any successor-plan restrictions.

For vesting, final filings and the distribution process, use the separate business-sale plan termination guide. Do not terminate a plan merely to make the closing checklist shorter.

Model three separate contribution decisions

  1. Employee elective deferrals: check the election date, available compensation, payroll implementation and amounts already deferred elsewhere. A later employer contribution deadline does not let an S corporation owner defer wages already paid. Withheld employee money has its own prompt deposit rules.
  2. Employer contributions: calculate SEP, matching or profit-sharing contributions from eligible compensation, plan terms and applicable limits. Eligible retroactive adoption and prior-year employer funding may remain available by the employer's return due date, including a valid extension. That does not cure missing deferral elections or create eligible compensation.
  3. Defined benefit or cash balance funding: obtain an actuarial valuation, required minimum and deductible range, including the effect of other plans. A cash balance plan is a defined benefit commitment, not an owner-selected deposit equal to the sale proceeds. Test continuing funding, employee benefits and eventual termination costs before adopting or increasing it.

See the IRS explanation of adoption, deferral and employer contribution timing and the after-year-end deadline guide. Do not assume an extended tax return also extends actuarial minimum funding.

Hypothetical $5 million sale: cash is not the limit

This illustration is not a client result or a promised tax saving. Assume an owner sells S corporation stock for $5 million, has $200,000 of eligible W-2 compensation for the year, and the corporation is considering a SEP contribution. Ignore other plans and assume all adoption, eligibility and deduction requirements are satisfied.

The compensation-based employer amount is $200,000 × 25% = $50,000, below the $72,000 SEP dollar ceiling for 2026. The $5 million sale price does not increase that amount. If covered staff have $100,000 of eligible compensation and the same 25% SEP allocation applies, their contributions add $25,000. The company would need $75,000 for those owner and staff contributions, before administration or other obligations.

The deduction is not a $75,000 tax saving. Model which taxpayer receives it, applicable limitations, marginal rates, state treatment and later taxation of retirement distributions. A smaller deduction with affordable staff costs and retained liquidity may be better than the largest theoretical contribution. See the IRS SEP limits and SEP participation and allocation rules.

Put the closing and plan deadlines on one calendar

  • Before signing: resolve sponsor, plan disposition, employee coverage, actuarial exposure and whether the purchase price or escrow addresses unpaid contributions.
  • Before final payroll and closing: confirm timely deferral elections, payroll cutoffs, prompt deposits and who has authority and cash to fund obligations afterward.
  • By the applicable employer return deadline: document any permitted retroactive adoption and deductible employer payment. For ordinary calendar-year 2026 returns, S corporations and partnerships generally have March 15, 2027, or September 15 with a valid extension; sole proprietors and calendar-year C corporations generally have April 15, 2027, or October 15 with a valid extension.
  • For defined benefit minimum funding: the general outside deadline is 8½ months after the plan year ends, September 15, 2027 for a calendar-year 2026 plan. Earlier quarterly installments can apply. An October return extension does not move this deadline. Have the actuary confirm the schedule under Treasury Regulation 1.430(j)-1.
  • After closing: assign notices, testing, annual reports, final contributions and any termination work to named parties with specific dates. Short tax years, fiscal years and applicable relief can change the calendar.

Records and questions for the review

Prepare the sale term sheet and allocation, closing timeline, entity ownership before and after the deal, plan documents and amendments, recent Forms 5500 and actuarial reports, participant census and vesting, payroll and contribution history, loan balances, tax-return extensions and a cash forecast.

Ask for a written comparison of owner contributions, required staff contributions, setup and ongoing administration, actuarial and investment costs, and any merger or termination fees. Identify the employer that funds each item, the taxpayer claiming each deduction and the advisor responsible for implementation. No dollar fee or savings estimate is assumed here.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I put my business-sale proceeds directly into a retirement plan?

Cash can fund an otherwise permitted contribution, but sale proceeds do not create eligible compensation or expand plan limits. For an S corporation owner, use eligible W-2 compensation, not shareholder distributions or stock-sale capital gain. Other sale components require separate analysis.

Does a business sale automatically terminate the retirement plan?

No. An equity sale may leave the company as sponsor; an asset sale may leave the plan with the seller. The buyer terms, plan documents, employee transfers and applicable law determine continuation, merger or termination and who funds the obligations.

Can I make a retirement contribution after the sale closes?

Possibly, if the proper employer and plan remain able to act, eligible sale-year compensation supports it, and adoption, election, funding and deduction deadlines are met. Sale cash alone is not enough, and an extended return does not extend defined benefit minimum funding.

What costs should the owner compare before a sale?

Compare the owner contribution with required staff contributions, ongoing administration, actuarial and investment costs, and possible merger or termination fees. Identify the sponsor and taxpayer responsible for each payment and deduction before committing to a funding amount.
