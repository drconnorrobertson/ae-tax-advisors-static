Tax Planning Before Signing a Business Sale Letter of Intent

The headline purchase price is only the starting point. Review the proposed structure, property treatment and cash you actually retain before key terms become difficult to renegotiate.

Discuss My Business Sale Before I Sign

Put the tax review before the commercial commitment

Ask deal counsel and your tax advisor which proposed terms constrain the final agreement. An LOI may combine nonbinding commercial provisions with binding clauses; counsel must assess the document. Give the tax reviewer the draft, entity history, ownership, expected closing date and existing asset schedules.

Five decisions to model

DecisionWhy it changes the analysis
Asset sale or equity saleThe seller’s entity, basis and underlying assets affect character and possible entity-level tax.
Purchase price allocationEquipment, inventory, goodwill and real estate can produce different consequences.
Sell or retain the buildingA retained property creates a landlord relationship and separate cash-flow and tax questions.
Cash, escrow, earnout or seller noteCash timing differs from tax timing; contingent terms and recapture need review.
Post-sale workEmployment, consulting and restrictive-covenant payments need their own analysis.

Compare after-tax proceeds, not just the headline price

Illustrative situation: one offer pays more in total but includes a seller note, escrow and consulting payments. Another pays less at closing with fewer contingent amounts. Model debt payoff, transaction costs, tax character, payment timing and realistic collection risk for both. Do not assume every deferred payment qualifies for installment treatment. Review IRS Publication 544 with the proposed agreement.

The building needs its own decision

If you plan to retain the premises, negotiate the lease alongside the business transaction. Review rent, maintenance, capital repairs, guarantees and renewal terms, then revisit the property’s tax treatment after you stop participating in the business. See selling the business while keeping the building. A 1031 exchange does not generally cover the sale of a business or every asset sold with real estate.

Bring this review file

  • Draft LOI and proposed asset schedule or allocation.
  • Recent entity and owner returns, basis schedules and depreciation.
  • Debt, seller financing, escrow and earnout terms.
  • Any building lease or proposed property sale agreement.
  • Your lawyer’s timeline and unresolved commercial questions.

Ask for a written comparison, assumptions and a handoff to deal counsel and the return preparer. A tax consultation does not replace legal drafting, valuation or transaction diligence.

Common questions

Is it too early to get tax advice before signing an LOI?

No. Proposed structure and allocation should be reviewed while the commercial terms can still be discussed. Your lawyer should explain which LOI provisions are binding.

Can I sell my business but retain the building?

That may be an option, depending on ownership and buyer terms. Review the lease, financing and separate property consequences alongside the business sale.
