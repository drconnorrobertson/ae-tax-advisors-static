Rental Depreciation: From Property Basis to the Usable Deduction

By AE Tax Advisors · Published 2026-08-22 · Updated November 8, 2026.

Depreciation can reduce taxable rental income without matching the year's cash spending. The allowed amount depends on the property basis, classification, service date, elections and deduction limits. It is not calculated from the mortgage payment or the property's current market value alone.

Establish basis and rental readiness

Reconcile purchase costs, qualifying acquisition expenses, land and later improvements. Land is not depreciable. A former home converted to rental use requires a separate basis analysis. Depreciation generally starts when property is ready and available for its rental use.

Classify the actual assets

A qualifying residential rental building generally uses 27.5-year GDS depreciation; nonresidential real property generally uses 39 years. Applicable conventions, ADS and special facts can change the calculation. The short-stay passive-activity test does not by itself decide the building's recovery period.

Furniture, appliances, site improvements and building components need their own classifications. A study can support shorter lives for qualifying components, but there is no universal reclassification percentage.

Test bonus eligibility separately

The restored 100% allowance generally applies to eligible property acquired and placed in service after January 19, 2025. Asset type, acquisition history, related-party rules, elections and ADS requirements still matter. A study performed in 2026 does not reset an older property's dates.

Compare regular depreciation and any available expensing treatment using the full return. The largest first-year number may be suspended or produce a different state result.

Apply limits before counting a wage offset

Basis, at-risk, passive-activity and excess-business-loss rules can restrict deductions. Personal use can also change the rental-expense calculation. Real estate professional status requires its own tests and does not remove the need for material participation in the relevant rental activity.

The active-participation special allowance has ownership, income and filing-status limits. A qualifying short-term activity requires the rental-exception and participation analysis. Neither label creates an unlimited deduction.

Keep the sale calculation in view

Depreciation allowed or allowable affects adjusted basis. Sale proceeds must be allocated to the assets, with the appropriate gain and recapture treatment. Section 1245 and Section 1250 rules differ. A 1031 exchange does not automatically defer gain on every component or all transaction proceeds.

Review the return implementation workflow and lookback correction guide. Request a depreciation review with the closing documents, asset schedule, rental calendar and prior returns.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a cost segregation study guarantee a current deduction?

No. Property eligibility and taxpayer-level limitations both apply.

Does real estate professional status allow unlimited rental losses?

No. Material participation and other applicable limitations still need review.
