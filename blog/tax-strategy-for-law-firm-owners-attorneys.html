Law Firm Owner Tax Planning: Fees, Partner Pay and Retirement Plans

By AE Tax Advisors · Published 2026-09-19 · Updated November 8, 2026.

A law firm's tax plan should start with its fee arrangements, ownership and client-fund records. The amount in a bank account is not necessarily earned fee income or cash available for an owner distribution.

Separate client funds from firm revenue

Reconcile trust-account activity, earned fees, retainers, advanced costs and operating transfers. The legal rights under the fee agreement and applicable accounting method affect tax treatment. Coordinate tax reporting with the firm's professional obligations rather than treating every deposit alike.

Review owner compensation by entity

A partner's draw, guaranteed payment and distributive share are not the same as corporate payroll. An S corporation shareholder-employee requires reasonable compensation for services. Compare the full employment-tax, income-tax and administrative effects before recommending an election.

Calculate the QBI limitation at the owner level

Legal services are a specified service trade or business for Section 199A. The deduction depends on taxable income and applicable phaseout rules, along with other limits. Firm revenue by itself does not determine whether an individual owner qualifies.

Model retirement funding with staff costs

A cash balance or other defined benefit plan requires actuarial design and ongoing funding analysis. Include eligible employees, related businesses and the practice's cash-flow variability. An owner's age or income alone does not establish a deductible contribution.

Coordinate succession and outside investments

Review buy-sell terms, partner retirement obligations, goodwill ownership and payment character before an exit. Evaluate any real estate investment on its own economics and loss limitations; a cost segregation study does not automatically shelter legal-fee income.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does high firm revenue alone eliminate QBI?

No. Apply the owner-level taxable-income tests and other requirements.

Can partner draws be treated as S corporation wages?

No. Determine the entity classification and the actual payment treatment.
