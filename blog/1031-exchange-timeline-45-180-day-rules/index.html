The 1031 Exchange Timeline: 45-Day Identification and 180-Day Closing Rules

The 45-day and 180-day 1031 exchange periods both begin on the date you transfer the relinquished property. The identification period ends 45 calendar days after that transfer. The exchange period ends on the earlier of 180 calendar days after the transfer or the due date, including extensions, of your federal return for the transfer year. The two clocks run concurrently; the 180-day period does not begin after Day 45.

In a typical delayed exchange, the transfer date is the relinquished-property closing date shown in the exchange and closing records. If multiple relinquished properties are transferred as part of the same deferred exchange, the regulations generally measure the periods from the first transfer. Confirm the start date with the qualified intermediary and tax adviser before closing.

Two Concurrent Deadlines With No Routine Extension

When you transfer a relinquished property in a deferred exchange, two statutory periods begin under IRC Section 1031(a)(3):

  • 45 calendar days to identify potential replacement properties in writing (IRC Sec. 1031(a)(3)(A))
  • 180 calendar days to receive one or more properly identified replacement properties, subject to the earlier return-due-date limit (IRC Section 1031(a)(3)(B))

There is no routine extension for market conditions, financing delays, title problems, weekends, holidays, or good-faith effort. Targeted disaster relief can postpone a deadline only when the taxpayer and transaction qualify under a specific IRS notice or other guidance. Do not assume a general filing extension or disaster declaration automatically changes the exchange dates.

The IRS summarizes these federal timing rules in the Instructions for Form 8824. Revenue Procedure 2018-58 lists Section 1031 time-sensitive acts that may receive disaster relief when the IRS issues applicable guidance.

Worked Calendar Example

Assume an individual transfers the relinquished rental property on November 15, 2026:

  • Day 45 is December 30, 2026. A valid identification must be completed by the end of that day unless the replacement property has already been received.
  • Day 180 is May 14, 2027. Both periods are measured from November 15; Day 180 is not 180 days after December 30.
  • The return-due-date limit is April 15, 2027 for a calendar-year individual without an extension. Because April 15 arrives before May 14, the exchange period would end early. A timely Form 4868 extension generally moves the filing due date beyond Day 180, although it does not extend the time to pay tax.

Use the legal transfer date, not the contract date, listing date, date the buyer's funds arrive at escrow, or date you start searching. For calendar arithmetic, the transfer date is the reference date and the following calendar day is Day 1; Day 45 is the transfer date plus 45 days. Keep the closing statement and QI confirmation with the calculation.

The 45-Day Identification Period

By the end of the 45-day period, replacement property generally must be designated in a signed writing and clearly described by street address, legal description, or distinguishable name. The signed document must be delivered to the person obligated to transfer the replacement property or to another permitted person involved in the exchange. Delivery only to the taxpayer or the taxpayer's disqualified agent is not enough. In practice, investors commonly use the QI's identification form and obtain written confirmation of timely receipt.

The Form 8824 instructions provide an important exception: if you receive the replacement property before the 45-day period ends, it is automatically treated as identified. An identification also can be revoked or replaced before Day 45 using a signed writing delivered through the permitted procedure. After the period closes, the final valid list is locked.

The IRS provides three rules governing how many properties you may identify:

The Three-Property Rule

You may identify up to three replacement properties regardless of their combined fair market value. This is the most commonly used rule and provides straightforward flexibility. You can ultimately acquire one, two, or all three of the identified properties.

The 200% Rule

You may identify more than three properties, but their combined fair market value cannot exceed 200% of the value of the relinquished property sold. For example, if you sold a property for $1 million, you could identify four or five replacement properties as long as their total value does not exceed $2 million.

The 95% Rule

If you exceed both the three-property limit and the 200% threshold, you must actually acquire properties representing at least 95% of the total value of all identified properties. In practice, this rule is extremely difficult to satisfy and is rarely used intentionally. Investors who accidentally trigger it often find their exchange disqualified.

If you identify more than three properties and exceed the 200% limit, the 95% rule is not an extra extension or a planning safe harbor. It requires receipt, by the end of the exchange period, of identified property whose aggregate fair market value is at least 95% of the aggregate fair market value of everything identified. Test the rule using values at the end of the identification period and have the QI or tax adviser review the list before it becomes final.

The 180-Day Exchange Period

Under IRC Sec. 1031(a)(3)(B), you must close on at least one identified replacement property within 180 calendar days of selling the relinquished property. This 180-day period runs concurrently with the 45-day identification window, it does not start after the identification period ends. In other words, you have a total of 180 days from the sale, and the first 45 of those days are your identification window.

If you identify a replacement property on Day 44, Day 45 is still part of the 180-day period; only 136 calendar days remain after Day 44. Starting the search before the relinquished-property closing provides more time for due diligence, financing, title work, and backup options.

The Tax Return Deadline Trap

There is a critical interaction between the 180-day exchange period and your tax return due date that catches many investors off guard. Under the statute, the exchange period ends on the earlier of 180 days after the sale or the due date (including extensions) for filing your tax return for the year in which the relinquished property was sold.

The applicable return and extension form depend on who is the taxpayer in the exchange—an individual, partnership, S corporation, C corporation, trust, or other filer. Do not automatically use the owner's personal deadline when an entity is the exchanger. A filing extension extends the return due date, not the exchange beyond Day 180 and not the time to pay tax. Coordinate the extension before filing the return for the transfer year.

Reverse Exchanges and the Timeline

In a reverse exchange, you acquire the replacement property before selling the relinquished property. An Exchange Accommodation Titleholder (EAT) holds the parked property during the exchange period. Under Revenue Procedure 2000-37, the same 45-day and 180-day deadlines apply, but the clock starts when the EAT acquires the parked property.

From that acquisition date, the investor has 45 days to identify which property will be the relinquished property (the one being sold) and 180 days to complete the sale. Reverse exchanges add complexity because you must line up a buyer for the relinquished property within the timeline, and you are carrying the cost of two properties simultaneously.

Common Timeline Mistakes

Even experienced investors make preventable errors with 1031 exchange timelines. The most frequent mistakes include:

  • Waiting until the last minute to identify. A document that reaches the permitted recipient after Day 45 is late even if it was mailed earlier. Follow the QI's delivery procedure and retain time-stamped confirmation.
  • Forgetting that calendar days include weekends and holidays. Day 45 falls on a Sunday? The deadline is still Sunday. The IRS counts every calendar day with no exceptions for non-business days.
  • Not filing a tax return extension. As discussed above, failing to extend your return can cut your 180-day window short without warning.
  • Confusing the 180-day period as starting after the 45-day period. Both deadlines run from the same starting point, the date of sale. They are concurrent, not sequential.
  • Relying on an unsigned or misdirected message. A conversation with a broker is not identification. Use a signed document, describe the property unambiguously, deliver it to a permitted party, and retain proof of timely receipt. Confirm the QI's electronic-signature and delivery protocol before Day 45.

Practical Timeline Planning Tips

Smart investors begin planning their replacement property search well before the relinquished property closes. Here are steps that protect your exchange:

  • Start evaluating replacement properties while the relinquished property is still under contract, do not wait for closing day to begin your search.
  • Submit your written identification as early as possible within the 45-day window. Aim for Day 30 or earlier to leave a buffer.
  • Use the three-property rule strategically by identifying your top choice plus two backup properties in case financing or inspections fall through.
  • File for a tax return extension immediately if your exchange spans the turn of a tax year.
  • Work with a QI who provides clear written confirmation of receipt for your identification letter.
  • Build a closing timeline backward from Day 180 and build in at least two weeks of cushion for title issues, lender delays, and inspection repairs.

Pre-Closing Decision Checklist

  1. Identify the taxpayer. The same taxpayer generally must transfer the relinquished property and receive the replacement property; disregarded entities and entity changes need review.
  2. Engage the QI before the transfer. Receiving or controlling the sale proceeds can destroy deferred-exchange treatment before the 45-day clock even begins.
  3. Confirm investment or business intent. A principal residence or property held primarily for sale is not made eligible merely by meeting the deadlines.
  4. Calculate Day 45, Day 180, and the return due date in writing. Use the transfer date and the actual exchanger's federal return.
  5. Choose an identification rule. Model the three-property, 200%, and 95% tests before sending the signed list.
  6. Test value, liabilities, cash, and asset classes. Equal value and debt are planning shortcuts; the tax result comes from the complete boot, basis, and recapture calculations.
  7. Plan the filing. Determine whether an extension is needed and preserve records for Form 8824 and any state reporting.

Documents to Keep in the Exchange File

  • Relinquished-property purchase and sale agreement, closing statement, deed, and proof of transfer date
  • QI agreement, assignment notices, escrow records, and evidence that the taxpayer did not receive the proceeds
  • Every identification and revocation, signed and paired with proof of delivery and receipt
  • Fair-market-value support used for the three-property, 200%, or 95% identification test
  • Replacement-property contract, closing statement, deed, financing documents, and receipt date
  • Prior depreciation schedules and any cost-segregation study needed for Section 1245 and Section 1250 calculations
  • Return-extension confirmation, Form 8824 workpapers, boot and basis schedules, and state exchange forms

Frequently Asked Questions

When do the 45-day and 180-day 1031 exchange deadlines start?

Both periods begin on the date the relinquished property is transferred. The identification period ends 45 calendar days after that transfer. The exchange period ends on the earlier of 180 calendar days after the transfer or the due date, including extensions, of the federal return for the transfer year. The periods run concurrently.

What property qualifies for a 1031 exchange?

Real property held for investment or productive use in a trade or business. The definition is broad, so a rental house can be exchanged for a warehouse or raw land. Personal residences, property held primarily for sale such as flip inventory, and foreign real property do not qualify.

What is boot in a 1031 exchange?

Boot is money or other non-like-kind value received in the exchange and can create recognized gain. A decrease in liabilities is generally treated as money received, but money paid and liabilities assumed can affect the net calculation. Equal or greater debt is a useful planning shortcut, not the complete statutory calculation.

Does a 1031 exchange defer depreciation recapture?

A Section 1031 exchange may defer gain attributable to prior depreciation, but the result is not automatically all-or-nothing. Boot, the classes and fair market values of Section 1245 and Section 1250 real property exchanged, and the Form 8824 recapture calculations can cause current recognition.

What happens if Day 45 or Day 180 falls on a weekend or holiday?

The federal deferred-exchange periods use calendar days and ordinarily do not roll to the next business day. Targeted disaster relief can postpone a deadline only when the taxpayer and transaction qualify under a specific IRS notice or other guidance.

Do I need a written identification if I buy the replacement within 45 days?

The Form 8824 instructions state that replacement property received before the end of the 45-day period is automatically treated as satisfying the written-identification requirement. The acquisition still must be properly structured through the exchange, and the taxpayer should retain the closing and QI records.
