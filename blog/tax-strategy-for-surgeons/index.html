Tax Strategy for Surgeons: Practice, Retirement and Property Decisions

By AE Tax Advisors · Published 2026-08-10 · Updated November 8, 2026.

A surgeon's tax review should separate employment wages, practice income, facility interests, consulting work and rental activity. Each income stream has its own reporting and participation questions. High income alone does not determine the right entity or create an unlimited deduction.

Map the clinical and facility interests

Identify the legal owner, tax classification, services performed and income allocated from each practice or ambulatory surgery center. A partnership K-1 does not by itself establish passive status or an exemption from self-employment tax. Review the agreement, ownership and actual role.

Consulting or locum work should be assigned to the entity that actually contracts for and earns the income. Professional licensing, payer agreements and employment restrictions can affect the available structure.

Model compensation without a fixed salary formula

An S corporation must pay reasonable compensation for shareholder services before treating additional payments as distributions. Specialty, duties, hours, collections, staff contribution and market evidence matter. A universal salary range or percentage of profit is not a safe harbor.

Compare the full employment-tax calculation, including the Social Security wage base, Medicare rules and other wages. Income-tax, QBI, benefits and compliance costs also affect whether a change is worthwhile. See the S corporation comparison.

Design retirement plans around the entire workforce

For 2026, the regular 401(k) elective-deferral limit is $24,500 and the defined-contribution annual-additions limit is $72,000 before applicable catch-ups. These are separate limits, not amounts that can simply be added to an unrestricted employer contribution.

Eligible employees, related employers, compensation and contributions to other plans matter. Cash balance funding requires actuarial and coverage analysis. Obtain a design and funding schedule rather than relying on a standard six-figure deduction based only on age.

Review facility depreciation and related-party rent

A study must classify actual assets, reconcile basis and apply the relevant acquisition and service dates. Specialized medical use does not make every mechanical system short-life property. The owner still needs a deduction-usability analysis.

Renting a facility to a practice in which the owner materially participates can invoke self-rental rules. Net rental income and net rental losses do not necessarily receive symmetrical treatment. Cost segregation does not automatically make a facility loss deductible against clinical wages.

Keep personal rentals and charitable plans separate

For a short-term rental, test the rental exception and material participation independently, then apply the remaining loss limits. For ordinary rental real estate, one spouse's qualifying real estate professional status and the activity's material participation need separate support.

Charitable gifts require an eligible recipient, substantiation and the applicable valuation and percentage-limit analysis. An appreciated-property gift to a private foundation does not automatically receive the same treatment as a gift to a public charity.

Plan the practice exit by asset and transaction type

Allocate a practice sale among the assets actually transferred and evaluate compensation, goodwill, receivables and other categories. Section 1031 generally applies to qualifying real property, not an entire medical practice sale merely because the proceeds buy a building.

Request a coordinated practice review with current returns, ownership documents and the planned transaction. Confirm the advisory and implementation scope.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a standard S-corp salary for every surgeon?

No. Reasonable compensation must reflect the actual services and supporting evidence.

Can a surgeon automatically deduct an STR loss against wages?

No. The rental exception, material participation and all remaining deduction limits must be evaluated.

Can practice-sale proceeds automatically enter a 1031 exchange?

No. Section 1031 eligibility depends on qualifying real property and the exchange requirements, not simply reinvesting sale cash.
