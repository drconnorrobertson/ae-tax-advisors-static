Can You Deduct Equipment Lease Payments? Tax Lease vs. Purchase

Published on August 7, 2026 | Business Owner Tax Strategy | By AE Tax Advisors Team

Updated November 8, 2026.

Payments for equipment used in your business may be deductible rent if the arrangement is a true tax lease. If the agreement is a conditional sale, the business instead accounts for a purchase: depreciation may be available, qualifying interest is considered separately, and principal payments are not rent deductions. The contract's label or bookkeeping classification does not decide the federal tax result.

Compare the two treatments

Equipment arrangements for the business using the asset
QuestionTrue tax leaseConditional purchase
Who generally claims depreciation?The tax owner, generally the lessorThe buyer treated as tax owner
How are payments treated?Qualifying business rent, with timing rulesPrincipal reduces the obligation; qualifying interest is separate
Can the user claim bonus or Section 179?Generally not on an asset it does not own for taxOnly if the asset and taxpayer meet the applicable requirements
Does a small down payment set the deduction?NoNo; establish basis, service date and eligibility

Read the entire agreement before classifying it

The IRS identifies several signs of a conditional sale: payments building an ownership interest, title passing after a stated amount is paid, nominal purchase options, payments substantially above fair rental value, and an identifiable interest component. Evaluate the arrangement when made, including side agreements and mandatory purchase terms. A fair-market-value option is relevant but does not by itself settle every contract.

Supply the equipment quote, executed lease, payment schedule, purchase options, guarantees and any amendments. Identify who bears meaningful ownership risks and who receives the residual value. Do not claim the same cost as both rent and depreciation.

A purchase does not guarantee a full first-year deduction

Tax ownership is only the first question. Review business use, asset classification, acquisition and placed-in-service dates, basis and elections. Section 179 has annual, investment and business-income limits; bonus depreciation has separate eligibility and timing rules. Vehicles and listed property have additional restrictions. State treatment can differ.

A machine ordered in December but not ready and available for its business use until January generally does not become a December deduction merely because the financing closed. Read the Section 179 guide and bonus-depreciation eligibility discussion before forecasting the first-year amount.

Compare cash cost and tax timing separately

For an illustrative $100,000 financed purchase, tax basis is determined from the transaction rather than equated to the down payment. The depreciation deduction might be accelerated, spread over years, or limited. For a true lease, qualifying rent is generally considered under the applicable accounting and payment rules. Neither structure makes the equipment free.

Ask for a comparison showing down payment, total payments, interest, fees, purchase option, expected useful life, maintenance, disposition value and federal and state tax by year. Include the cost of keeping or returning the asset. A larger immediate deduction can be less valuable than lower overall financing cost or better operational flexibility.

Book accounting and special leases

A finance or operating classification under financial-accounting standards does not replace the tax analysis. Preserve a book-to-tax reconciliation where treatment differs. Vehicle arrangements with terminal rental adjustment clauses have specific statutory requirements; the presence of “TRAC” in a document is not a substitute for checking those requirements.

At buyout, return or sale

Reconcile remaining basis, payments already capitalized and any final option payment. Returning financed property can involve debt cancellation or a disposition; selling depreciated equipment can create gain and recapture. Do not assume either event always creates gain, or that a buyout cost should be counted twice.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I deduct equipment lease payments?

Qualifying rent under a true tax lease may be deductible. Under a conditional sale, analyze depreciation and interest instead of deducting principal as rent.

Does a $1 buyout guarantee a full deduction?

A nominal buyout can indicate a purchase, but a first-year deduction still depends on basis, business use, service date and applicable depreciation rules.

Does a finance lease on my books settle the tax treatment?

No. Financial-accounting classification and federal tax ownership are separate analyses.

Can the lessee and lessor both depreciate the equipment?

The same ownership interest cannot simply be depreciated by both parties. Establish which party is treated as tax owner under the actual arrangement.
