Oil and Gas Working Interests: Tax Rules and Loss Limits

By AE Tax Advisors · Published 2026-08-13

Updated November 8, 2026.

A qualifying oil-and-gas working interest can fall outside the passive-activity rules without a material-participation test. The exception depends on how the interest is held and the owner's liability. It does not make every drilling expense immediately deductible or every loss usable against wages.

Review the ownership and liability structure

Section 469(c)(3) applies to a working interest held directly or through an entity that does not limit the taxpayer's liability. A structure that limits liability may lose this special exception, but the remaining passive-activity analysis must still be performed; loss of the exception does not automatically prove every activity is passive.

Changes during the year, including conversion to a limited interest, have special rules. Have the legal and tax reviewers examine the actual agreement and timing. The tax exception should not be a reason to accept liability without understanding it.

Separate drilling costs from equipment and acquisition costs

Intangible drilling and development costs can qualify for an election under Section 263(c). Tangible equipment, lease acquisition costs and other expenditures can follow different capitalization, depreciation or depletion rules. Obtain an itemized schedule supported by the operator's records; do not assume a fixed percentage of the investment is deductible.

Timing depends on payment, economic performance, service dates and the applicable rules. A December payment does not guarantee a same-year deduction for all future work. Equipment bonus depreciation also requires its own eligibility analysis.

Apply the loss limits and AMT computation

Outside basis where relevant, amount at risk and other individual loss limitations can restrict the benefit even when the working interest is nonpassive. Nonrecourse financing should not be assumed to supply deductible at-risk capacity.

Intangible drilling costs can create an alternative minimum tax preference. The independent-producer exception and its limitation require the Form 6251 computation; it is not enough to assume that all IDC is exempt from AMT. Calculate the regular and alternative-minimum-tax results together.

Model later income and disposition

Working-interest income can create self-employment tax, while depletion and later sale treatment have separate rules. A royalty interest generally follows a different tax analysis from an operating working interest. Do not treat royalty income as automatically available to absorb a passive loss.

Review ongoing capital calls, operating costs, depletion, potential recapture and the expected holding period. A large deduction does not establish a positive investment return or compensate for an unexamined liability.

Records for a tax review

  • Offering, ownership and liability documents, including any planned conversion.
  • Operator cost allocations, payment dates and work schedules.
  • Financing, basis and at-risk schedules.
  • Proposed elections, AMT calculations and state adjustments.
  • Projected operating income, capital calls and disposition treatment.

Request a tax-scope review before relying on a proposed deduction. The investment and legal risks require their own evaluation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does every oil-and-gas interest avoid passive-loss rules?

No. The working-interest exception depends on the ownership and liability structure, and other rules may apply.

Does limited liability automatically make every loss passive?

No. It can remove the special working-interest exception; the remaining activity and participation rules still require analysis.

Is the whole investment deductible when paid?

Not automatically. Cost classification, elections, timing, basis, at-risk and other limitations must be tested.
