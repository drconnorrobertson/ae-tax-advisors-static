Can My Spouse Qualify for Real Estate Professional Status?

Published on August 10, 2026 | Updated on November 6, 2026 | Real Estate Tax | AE Tax Advisors

Yes. One spouse can qualify as a real estate professional while the other works full time outside real estate. On a joint return, losses from rental activities in which the household satisfies material participation may reduce other income. Qualifying status alone does not make every rental loss deductible.

Two Tests One Spouse Must Meet Independently

Under Section 469(c)(7), the qualifying spouse must perform more than 750 hours of services in real property trades or businesses in which that spouse materially participates, and those services must exceed half of that spouse's personal services in all trades or businesses for the year. Exactly 750 hours is not enough. The other spouse's work hours do not enter this individual's more-than-half calculation.

Job title, a real estate license, or not having another job does not establish qualification. Identify the actual services and qualifying businesses. Employee services generally do not count unless the person owns more than 5% of the employer. Review the real estate professional qualification guide before counting hours.

Then Test Material Participation in the Rentals

Spouses' participation can be combined for material participation, even though their hours cannot be combined for the two professional-status tests. Apply an available test under Treasury Regulation 1.469-5T, such as more than 500 hours. The more-than-100-hour test also requires that no other individual participate more. Document the work of managers and contractors when a test compares participants.

A property manager is not an automatic disqualification. Do not claim the manager's work as your own. The facts-and-circumstances management test has restrictions when someone else is paid to manage, whereas another test may apply on its own terms.

The Aggregation Election Is a Choice, Not a Requirement

Without an election, each rental real estate interest is generally tested separately. An eligible taxpayer may elect under Treasury Regulation 1.469-9(g) to treat all rental real estate interests as one activity. This can help a portfolio meet participation requirements, but it also affects later years and the treatment of a sale of one property. Do not group automatically simply to reach an hours target.

The election ordinarily requires a statement with the original return. If an election was missed, have the preparer evaluate available relief and the actual filing history; do not backdate a statement or assume relief applies. Retain the filed statement and evidence of acceptance or filing.

Illustrative Example: Qualification Is Not the Final Calculation

Assume one spouse performs 1,000 eligible hours in a rental business in which that spouse materially participates and 200 hours in another business. The 1,000 hours exceed 750 and half of the 1,200 total service hours. The other spouse's separate full-time job does not change that test. If the first spouse instead performs 600 eligible real estate hours, the household cannot add 200 hours from the second spouse to clear 750.

Next, the couple must establish material participation for each rental or for the combined activity under a valid aggregation election. If those requirements are met, a current rental loss can be nonpassive. Basis, at-risk, personal-use and excess-business-loss limitations may still restrict the deduction. Previously suspended losses are not automatically released just because the owner qualifies this year. See the separate suspended-loss rules.

A Practical Records Checklist

  • Separate each spouse's annual work calendar, including non-real-estate employment and businesses.
  • For each task, record the date, property, person performing it, time and actual service, with emails, invoices or tenant records as support.
  • Separate operational work from investor-only monitoring; do not count the same time twice.
  • Keep property-management contracts and records of tasks delegated to other people.
  • Retain grouping elections, prior Forms 8582, depreciation schedules and basis/at-risk workpapers.

IRS Publication 925 permits reasonable proof of participation; a contemporaneous daily log is not an absolute legal requirement. Keeping reliable records as work occurs is nevertheless more useful than reconstructing an unsupported estimate after an inquiry. No documentation method guarantees an audit result.

What to Review Before Filing

Ask the preparer to show the individual qualification calculation, the rental participation test, the election history and the allowable-loss calculation separately. Frequently Asked Questions

Can one spouse qualify while the other works full time?

Yes. One spouse must independently meet both real estate professional tests. On a joint return, qualifying rental losses may offset household income if material participation and the other deduction limits are satisfied.

Can we combine our hours at any stage?

Spouses cannot pool hours to meet the more-than-750-hour and more-than-half tests. Spousal participation does count for the separate material participation analysis.

Does hiring a property manager disqualify us?

No. Record your own work and evaluate the applicable participation test. A paid manager affects certain tests, but does not automatically prevent an owner from meeting the more-than-500-hour test.

Is a rental aggregation election required?

No. Without a valid election, test each rental real estate interest separately. An eligible taxpayer may elect to treat all rental real estate interests as one activity, but should consider ongoing obligations and future sale consequences.

Does a time log guarantee the deduction?

No. A credible log and supporting records help establish actual participation. The activity, eligible services, elections, basis, at-risk amount and other loss limits must also support the return position.

Can rental losses offset business income if my spouse qualifies as a real estate professional?

Potentially, if your spouse independently meets both real estate professional tests and materially participates in the relevant rental activities. Spousal participation counts for material participation, but one spouse must independently meet both real estate professional tests; the household cannot pool hours for those qualification tests.
