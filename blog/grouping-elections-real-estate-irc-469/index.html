Grouping Elections for Real Estate Under IRC 469

For an owner with several properties, grouping can change how participation is measured and when losses may be used. It does not create real estate professional status, make an unsupported deduction allowable, or automatically free suspended losses. First identify the applicable rule: general activity grouping under Treas. Reg. 1.469-4 is different from a qualifying real estate professional's election under Treas. Reg. 1.469-9(g) to treat all rental real estate interests as one activity.

Reviewed November 6, 2026. Federal rules and examples below are planning guidance, not a determination that an owner qualifies.

Two Different Rules: Economic-Unit Grouping and the REPS Election

General activity grouping, section 1.469-4: Trade or business activities and rental activities may be combined when they form an appropriate economic unit, subject to specific restrictions. Relevant factors include the type of business, common control, common ownership, geographic location and operational interdependencies. Shared ownership, an LLC or the same booking platform alone does not establish an appropriate group. The facts and consistency rules matter.

The real estate professional (REPS) election, section 1.469-9(g): A qualifying taxpayer generally treats each rental real estate interest as a separate activity unless a valid election treats all interests as a single rental real estate activity. This is an all-interests election, not permission to pick only the properties with favorable losses. Pass-through entity interests and publicly traded partnerships require additional review under sections 1.469-9(e) and (h).

Rental activities generally remain passive even when the owner materially participates, unless an exception applies. The REPS exception requires both annual qualification and material participation in the relevant rental activity. A short-term accommodation that meets an exception to the passive rules' definition of a rental activity follows a different analysis; it is not automatically included in the REPS rental election. Classify each activity before combining hours.

Authorities: Treas. Reg. 1.469-4, grouping and restrictions; Treas. Reg. 1.469-9, qualifying taxpayers and rental elections; IRS Publication 925, rental exceptions and passive activity rules.

Establish REPS Separately from Rental Material Participation

An individual must perform more than 750 hours in real property trades or businesses in which that individual materially participates, and more than half of all personal services performed in all trades or businesses must be in those qualifying real property trades or businesses during the same tax year. Exactly 750 hours is not enough. Employee services generally do not count as qualifying real property services unless the ownership exception applies; the statutory five-percent-owner definition generally requires more than 5% ownership.

On a joint return, one spouse must independently satisfy both REPS thresholds. The spouses cannot simply combine their hours to reach 750. Spousal work can count when evaluating material participation, including in a rental activity, even though each spouse's personal-service hours remain separate for REPS qualification. Investor-type work is not automatically participation, and every hour should be allocated once to the activity actually served.

After establishing REPS, test material participation separately for each rental interest, or for the combined rental activity if a valid all-interests election applies. Having fewer than 500 hours at a property does not by itself mean the property is passive: another applicable test may be satisfied.

The Seven Material Participation Tests: Correct Thresholds

Treas. Reg. 1.469-5T(a)-(f) provides the alternatives and their limitations. This summary does not make every test available to every kind of rental or ownership interest.

  1. Test 1: More than 500 hours in the activity during the year.
  2. Test 2: The owner's participation is substantially all participation by all individuals, including nonowners.
  3. Test 3: More than 100 hours, and at least as much participation as any other individual, including a paid manager. This is an individual-by-individual comparison, not a comparison with the management company's total hours.
  4. Test 4: More than 500 hours combined in significant participation activities. Each counted activity must be an eligible trade or business activity with more than 100 hours of participation and must not qualify under another material participation test. This is not a 750-hour test or a shortcut for rental activities as defined in the passive activity regulations.
  5. Test 5: Material participation, without using this test, in any five of the preceding ten tax years.
  6. Test 6: A qualifying personal service activity with material participation in any three prior tax years. Ordinary capital-intensive rental real estate is not a personal service activity for this test.
  7. Test 7: Regular, continuous and substantial participation under all facts and circumstances. It requires more than 100 hours; management work cannot count for this test if another person is paid to manage or another individual spends more management time.

Limited partnership interests generally restrict the available tests to Tests 1, 5 and 6. Including such an interest in a REPS elected group can apply those restrictions to the combined activity, subject to the less-than-10%-of-gross-rental-income exception in section 1.469-9(f). Have the advisor review the actual interest and entity documents rather than relying on the entity's label.

Hypothetical: Eight Rentals and 560 Hours

Assumed facts, not a client result: An individual performs 900 qualifying hours in a personally operated real estate brokerage in which the individual materially participates, 100 hours in an unrelated business, and 70 hours at each of eight long-term rentals. The brokerage work is not employee work. Total services are 900 + 100 + (8 × 70) = 1,560 hours. The brokerage alone supplies more than 750 qualifying hours and more than half of total services: 900 > 780. REPS is therefore established independently of whether the rental activities satisfy material participation.

Assume each rental has substantial work by a paid manager, so the owner's work is not substantially all participation; there is no qualifying prior-year participation history. At 70 hours per separate rental, Tests 1, 3 and 7 are not met; Test 2 is excluded by the facts, Test 5 by the history, and Tests 4 and 6 do not apply to these ordinary rental activities. Without the election, the current rental losses are passive on these assumptions, though passive income or an applicable exception could still permit use.

With a valid section 1.469-9(g) election, 8 × 70 = 560 rental hours satisfy Test 1 for the combined activity. Only the 560 rental hours support that test; the 900 brokerage hours cannot be imported into it. Current-year losses may then be nonpassive, subject to the separate deduction limits below. If the owner already met another applicable test at each property, the election might provide no current participation benefit.

Can Rentals Be Grouped with an Operating Business?

Under the general section 1.469-4(d)(1) rules, rental and trade or business activities must form an appropriate economic unit, and either one activity must be insubstantial relative to the other or each business owner must hold the same proportionate rental ownership. Under that ownership route, only the rental portion used in the business may be grouped. Common ownership alone is not sufficient.

REPS has an additional restriction: For purposes of section 1.469-9, a qualifying taxpayer may not group rental real estate with another activity, such as a construction, development or brokerage business. Section 1.469-9(e)(3) permits qualifying management work to count only to the extent it manages the taxpayer's own rental real estate interests, even if performed through a separate management entity. Managing other people's properties does not add hours to the owner's rental group.

Do not treat a profitable self-rental or an operating-company grouping as an automatic way to use a rental loss. Income recharacterization rules, activity classification and the applicable grouping restriction need separate analysis.

Nonpassive Does Not Mean Fully Deductible

  • Substantiation and personal use: Verify that expenses and depreciation are allowable and consider vacation-home or other personal-use limits.
  • Basis and at-risk limits: Apply any partnership or S corporation basis restrictions and the section 465 at-risk rules before passive loss limits.
  • Passive losses and carryovers: Review current losses and prior suspended losses separately. Becoming nonpassive does not automatically release prior passive losses against wages or other nonpassive income.
  • Further owner-level limits: The section 461(l) excess business loss limit applies after passive loss rules where applicable. State conformity, net operating losses and other return items can change the timing and value of an allowed deduction.

For a former passive activity, prior suspended losses may offset current net income from that same activity; remaining losses generally continue under the passive rules and may be usable against other passive income or under an applicable exception. For example, assuming $40,000 of prior suspended losses and $10,000 of current net income in the elected group, with no other available use, $10,000 can offset that income and $30,000 remains suspended. The election itself does not make all $40,000 currently deductible. See section 1.469-9(e)(2)-(4) and Publication 925.

Hypothetical: Six Rentals, 520 Hours and a $180,000 Loss

Conditional arithmetic illustration, not an actual reported outcome: Assume 900 qualifying nonemployee brokerage hours in an activity in which the owner materially participates, 100 unrelated-business hours, and rental hours of 100 + 90 + 90 + 80 + 80 + 80 = 520. Total services are 1,520; the brokerage alone exceeds 750 hours and half of total services (900 > 760). No brokerage hour is used to satisfy rental material participation.

Assume substantial paid-manager work at every property, no qualifying participation history and ordinary long-term rentals. No property exceeds 100 hours, the owner does not perform substantially all work, and no other material participation test applies on these facts. With a valid all-interests election, the 520 combined rental hours exceed 500, making the current-year rental loss nonpassive. Without the election, a different result is possible if the facts support another test, passive income or an exception; merely falling below 500 at each property is not conclusive.

If the entire $180,000 current-year loss is otherwise allowable after all relevant limits, and every deductible dollar actually reduces tax at an assumed combined federal and state marginal rate of 40%, the simplified reduction is $180,000 × 40% = $72,000. This is not a guaranteed saving attributable solely to the election. A full return comparison must consider carryovers, rate brackets, state rules, other deduction interactions and whether losses were already usable without the election.

A cost segregation study may change the timing of supported depreciation deductions. It does not establish REPS, supply participation hours or override basis, at-risk, passive loss, personal-use or excess-business-loss limits. Additional depreciation can remain deferred even when an election is valid.

Filing, Consistency and Selling One Property

REPS election statement: Section 1.469-9(g)(3) requires a statement with the original income tax return declaring that the taxpayer qualifies for that year and elects under section 469(c)(7)(A). Retain the filed statement and filing evidence. A general economic-unit explanation is not a substitute. If the election was missed, review eligibility for Revenue Procedure 2011-34 late-election relief; do not assume an ordinary amended return automatically cures it.

The REPS election binds the year made and future qualifying years. It has no effect in a nonqualifying year but resumes in later qualifying years. Revocation requires a material change in facts and circumstances and the required statement; a less favorable result or a break in REPS status alone does not qualify.

General grouping disclosure: Section 1.469-4 uses separate consistency and disclosure rules. Review Revenue Procedure 2010-13 for statements identifying new groups, additions or regroupings, including activity names, addresses and applicable EINs. A grouping ordinarily continues unless it was clearly inappropriate or a material change makes it clearly inappropriate. Do not casually change groups each year to improve a deduction.

Disposition tradeoff: An elected rental group is one activity for the passive loss and disposition rules. Selling one building generally is not a disposition of the entire activity, so it does not automatically release all suspended losses. Compare a taxable sale of the entire interest to an unrelated party with a partial sale, gift, related-party sale or exchange. Publication 925 describes a narrow substantially-all partial-disposition rule requiring reasonably certain allocation of current and prior income, deductions and credits; have the preparer assess it rather than assuming every single-property sale qualifies.

Election-Review Checklist for Your Advisor

  1. Inventory activities and ownership: List properties, rental terms, personal use, entities, ownership percentages and limited partnership or publicly traded partnership interests.
  2. Find prior filings: Collect original returns, election statements, grouping disclosures, Schedule E, K-1 activity statements and suspended-loss schedules. Confirm which election actually exists.
  3. Test the person and the activity: Document REPS hours and total working time for each spouse separately, then identify the applicable material participation test for each rental or valid elected group.
  4. Support the hours: Keep dates, specific services, property allocation, calendars, emails, invoices and manager records. For Test 3, substantiate the comparison with each other individual's participation. Publication 925 permits reasonable methods; a daily time log is useful but is not the only permitted evidence.
  5. Model both choices: Compare eligible deductions with and without the election, current and suspended losses, financing and basis, expected sales and years when REPS may lapse.
  6. Agree on implementation: Ask who prepares and files the statement, checks late relief if needed, maintains the annual hours and carryover schedules, and coordinates with the return preparer. Obtain written scope and fees before authorizing work.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the requirements for real estate professional status?

In the same tax year, an individual must perform more than 750 hours in real property trades or businesses in which the individual materially participates, and those services must exceed half of all personal services in all trades or businesses. One spouse must independently satisfy both thresholds on a joint return. Employee services have an ownership exception. REPS qualification does not replace rental material participation.

What are the seven material participation tests?

The alternatives are more than 500 hours; substantially all participation; more than 100 hours and at least as much as any other individual (Test 3); more than 500 combined hours in eligible significant participation activities (Test 4); qualifying participation in five of ten prior years; qualifying personal service activity participation in three prior years; and regular, continuous and substantial participation under facts and circumstances (Test 7). Test 4 requires more than 100 hours in each eligible trade or business activity and no other test met. Rental, limited-partner and management restrictions apply.

What documentation does the IRS accept for hours?

Publication 925 permits reasonable methods that establish services and approximate hours, including calendars and supported narratives. Keep dated tasks, property allocations and corroborating records. Document other individuals' work when the selected test requires a comparison. Spousal participation can support material participation, but spouses cannot combine personal-service hours to meet the REPS thresholds.

Do I need an aggregation election?

Not automatically. A qualifying real estate professional generally tests each rental real estate interest separately unless a valid section 1.469-9(g) election treats all interests as one rental activity. Check whether another participation test already works, prior elections, ownership restrictions, suspended losses and planned sales. General economic-unit grouping under section 1.469-4 is a different rule.

Does the election release all prior suspended losses?

No. Prior passive losses remain subject to the former-passive-activity rules. They can offset current net income from the same activity; remaining amounts generally retain passive treatment and may be usable against other passive income or under an applicable exception. An election alone does not free them against wages. Current losses also require review of basis, at-risk, personal-use and excess-business-loss limits.

What happens when I sell one property in the elected group?

The group is one activity for passive loss and disposition purposes. Selling one property generally does not dispose of the entire activity or automatically release all suspended losses. Review the narrow substantially-all partial-disposition rule, allocation records and transaction type before assuming a release. A less favorable outcome alone does not permit revoking the REPS election.

Identify which grouping rule is under discussion

An election by a qualifying real estate professional to treat rental real estate interests as one activity is not interchangeable with general grouping under the passive-activity regulations. Grouping rentals with a trade or business has additional restrictions. Decisions may affect participation, disclosures and future dispositions, so retain the original statement and rationale.

Illustrative decision

An owner asks to group a rental with a profitable operating company to use losses. Common ownership alone does not establish that the proposed grouping is permitted or produces a nonpassive loss. Have the advisor identify the exact authority, conditions and ongoing consequences before filing.

Records and decisions to prepare

  • Identify the relevant grouping authority
  • Map ownership and economic relationships
  • Locate prior elections and disclosures
  • Model the effect of selling one component
  • Document consistency requirements for future returns

Can I group a rental property with my operating business for tax purposes?

Under the general section 1.469-4 rules, only if the activities form an appropriate economic unit and meet a rental/business grouping exception. For purposes of the REPS rules in section 1.469-9, a qualifying taxpayer may not group rental real estate with an operating business. Qualifying work managing the taxpayer's own rentals may count toward rental participation; unrelated business hours do not. Review classification, ownership, prior elections and loss limits before filing.

For the underlying rules, see the official tax guidance. The relevant tax year, entity documents, actual transactions, and state filings determine the result.
