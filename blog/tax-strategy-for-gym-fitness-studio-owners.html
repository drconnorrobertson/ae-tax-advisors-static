Tax Planning for Gym Owners Before a Buildout or Expansion

A profitable gym can need tax advice before it needs another tax return: when an equipment order is about to be signed, a landlord offers a buildout allowance, or a second location changes staffing and retirement-plan obligations. Those decisions affect different parts of the return and need to be reviewed together.

This guide is for owners of established gyms, fitness studios and multi-location operators who own their premises or pay for improvements to leased space. Use it to prepare for a planning engagement before committing to a purchase, lease or opening date.

Start with the decision and the deadline

Give the adviser a short description of the project, the entity signing each contract, the expected opening date and the decisions that are still reversible. Include the operating business, any separately owned property company and other businesses under common ownership.

Ask for a comparison of cash needs and taxable income with and without the project. A deduction does not pay the equipment loan or fund next month's payroll. The useful question is whether the proposed treatment fits the whole business, including existing profits, financing payments and reserves.

Reconcile prepaid memberships before projecting income

An annual membership paid upfront can create a timing difference between cash collected, classes delivered and income reported for tax. Cash-method businesses generally include income when received. Eligible accrual-method businesses may use a permitted advance-payment deferral method, but that does not allow an unrestricted spread over the membership's full term. The remaining deferred amount generally enters income in the following tax year. A change in method may require Form 3115. See IRS Publication 538, Accounting Periods and Methods.

Bring the membership agreement, service dates, refund and cancellation terms, and a reconciliation from gross collections through refunds and processing fees to bank deposits. Identify enrollment fees, recurring dues and prepaid personal-training packages separately. A label such as "deposit" or a deferred-revenue entry in the booking software does not settle the tax treatment.

Use the fitness studio membership revenue workbook to organize the terms and open questions. Ask the tax preparer to confirm the current method and any required change before treating advance collections as available expansion cash.

Separate equipment financing from the deduction decision

Financing a purchase does not ordinarily limit an asset's tax basis to the down payment. Cost basis can include debt obligations and costs such as freight and installation. Keep the purchase invoice and financing agreement separate from the loan-payment schedule. See IRS Publication 551, Basis of Assets.

For an agreement called a lease, determine whether it is a true lease or a purchase for federal tax purposes. Rent treatment and depreciation treatment are different; the contract's label is not decisive. Have the reviewer examine ownership provisions, purchase options and the facts when the agreement was made. The IRS equipment lease guidance explains that distinction.

  • Confirm service readiness. Equipment must be ready and available for its intended business use. Paying a deposit or taking delivery of an uninstalled machine does not establish that date by itself.
  • Compare available methods. Current federal law provides 100% bonus depreciation for eligible property acquired and placed in service after January 19, 2025. Eligibility and acquisition rules still matter. Section 179 has separate dollar, investment and business-income limits.
  • Model more than the first year. Ask how the deduction affects later depreciation, a future sale and the owner's ability to use a loss. Confirm state treatment separately.

Have the preparer document the applicable treatment rather than accepting a vendor's "write it all off" estimate. See IRS Publication 946, How To Depreciate Property.

Review the lease and construction budget together

For leased premises, identify who pays for each improvement, who is treated as its owner for tax purposes, and how landlord reimbursements or rent credits work. A tenant improvement allowance can change the income and basis analysis. Do not assume the studio can depreciate every construction invoice simply because the work is inside its premises. The IRS ruling on qualified lessee construction allowances explains why the lease terms and use of the allowance matter.

For an owned building, bring the closing statement, land allocation support, existing depreciation schedule and separately identified renovation costs. Keep movable equipment, building systems, structural work and site improvements distinguishable in the construction records.

Qualified improvement property generally concerns qualifying interior improvements made by the taxpayer to an existing nonresidential building after the building was first placed in service. Enlargements, elevators and escalators, and internal structural framework are excluded. Reinforcing a structural slab for heavy equipment does not automatically turn that work into short-life equipment. Review the actual component and applicable rules in IRS Publication 946.

If the unresolved question is primarily the property's depreciation classification, start with a cost segregation feasibility and study-scope discussion. A broader gym tax engagement should also address membership receipts, financing, staffing and the effect on the owner's return.

Review instructor arrangements before adding another location

A signed contractor agreement or Form 1099 does not decide whether a trainer is an employee. For federal employment taxes, the IRS considers behavioral control, financial control and the parties' relationship. Document the actual arrangement: who sets class methods and schedules, provides equipment, collects member payments, bears expenses and controls how the work is done. No single factor settles the question. See the IRS employee-or-contractor guidance.

Bring agreements alongside a description of day-to-day practices. Ask who will review the classification, implement any payroll changes and address any prior-period issue. Separately confirm whether employment counsel needs to review wage-law or state requirements. Include the resulting payroll costs in the expansion budget rather than treating classification only as a year-end reporting question.

Check employee eligibility before choosing an owner retirement contribution

Opening a second studio can change which employees must be considered in retirement-plan design. An owner-only 401(k) cannot simply continue excluding employees who meet eligibility requirements. Gather an employee census with age, hire date, compensation and service history, including part-time staff. See the IRS guidance on one-participant 401(k) plans.

Provide ownership details for every related business, even if each location has a different EIN. Ask the plan administrator to determine whether controlled-group or affiliated-service-group rules apply. The IRS includes this related-employer review in its retirement-plan internal-controls checklist.

If a cash balance plan is being considered, request a design that shows employee costs, administration and funding obligations as well as the owner's proposed benefit. Defined benefit plans require actuarial work and can involve minimum funding requirements; a larger potential deduction alone is not a reason to establish one. See the IRS defined benefit plan overview.

What to request in a gym tax advisory proposal

Ask the proposal to identify these deliverables and the work that is priced separately:

  1. A decision list. Identify which equipment, lease, staffing or expansion decisions will be reviewed and when each answer is needed.
  2. A tax and cash-flow comparison. State the assumptions, available records, unresolved questions and owner-level limitations used in the model.
  3. A records and implementation plan. Assign responsibility for revenue reconciliation, depreciation schedules, payroll, plan administration, any elections and return reporting.
  4. A defined engagement. Name the entities and tax years covered, written work product, support period, fees and outside specialists. Confirm how the adviser will coordinate with your existing CPA, bookkeeper and attorney.

Compare AE's business owner tax advisory scope and published fees with the work your gym actually needs. Return preparation, amendments, cost segregation and additional work are separately scoped. The signed agreement determines what is included.

This is general federal tax information, not a conclusion about your gym's filing position. State rules, your accounting method, ownership and the transaction documents can change the result.

Discuss your gym's next investment or expansion

In a complimentary discovery call, describe your locations, whether you own or lease the premises, the decision ahead and its deadline. Use the conversation to discuss whether a paid advisory engagement fits and what it should cover.

A short overview is enough for booking. Do not send tax returns, account numbers, employee records or other confidential documents in booking notes. Arrange an agreed secure channel before sharing records.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I meet a tax adviser before signing a second-location lease?

Yes, if the lease, buildout allowance, equipment financing or staffing plan creates decisions that still can be changed. Bring the proposed agreements, opening schedule and current financial statements. Ask what needs analysis before you commit.

Can a gym deduct financed equipment before paying off the loan?

Financing does not ordinarily restrict purchase basis to the down payment. The deduction still depends on ownership, eligible property, acquisition and service dates, business use and the applicable depreciation rules. An agreement treated as a true lease follows different rules.

Does a deferred-revenue balance defer tax on annual memberships?

Not by itself. Tax treatment depends on the accounting method and the payment's terms. Cash-method businesses generally report income when received. Eligible accrual-method businesses may use a permitted advance-payment method, generally with remaining deferred income included in the following tax year.

Can each gym location have a separate owner-only retirement plan?

Do not assume separate entities or EINs permit employee exclusions. Have the plan administrator review common ownership, related-employer rules and employee eligibility across the businesses before selecting a plan or contribution.
