How to Use Form 3115 for Cost Segregation Catch-Up

What Is Form 3115 and Why Does It Matter for Real Estate Investors?

Form 3115, Application for Change in Accounting Method, is one of the most powerful and underused tools available to real estate investors. Filed under IRC Section 446, it allows taxpayers to correct their depreciation method on rental and commercial properties without amending prior-year returns. For investors who placed property in service years ago but never performed a cost segregation study, an eligible Form 3115 method change may recover missed depreciation through a Section 481(a) adjustment. Eligibility and deduction limits determine the available benefit.

The IRS generally requires consent to change an established depreciation method. For an eligible depreciation method change, Form 3115 is the filing mechanism. Errors, first-year corrections and late or revoked elections may require amendment, an administrative adjustment request or separate election relief instead; confirm the applicable procedure before filing.

The IRC 481(a) Adjustment: Claiming All Missed Depreciation at Once

The real power of Form 3115 lies in the IRC Section 481(a) adjustment. This provision requires taxpayers to compute a "cumulative catch-up" amount representing the difference between the depreciation they actually claimed and the depreciation they should have claimed under the new method. When a cost segregation study reclassifies building components into shorter recovery periods, the 481(a) adjustment is almost always a negative number, meaning it represents additional deductions the taxpayer missed.

A negative Section 481(a) adjustment is generally taken into account in the year of change rather than by amending each affected return. A positive adjustment is generally spread over four tax years. Applicable loss and deduction limits can defer the tax benefit even when the adjustment is reported in the current year. The example below assumes the taxpayer qualifies for the method change and can currently use the deduction.

Dollar Example: The Catch-Up in Action

Consider an investor who purchased a $1.2 million residential rental property in 2019. For seven years, the investor depreciated the entire building component (roughly $960,000 after land allocation) on a straight-line basis over 27.5 years, claiming approximately $34,909 per year in depreciation.

In 2026, the investor commissions a cost segregation study. The study reclassifies $336,000 of building components into 5-year, 7-year, and 15-year property. Under the corrected method, the investor should have claimed significantly higher depreciation in earlier years due to the accelerated recovery periods. The 481(a) adjustment calculation reveals $187,000 in cumulative missed depreciation deductions. The investor claims the entire $187,000 as a current-year deduction on the 2026 return, in addition to the regular depreciation for that year. At a 37% federal rate, that translates to approximately $69,190 in immediate tax savings from a single filing.

Automatic vs. Non-Automatic Changes: Which Applies to Cost Segregation?

The IRS classifies accounting method changes into two categories: automatic and non-automatic. Automatic changes follow the procedures in Rev. Proc. 2015-13, as modified, and the applicable automatic-change list. Non-automatic changes require advance IRS consent and involve a user fee.

A cost segregation depreciation correction may qualify for automatic consent if it meets the applicable scope and eligibility conditions. Certain errors and late or revoked elections require different procedures; filing Form 3115 alone does not cure them. For an eligible automatic change, attach Form 3115 to the timely filed return, including extensions, and send the signed duplicate using the current IRS instructions. No user fee is required for automatic changes.

Designated Change Numbers (DCNs) for Depreciation

Each automatic change on Form 3115 requires a Designated Change Number (DCN). Rev. Proc. 2025-23 lists DCN 7 for an eligible change from an impermissible to a permissible depreciation method under section 6.01. Check subsequent modifications and the list effective for the filing date, because other depreciation changes can use different DCNs and eligibility conditions. Your tax advisor should confirm the correct DCN based on the specific nature of the reclassification and the current revenue procedure in effect at the time of filing.

Step-by-Step Process for Filing Form 3115

Step 1: Commission a Cost Segregation Study

Engage a qualified engineering-based cost segregation firm to analyze your property. The study should identify and reclassify building components into the appropriate MACRS recovery periods (5-year, 7-year, 15-year, and remaining structural components). The study must be defensible under the IRS Cost Segregation Audit Techniques Guide.

Step 2: Calculate the IRC 481(a) Adjustment

Your tax advisor computes the difference between total depreciation actually claimed from the placed-in-service date through the end of the year preceding the year of change, and the total depreciation that should have been claimed under the new method for the same period. The difference is your 481(a) adjustment.

Step 3: Complete Form 3115

If eligible for automatic consent, complete Form 3115 under Rev. Proc. 2015-13, as modified, and the applicable automatic-change procedure. Include the correct DCN, the Section 481(a) calculation, required schedules and required attachments. Retain the cost segregation study and supporting records; follow any procedure-specific filing requirements.

Step 4: File with Your Return and Send the National Office Copy

For an eligible automatic change, attach the original Form 3115 to the timely filed federal income tax return for the year of change, including extensions, and file a signed duplicate separately. Use the destination and delivery method in the current Form 3115 instructions and any applicable procedure-specific rules.

Timing Requirements

For an automatic change, the return attachment follows the applicable taxpayer's federal return deadline, including extensions; individual and entity return deadlines can differ. Unless the applicable procedure provides otherwise, file the signed duplicate no earlier than the first day of the year of change and no later than the date the original is filed with the return. Non-automatic requests generally must be filed during the requested year of change.

For an eligible depreciation method change, Form 3115 and a Section 481(a) adjustment can account for prior depreciation from both open and closed tax years without amending each affected return. Eligibility, filing deadlines and deduction limits still apply.

Common Mistakes Investors Make

Choosing the wrong correction procedure. An eligible change to an established depreciation method generally uses Form 3115 and a Section 481(a) adjustment. Certain errors, first-year corrections and election issues require a different procedure. An amended-return refund claim has separate limitation rules, and Form 3115 does not guarantee audit protection or an immediately usable deduction.

Filing under the wrong change category. Using non-automatic procedures when automatic procedures apply wastes time and money (the user fee alone can exceed $10,000). Cost segregation reclassifications qualify as automatic changes for most taxpayers.

Missing the duplicate filing requirement. Failing to send the copy to the IRS National Office can invalidate the change. Both filings are mandatory.

Improper 481(a) calculations. For a change under section 6.01 of Rev. Proc. 2025-23, compare depreciation taken into account under the present method with depreciation allowable under the proposed method for open and closed years before the year of change. Reconcile capitalization and the related basis adjustment under Section 1016(a)(2); do not treat allowable depreciation as irrelevant.

Waiting too long to review eligibility. Review the asset history, any planned disposition and the filing calendar before choosing the year of change. The adjustment amount and usable deduction depend on the facts, not simply on filing as early as possible.

Why You Do Not Need to Amend Prior Returns

For an eligible depreciation method change, the Section 481(a) adjustment prevents omitted or duplicated deductions without amending each affected year. It may include closed years, but the filing remains subject to eligibility conditions and deadlines. Any audit protection depends on the applicable procedure and facts; it is not a guarantee against examination. Error corrections and election relief must be evaluated separately.

Take Action on Missed Depreciation

If you own rental property, short-term rentals, or commercial real estate and have never performed a cost segregation study, you are likely leaving significant tax deductions unclaimed. An eligible Form 3115 method change can recover missed depreciation without amending each affected return. Confirm the applicable procedure, calculation, filing deadlines and deduction limits before projecting tax savings.
