A partner died during the year. Can we allocate the full year's profit using the old percentages?

A partner's death generally closes the partnership tax year with respect to that partner, while the partnership itself may continue.

By AE Tax Advisors Team · Updated October 28, 2026

A partner's death generally closes the partnership tax year with respect to that partner, while the partnership itself may continue. Income must be allocated under the applicable varying-interest and closing rules. The deceased partner's final return and the estate or successor's reporting cannot simply use one full-year ownership percentage.

A specific business-owner situation

A partner dies halfway through a profitable year and the estate holds the interest until a later buyout. The business has separate predeath and postdeath reporting periods to address.

Illustrative example, not a client result.

What to establish before filing

  1. Identify death and transfer dates.
  2. Apply the permitted allocation method and special-item rules.
  3. Prepare the required partner and successor statements.

Which fact changes the answer?

An estate retaining the interest after death differs from an immediate transfer or redemption. The actual successor and dates determine later allocations.

A mistake to avoid

Do not allocate postdeath business income to the deceased person's final return solely because the K-1 template was unchanged.

Records that support the decision

  • Operating and buyout agreements.
  • Dated income records.
  • Estate taxpayer identification.

The decision to document

Coordinate the partnership and estate preparers before issuing final and successor K-1s.
