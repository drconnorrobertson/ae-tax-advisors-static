Which Retirement Plan Can Support the Largest Deduction?

By AE Tax Advisors · Published 2026-09-13

Updated November 8, 2026.

A defined benefit or cash balance plan can support substantial funding for some owners, but there is no universal plan that guarantees the largest usable deduction. Contribution capacity, deductibility, employee obligations and future funding are different questions.

Compare the 2026 limits correctly

  • IRA: The regular contribution limit is $7,500, with a $1,100 age-50 catch-up. Compensation and income rules affect eligibility and deductibility.
  • SEP: Employer contributions are subject to the applicable compensation percentage and the $72,000 ceiling. Self-employed owners use a special computation.
  • 401(k): Regular employee deferrals are limited to $24,500. Annual additions generally are limited to the lesser of $72,000 or 100% of applicable compensation, excluding eligible catch-up.
  • Defined benefit: The $290,000 annual benefit limit is not a yearly contribution allowance. Funding is actuarially determined.

SIMPLE plans have their own indexed limits and employer requirements. They can be useful where administration and staff benefits matter more than maximum owner funding. Compare the actual design rather than ranking plans only by a headline number.

A contribution is not always a current deduction

Traditional employee deferrals, employer contributions, Roth contributions and after-tax amounts have different tax treatment. An allowable deposit may not reduce current taxable income. The amount the employer may deduct can also differ from the amount a plan may receive.

Combined plans need a combined calculation

A business may sponsor both defined contribution and defined benefit plans. Separate Section 415 limits do not eliminate the combined employer-deduction analysis under Section 404. PBGC status, plan terms, compensation and staff coverage can change the result.

For a hypothetical $300,000 proposal, ask which amounts are employee deferrals, employer defined-contribution funding and actuarial defined-benefit funding. Then ask which are deductible this year, what goes to employees, and what remains required in a lower-profit year. A marginal tax rate multiplied by an unverified deposit is not a reliable savings estimate.

Evaluate the employee census and the next several years

Provide ages, service, compensation, ownership and existing benefits for everyone who may need coverage. Review related employers and eligible part-time workers. Ask for annual fees, required funding ranges, testing, filings and termination obligations.

The best fit may be a smaller flexible plan when income is volatile or cash needs are high. A large current deduction is only one part of the decision. Use our defined benefit versus 401(k) comparison and large-contribution review, then bring a written proposal for tax review.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a cash balance plan guarantee a $300,000 deduction?

No. Actuarial funding, compensation, employee coverage, plan terms and deduction rules determine the result.

Are Roth contributions tax-deductible now?

No. Roth contributions generally do not create a current income-tax deduction.

Can I simply add the maximum figures from two plans?

No. Apply each plan’s limits and the combined employer-deduction rules.
