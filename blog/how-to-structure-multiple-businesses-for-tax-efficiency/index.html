Multiple-Business Tax Structure: Compare the Whole Owner Return

By AE Tax Advisors · Published 2026-08-21

Updated November 8, 2026.

Start a multiple-business structure review with the owners, activities and decisions each entity must support. Creating another LLC does not itself produce a deduction or lower the household's total tax. Compare the proposed structure with the current one using all affected returns.

Map activities and ownership first

List services, inventory, property, employees, debt, contracts and owners for each business. Identify why separation is being considered: a different partner, risk profile, lender requirement, sale plan or tax classification. Obtain legal advice on liability and contracts alongside the tax review.

Keep federal tax classification distinct from state-law entity form. A domestic single-member LLC is generally disregarded unless an election applies; a domestic multi-member LLC generally defaults to partnership treatment. Corporate and S elections have separate eligibility and timing rules.

Compare S corporation treatment using defensible wages

An eligible service business may benefit from S corporation treatment, but reasonable compensation for the owner's services remains required. There is no universal salary percentage or guaranteed payroll-tax saving.

Model payroll wage bases, other earnings, employer taxes, state charges, QBI, retirement contributions and administrative costs. A projected reduction in self-employment tax alone does not establish the net benefit.

Keep real estate ownership and exit consequences visible

Partnership or disregarded treatment can provide useful flexibility for real estate, but ownership, allocations, debt and transfer plans determine the result. Distributing appreciated property out of a corporation can trigger gain. Review an intended sale or transfer before putting property into the entity.

A C corporation's 21% federal rate is one part of the comparison. State taxes, distributions, exit taxes, accumulated earnings rules and the use of retained cash matter. The corporate rate is not the owner's total effective rate.

Support every related-party payment

Management services, rent and equipment leases require real activity, written terms, reasonable pricing and records. Moving money between commonly owned accounts does not by itself create an allowable expense.

Include the receiving entity's income and costs in the same model. Related-party timing, Section 482 allocations and self-rental rules can change the apparent result. A payment that reduces one return may create equal or greater tax elsewhere.

Test rules that aggregate businesses

Common ownership can matter for retirement-plan coverage, gross-receipts tests and other controlled-group or affiliated-service-group rules. Separate EINs do not necessarily create separate limits.

For QBI, evaluate each qualified trade or business and any permitted aggregation. A related business serving a specified service trade or business may itself be subject to the related-SSTB rules. Separating a medical or consulting practice's rent or management fees does not automatically avoid those restrictions.

Compare implementation and ongoing cost

  • Current and proposed entity and owner returns, including state taxes.
  • Payroll, benefits, retirement-plan testing and required filings.
  • Legal transfers, lender consent, licenses and insurance.
  • Bookkeeping, intercompany reconciliation and annual compliance costs.
  • Future distributions, partner changes, sales and unwinding costs.

Document a business reason and a responsible person for each implementation step. Request a coordinated entity review with the ownership map, returns and proposed transaction before forming another company or transferring assets.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does another LLC automatically lower tax?

No. Classification, actual activity, payments and the combined owner return determine the result.

Can separate entities create separate retirement-plan limits?

Not necessarily. Controlled-group and affiliated-service-group rules may require aggregation.

Can management fees move profit to any entity I choose?

Payments must reflect actual services and supported terms, and related-party rules and the recipient income must be included.
