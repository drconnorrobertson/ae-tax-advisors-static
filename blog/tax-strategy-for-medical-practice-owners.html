Medical Practice Owner Tax Planning: Buy-Ins, QBI & Property

A medical practice owner may own several different interests: operating-company equity, professional compensation, a share of the office building and outside rentals. A useful tax plan maps those interests before recommending an election, a retirement contribution or a property study. A high practice profit alone does not establish which deductions the owner can use.

For engagement scope, review planning for physicians and dentists. For a second location acquisition, use the second-practice acquisition guide. A partial buy-in has its own questions, covered below.

Buying into an existing medical practice: what exactly are you buying?

Start with the transaction form. Buying assets, purchasing another owner's partnership interest, contributing capital for a newly issued interest and buying corporate shares can produce different tax basis and reporting consequences. A payment labeled a “buy-in” is not enough to determine the result.

  • Partnership interest purchase: distinguish your basis in the interest from the partnership's basis in its assets. An interest transfer generally does not reset every asset's basis. Review whether a Section 754 election or mandatory basis-adjustment rule applies, and who will maintain any transferee-specific Section 743(b) adjustment.
  • Capital contribution: identify what the practice receives, the ownership issued, existing liabilities and the allocation provisions. Do not treat a contribution as identical to buying an existing partner's interest.
  • Corporate shares: distinguish stock basis from the corporation's asset basis. Do not assume a share purchase creates new depreciation on the practice's equipment or building.
  • Separate property interest: determine whether the office building is included, purchased separately or retained by existing owners under a lease. The practice buy-in price may not buy any real estate.

The IRS partnership guide and Section 754 guidance explain the underlying distinction. Before signing, ask the tax adviser and counsel to reconcile the agreement, ownership chart, liabilities, opening basis and responsible return preparer. State professional-ownership rules need their own legal review.

Owner compensation and entity structure

For an S corporation, reasonable compensation depends on the shareholder's services and facts. A preset salary percentage or a desired tax saving is not a substitute for documenting clinical and management work. For a partnership, analyze guaranteed payments and distributive shares under the partnership rules rather than importing an S-corporation wage model.

Compare payroll costs, benefits, eligibility, state treatment, compliance and the expected exit. The IRS compensation guidance is a starting point. A management services organization also requires a legitimate operational and legal analysis; creating an MSO does not automatically convert medical income into non-service income for tax purposes.

QBI in 2026: health is a specified service business

Health services fall within the specified service trade or business rules. For 2026 married filing jointly returns, the Section 199A threshold is $403,500 and the phase-in range ends at $553,500; these figures use taxable income before the QBI deduction, not gross practice revenue. Other filing statuses have different thresholds. Consult Revenue Procedure 2025-32, Section 4.26 for the 2026 table.

A separate entity is not a guaranteed way around SSTB treatment. Under Treasury Regulation 1.199A-5, property or services supplied to an SSTB with at least 50% common ownership can themselves be treated as SSTB activity for the related parties. This matters when the medical practice rents from an owner-controlled building entity or uses a commonly owned management company.

Retirement contributions or other legitimate deductions may affect taxable income, but their interaction with QBI and owner-level limits needs a full-return calculation. Do not assume that shifting revenue, reducing salary or creating an LLC preserves a 20% deduction.

Retirement funding with employees and multiple practices

Request a plan design based on the employee census, ownership across related businesses, compensation and sustainable funding. A cash balance arrangement requires actuarial and employee-coverage review. A large projected contribution is not a guaranteed deduction for every physician, and the cost includes more than the owner's contribution.

Review employee obligations, administrator fees, funding in lower-income years and an anticipated buy-in or sale. See related-business retirement plan rules and IRS Publication 560. Tax-deferred retirement funding and permanently eliminating tax are different outcomes.

Practice building, tenant improvements and equipment

Document who owns each asset and who paid for improvements. A tenant cannot depreciate the landlord's entire building, and separately listed equipment must not be duplicated in a property study. Classification of specialized electrical, plumbing and other build-out components depends on function and supporting facts, not the medical label alone.

Bonus depreciation and Section 179 require eligibility, applicable acquisition and placed-in-service dates, elections and other conditions. For a separately owned building leased to the practice, self-rental rules can make net income nonpassive while losses remain passive. Review basis, at-risk, passive-loss and excess-business-loss limits before estimating usable benefit. Start with study scope and feasibility, Publication 946 and Publication 925.

Define the paid review before implementing

Ask for an ownership map, basis and allocation questions, modeled alternatives, assumptions, required records and an implementation checklist. Assign elections, payroll, legal documents, plan administration, studies and return filing to the responsible professionals. If you retain your CPA, confirm the handoff before purchasing a separate planning engagement.

For the initial conversation, describe your practice role, entity type, property interests, employee count and transaction or filing deadline. Arrange secure document delivery before sending returns or financial records. Published fees are a starting point; the signed scope determines whether returns, studies and additional work are included.

Frequently asked questions

Does buying into a practice reset the tax basis of its equipment?

Not automatically. Asset acquisitions, partnership interest purchases, capital contributions and corporate share purchases have different consequences. Review inside and outside basis and any applicable elections.

Does a separate management or real estate company avoid medical SSTB rules?

Not automatically. Related-party property and service rules can extend SSTB treatment to commonly owned activities. Analyze actual ownership and services before projecting QBI.

Will depreciation on my clinic building offset my practice income?

Not necessarily. Ownership, participation, self-rental treatment, basis, at-risk and other loss limitations determine current use. A study deduction is not a guaranteed refund.
