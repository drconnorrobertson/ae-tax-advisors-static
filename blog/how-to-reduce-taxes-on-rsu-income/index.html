RSU Tax Planning: Withholding, Share Basis and Sale Decisions

By AE Tax Advisors · Published 2026-08-18

Updated November 8, 2026.

RSU planning starts with the award terms and the date shares or cash are delivered. Compensation, payroll withholding and a later stock sale are related but different events. Check the employer's records before assuming the broker's sale proceeds represent all taxable income.

Identify the compensation event

In a typical share-settled RSU arrangement, the value delivered is wage income when the shares are transferred, often at vesting. Vesting and settlement are not identical in every plan. Deferred-settlement awards and special private-company provisions require separate analysis.

RSUs are not incentive stock options. An ordinary RSU promise generally is not transferred property on which the employee can simply make a Section 83(b) election at grant. Review the actual award before applying restricted-stock or option rules.

Compare withholding with projected tax

Shares withheld or sold to cover payroll do not necessarily cover the final tax liability. Supplemental wage withholding can differ from the employee's marginal income-tax rate. Additional wages, bonuses, investment income and state taxes affect the full-year projection.

Coordinate any additional payroll withholding or estimated payments using the applicable payment rules. The employer's withholding method is not a prediction of the final tax rate.

Prevent double taxation in the sale reporting

The amount already included as compensation generally becomes part of the acquired shares' basis. A later sale produces a separate capital gain or loss measured from the correct adjusted basis, not automatically from zero.

Reconcile the W-2, vesting or settlement statement, sell-to-cover transactions and Form 1099-B. Broker-reported basis can require an adjustment on the tax return. A same-day sale can still have a small gain or loss from price movement or transaction costs.

Separate wage income from capital gains

Holding shares after settlement exposes the owner to market risk; it does not convert previously recognized wages into long-term capital gain. The holding period applies to subsequent appreciation or loss. Capital losses generally offset capital gains first, with a limited ordinary-income deduction and carryforward rules.

Tax-loss harvesting also requires wash-sale review, including acquisitions through compensation plans and other accounts. Do not evaluate only the brokerage account where the loss was realized.

Review donations and retirement funding on their own merits

A charitable gift of appreciated shares can have different treatment depending on holding period, recipient, valuation and deduction limitations. Donating shares does not erase wage income already recognized on delivery. Retirement deferrals likewise have plan and annual limits; they are not an unlimited offset to an RSU event.

Track work locations and state rules

A move between states may leave compensation sourced partly to work performed before the move. The relevant service period, residence, allocation method, withholding and credits depend on the states and award. Keep grant, vesting and settlement dates with a work-location record.

Request an equity-compensation review with the award agreement, employer statements, W-2s, brokerage records and state work history. Evaluate diversification and cash needs as well as taxes before deciding to retain or sell shares.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does holding settled shares turn the RSU wage income into capital gain?

No. The wage event and later investment gain or loss are separate.

Can I rely on the basis shown on Form 1099-B?

Reconcile it with compensation already reported and the employer records; an adjustment may be necessary.

Does sell-to-cover guarantee enough tax was paid?

No. Withholding may differ from the final full-year liability.
