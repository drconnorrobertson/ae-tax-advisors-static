How Do I Deduct a Home Office Through My S-Corp?

Published on August 16, 2026 | Augusta Rule and Business Deductions | AE Tax Advisors

If you operate your business through an S-Corp and work from a home office, the method for claiming the deduction differs significantly from what sole proprietors use. As an S-Corp shareholder-employee, you cannot simply file Form 8829 and deduct home office expenses on your personal return. Instead, you must route the deduction through your S-Corp using an accountable plan under IRC Section 62(c). This approach actually provides better tax results in many cases because it eliminates both income tax and payroll tax on the reimbursed amounts.

Why the Standard Home Office Deduction Does Not Apply

An S-corporation shareholder-employee should not claim the corporation’s home-office expenses as though operating a sole proprietorship on Schedule C. Ordinary employee home-office costs are not deductible as miscellaneous itemized deductions under current federal rules. Review a qualifying reimbursement arrangement instead of relying on an expired 2025 cutoff.

A qualifying corporate reimbursement may provide a different treatment from an unreimbursed employee expense. The underlying business use, actual expense and reimbursement process must all be supported; adopting a policy does not automatically make every home cost deductible.

Setting Up the Accountable Plan for Home Office

The arrangement must require business connection, timely substantiation and return of excess reimbursements. A written policy and corporate approval are useful evidence, but no particular document alone establishes compliance. Identify the home-office costs covered and follow the process in practice.

Review the applicable regular-and-exclusive-use and business-purpose requirements. For employee use, the employer-convenience requirement also matters; choosing to work at home is not automatically sufficient. Keep evidence of how the office is used and why the corporate work requires it.

Calculating the Reimbursement Amount

Do not automatically use the individual $5-per-square-foot simplified deduction as a corporate reimbursement rate. Review substantiated actual expenses and a reasonable business-use allocation for the shareholder-employee arrangement. The simplified home-office option does not turn an employee expense into an allowable personal deduction.

For an actual-expense calculation, document the qualifying office area and a reasonable allocation of eligible costs. Separate direct office costs from shared household costs and exclude personal amounts. Mortgage principal is not an expense. Review interest, property tax, utilities, repairs and any depreciation separately to avoid duplicate deductions or an unsupported reimbursement. An example based only on square footage is insufficient unless the expense pool itself is eligible.

The Reimbursement Process

Each month or quarter, you submit an expense report to the S-Corp documenting the home office percentage and the actual expenses incurred. Attach copies of mortgage statements, utility bills, insurance declarations, and repair invoices. The S-Corp reviews the submission, approves it, and issues a reimbursement payment from the business bank account. The reimbursement is recorded as a rent or occupancy expense on the S-Corp's books and is deductible under IRC Section 162.

A qualifying reimbursement is generally excluded from employee wages, but the corporation’s expense and the owner’s overall income can still affect other return calculations. Reconcile the corporate deduction, shareholder reporting and any personal deductions so the same expense is not claimed twice.

Documentation Best Practices

Maintain a floor plan or diagram showing your office space and its measurements relative to the total home. Keep a log of business activities conducted in the office. Retain all bills, invoices, and statements that support the expense calculations. If the IRS audits the S-Corp and questions the home office reimbursement, you will need to demonstrate both that the space qualifies under Section 280A(c) and that the amounts are accurately calculated.

The home office deduction through an S-Corp accountable plan is one of the most straightforward ways to recover tax benefits that the TCJA eliminated for employees. AE Tax Advisors helps S-Corp owners structure these arrangements with proper documentation and compliance from day one.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can an S-corp owner file Form 8829 for corporate work?

A shareholder-employee should not use a sole-proprietor Schedule C home-office deduction for the corporation’s activity. Evaluate the corporate reimbursement route.

Can the corporation reimburse $5 per square foot automatically?

Do not assume the individual simplified deduction creates a corporate reimbursement safe harbor. Review actual substantiated costs and the applicable requirements.

What records support the reimbursement?

Keep the qualifying-use explanation, measured floor plan, eligible bills, allocation calculation, expense report, approval and payment records.

Does a written policy make every payment tax-free?

No. Business connection, timely substantiation and return of excess must be satisfied in practice, and the underlying expense must qualify.
