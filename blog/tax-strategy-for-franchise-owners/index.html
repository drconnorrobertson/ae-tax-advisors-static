Franchise Owner Tax Strategy: Opening Costs, Build-Outs and Multiple Units

Published on August 11, 2026 | Tax Strategy | AE Tax Advisors | Updated November 6, 2026

Opening another franchise unit creates several tax schedules, not one opening-year write-off. Franchise rights, training before opening, equipment, tenant improvements and a building purchase can each follow different rules. Reconcile the franchise agreement and project ledger before estimating deductions.

Separate franchise rights from operating expenses

An acquired franchise right that is a Section 197 intangible generally is amortized over 180 months, beginning with the later of the acquisition month or the month the business or income-producing activity begins. An opening-year deduction may cover only part of a year. The Form 4562 instructions explain the commencement rule and separate startup-cost treatment.

Do not assign every payment to that same schedule. Initial rights, acquired goodwill, startup activities, organizational expenses and recurring royalties require classification. Ongoing royalties and advertising charges may be ordinary operating expenses, but the agreement, business start date, accounting method, prepayments and capitalization rules control timing. A description such as “marketing” on an invoice does not settle the tax treatment.

Build-out deductions depend on what was built

Qualified improvement property generally concerns improvements made by the taxpayer to an existing nonresidential building's interior after the building was first placed in service. Enlargements, elevators or escalators, and the internal structural framework are excluded. A new building is not automatically QIP, and neither is every leftover construction cost after equipment is identified.

Keep separate schedules for equipment, eligible interior improvements, building components, land improvements and land. Eligible property acquired and placed in service after January 19, 2025 can receive 100% bonus depreciation, subject to acquisition rules, exclusions and elections. Section 179 has its own eligibility and income limits. Some real-property elections require ADS depreciation that affects bonus eligibility. See IRS Publication 946.

A cost segregation study supports asset classification; it cannot promise a standard reclassification percentage or make all opening costs immediately deductible. Reconcile study results to invoices and the preparer's fixed-asset ledger before claiming them.

Multiple units: do not plan one LLC at a time

Separate entities may serve business or legal purposes, but tax rules can aggregate related operations. Review common ownership before applying gross-receipts thresholds or retirement-plan coverage. Employees across related entities can affect eligibility, nondiscrimination testing and employer contributions. An owner-only plan is not an automatic option because the owner takes payroll from a separate management company. IRS Publication 560 is the starting point for plan design and contribution rules.

S corporation compensation must reflect the owner's services across the actual operating arrangement. A percentage of distributions is not a salary safe harbor. Model payroll taxes, state treatment, benefits, administrative costs and QBI together using IRS S corporation compensation guidance. Restaurant tip credits, if relevant, require payroll and statutory eligibility calculations rather than an assumed amount per employee.

When the franchise owner also owns the premises

Document the property entity, lease, rent and debt separately from the operating unit. Self-rental can make net rent nonpassive when the owner materially participates in the tenant business, but it does not automatically let rental depreciation losses offset franchise profit. Review passive losses, basis, at-risk limits and any valid grouping before treating accelerated depreciation as current tax savings. See IRS Publication 925.

QBI classification depends on the business activity, not the fact that it is a franchise. A restaurant and a health-services franchise may face different specified-service rules. Deductions, wage and property limitations, and the household taxable-income cap can change the result. See IRS qualified business income guidance.

Illustrative opening-month records exercise

Assume an owner acquires franchise rights before opening a second unit and later completes its fit-out. This example illustrates documentation, not savings or a recommended structure.

  • Use the rights agreement and operating start date to establish when amortization begins; count the eligible months rather than claiming a full annual amount automatically.
  • Split the contractor's invoice into equipment, eligible QIP, excluded structural work and other assets, supported by plans and invoices.
  • Record when each asset was ready and available for business use, even if the invoice or loan payment occurred earlier.
  • Give the plan administrator a combined ownership chart and employee census for all related units.
  • Reconcile the property entity's return and suspended losses with the operating company's forecast.

Keep a separate cash forecast. Loan principal, landlord allowances, deposits and deductions do not move through taxable income in the same way.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I deduct the full initial franchise fee when I open?

Generally, an acquired Section 197 franchise right is amortized over 180 months, starting with the later of acquisition or commencement of the business or income-producing activity. Other payments require their own classification.

Is all franchise build-out work qualified improvement property?

No. QIP has an existing-building interior-improvement definition and excludes enlargements, elevators or escalators, and internal structural framework. Equipment and other assets require separate classification.

Can each unit use a separate owner-only retirement plan?

Separate entities do not automatically separate employees for retirement-plan rules. Common ownership and related-employer requirements must be reviewed with the plan administrator.
