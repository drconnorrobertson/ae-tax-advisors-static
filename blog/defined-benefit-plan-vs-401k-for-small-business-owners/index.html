Defined Benefit Plan vs 401(k): Funding, Staff and Tax Limits

By AE Tax Advisors · Published 2026-08-03

Updated November 8, 2026.

A 401(k) limits annual contributions. A defined benefit plan promises a benefit and uses actuarial funding calculations. Neither is automatically better for a business owner; staff, compensation, cash flow and the intended funding period drive the choice.

What the 401(k) limits mean in 2026

The employee deferral limit is $24,500. Annual additions generally are limited to the lesser of $72,000 or 100% of applicable compensation, before eligible catch-up. Employer contributions and voluntary after-tax amounts share that limit. The standard catch-up is $8,000, with $11,250 instead for eligible participants attaining ages 60 through 63.

Plan design can include matching or profit-sharing obligations, so do not assume every contribution can be skipped without consequences. Owner-only treatment requires reviewing all eligible employees and related employers.

A defined benefit limit is not an annual contribution allowance

The 2026 Section 415(b) annual benefit limit is $290,000, subject to its adjustments. It is not permission to contribute $290,000 each year. An actuary calculates funding from the promised benefit, age, service, compensation, existing assets, assumptions and workforce. Investment performance can change required contributions.

A cash balance plan is a type of defined benefit plan. Its hypothetical account presentation does not make it a simple discretionary savings account. Review funding obligations, administration, participant notices and termination consequences.

Employee costs can change the decision

Both designs require employee coverage and nondiscrimination analysis where applicable. Obtain a census-based proposal showing owner and staff contributions, annual fees, funding ranges and who completes required filings. A large owner contribution estimate without staff cost is incomplete.

Combining plans needs a combined deduction analysis

Separate Section 415 limits do not guarantee that the employer can deduct both headline contribution amounts. Section 404(a)(7), plan design and PBGC status can affect combined deductions. Coordinate the administrator, actuary and return preparer before funding.

Compare stable and lower-profit years, not only the best current year. Model current income-tax effects, any Roth treatment, QBI interactions and expected distributions in retirement. Money contributed is committed to a retirement arrangement; a deduction is not free cash.

For the funding question, see evaluating a $300,000 contribution proposal. Review a written, census-based design before selecting a plan.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the $290,000 defined benefit limit an annual deposit limit?

No. It is an annual benefit limit under Section 415(b); contributions are determined actuarially.

Can I deduct the maximum contributions to both plans automatically?

No. Combined-plan deduction rules and the actual plan design must be evaluated.

Can every 401(k) employer contribution be skipped?

No. The plan document and any required matching or other contributions matter.
