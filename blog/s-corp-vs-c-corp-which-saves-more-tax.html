S Corp vs C Corp: Compare Owner Pay, Taxes and Exit Costs

Compare S corporation and C corporation tax treatment using the same forecast for owner wages, retained earnings, distributions, state tax and a sale.

How S corporation taxation works

An eligible business may elect S corporation treatment. Income, losses, deductions and credits generally pass through to shareholders. Owners can owe tax on allocated income even when the business retains cash. An S corporation can also owe certain entity-level taxes, and shareholders must check basis and other limits before using losses.

How C corporation taxation works

A C corporation files and pays tax as its own taxpayer. Shareholders may have a second layer of tax when distributed earnings are dividends. A useful comparison follows corporate cash, owner wages and other payments, later distributions, and the tax effect of the eventual exit.

Run the comparison with two cash plans

Use the same revenue, operating costs and owner duties for both structures. In the first scenario, assume the business retains cash for hiring and equipment. In the second, assume it distributes cash to cover the owner’s spending. For each year, track payroll costs, entity tax, owner tax, and actual cash available to the company and household.

Check the assumptions that can change the result

Confirm S election eligibility, wages for shareholder-employees, owner basis, and the limits on any qualified business income deduction. Add state filing costs, franchise and entity taxes, employee benefits, and likely sale terms. Compare an asset sale and equity sale when either is plausible.

Put the implementation costs on the same timeline

Ask the preparer and attorney which filings, consents, payroll changes and recordkeeping are required. If the business already has a corporate election or appreciated property, review the consequences of a conversion before choosing a date. Keep the cost of maintaining the current structure in the comparison.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is an S corporation always cheaper than a C corporation?

No. Compare total entity, owner and payroll tax along with the cash each structure leaves after taxes. Reinvestment, owner distributions, state rules and the expected exit can change the result.

Can S corporation shareholders owe tax on profits the business keeps?

Yes. S corporation income generally passes through to shareholders even if the company does not distribute enough cash to cover the tax. Include estimated payments and any tax distributions in the forecast.

Can an existing corporation change its federal tax treatment?

The answer depends on the current classification, eligibility and timing. Review election rules and potential tax on built-in gains, accumulated earnings, or transfers with your preparer before deciding.
