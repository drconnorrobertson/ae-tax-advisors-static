STR Material Participation: All 7 Tests and the Records You Need

By AE Tax Advisors · Published 2026-08-06

Updated November 8, 2026.

A short-term rental that meets an exception to the passive rules' definition of rental activity may be nonpassive if the owner materially participates. First classify the activity, then apply an available participation test. Neither a short average stay nor a cost segregation study alone makes a loss deductible against salary.

Start with the activity, not the hour count

An average customer-use period of seven days or less is one rental-activity exception. Other exceptions have different conditions. Calculate actual use from the records; a listing's advertised minimum stay is not the annual average. This classification is for Section 469 and does not by itself determine Schedule C versus Schedule E reporting or self-employment tax.

If the activity remains a rental activity under these rules, material participation alone generally does not remove its passive classification. Real estate professional status has separate annual requirements. See the seven-day rule before choosing a test.

The seven tests

  1. More than 500 hours. Your qualifying participation in the activity exceeds 500 hours during the year. Exactly 500 does not satisfy this test.
  2. Substantially all participation. Your participation is substantially all participation by every individual, including nonowners. There is no universal 80% safe harbor. Account for cleaners, co-hosts and managers.
  3. More than 100 hours and the individual comparison. You exceed 100 hours and participate at least as much as any other individual. Compare each person's hours, not only the management company's name or combined staff total.
  4. Significant participation activities. Participation in all eligible significant participation activities totals more than 500 hours. Each counted activity has more than 100 hours and does not satisfy another material participation test. Do not apply this shortcut to ordinary rental activities without checking eligibility.
  5. Five of the preceding ten years. You materially participated in the activity, without relying on this fifth test, in any five of the ten immediately preceding tax years.
  6. Qualifying personal service activity. You materially participated in that personal service activity for any three preceding tax years. Operating an ordinary capital-intensive rental is not converted into a personal service activity because the owner has another professional job.
  7. Facts and circumstances. Participation is regular, continuous and substantial, and exceeds 100 hours. For this test, management participation does not count when another person is paid to manage or another individual spends more management time.

Not every test is available to every interest. Limited-partner restrictions and entity facts require separate review. Passing one available test can be sufficient; there is no requirement to pass all seven.

Two examples of the 100-hour comparison

Assume an eligible activity with no special ownership restriction. An owner documents 160 hours, one cleaner 130 and a maintenance worker 100, with no other individual exceeding 160. Test 3 may be met even though the combined outside work is 230 hours. The comparison is individual by individual.

If a manager instead performs 190 hours while the owner performs 160, Test 3 is not met. That does not end the entire analysis: another available test might apply, but it requires its own facts. Do not add unsupported owner hours to make a comparison work.

Spouse participation and grouping are separate decisions

A spouse's participation generally counts when evaluating material participation, regardless of ownership or whether the spouses file jointly. Keep each person's tasks and hours identifiable. In contrast, the real estate professional thresholds cannot be met by simply combining both spouses' hours; one spouse must independently satisfy the annual REPS requirements.

For multiple activities, general grouping under Treasury Regulation 1.469-4 requires an appropriate economic unit and compliance with restrictions, consistency and disclosure rules. The REPS all-rental election under 1.469-9(g) is different. Owning several STRs does not automatically make them a valid group. See the grouping and REPS election guide.

Keep evidence of work actually performed

The IRS permits reasonable methods of proving participation, including calendars and supported summaries. Daily logs are useful but are not the sole permitted method. Record the date, property or activity, task, person and approximate duration; retain booking messages, invoices and other corroboration. Separate investor-level monitoring from operational work and avoid double-counting simultaneous tasks.

For the individual-comparison test, obtain usable information about the work done by other individuals. An owner's log alone does not establish that nobody else worked more.

Check the loss after participation

A nonpassive classification does not establish basis, amount at risk, the validity of the underlying deductions or freedom from other loss limitations. Depreciation must use the proper property classification, historical acquisition rules and elections. Coordinate the activity analysis with the tax return rather than treating an hours spreadsheet as the final answer.

Use STR tax planning to connect the records, depreciation and return treatment. Bring your stay history, hours, manager records and prior return for review.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is 100 hours enough for material participation?

Not by itself. Test 3 requires more than 100 hours and at least as much participation as any other individual. Other tests have different conditions.

Is there an 80% safe harbor for substantially all participation?

No universal 80% threshold appears in the test. Evaluate the actual participation of all individuals.

Does a property manager automatically prevent material participation?

No. The applicable test and actual individual hours matter. A paid manager creates particular restrictions for the facts-and-circumstances test.

Can spouses combine hours?

Spouse participation generally counts for material participation. REPS qualification is separate and requires one spouse to satisfy its own thresholds.

Do all STRs use the REPS grouping election?

No. General activity grouping and the REPS all-rental election are different rules. Classify the activities and review the applicable requirements.
