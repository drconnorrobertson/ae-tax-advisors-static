Home Internet Reimbursement: A Business Owner’s Guide

Practical expense decisions for business owners.

Request Your Free Tax Assessment

The short answer

A company may reimburse a supported business share of home internet under a qualifying reimbursement arrangement. Paying the full household bill or a fixed allowance without support requires further review. This guide is for business owners designing the payment process for staff or owner-employees.

By AE Tax Advisors Team · Published 2026-09-22 · Updated September 26, 2026

Define the work the connection supports

Start with the company’s operational needs: client calls, secure access to business files, property-management communications or other duties performed from home. Record which roles need the connection and who approves reimbursement. This makes the policy understandable to the person reviewing a claim rather than leaving each employee to guess what the company intended.

Keep equipment, installation, monthly service and unrelated bundles distinct. A bill that combines broadband, entertainment and a device installment should not enter the expense report as one unexplained business amount. Ask the claimant to identify the relevant service period, credits and the portion actually being submitted.

Support an allocation instead of selecting a standard percentage

There is no universal allocation in this guide. Review the work pattern, shared household use, service requirements and facts supporting the proposed business share. The method should fit the expense being allocated. A floor-area percentage used elsewhere in a home-office calculation does not automatically measure broadband use.

Document the rationale in plain language and retain the bill. Revisit it when duties, household use or the service plan materially changes. A dedicated business connection presents different facts from a family connection shared with entertainment and personal activity. Avoid treating a proposed allocation as approved until the reviewer has examined those facts.

Example: reconcile the bill, claim and payment

Suppose an owner-employee submits a $120 monthly bundle. The invoice identifies $30 of entertainment service and $90 of broadband. A documented review supports a hypothetical 50% business allocation of the broadband portion. The proposed reimbursement is $45, not $60 and not $120. The business must still review the eligibility of the arrangement and the support for that allocation.

If the company already paid $45 directly toward the same bill, an additional $45 expense-report payment could duplicate it. Track the service month and invoice reference so two payment systems do not pay the same supported cost. The numbers illustrate a workflow; neither 50% nor $45 is an IRS safe harbor.

Apply the reimbursement conditions

Publication 15 requires an accountable arrangement to have a business connection, timely substantiation and timely repayment of excess advances. Amounts meeting its conditions are not wages; unsupported or excess amounts can receive nonaccountable-plan treatment. Publication 463 provides additional reimbursement guidance. Review owner status and the actual arrangement before payroll decides the treatment.

A recurring reminder can help the company obtain missing bills, but an approval workflow is only useful if someone resolves the exceptions. Decide who follows up on missing documentation, who reviews disputed allocations and how overpayments are recovered or reported. Do not silently carry an unresolved claim into the next tax year.

Use a claim checklist that can be reconciled

  1. Identify the employee or owner-employee, company and service month.
  2. Attach the itemized bill and proof of payment when applicable.
  3. Separate excluded bundle items and explain the proposed business share.
  4. Check for a company payment or reimbursement already made for that month.
  5. Record reviewer approval, the approved amount and payment reference.
  6. Resolve any difference between an advance and the substantiated amount.

At year-end, compare approved claims with the reimbursement ledger and payroll treatment. Keep direct business charges and reimbursements separate enough to detect duplicates. If the business deducted a cost elsewhere, have the preparer reconcile the treatment before filing.

Keep the owner’s direct deduction question separate

A sole proprietor paying for a business connection needs a direct expense and allocation analysis rather than assuming an employee reimbursement to themself. Return to the business phone and internet deduction overview for that starting point. A company-provided phone has separate guidance covered in the employer phone guide.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can the company reimburse the whole home internet bill?

Do not assume the whole household bill qualifies. Review the business need, personal or bundled services, allocation support and reimbursement arrangement.

Is an internet allowance the same as a phone benefit?

No. The payment should be evaluated under the rules and facts applicable to internet reimbursement. A phone-benefit conclusion cannot simply be copied.

  • IRS Publication 463: reimbursement arrangements
