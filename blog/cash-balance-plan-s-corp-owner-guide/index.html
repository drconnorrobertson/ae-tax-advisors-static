Cash Balance Plan for S-Corp Owners: Funding and Fit

By AE Tax Advisors · Published 2026-06-16

Updated November 8, 2026.

A cash balance plan is a defined benefit pension plan with actuarial funding requirements. It can support substantial retirement funding in suitable circumstances, but an owner's age or desired tax deduction does not establish a permitted contribution.

Request an actuarial illustration from the full census

Provide ages, compensation, service and ownership for all potentially eligible employees and related businesses. Plan benefits, compensation history, existing plans and actuarial assumptions affect the result. A generic age-to-contribution table is not a funding calculation.

The 2026 Section 415(b) annual benefit limit is $290,000, subject to its rules. That is an annual benefit limit, not a universal annual contribution allowance. Have the actuary explain the permitted deduction range and required contribution separately.

Use eligible wages rather than distributions

S corporation distributions are not plan compensation. Review W-2 wages and the plan's compensation definition, along with reasonable compensation for the owner's services. Increasing salary just to target a pension deduction can have payroll and other tax consequences.

Employer contributions can reduce corporate income passing through to owners, which can affect QBI and other calculations. Model the actual return instead of multiplying a proposed contribution by one assumed combined tax rate.

Include employees and related employers

Coverage, nondiscrimination, participation and aggregation rules can require benefits for people beyond the owner. A spouse or part-time worker may also affect the design. Do not promise a fixed employee-cost percentage before testing the census.

Combining a cash balance plan with a 401(k) adds coordination requirements. The separate plan limits do not mean every advertised maximum can simply be added together.

Evaluate the funding commitment

The plan promises benefits and generally creates minimum funding obligations. Investment performance and changing demographics can alter future contributions. Discuss what happens in a weak business year, when hiring staff, or before a sale.

Amending, freezing or terminating a plan has formal requirements and does not eliminate already earned benefits. Ongoing administration, actuarial work, participant reporting and any applicable PBGC obligations must be priced into the decision.

Calendar adoption, deduction and funding separately

Plan adoption, deductible contribution timing, minimum funding and return filing are different deadlines. Some employer plans can be adopted after year-end under applicable rules, but that does not make every pension step retroactive. Obtain a written implementation calendar.

Request a feasibility review with payroll, the employee census, existing plan documents and a multi-year cash forecast. Use the retirement comparison to evaluate alternatives.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the $290,000 benefit limit mean I can contribute that amount?

No. It is an annual benefit limit. Permitted and required contributions depend on the plan and actuarial calculation.

Can I use S corporation distributions as plan compensation?

No. The plan compensation and wage rules must be applied.

Can I stop funding whenever business slows?

A defined benefit plan can have required contributions. Review formal amendment, freeze and termination rules before adoption.
