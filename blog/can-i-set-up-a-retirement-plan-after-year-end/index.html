Can I Set Up a Retirement Plan After the Tax Year Ends?

Published on July 31, 2026 | Retirement Plans | AE Tax Advisors

Reviewed November 6, 2026. The calendar below addresses the 2026 tax year, not a missed deadline for an earlier return.

Yes, an employer-funded retirement plan may still be available after December 31. The important distinction is between adopting the plan, electing a salary deferral, depositing money and claiming a deduction. Those are not interchangeable deadlines.

IRC Section 401(b)(2) permits eligible qualified plans to be adopted by the employer's tax-return due date, including extensions, with an election to treat adoption as of the prior tax year's last day. This can cover profit-sharing and defined benefit plans, including cash balance plans. It does not generally permit retroactive deferrals of wages already paid. See the current statute and the IRS timing analysis. Older summaries that impose a universal December 31 adoption deadline miss this distinction.

SEP IRA: adoption and employer funding after year-end

A SEP can generally be established and funded by the business's income-tax return deadline, including a valid extension. Confirm eligible employees, give required plan information and establish their SEP-IRAs as well as the owner's. An owner-only tax deduction cannot ignore staff entitled to an allocation.

For 2026, the SEP contribution limit per employee is the lesser of 25% of eligible compensation or $72,000. A new SEP does not permit employee salary deferrals or catch-up contributions. The self-employed owner's deduction uses a special calculation, generally a 20% rate when the plan rate is 25%, applied to net earnings after the deductible part of self-employment tax and before the owner's contribution deduction. Do not simply multiply gross profit by 25%.

For example, $200,000 of eligible S corporation W-2 compensation supports a $50,000 employer SEP contribution at 25%, subject to the other requirements. For a sole proprietor with $200,000 of net earnings after the deductible half of self-employment tax but before their own contribution, the 20% calculation yields $40,000. These are hypothetical contribution calculations, not promised tax savings. IRS SEP contribution limits, SEP FAQs and Publication 560 explain the separate rules.

Solo 401(k): employer contributions are not salary deferrals

A qualifying owner-only 401(k) can be adopted after year-end for prior-year employer contributions under the retroactive-adoption rule. An employer generally must actually pay those contributions by its return due date, including a valid extension, to deduct them for that year. The document must authorize the contribution, and eligibility, allocation and deduction limits still apply.

For ordinary employee deferrals, the cash-or-deferred arrangement and election generally must exist before compensation is paid or otherwise available. An S corporation cannot establish a plan in February and turn the owner's prior-year wages into elective deferrals. For a self-employed owner in an existing plan, the deferral election generally must be made by year-end; a timely election and the later deposit are different events.

Deposit timing is separate: for a self-employed owner in an existing owner-only plan, timely elected deferrals generally can be deposited by the applicable return deadline, including extensions. For employee payroll withholding in an ERISA-covered plan, deposit as soon as the funds can reasonably be separated from employer assets. The employer's tax-return deadline is not permission to hold employee contributions. See Department of Labor deposit guidance.

The narrow first-year sole-proprietor exception

For plan years beginning after December 29, 2022, an individual who owns the entire interest in an unincorporated business and is its only employee can establish a new 401(k) after year-end and make prior-year elective deferrals for the first plan year only. Adopt the plan, make the election and pay the deferrals by the individual's return deadline without extensions. For an ordinary calendar-year 2026 sole proprietor, that is April 15, 2027, not October 15.

This exception does not cover an S corporation, a partnership, a business with another employee or a later year of an existing plan. A spouse participating as another employee takes the business outside this only-employee exception. A plan marketed as a “Solo 401(k)” does not automatically qualify. Confirm the facts and provider's operational cutoff before relying on it. The current Section 401(b)(2), IRS Issue Snapshot and Publication 560 first-year deferral discussion address this rule.

Cash balance plans: adoption and actuarial funding are different

A defined benefit plan, including a cash balance plan, is not subject to a universal December 31 adoption cutoff. Retroactive adoption under Section 401(b)(2) may be available through the employer's extended return due date. However, that does not extend minimum funding requirements or remove the need for actuarial design and a signed plan.

For a single-employer defined benefit plan subject to Section 430, the general minimum-funding payment deadline is 8½ months after the plan year ends. For a calendar-year 2026 plan, that is September 15, 2027. Earlier quarterly installments can apply. A sole proprietor's October 15 extended return deadline does not extend September minimum funding. Coordinate adoption and payment early enough to satisfy both rules; do not wait until October to ask for a prior-year cash balance plan.

The enrolled actuary should calculate required funding and the deductible range, employee benefits, effects of other retirement plans, continuing funding obligations and any PBGC requirements. An annual benefit limit is not an automatic contribution allowance. Treasury Regulation 1.430(j)-1 governs the minimum-funding timing; tax deduction and plan-adoption rules must also be satisfied.

SIMPLE IRA: a separate October 1 rule

An employer that has not previously maintained a SIMPLE IRA generally must establish it between January 1 and October 1 of the year. A new employer formed after October 1 may establish one as soon as administratively feasible after starting. An employer that previously maintained a SIMPLE IRA generally must establish a new one effective January 1. A SIMPLE IRA is not ordinarily a plan you first open the following spring for the prior tax year. Employee notices and contribution deposits have their own requirements. See the IRS SIMPLE IRA FAQs.

A deadline calendar for 2026 contributions

These are ordinary calendar-year deadlines before special relief. Verify the return type, plan year, short-year rules, valid extension and provider cutoff. Extending a return does not extend the time to pay the income tax itself.

  • During 2026: make ordinary employee deferral elections before compensation is available and meet payroll deposit rules. Existing self-employed deferral elections generally need action by December 31. The usual new SIMPLE IRA setup cutoff is October 1, 2026.
  • March 15, 2027: ordinary 2026 S corporation and partnership return due date. A valid extension generally moves the eligible adoption and deductible employer contribution window to September 15, 2027.
  • April 15, 2027: ordinary 2026 sole-proprietor and calendar-year C corporation return due date. A valid extension generally moves the eligible employer-plan window to October 15, 2027. The new sole-proprietor first-year deferral exception does not get that extension.
  • September 15, 2027: general outside minimum-funding deadline for a calendar-year 2026 single-employer defined benefit plan, subject to earlier installments. It can be earlier than the sponsor's extended return deadline.

Check compensation, employees and cost before opening an account

For an S corporation owner, retirement contributions depend on eligible W-2 compensation, not shareholder distributions. For a self-employed owner, use adjusted net earnings. Business-sale capital gain or an ordinary-income sale allocation is not automatically eligible compensation. See the IRS S corporation guidance and the sale-year retirement review.

For 2026, the usual 401(k) elective deferral limit is $24,500 and the defined contribution annual-additions limit is the lesser of $72,000 or 100% of compensation, before eligible catch-up contributions. These are ceilings, not a promise that every owner can contribute them. Review deferrals to other employers' plans, related-business aggregation, plan terms and any catch-up or Roth requirements with the administrator. See the IRS 401(k) limits.

Bring employee census and ownership information, payroll and net-earnings calculations, existing plan documents, prior contributions, signed elections, return extensions and proof of deposits. Ask for owner and staff funding together with setup, administration, actuarial and investment costs. A new deduction should not create a cash obligation you cannot sustain.

If a plan already exists, ask whether its terms permit an additional employer contribution or whether a timely amendment is available. Retroactive adoption is not blanket permission to backdate elections, amend accrued benefits or ignore correction rules.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I establish a Solo 401(k) after December 31?

Yes, eligible retroactive adoption can support prior-year employer contributions by the applicable employer return deadline, including extensions. It generally does not create retroactive salary deferrals. A first-year sole proprietor who owns the entire unincorporated business and is its only employee has a separate exception with an unextended return deadline.

Must employee deferrals always be deposited by December 31?

No. The election and deposit are separate. Existing self-employed owner-only plans generally require the election by year-end but may allow deposit by the applicable extended return deadline. Employee payroll withholding has prompt deposit requirements. Under the narrow new-plan sole-proprietor exception, first-year deferrals must be paid by the unextended return deadline.

Can I adopt a cash balance plan after year-end?

Potentially, under the qualified-plan retroactive-adoption rule, but actuarial minimum funding has a separate deadline. For a calendar-year 2026 single-employer plan subject to Section 430, the general outside minimum-funding deadline is September 15, 2027, with earlier installments possible. An October tax-return extension does not move that deadline.

What is the SEP IRA limit for 2026?

The limit is the lesser of 25% of an employee’s eligible compensation or $72,000. Self-employed owners use an adjusted net-earnings calculation. New SEPs do not allow employee salary deferrals or catch-up contributions, and eligible staff may also need employer contributions.

Can I combine a 401(k) and a cash balance plan?

Yes, if the design and applicable rules support both. An actuary and plan administrator must coordinate employee coverage, benefit and contribution limits, and combined-plan deduction rules. Do not simply add two advertised maximums or treat a cash balance benefit limit as a deductible deposit.

Can I set up a retirement plan after the tax year ends?

Often yes: a SEP or eligible qualified employer plan can be adopted after year-end by the applicable return deadline, including extensions. Deferrals have separate rules, including a narrow first-year sole-proprietor exception with an unextended deadline. Defined benefit funding can be due earlier than the extended return.

For the underlying rules, see the official tax guidance. The relevant tax year, entity documents, actual transactions, and state filings determine the result.
