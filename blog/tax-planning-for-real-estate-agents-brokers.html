Tax Planning for Real Estate Agents and Brokers

Commission income can arrive unevenly while payroll, lead-generation bills and property costs continue every month. A useful tax plan connects those cash flows to the entity, worker arrangements and rentals you actually own. High commissions alone do not establish that a particular election or deduction is appropriate.

Should a high-producing agent elect S corporation taxation?

Compare projected net profit, reasonable owner compensation, other wages, state taxes, payroll administration and retirement funding. Self-employment tax has Social Security limits and Medicare rules; applying 15.3% to every dollar of profit can overstate the comparison. An S corporation must pay reasonable compensation for owner services before treating payments as distributions. There is no universal $80,000 or $100,000 profit threshold that makes an election worthwhile.

Confirm that licensing rules and brokerage contracts permit the proposed payment arrangement and that the election can take effect for the intended year. Model compliance costs as well as federal taxes. See the IRS compensation guidance and our S corporation decision checklist after a strong commission year.

Vehicle deductions require records and an eligible method

Separate business travel to showings, inspections and closings from personal driving and commuting. Keep dates, destinations, business purpose and mileage, together with total annual mileage and supporting expense records. A business logo or a full appointment calendar does not make every trip deductible.

Compare standard mileage with actual expenses only when each method is available. For an owned car, using standard mileage generally requires selecting it in the first year the car is available for business use. Later changes have depreciation restrictions. A leased vehicle using standard mileage generally must continue that method for the lease period, including renewals. Prior Section 179, bonus depreciation or other depreciation choices can prevent standard mileage. Consult Publication 463 before changing methods.

Marketing, technology and team expenses

Track MLS charges, professional photography, staging, signage, lead platforms, CRM software and website costs by business purpose and category. Ordinary and necessary business expenses may be deductible, but not every payment is fully deductible in the year paid. Assets, prepaid services, personal expenses and certain association-dues components can require different treatment. Keep invoices and the allocation between your business and reimbursed or shared team costs.

Separate the agent's own costs from brokerage and team spending. Document who contracted for the service, who paid and whether another party reimbursed it. This improves deduction support and shows which channels produce clients rather than merely generating activity.

Home office and meeting arrangements are separate tests

A home office generally must meet exclusive and regular business-use requirements and an applicable qualifying test. Administrative or management use may qualify as a principal place of business when there is no other fixed location where substantial administrative or management work is conducted. Access to a brokerage desk does not by itself settle that question. Review Publication 587 before choosing actual costs or the simplified method.

An S corporation owner-employee has a different reimbursement analysis from a sole proprietor. An accountable arrangement requires a business connection, substantiation and return of excess advances. Do not assume that paying yourself a home-office allowance makes it deductible.

Section 280A(g) generally excludes rental income when a dwelling used as a residence is rented for fewer than 15 days during the year. That owner-level exclusion does not automatically establish a business deduction. A separate business needs an ordinary and necessary purpose, reasonable rent and records; entertainment restrictions can matter. A sole proprietor cannot create a deductible payment by renting a home to themselves. See IRS Topic 415 and the home-rental documentation guide.

Retirement planning with employees and uneven commissions

Choose a plan using the employee census, related-business ownership, compensation and sustainable funding. An owner-only 401(k) is not a way to exclude otherwise eligible employees. A SEP and a 401(k) have different contribution and employee-coverage rules, and a cash balance plan needs actuarial analysis and ongoing funding review.

Separate the deadlines for adopting a plan, making a salary-deferral election, depositing employee contributions and funding employer contributions. Some employer funding can occur after year-end; that does not mean every election can wait until tax preparation. S corporation distributions are not retirement-plan compensation. A sole proprietor can also sponsor a qualified retirement arrangement, using the applicable self-employed calculation. See Publication 560.

Rental ownership does not automatically shelter commissions

A cost segregation study identifies eligible components and recovery periods, not a guaranteed current deduction against commissions. Bonus depreciation depends on asset eligibility, acquisition and placed-in-service dates, elections and other rules. Owner-level basis, at-risk, passive-loss and excess-business-loss limits can delay the benefit.

Real estate professional qualification and material participation are separate tests. Being licensed or working in real estate does not automatically make every rental nonpassive. Review the actual activities, employee ownership restrictions where applicable, participation records and any valid grouping election. Flipping or dealer activity also needs separate inventory and ordinary-income analysis. Start with Publication 925 and the commission-and-rental ownership guide.

Team classification and estimated payments

Licensed real estate agents can qualify as statutory nonemployees for federal tax purposes when the statutory pay and written-contract conditions are met. That treatment does not automatically extend to assistants or every team member, and it does not resolve every state employment-law issue. Review the IRS statutory nonemployee conditions and classify each role from its actual facts.

Evaluate withholding and estimated payments together. Whether estimates are needed depends on the return, prior-year tax, current-year income and payments already made. Uneven commissions may make the annualized-income installment method relevant. Reforecast after a major closing, property sale or change in team costs rather than assuming equal quarterly revenue. See Publication 505.

Define the review you want to buy

Ask the advisor to separate entity modeling, payroll setup, return preparation, property analysis and ongoing estimate updates. A proposal should identify the responsible professional, required records, implementation dates and any outside study or plan-administration fees. It should also say how work is handed to your existing preparer.

For an initial discussion, describe your expected commission profit range, entity type, team size, rental interests and upcoming decision. Arrange secure delivery before sending returns or financial records. See individual agent advisory scope, brokerage-owner planning scope and published fees.

Frequently asked questions

Should real estate agents form an S corporation?

Not solely because commissions exceed a preset amount. Compare reasonable compensation, projected profit, state rules, payroll and administration costs, retirement funding and the permitted brokerage payment arrangement.

Can real estate agents deduct vehicle expenses?

Eligible business travel may be deductible, but personal use and ordinary commuting are not. The available mileage or actual-expense method depends on prior choices, ownership or leasing and depreciation rules, supported by records.

Are marketing and lead-generation costs fully deductible?

Ordinary and necessary business costs may qualify, but capitalization, prepayments, personal use and nondeductible dues components can change the amount and timing. Review invoices and the accounting method rather than deducting every payment immediately.
