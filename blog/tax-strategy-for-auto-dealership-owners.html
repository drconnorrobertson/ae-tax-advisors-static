Tax Strategy for Auto Dealership Owners: Inventory, Interest and Facilities

Published on August 10, 2026 | Updated November 6, 2026 | Business Owner Tax Planning | AE Tax Advisors

A dealer planning a showroom renovation or buying the leased dealership property needs a coordinated review of inventory, financing and the entity that owns each asset. A large study deduction is not enough: the preparer must determine whether bonus depreciation is available and whether the owner can use the resulting loss.

Separate vehicle inventory from operating assets

Vehicles held for sale are inventory, not depreciable equipment simply because the dealership financed them. Track vehicle and parts inventory, demonstrators, service equipment and property improvements separately. Demonstrator use and employee personal use require their own classification and fringe-benefit review.

LIFO is a formal inventory method with election and conformity requirements. Rising costs can defer income, but inventory reductions, price changes, a sale or an entity transaction may reverse benefits. Review reserves and transaction consequences before choosing or changing the method. Manufacturer incentives, holdback and floor-plan assistance need consistent treatment supported by the agreements; do not net every payment against interest automatically.

Calculate floor-plan interest before promising bonus depreciation

Qualifying floor-plan debt finances motor vehicles held for sale or lease and is secured by the acquired inventory. A loan secured by office equipment does not become floor-plan financing simply because a dealership borrowed it.

Where Section 163(j) applies, the deduction limit generally combines business interest income, 30% of adjusted taxable income and floor-plan financing interest. For tax years beginning after 2024, depreciation, amortization and depletion deductions are added back when computing adjusted taxable income, subject to other required adjustments. Determine any small-business exception, aggregation and tax-shelter restrictions before applying the calculation.

The bonus restriction is not a blanket election that eliminates bonus on every asset the owner holds. Under Treasury Regulation 1.168(k)-2(b)(2)(ii)(G), floor-plan interest is taken into account for this purpose when the trade or business's total business interest expense exceeds business interest income plus the applicable adjusted-taxable-income amount. The restriction then applies to property placed in service by that trade or business in that year. Merely having floor-plan debt does not establish that this condition is met.

The regulation also distinguishes property leased by a lessor whose trade or business does not trigger that condition. Identify the taxpayer, trade or business, ownership and actual use before analyzing a separate property entity. A separate LLC alone does not establish a different federal taxpayer or guarantee bonus eligibility.

No fixed percentage of a dealership qualifies for shorter lives. Floors, lighting, electrical systems and other components require functional analysis; labels do not establish their classification. Land is not depreciable. Qualifying property acquired and placed in service after January 19, 2025 may qualify for 100% bonus, subject to the statutory exclusions and acquisition rules. Earlier acquisitions require their applicable rules. ADS-required property and the floor-plan restriction need separate attention.

Qualified improvement property generally concerns qualifying interior improvements made by the taxpayer to an existing nonresidential building; enlargements, elevators and internal structural framework are excluded. Buying a renovated building does not automatically turn its purchase price into QIP. Section 179 has separate eligibility, investment and income limits. For an existing facility, determine whether correction requires a method change and Form 3115 or another permitted procedure; not every prior-year item can be caught up the same way.

Check the owner's usable deduction and workforce costs

Basis, at-risk, passive-loss and excess-business-loss limits may restrict current use of pass-through losses. Renting a building to a business in which the owner materially participates does not automatically make rental losses nonpassive. Evaluate the self-rental rules and any valid grouping separately.

Retail status does not guarantee a full QBI deduction. Qualified income, taxable-income limits, wages, qualified property, losses and aggregation rules still matter. For an S corporation, document reasonable compensation from the owner's actual services rather than a standard salary range. A retirement-plan analysis must include eligible employees, related employers, testing and funding obligations; management titles alone do not justify excluding staff.

Illustrative review: a service-department renovation

A dealer proposes new lifts, expanded service bays and lot improvements while refinancing inventory. Before forecasting deductions, the preparer calculates Section 163(j), the study provider classifies supported costs, and the owner identifies whether the dealership or a separate landlord owns each improvement. The filing team then models owner-level limitations and state treatment. This is a decision sequence, not a client result or savings projection.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does floor-plan financing automatically eliminate bonus depreciation?

No. The annual calculation, the relevant trade or business and the regulatory floor-plan condition matter. Property classification and other bonus requirements must also be satisfied.

Are vehicles held for sale eligible for equipment depreciation?

Inventory held for sale is not depreciable operating equipment. Demonstrators and other business-use vehicles require a separate facts-and-use review.

Does a separate real-estate LLC guarantee deductible losses?

No. Determine federal tax ownership, depreciation eligibility, related-party treatment and owner-level loss limits. Separate legal title alone does not establish current deduction usability.
