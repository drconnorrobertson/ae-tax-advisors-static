Rental QBI When You Also Own an Operating Business

Separate rental trade-or-business status, commonly controlled self-rentals, SSTB restrictions and QBI aggregation from passive-loss rules.

Book a Call

By AE Tax Advisors Team · Updated November 6, 2026

Do not use one tax label for three different questions

For a high-income owner, rental QBI requires more than calling the property active or putting it in an LLC. Determine whether the rental is a trade or business for Section 199A, calculate any QBI deduction limitations, and separately analyze passive-activity treatment. A favorable result under one set of rules does not establish the others.

Three routes to review for rental business status

  • Section 162 facts: assess the actual rental operations as a trade or business.
  • Rental real estate safe harbor: evaluate all conditions in Revenue Procedure 2019-38, including records and excluded arrangements. Missing the safe harbor does not necessarily end the Section 162 analysis.
  • Commonly controlled rental: Regulation 1.199A-1(b)(14) provides a separate rule for property rented to a trade or business conducted by the individual or a commonly controlled relevant pass-through entity.

The commonly controlled rule is not a statement that every lease to any corporation qualifies. Identify the tenant's tax classification and ownership and apply the regulatory control test. Self-rentals to commonly controlled businesses and triple-net leases are excluded from the revenue procedure's safe harbor; that exclusion is not itself proof they can never be a Section 199A trade or business.

Practice owners: check the SSTB anti-abuse rule

A medical, legal or other specified service business presents an additional issue. Where a business provides property or services to a commonly owned SSTB, the relevant portion can be treated as an SSTB under Regulation 1.199A-5(c)(2). A separate property LLC is not an automatic way to remove practice-related income from the SSTB restrictions. Apply the tax year's threshold and phase-in rules to the actual household taxable income.

For the numeric interaction, see how cost segregation changes the QBI deduction.

High income changes the calculation, not just eligibility

Even when rental income is eligible QBI, the result can depend on wages, unadjusted basis immediately after acquisition of qualified property, taxable-income limits, net capital gain and QBI losses. Depreciation can reduce QBI as well as taxable income. Do not simply multiply gross rents by 20%, or assume a larger depreciation deduction always improves the combined federal result by the same amount.

QBI aggregation is not passive-activity grouping

Section 199A aggregation has its own ownership, business, consistency and disclosure requirements. Section 469 grouping addresses passive-activity rules. Keep the elections and workpapers separate. An activity grouped for one purpose is not automatically aggregated for the other. Compare results with and without a permissible QBI aggregation and retain the applicable annual disclosures.

A useful owner example

Assume an owner has a profitable operating company, a building leased to that company and an unrelated residential rental. Prepare separate rental income and depreciation schedules. Determine the route to trade-or-business status for each rental, then check SSTB and wage/property limits as applicable. Finally, reconcile the QBI computation with the separate passive-loss schedules. This is a review sequence, not a promise of an additional 20% deduction.

Bring the right file

  • Ownership percentages, tenant classification and signed leases.
  • Rental books, service records, property basis and wage information.
  • Section 199A statements attached to K-1s and prior aggregation disclosures.
  • QBI-loss carryforwards and separate passive-loss records.
  • A complete household projection for the relevant tax year.

Questions business owners ask

Does a rental need the 250-hour safe harbor to qualify for QBI?

Not always. The safe harbor is one route. Section 162 facts and the commonly controlled rental rule may provide other routes, subject to their requirements.

Can a doctor move practice rent into a separate LLC to avoid SSTB limits?

A separate LLC does not automatically avoid the restrictions. Review the commonly owned SSTB property or services rule and the owner’s applicable income limits.

Is QBI aggregation the same as grouping for passive losses?

No. Section 199A aggregation and Section 469 grouping are separate analyses with different requirements and disclosures.
