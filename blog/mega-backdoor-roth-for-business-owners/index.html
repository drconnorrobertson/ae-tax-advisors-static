What Is the Mega Backdoor Roth and Can Business Owners Use It?

Published on August 25, 2026 | Retirement Plans | AE Tax Advisors

A mega backdoor Roth uses voluntary after-tax contributions to an eligible retirement plan followed by a permitted Roth conversion or rollover. It is different from a designated Roth elective deferral and from a nondeductible IRA contribution. Business ownership alone does not establish access or contribution capacity.

Find the remaining 2026 annual-additions room

For 2026, employee elective deferrals are limited to $24,500. A plan that permits catch-up contributions can allow another $8,000 for an eligible participant age 50 or older, or $11,250 instead for someone attaining age 60, 61, 62 or 63 during 2026. The annual-additions ceiling is the lesser of $72,000 or 100% of applicable compensation, excluding eligible catch-up contributions. Employee deferrals, employer contributions and voluntary after-tax employee contributions share that ceiling. The corresponding dollar ceilings including catch-up are $80,000 or $83,250, not amounts everyone can contribute.

Illustration for an eligible participant under age 50 with sufficient compensation: $72,000 minus $24,500 regular employee deferral minus $20,000 employer contribution leaves $27,500 of potential voluntary after-tax room. This is arithmetic capacity only. The plan document, testing, compensation and other contributions may reduce it to a smaller amount or zero. The $27,500 after-tax contribution is not a current income-tax deduction.

Confirm the plan permits each step

Ask whether the signed plan document allows voluntary after-tax employee contributions, separately accounts for their basis and earnings, and permits either an in-plan Roth conversion or an eligible in-service distribution and rollover. A brokerage account labeled solo 401(k) does not prove these features exist. Distribution restrictions and administrator procedures matter; do not contribute money assuming it can immediately be rolled out.

Employees and related businesses can change the result

An owner-only plan is for an owner, or owner and spouse, without other eligible employees. Do not equate this with having no full-time staff: review age, service, eligible long-term part-time employees, and employees of controlled-group or affiliated-service-group businesses. A spouse needs actual services and compensation. A plan administrator must determine coverage before an owner-only design is used.

Voluntary after-tax contributions can be subject to actual contribution percentage (ACP) testing when applicable. A safe-harbor 401(k) design does not automatically exempt voluntary after-tax employee contributions from testing. Have the administrator confirm eligible participants, highly compensated employee treatment, top-heavy rules and corrective-distribution risk before projecting owner capacity.

Conversion tax depends on basis and earnings

After-tax contribution basis is not taxed again, but earnings and other pre-tax amounts moved to Roth are generally taxable. A distribution containing both pre-tax and after-tax amounts requires allocation under the rollover rules; do not simply assume any withdrawal can consist solely of basis. IRS guidance permits properly directed simultaneous distributions to different destinations under its conditions. The administrator should document basis, taxable amounts and Forms 1099-R; the return preparer reconciles the reporting. Qualified Roth distributions require their applicable conditions, including holding-period rules.

Distinguish three separate Roth routes

A designated Roth elective deferral shares the $24,500 employee deferral limit with traditional deferrals. Voluntary after-tax contributions use remaining annual-additions capacity and need a separate conversion or rollover path. For IRAs, the 2026 regular contribution limit is $7,500 plus $1,100 catch-up at age 50 or older, subject to eligibility; the IRA pro-rata rule aggregates traditional, SEP and SIMPLE IRA balances. Neither the IRA limit nor an assumed tax-free conversion determines 401(k) capacity.

For 2026, an affected participant whose 2025 FICA wages from the sponsoring employer exceeded $150,000 generally must make catch-up contributions as Roth contributions. This is a prior-year employer-wage test, not a test of household income or S-corp distributions. Have payroll and the administrator confirm applicability and plan support. Roth contributions do not produce a current income-tax deduction; qualified Roth distributions can be tax-free.

Evaluate funding and administrator scope before implementation

Ask for a contribution schedule showing compensation, employee deferrals across jobs, employer amounts and remaining after-tax capacity. Confirm whether a cash balance plan changes combined-plan deductions and staff costs; it does not automatically supply a six-figure contribution. Request written conversion procedures, testing responsibility, fees, correction deadlines and a secure handoff to the return preparer. AE tax planning should be coordinated with the plan administrator rather than treated as execution of an investment or rollover.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much mega backdoor Roth capacity is available in 2026?

Start with the lesser of $72,000 or applicable compensation and subtract regular employee deferrals and employer contributions. Plan terms, other contributions and testing can reduce or eliminate the remaining room.

Does a safe-harbor 401(k) automatically permit after-tax contributions?

No. The plan must permit them and applicable ACP testing still needs review. Safe-harbor status alone does not establish after-tax contribution or conversion rights.

Is converting after-tax contributions entirely tax-free?

Basis is not taxed again, but earnings and other pre-tax amounts converted to Roth are generally taxable. Distribution allocation and reporting must be checked.

Does a mega backdoor Roth reduce this year’s taxable income?

The voluntary after-tax contribution does not create a current deduction. Taxable conversion amounts can increase current income; qualified future Roth distributions have separate requirements.
