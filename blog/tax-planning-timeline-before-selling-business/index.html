The 3-Year Tax Planning Timeline Before Selling Your Business

Published on June 26, 2026 | Updated November 6, 2026 | Selling Your Business | AE Tax Advisors

Find Out What This Is Worth in Your Situation

Every strategy on this page depends on your income, your entity structure, and your state. Book a call and we will tell you what applies to you and what it is worth.

Choose a Time to Talk With AE Tax

Use a three-year timeline as a planning schedule, not a promise that three years establishes every tax benefit. Stock holding periods, original issuance, entity history and the actual sale structure can require a different lead time. If an offer is already on the table, start with the decisions still open rather than assuming planning is either too late or guaranteed to work.

Planning for a sale fits within a broader year-round business tax planning process. Keep current forecasts, owner cash needs and implementation responsibilities coordinated while exploring the transaction. Review AE Tax Advisors’ business planning service for engagement scope.

Year One: Confirm the Structure and the Actual Stock Dates

Collect entity returns, formation and conversion records, stock issuance documents, ownership history, basis schedules and any prior exclusions. Compare asset-sale and ownership-interest-sale outcomes before changing entities. Converting to a C corporation can create ongoing corporate and distribution taxes, and does not automatically make an existing owner's shares qualified small business stock (QSBS).

For Section 1202, distinguish the stock's acquisition date from its issuance date, including applicable holding-period tacking. These dates control different tests:

Stock datesHolding period and exclusionLimits to check
Acquired on or before July 4, 2025Generally more than five years. Stock acquired after September 27, 2010 can qualify for 100%; earlier cohorts have different percentages.$10 million remaining per-issuer dollar limit, compared with the 10-times-basis alternative.
Acquired after July 4, 202550% at at least three years, 75% at at least four years, and 100% at at least five years.$15 million dollar limit for 2026, compared with the 10-times-basis alternative; inflation adjustments begin after 2026.
Issued on or before versus after July 4, 2025Separate original-issuance and gross-assets test.$50 million gross-assets ceiling for earlier issuance; $75 million for later issuance in 2026, with later inflation adjustments.

The eligible-gain ceiling is the greater of the remaining dollar limit or 10 times the adjusted basis of that issuer's QSBS sold during the year, disregarding basis additions after issuance. Apply the exclusion percentage after the ceiling. Prior issuer exclusions, mixed acquisition cohorts and married-return rules prevent treating these as automatic independent allowances. The current Section 1202 statute and effective-date provisions control.

Also establish original-issue eligibility, the qualified business, the active-business asset test and C corporation status during substantially all of the holding period. Many service, leasing and real estate businesses do not qualify. A tax election alone does not cure ineligible stock. On incorporation of an existing business, contributed-property value and pre-incorporation appreciation need separate analysis. Use the QSBS owner guide for the detailed evidence review.

If the company owns the building, do not simply transfer it to another entity to prepare for sale. A distribution of appreciated corporate property can create tax. Have counsel, the lender and the tax advisor evaluate a retained-building structure before committing to it.

Year Two: Build the Cash and Loss Projections

Reconcile financial statements, owner compensation and related-party transactions with the records a buyer will review. Request separate projections for cash at closing, seller financing, escrow or holdbacks, debt payoff, transaction expenses and estimated taxes.

An eligible installment sale generally requires at least one payment after the sale year. The method applies to eligible gain as payments are received, not automatically to every asset or all cash in a business deal. Depreciation recapture is generally reported in the sale year even without a payment; inventory and other excluded items need current recognition. Interest is separate, and debt relief, escrow restrictions and contingent payments can change the calculation. See IRS Publication 537 and the installment-sale planning guide.

Hypothetical installment illustration: suppose a single eligible asset sells for $8 million with $1 million of adjusted basis, no selling expenses, no debt assumed and no recapture. Gross profit is $7 million and the gross-profit percentage is 87.5%. A $1.6 million principal payment includes $1.4 million of gain and $200,000 of basis recovery; interest is reported separately. These assumptions do not describe a mixed-asset business sale. Deferral is not a guaranteed lower tax rate and adds collection risk.

Retained-property depreciation needs a usability test. A cost segregation study may identify supported depreciation deductions, but a passive rental loss does not automatically offset ordinary consulting income or gain from selling a materially participated business. Classify actual sale income; separate current deductions from old carryforwards; apply relevant basis, at-risk, Section 469 and Section 461(l) limits. For an existing property, also confirm the correct accounting-method procedure. Use the sale-year rental-loss workpaper before treating an offset as available or reducing estimates. Publication 925 and the Form 461 instructions explain the loss gates.

Year Three: Confirm the Deal Before Signing

Compare the legal structure and its tax treatment, including any proposed election. A C corporation asset sale can produce corporate tax and a second shareholder tax when proceeds are distributed. A genuine shareholder stock sale generally does not itself cause the corporation to sell its assets, but a deemed-asset-sale election changes that analysis. Section 338(h)(10) is limited to eligible acquisitions from specified corporate sellers or S corporation shareholders; it is not a universal option for an individually owned C corporation. All required parties must agree. Check the Form 8023 instructions with deal counsel.

For a covered asset acquisition, reconcile the negotiated allocation and both parties' reporting. Form 8594 asset classes do not by themselves determine the seller's tax rate. Acquired amortizable goodwill can generate Section 1245 ordinary recapture, with remaining gain potentially subject to Section 1231 netting and the five-year lookback. Self-created goodwill requires its own capital-asset and ownership analysis. Inventory, receivables, service payments and non-compete consideration need separate treatment. See Publication 544 and the seller's allocation and character guide.

Recheck holding periods using the actual sale date. Newly issued stock acquired in 2026 cannot reach three years merely because a plan labels 2028 “Year Three.” Assuming eligibility and no tacking, a sale after its third anniversary in 2029 could reach the 50% tier, not the 100% tier. Delaying installment payments does not extend the holding period of stock already sold.

If otherwise qualifying stock is sold before the Section 1202 holding period is met, discuss Section 1045 separately: eligible QSBS must have been held for more than six months, and qualifying replacement stock must be purchased within the 60-day window. It is a conditional deferral election, not an automatic substitute for exclusion or an investment recommendation.

Keep Tax Illustrations Separate from Expected Savings

The arithmetic $10 million × 23.8% = $2.38 million assumes $10 million of taxable gain, not merely a $10 million sale price, and an assumed 20% capital-gain rate plus 3.8% NIIT applying to all that gain. It is a hypothetical federal-rate comparison, not a client result, tax-return calculation or amount automatically avoidable through planning. Basis, gain character, the NIIT analysis, QSBS eligibility and caps, state conformity, and other return items can all change the outcome. A three-year head start does not guarantee any exclusion.

Assign the Closing and Return Handoff

  • Owner and bookkeeper: reconcile financials, basis, depreciation, debt, retained assets and the evidence behind stock dates and participation.
  • Deal counsel: identify signing deadlines, legal sellers, lease terms, consents, election agreements and restrictions on pre-sale transfers.
  • Tax advisor and return preparers: produce the allocation/character schedule, QSBS eligibility file, installment projection, rental-loss workpaper and entity/owner estimated-tax calendar.
  • Closing team: deliver final settlement statements, payment terms and the signed allocation to the named preparers. Confirm who reconciles later changes and files required forms.

List deliverables, missing records, responsible professionals and due dates before signing. AE's agreed advisory scope does not automatically include legal representation, valuation, engineering studies or every entity and owner return. Confirm those responsibilities in the engagement.

Discuss Your Sale Timeline and Advisory Fit

Bring the expected sale date, entity structure, draft terms and unresolved tax questions. Use the discovery call to identify the review scope and which outside professionals need to be involved.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is three years enough to obtain a full QSBS exclusion?

Not automatically. Stock acquired after July 4, 2025 can reach a 50% exclusion at at least three years, 75% at four years and 100% at five years, if all other requirements are met. Earlier stock generally requires more than five years, and its acquisition cohort affects the percentage.

Can an installment note spread all business-sale tax over future years?

No. Only eligible gain follows the installment method. Depreciation recapture is generally recognized in the sale year, even without payment, and inventory and other excluded items require separate treatment. Interest, debt relief, escrow and payment terms also matter.

Can depreciation on a building I keep offset the business sale?

Only if the deduction is otherwise allowed and the applicable loss limits permit its use. Classify the sale income and apply basis, at-risk, passive-activity and excess-business-loss rules. A cost segregation study alone does not establish an offset.

Does allocating the price to goodwill guarantee capital-gain treatment?

No. Allocation and tax character are separate questions. Acquired amortizable goodwill can produce ordinary recapture; remaining gain may enter Section 1231. Self-created goodwill, ownership, holding periods and the seller entity require their own analysis.

What if I already signed a letter of intent?

Provide the complete letter, draft agreement and proposed closing date. Have counsel identify which terms are binding or still negotiable, then focus the tax work on the remaining choices and reporting responsibilities. Do not assume either that all planning is lost or that closed decisions can be changed.

Planning an exit or planning throughout the year?

A sale creates transaction-specific deadlines. If you need ongoing planning before a sale is on the calendar, use the quarterly business tax planning checklist and review business-owner tax advisory services. Keep sale advice tied to your transaction documents and decision dates.
