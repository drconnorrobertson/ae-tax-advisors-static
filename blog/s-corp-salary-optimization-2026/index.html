S-Corp Salary Planning for 2026: Compensation, Payroll and QBI

By AE Tax Advisors · Published 2026-08-04

Updated November 8, 2026.

Start S corporation salary planning with reasonable compensation for the owner's work. Then model payroll tax, pass-through income, QBI, retirement contributions and state taxes together. A desired tax saving does not establish a defensible wage.

Document the services being paid for

List duties, working time, responsibility, experience and comparable compensation. Consider how much business income comes from the owner's services versus employees or invested capital. A fixed 60/40 salary-distribution split is not an IRS safe harbor. Review compensation when the business or the owner's role changes.

Calculate payroll tax using the right bases

For 2026, the Social Security wage base is $184,500. Medicare generally continues above that amount. Additional Medicare Tax has its own household-income thresholds, while employer withholding above $200,000 of wages is a separate rule. It is not correct to multiply every dollar moved from wages to distributions by 15.3%.

Outside wages can affect the employee's Social Security calculation, but they do not simply remove the corporation's employer-tax obligations. Include payroll administration and state taxes in the comparison. Payments for services may be recharacterized as wages if compensation is insufficient.

Retirement limits interact with wages

Employee deferrals for 2026 are limited to $24,500 and annual additions generally to $72,000 before eligible catch-up. Employer contributions for an S corporation owner use eligible W-2 compensation rather than distributions.

Assume an under-50 owner has $130,000 of eligible wages, no other deferrals and a plan allowing the contributions. A 25% employer amount is $32,500; adding $24,500 of employee deferrals gives $57,000. Actual plan terms, employee coverage, deduction rules and available cash still control. Employee deferrals do not generally remove wages from Social Security or Medicare tax.

Check the QBI result separately

Owner wages and employer expenses reduce pass-through profit, while eligible wages can support a wage limitation when it applies. More salary can increase one limit but decrease QBI. Compare the final individual return, not just the corporation's wage expense.

Implement through payroll and records

  • Keep the compensation analysis and review date.
  • Run wages, deposits and Forms W-2/941 correctly.
  • Track reimbursements, distributions and loans separately.
  • Reconcile retirement funding and all other employer plans.
  • Update the projection before year-end payroll decisions.

See QBI for S corporations and retirement-plan options, or review the compensation and tax projection.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there an IRS-approved salary percentage?

No. Reasonable compensation depends on services and evidence, not a fixed percentage of profit.

Does every dollar of distributions save 15.3%?

No. Compensation, wage bases, other earnings, state taxes and costs affect the actual comparison.

Do distributions count as retirement-plan compensation?

No. An S corporation owner’s plan contributions generally depend on eligible wages and plan rules.
