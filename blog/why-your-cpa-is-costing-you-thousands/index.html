Is Your CPA Engagement Missing Tax Planning? A Scope Review

By AE Tax Advisors · Published 2026-05-14 · Updated November 8, 2026.

Return preparation and forward-looking planning can be separate services. Before concluding that your CPA is costing you money, compare the engagement you purchased with the work you now need.

Read the engagement letter

Identify covered returns, planning meetings, notices, bookkeeping and specialist work. Ask whether advice is included throughout the year or billed separately. An accurate return does not promise that every optional strategy has been evaluated.

Request an explanation before switching

Ask which elections were considered, why deductions were limited and what records were missing. A strategy may be unsuitable because of timing, eligibility, cost, cash flow or the planned sale of an asset. Give the current professional an opportunity to explain.

Evaluate recommendations using net benefits

Separate a deduction from a credit and from actual cash savings. Request a comparison that includes fees, payroll or administration costs, state differences, future tax consequences and alternatives. Describe uncertain assumptions rather than treating a projection as guaranteed.

Use specialist reviews where needed

Rental depreciation, retirement-plan design and entity changes can require different expertise. Ask who performs the analysis, who prepares the filing and who coordinates implementation. For depreciation corrections, Form 3115 is appropriate only when the facts and applicable procedure support it.

Check loss limitations

Real estate professional status does not alone make every rental loss deductible. Material participation and other applicable limitations remain relevant. Likewise, a short-term rental exception is not an automatic offset against wages.

Make a transition plan

If a second opinion identifies useful work, agree on scope, records, price and deadlines. Obtain prior returns, depreciation, basis and carryforward schedules before transferring responsibility. Avoid duplicate or conflicting filings.

Request a planning scope review with your existing engagement and most recent returns. The purpose is to identify supported opportunities and implementation needs.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a large refund evidence of better planning?

Not by itself. A refund also depends on withholding and payments; compare total tax and costs.

Should I switch firms before obtaining my records?

Arrange access to prior returns and supporting schedules as part of the transition.
