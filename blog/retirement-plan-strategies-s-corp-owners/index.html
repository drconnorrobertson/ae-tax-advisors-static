Retirement Plan Strategies for S-Corp Owners: Solo 401(k), SEP IRA, and Defined Benefit Plans

Published on July 25, 2026 | Entity Structuring and Planning | AE Tax Advisors

For an S-corp owner, retirement planning starts with reasonable W-2 compensation, the employee census and available funding. Distributions are not retirement-plan compensation. Compare an owner-only 401(k), SEP and defined benefit plan by contribution capacity, staff obligations, administration and future funding, not by a guaranteed deduction.

2026 owner-only 401(k) limits

For 2026, employee elective deferrals are limited to $24,500. A plan that permits catch-up contributions can allow another $8,000 for an eligible participant age 50 or older, or $11,250 instead for someone attaining age 60, 61, 62 or 63 during 2026. The annual-additions ceiling is the lesser of $72,000 or 100% of applicable compensation, excluding eligible catch-up contributions. Employee deferrals, employer contributions and voluntary after-tax employee contributions share that ceiling. The corresponding dollar ceilings including catch-up are $80,000 or $83,250, not amounts everyone can contribute.

An owner-only plan is for an owner, or owner and spouse, without other eligible employees. Do not equate this with having no full-time staff: review age, service, eligible long-term part-time employees, and employees of controlled-group or affiliated-service-group businesses. A spouse needs actual services and compensation. A plan administrator must determine coverage before an owner-only design is used.

A $200,000 W-2 example that respects the ceiling

Assume an owner under age 50, $200,000 of eligible W-2 compensation, no other plan contributions and sufficient funding. A 25% employer calculation would be $50,000. But with a $24,500 employee deferral, only $47,500 of employer contribution fits the $72,000 annual-additions ceiling. The total is $72,000, not $74,500. A lower salary of $150,000 could support a $37,500 employer contribution plus $24,500 deferral, or $62,000, subject to plan terms and the other limits. These are contribution examples, not tax-saving forecasts.

Compare SEP funding with 401(k) deferrals

An ordinary SEP generally uses employer contributions only, up to 25% of eligible W-2 compensation and the 2026 $72,000 limit. At $200,000 of eligible W-2 compensation, the percentage calculation is $50,000. Review employee eligibility and uniform allocation requirements; a SEP is not permission to fund only the owner. SEP balances can also affect the IRA pro-rata calculation for a later Roth conversion. Grandfathered SARSEPs have separate rules.

Coordinate all jobs, entities and payroll

For 2026, an affected participant whose 2025 FICA wages from the sponsoring employer exceeded $150,000 generally must make catch-up contributions as Roth contributions. This is a prior-year employer-wage test, not a test of household income or S-corp distributions. Have payroll and the administrator confirm applicability and plan support. Roth contributions do not produce a current income-tax deduction; qualified Roth distributions can be tax-free.

The personal elective-deferral limit generally spans 401(k) and 403(b) plans, including another employer’s plan. Related employers may also have to aggregate plan limits and employee coverage. Wages should reflect services rather than be set solely to maximize a retirement deduction. Employer contributions may affect QBI and the overall income-tax benefit; traditional deferrals do not remove wages from Social Security or Medicare tax.

Cash balance and after-tax features are separate design decisions

A cash balance plan’s contribution is actuarially determined from promised benefits, age, compensation, existing assets and workforce facts. Combined-plan deduction limits require administrator and actuary review. Voluntary after-tax contributions require plan permission and remaining annual-additions capacity; conversion or rollover rights and applicable testing must also be confirmed. They do not supply an extra current deduction.

Implementation calendar and handoff

Separate plan adoption, deferral election, payroll deposit and employer funding deadlines. Employer contributions can generally be funded by the employer return due date including extensions. Employee salary deferrals generally require a timely election before compensation is available; payroll deposits have their own rules. A special first-year rule allows a sole proprietor with no employees to establish a new 401(k) after year-end and make first-year elective deferrals by the individual return deadline without extensions, if the requirements are met. It does not permit an S-corp owner to retroactively defer already-paid wages. Ask the administrator for a dated written calendar.

Ask the administrator and tax preparer to reconcile W-2 compensation, deferrals at other employers, employer funding, related-company ownership, the employee census and required filings. Clarify whether tax advisory, plan design, payroll execution, Form 5500-series work and return preparation are separate engagements.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can S-corp distributions fund employer retirement contribution limits?

Distributions are not eligible W-2 compensation. Employer contributions for an S-corp owner generally use eligible wages and remain subject to plan and annual limits.

Can a $200,000 W-2 owner contribute $74,500 in 2026 without catch-up?

Not under the ordinary $72,000 annual-additions ceiling. With a $24,500 deferral, only $47,500 remains for employer and voluntary after-tax contributions, assuming all other requirements are met.

Is a solo 401(k) available with part-time employees?

An owner-only design cannot exclude otherwise eligible employees merely because they are part-time. Review service, related employers and the plan’s participation rules.

Does a cash balance plan guarantee a large owner deduction?

No. An actuary and administrator must assess promised benefits, age, compensation, assets, staff coverage, funding and combined-plan deduction limits.

How does my S corporation salary limit employer retirement contributions?

Employer retirement contributions for an S corporation owner are generally tied to W-2 compensation, not distributions. Increasing wages solely to chase a larger contribution may cost more payroll tax or reduce QBI; run an integrated calculation.

For the underlying rules, see the official tax guidance. The relevant tax year, entity documents, actual transactions, and state filings determine the result.
