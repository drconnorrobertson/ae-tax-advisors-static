How to Report Cost Segregation on a Tax Return

By AE Tax Advisors · Published 2026-03-03

Updated November 8, 2026.

Report a cost segregation study through the taxpayer's fixed-asset records and applicable depreciation forms. The report is not a stand-alone tax deduction. The return preparer must reconcile basis, ownership, asset classes, service dates, elections and loss limitations before using the study's projected amounts.

1. Reconcile the report to tax basis

Identify the taxpayer that owns the assets and the return on which the activity is reported. Reconcile the study to the closing statement, land allocation, construction invoices, capitalized costs and existing depreciation schedule.

Land is not depreciable. A property's purchase price, loan balance and depreciable tax basis are different numbers. Seller credits, prior use, improvements and transaction structure can change the starting basis. Resolve differences between the report and books before entering assets.

2. Build an asset-level schedule

For each supported component, retain the description, allocated basis, recovery period, method, convention and placed-in-service date. Keep the remaining building basis separate from land and shorter-lived property.

Reclassification must have a technical basis. Do not apply a generic percentage to every building or assume that every component receives the same treatment. The study and tax workpapers should together explain how the schedule was derived.

3. Check bonus and Section 179 separately

Eligible shorter-lived property may qualify for bonus depreciation, but its acquisition and service dates, used-property requirements, related-party facts, elections and ADS treatment matter. The restored 100% allowance generally applies to eligible property acquired and placed in service after January 19, 2025.

A study completed now does not reset an older property's dates. Section 179 has different asset, business-use, lessor and income limits. Evaluate the actual provisions rather than treating every short recovery period as an immediate deduction.

4. Enter depreciation on the correct return

Form 4562 reports applicable depreciation, including the special allowance and MACRS categories. The deductions then flow to the entity return or activity schedule that owns the assets. A partnership, S corporation, sole proprietor and individual rental owner can have different reporting and owner-level consequences.

The engineering report normally stays in the permanent records with the detailed asset workpapers. It does not replace Form 4562, a required election statement or the schedules supporting the filed amount.

5. Choose the correction route for an existing property

For property depreciated in earlier years, determine whether the issue is a qualifying accounting-method change, an error correctable by amendment, or another adjustment. Form 3115 is not automatically appropriate for every depreciation mistake.

A qualifying method change may require a Section 481(a) adjustment reconciling depreciation allowed or allowable under the old and new methods. Verify the current automatic-change procedure, designated change number, scope restrictions, filing timing and duplicate-copy rules. A return extension does not eliminate those requirements.

See the Form 3115 implementation guide for the filing workflow. Keep the adjustment separate from current-year depreciation so the same amount is not deducted twice.

6. Apply the entity and owner limits

The depreciation calculation is only the starting point. Pass-through basis, at-risk amounts, passive-activity restrictions and excess-business-loss rules can restrict the deduction. A short-term rental needs the relevant rental-exception and participation analysis; real estate professional status alone does not prove material participation in every rental.

A rental loss can have value by offsetting eligible passive income without offsetting wages. Conversely, nonpassive classification does not remove all other limits. Retain the calculations showing the allowed amount and any carryforward.

7. Reconcile state treatment and later years

State conformity can differ from federal bonus depreciation. Track required additions, subtractions and state basis. A pass-through owner's state reporting may need information beyond the federal K-1.

Carry corrected schedules into future years. A sale can produce recapture or other gain based on adjusted basis and asset character. A Section 1031 exchange does not automatically defer every component or all gain; personal property and boot require separate analysis.

A return handoff checklist

  • Final study and an asset schedule tied to supported tax basis.
  • Ownership documents, closing records and land allocation.
  • Acquisition and placed-in-service evidence for each material asset.
  • Prior depreciation, elections, method changes and carryforwards.
  • Form 4562 and any Form 3115 or election statement workpapers.
  • Entity basis, at-risk, passive-loss and owner-return calculations.
  • State schedules and responsibility for future updates.

Ask the study provider and preparer to agree who resolves technical questions, prepares forms and signs the return. Request an implementation review if the report and filed depreciation do not reconcile. Confirm whether the engagement covers the study, return work, amendments or each separately.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I attach the study instead of Form 4562?

No. The study supports the asset records; the applicable tax forms and statements still must be prepared.

Does every lookback study require Form 3115?

No. The proper correction procedure depends on the issue, method history and applicable rules.

Can I use today’s bonus rate for an older property?

The study date does not reset the asset’s acquisition or placed-in-service date.

Does the report’s tax-saving estimate equal my deduction?

No. The return must apply eligibility, elections, state treatment and entity and owner limitations.
