Tax Planning for High-Income Consultants

Tax planning for an independent consultant starts with expected contract collections, net profit, and the cash needed for owner pay, taxes, and retirement. Model the decisions together and update the projection when a contract starts, ends, or changes materially.

Build a quarterly cash and tax projection

List signed work, expected collections by month, ordinary operating costs and significant one-time expenses. Reconcile Forms 1099 to the books without treating both the form and its underlying deposit as separate income. Have your preparer check federal and state estimated payments using the applicable rules and your household income. See the IRS estimated tax guide.

Compare the full cost of an S corporation election

Run the current filing setup and proposed election using the same expected profit. Model reasonable owner wages, employment taxes, state costs, payroll, return preparation, health insurance reporting, retirement contribution capacity and the owner’s potential QBI deduction. Profit alone does not settle the choice. Read the IRS guidance on S corporation compensation.

For illustration, if an election is modeled to reduce combined taxes by $4,500, and payroll and additional filing cost $3,200, the remaining modeled benefit is $1,300. If a less profitable year reduces that tax difference to $2,000, the same costs exceed it by $1,200. Compare both cases before making an election.

Review QBI using household taxable income

Consulting may be a specified service trade or business for Section 199A. Check the definition against the actual work and apply the relevant taxable-income limits using the correct filing status and tax year. Use the IRS qualified business income deduction guidance.

Choose a retirement plan that fits irregular collections

Compare the income measure that determines contributions, whether the business has eligible employees, related businesses, and funding deadlines. A one-participant 401(k) has different eligibility and administration considerations from a plan covering staff. Test affordability in a slow year before committing to recurring funding.

Prepare the records before the planning meeting

Bring your year-to-date profit and loss statement, open invoices, contract pipeline, prior returns, payroll records if applicable, estimated payments, and any retirement plan documents. List client-site travel and mixed-use expenses separately with their dates, amounts and business purposes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are consultants classified as an SSTB for the QBI deduction?

Consulting may be a specified service trade or business under Section 199A. Review what the business actually does and apply the rules for the owner’s taxable income, filing status, and tax year.

Should a solo consultant elect S-Corp status?

An election can make sense if the expected overall benefit exceeds added payroll, filing and state costs. Compare both structures using reasonable owner compensation and a slower-year scenario.

Can consultants deduct travel to client sites?

Qualifying business travel away from the tax home may be deductible with adequate records. Commuting, temporary assignments and mixed business and personal trips can have different treatment.
