Hotel Cost Segregation: Plan for Renovations and the Eventual Sale

AE Tax Advisors | Updated November 3, 2026

A hotel cost segregation decision should include more than the largest first-year deduction. The owner also needs a record of what was acquired, what has already been depreciated, what a renovation removes and what will be transferred at sale. Without that continuity, an attractive initial estimate can leave important implementation questions unanswered.

This guide focuses on the ownership cycle. For the study scope, acquisition checklist and a reconciled basis example, start with AE Tax Advisors' hotel cost segregation service guide. Use the two together: one defines the study and handoff; the other helps prepare for later renovation and exit decisions.

Build one asset history from acquisition through sale

Keep the original purchase allocation, closing statement, depreciation schedule and study report together. Identify separately purchased furniture and equipment rather than assuming every item is part of the building account. Keep the legal owner and property address on each schedule so the records remain usable if the operating business and real estate sit in different entities.

Under the general depreciation system, nonresidential real property ordinarily has a 39-year recovery period; land is not depreciable. Other assets require their own classification and applicable depreciation analysis. IRS Publication 946 explains the framework. A hotel label does not put every furnishing, finish, mechanical system or site cost into a single shorter-life category.

Ask the analyst to reconcile the report total to the supported basis and explain how assets already listed separately are treated. Keep an unresolved-items list for missing invoices, estimated amounts and ownership questions. A percentage borrowed from another property is not a substitute for this reconciliation.

Give each PIP or renovation its own project record

A property improvement plan can contain furnishings, construction, technology, repairs and exterior work in one budget. Break the actual invoices into reviewable groups. Preserve contracts, change orders, credits and completion records, and identify the room blocks or areas affected. Distinguish an approved budget from the amount actually incurred.

Do not treat a renovation as proof that every removed component has a deductible remaining basis. Ask the preparer to assess available disposition treatment and its requirements. A component history helps establish the facts; it does not eliminate the need to evaluate the filing position.

Separate the report from the owner's deduction analysis

The study's asset classifications and the owner's ability to use deductions answer different questions. Rental-activity exceptions, material participation and at-risk limits require attention to the actual activity and taxpayer. IRS Publication 925 explains these distinctions. Neither the hotel name nor an owner-operator title proves that every applicable test is met.

Prepare an ownership chart, management agreement and description of who performs the work. Keep contemporaneous records of the owner's activities, dates and responsibilities. Tell the preparer about outside management, other investors and any related operating entity. For a partnership investment, request the information needed to understand the K-1 and owner-level review rather than assuming a projected entity deduction equals a usable personal deduction.

Model the sale by asset category

A hotel sale may transfer land, real property, equipment and other business assets together. Reconcile the proposed sale allocation to the asset schedule, supported basis and depreciation history. A single building-level sale price is not enough to determine every component's tax treatment.

For section 1245 property, ordinary-income recapture generally is limited to the smaller of gain or depreciation and amortization allowed or allowable. Section 1250 and unrecaptured section 1250 gain have different rules. Do not assume every dollar of depreciation is taxed at one flat rate. IRS Publication 544 explains the distinctions and reporting framework.

Ask for a written scenario that identifies the assumed sale date, selling costs, asset allocation, depreciation through disposition and the treatment of each category. Show federal and state estimates separately and identify the assumptions that could change. If you use the rental property sale calculator, treat its simplified result as a preparation tool, not a substitute for a hotel's complete asset and entity analysis.

Do not assume a 1031 exchange covers everything transferred

Section 1031 is limited to qualifying real property held for business or investment. Equipment and furnishings transferred with a hotel do not automatically qualify. Cash, other property and recapture rules can create recognized income. The IRS also describes circumstances in which section 1245 recapture can arise in an exchange even when other gain is deferred. Review the IRS exchange overview and Publication 544 with the preparer and exchange adviser.

Before signing transaction documents, give the advisers the proposed allocation, existing study, depreciation schedules and list of assets included in the deal. Ask them to distinguish real-property exchange eligibility from depreciation classification. Request a scenario showing what is expected to be deferred, what may be recognized and what additional information is still needed. Do not infer the outcome from the fact that a qualified intermediary is involved.

Compare study economics across your intended holding period

Put the study and implementation fees, timing of usable deductions and eventual disposition in the same model. Compare a shorter holding period with a longer one. Document the assumptions behind the income-tax rate, deduction usage and sale allocation instead of treating an early deduction as permanent cash savings.

The useful output is a decision you can review: commission a study, resolve missing records, scope a prior-year review, or retain the current approach. Ask who is responsible for each next action and what has to happen before the filing position is finalized.

Questions hotel owners should settle before engagement

Does a 1031 exchange defer every tax item in a hotel sale?

No. Section 1031 applies to qualifying real property, not automatically to every asset transferred with a hotel. Separately review equipment, furnishings, other nonqualifying property, cash and recapture. Ask the preparer and exchange adviser to evaluate the actual asset allocation before closing.

Does a hotel cost segregation study establish a standard reclassification percentage?

No. The result depends on the actual property, supported costs, ownership, use and asset classifications. A feasibility estimate is not a completed report. Avoid carrying a percentage from another hotel into your purchase or sale model as a verified result.

Does owning or managing a hotel prove that a loss is currently deductible?

No. Activity classification and the owner-specific participation and deduction limitations require separate analysis. Keep records of actual work and management responsibilities, and tell the preparer about other owners, entities and activities.

Does a renovation automatically create a deduction for every removed component?

No. Give the preparer the removal records and the old asset schedule so any available disposition treatment and required elections can be evaluated. A cost segregation report can help organize component information, but it does not by itself establish the filing result.

What should I request before commissioning a hotel study?

Request a written scope, required records, basis reconciliation, component schedules, reviewer responsibilities, fees and a clear handoff to the return preparer. Explain your renovation and sale plans before accepting a projection based only on the first year.
