1031 Exchanges for Short-Term Rentals: Use, Timing and Recapture

By AE Tax Advisors · Published 2026-07-22

Updated November 8, 2026.

A short-term rental can qualify for a Section 1031 exchange when the relinquished and replacement real property satisfy the business-or-investment holding requirement and the transaction meets the exchange rules. An Airbnb listing, a seven-day average stay or material participation does not establish exchange eligibility by itself.

Separate business use from vacation use

Document how the property was actually held and used: rental history, fair-market rent, personal stays and plans for the replacement property. Property held primarily for sale and property used only as a personal residence do not qualify as investment real estate merely because the owner expects appreciation.

Revenue Procedure 2008-16 provides a dwelling-unit safe harbor for the holding-purpose requirement. For relinquished property, own it for at least 24 months immediately before the exchange. In each of the two preceding 12-month periods, rent it at fair rental for at least 14 days and limit personal use to the greater of 14 days or 10% of fair-rental days.

The replacement dwelling has parallel requirements for the 24 months immediately after the exchange, including the rental and personal-use tests in each 12-month period. These are safe-harbor conditions, not a universal rule that every property held less than two years fails. Outside the safe harbor, the holding-purpose facts require analysis. Meeting this safe harbor does not waive the other exchange requirements.

Arrange the exchange before receiving sale proceeds

A standard deferred exchange typically uses a qualified intermediary and restrictions that prevent actual or constructive receipt of proceeds. Put the exchange documents in place before the sale closes. Finding another property after receiving unrestricted sale funds generally does not turn the sale into a deferred exchange.

Identify replacement property within 45 days. Receive it by the earlier of 180 days after the transfer or the due date, including extensions, of the return for the transfer year. An extension of the income-tax return may therefore matter. These deadlines are strict; specific IRS disaster relief can sometimes modify them, so a statement that they can never be extended is too broad.

Allocate the sale among real and personal property

Current Section 1031 generally covers qualifying real property, not furniture, linens or other personal property. An exchange can therefore contain both deferred real-estate gain and currently recognized amounts. Classification under the exchange rules must be reviewed rather than assuming every cost-segregation category has the same treatment.

Cash, nonqualifying property and net liability relief can create recognized gain. The amount and character require a complete closing statement and basis calculation. Buying a property with the same headline price does not, by itself, establish full deferral.

Cost segregation makes the gain analysis more detailed

Depreciation on Section 1245 assets can produce ordinary-income recapture. Unrecaptured Section 1250 gain for individuals can face a maximum 25% federal rate, but that category is not the same as saying all depreciation is ordinary income capped at 25%. Certain real-property recapture and exchange rules also require separate calculations.

For example, selling an STR with furniture and a building requires an allocation of proceeds and adjusted basis among the assets. A qualifying building exchange does not automatically defer gain on sold furniture. Bring the original study, depreciation ledger and sale allocation to the preparer before closing.

Coordinate the intermediary, closing professional and tax preparer around the actual dates, ownership and assets. See missed-depreciation corrections if the historic schedule is incomplete, or request a pre-sale tax review.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a vacation rental qualify for an exchange?

Yes, when its holding purpose and the transaction meet Section 1031. Personal use and the replacement property must be reviewed.

Is the deadline always 180 days?

The receipt deadline is generally the earlier of 180 days or the return due date including extensions. Identification generally has a separate 45-day deadline, subject to specific applicable relief.

Does exchanging the building defer every cost-segregation recapture amount?

No. Asset classification, sale allocations, nonqualifying property and recapture provisions require separate calculations.
