Real Estate Syndication K-1: Income, Losses and Distributions

By AE Tax Advisors · Published 2026-08-09 · Updated November 8, 2026.

A syndication K-1 reports allocated tax items, not simply the cash paid to an investor. Reconcile it with the partnership statements, investment history and owner-level limitations before using its income or loss on a return.

Separate allocation from cash

A partnership can allocate taxable income without distributing matching cash. A distribution can also have consequences different from an income allocation. Track both rather than treating all deposits as current taxable profit.

Maintain outside basis and debt records

The K-1 capital account is not automatically the investor's outside basis. Contributions, distributions, allocated items and liability changes can affect that basis. At-risk amounts follow a separate set of rules.

A loss reported by the partnership may be limited before the passive-activity analysis. Keep the limitation and carryforward schedules with each investment.

Determine the activity treatment

Many passive real estate investors receive passive items, but classification depends on the activity and investor's facts. Interest and dividends are generally portfolio income, not passive income simply because they appear on a K-1.

Real estate professional status does not automatically make every syndication loss usable. Participation, ownership form and other rules still need review.

Read the supporting statements

Identify depreciation information, qualified business income, credits, state allocations, foreign items and other codes. A tax preparer may need the full statement package, not just the first K-1 page.

Plan for timing and a sale

If a final K-1 is delayed, evaluate an extension and required tax payments using a supported estimate. A filing extension generally does not extend the payment deadline.

A sale of the property or partnership interest can produce multiple kinds of gain and changes in liabilities. Do not assume all depreciation-related gain has a single 25% rate or that every old suspended loss automatically offsets it.

Request a K-1 review with the entire package, contribution and distribution history, prior carryforwards and any sale documents.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the cash distribution the same as taxable income?

No. Allocated income and cash distributions are separate parts of the analysis.

Can I deduct every K-1 loss immediately?

No. Outside basis, at-risk, passive-activity and other limitations can apply.
