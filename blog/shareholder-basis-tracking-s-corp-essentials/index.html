S-Corp Shareholder Basis: Stock, Loans and Distributions

By AE Tax Advisors · Published 2026-07-03

Updated November 8, 2026.

An S corporation K-1 does not establish the shareholder's deductible loss or tax-free distribution amount by itself. The shareholder needs stock and debt basis records that reconcile across years and match the actual transactions.

Maintain stock and debt schedules separately

Stock basis generally starts from the applicable acquisition or contribution basis and changes with allocated income, distributions, nondeductible expenses and losses under the ordering rules. It is not the same as book equity or the corporation's bank balance.

Qualifying debt basis generally requires bona fide indebtedness running directly to the shareholder. Corporate borrowing and an unpaid personal guarantee do not automatically provide it. Actual payments, restructuring and the debt's substance require review.

Loss capacity and distribution capacity differ

Eligible stock and debt basis can support loss deductions, subject to their rules and later limitations. Debt basis does not make a shareholder distribution tax-free. Review stock basis for distributions and keep loan repayments separate from distributions.

For a simplified illustration, a shareholder has $20,000 stock basis and $30,000 qualifying debt basis before the year's loss adjustments. That does not mean a $50,000 distribution is tax-free. The distribution and loss ordering calculations must be performed using the separate schedules.

Calculate annual adjustments in order

Income, distributions, nondeductible expenses and losses do not simply net in any sequence the preparer chooses. Reduced debt basis can also require restoration under the net-increase rules in later years. Use Form 7203 and its instructions to reconcile the ordering and any relevant election.

A loan repayment after debt basis has been reduced can produce taxable income. Trace each note and repayment rather than combining everything in a single due-to-shareholder account.

Prior C corporation earnings add another layer

An S corporation with accumulated earnings and profits can have special distribution ordering and dividend consequences. The accumulated adjustments account is not the shareholder's stock basis, and an AAA balance is not an unlimited tax-free distribution allowance.

Shareholders who acquire stock at different times or prices can have different basis even when they receive identical K-1 allocations. Ownership transfers and final dispositions need shareholder-specific calculations.

Carry suspended losses forward by their cause

A loss blocked by basis is different from one blocked by at-risk, passive-activity or excess-business-loss rules. Restoring basis may allow an amount to reach the next limitation; it does not automatically make the entire carryforward usable. A sale of stock requires a specific review of remaining suspended losses.

Bring prior Forms 7203, all K-1s, contribution and distribution records, notes and repayments to a basis review. Reconcile opening balances before filing the next return.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can corporate bank debt give me S corporation debt basis?

Not automatically. Qualifying debt generally must run directly to the shareholder, and substance matters.

Can debt basis shelter a distribution?

No. Debt basis and stock basis have different roles; distributions generally require a stock-basis analysis.

Does a larger AAA balance mean all distributions are tax-free?

No. AAA and shareholder stock basis are different calculations, with special rules when earnings and profits exist.
