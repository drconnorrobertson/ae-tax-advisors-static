S-Corp Stock in Divorce: Basis, Distributions and Transfer Records

By AE Tax Advisors · Published 2026-08-03 · Updated November 8, 2026.

A divorce settlement can assign valuable S corporation stock without giving the receiving spouse a matching tax basis. Review the transfer and the corporation's records before signing. The settlement valuation and the shareholder's tax investment are separate numbers.

Establish the transfer rule and date

Section 1041 generally provides nonrecognition for qualifying transfers between spouses or former spouses incident to divorce, with carryover basis. Exceptions and timing requirements matter, including transfers involving a nonresident alien spouse and certain trusts.

Retain the decree, settlement, transfer agreement and effective ownership date. A qualifying transfer usually does not create a fair-market-value step-up merely because that value appears in the settlement.

Reconstruct stock basis

Begin with acquisition or contribution records and reconcile income, losses, deductions and distributions through the transfer. Each spouse's basis may differ even when they filed a joint return. Form 7203 is a shareholder-level computation.

For example, stock valued at $500,000 with $80,000 of adjusted basis is not equivalent to $500,000 cash for future tax purposes. A later sale requires its own gain computation; this example does not establish the final tax rate or liability.

Keep shareholder debt separate

A loan to the corporation is a separate asset from the shares. Determine who owns the debt, its tax basis, repayment terms and whether it transfers. A guarantee does not automatically create shareholder debt basis.

Review suspended items by limitation. Basis-limited losses, at-risk losses and passive losses can follow different rules. Do not assume that transferring shares releases every loss or that every carryforward transfers in the same way.

Review distributions and income allocation

The accumulated adjustments account is a corporate tax account, not a substitute for either spouse's stock basis. Distribution treatment depends on the applicable ordering rules, shareholder basis and any accumulated earnings and profits.

Coordinate distributions around the transfer with ownership and the settlement. Review the year's S corporation allocation rules and any available closing-of-the-books election instead of simply assigning all income to the person who received the cash.

Protect eligibility and evaluate alternatives

Confirm that the recipient and any trust are eligible S corporation shareholders. A redemption, direct transfer and other settlement assets can have different consequences. Prior C corporation history and potential built-in gains tax also affect the analysis.

The tax adviser and divorce attorney should reconcile stock value, basis, loans, carryforwards, distribution rights and future tax obligations in one settlement worksheet. Request a shareholder-basis review with the historical returns and proposed transaction documents.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a divorce valuation establish new stock basis?

Generally no for a qualifying Section 1041 transfer; carryover basis applies.

Is the corporate AAA account the same as my stock basis?

No. They are different computations and must be reconciled separately.
