Tax Strategy for E-Commerce Owners: Inventory, Payments and Warehouses

Published on August 10, 2026 | Updated November 6, 2026 | Business Owner Tax Planning | AE Tax Advisors

For an online business owner, cash in the bank, marketplace payouts and taxable profit are different measures. Before changing entity structure or buying a warehouse for a deduction, reconcile the sales and inventory records and map where the business actually operates. A tax review should connect those records to filing responsibilities.

Reconcile gross payments to revenue and cash

Use marketplace settlement reports, payment-processor records, refunds, chargebacks, platform fees and bank deposits together. Form 1099-K reports gross payment transactions; it is not a profit calculation or an additional sale to add on top of revenue already recorded. Request corrections for errors and document reconciliation differences rather than ignoring the form.

Current federal rules generally require third-party settlement organizations to report more than $20,000 in payments and more than 200 transactions. Payment-card reporting does not use that threshold, and forms can arrive below the threshold. These are information-reporting rules, not an income exclusion: report taxable business income whether or not a form arrives. State reporting requirements can differ.

Choose and consistently apply the inventory method

Under a conventional inventory method, stock purchased for resale generally enters cost of goods sold as sold, so buying unsold inventory does not create an immediate deduction merely because cash left the account. A qualifying small business may use Section 471(c), including the non-incidental materials-and-supplies method or permitted financial-statement/book treatment.

For tax years beginning in 2026, the Section 448(c) gross-receipts threshold is $32 million, measured using the prior three-year average with applicable aggregation and other rules. Tax shelters do not qualify for the small-business inventory exception merely by being below that amount. The non-incidental method generally treats inventory as used or consumed when provided to customers; the exception is not permission to deduct every year-end purchase immediately. Evaluate the actual method, payment timing and books.

Changing a method generally requires the applicable consent procedure, often Form 3115. Ask the preparer to assess eligibility, the adjustment and filing requirements before changing bookkeeping treatment. Retain evidence for damaged, obsolete or disposed stock; a management estimate alone does not establish a tax write-down.

Map fulfillment and sales-tax responsibilities state by state

Inventory locations, employees, direct website sales and marketplace sales can create different obligations. Marketplace collection does not, by itself, answer whether the seller must register, file returns or address income or franchise tax. Physical-presence and economic-nexus rules, thresholds, measurement periods and marketplace exclusions vary by state and change over time.

Export fulfillment-location history and sales by destination and channel. Have the responsible state-tax specialist document the applicable authority and effective dates before registering or deciding no filing is needed. Do not use a single national dollar or transaction threshold. The Texas Comptroller's marketplace guidance, linked below, illustrates why marketplace and direct-sales responsibilities must be separated; it is not a rule for other states.

Inventory and import-related costsOwnership, capitalization, applicable inventory method, returns and obsolete stock evidence.

Racking, conveyors and packing equipmentAsset classification, business use, useful life and eligibility for depreciation elections.

A cost segregation study must support classifications; warehouse components do not automatically produce a 30% or 40% reclassification. Land is not depreciable. Qualifying property acquired and placed in service after January 19, 2025 may receive 100% bonus, subject to acquisition rules and exclusions; Section 179 has its own limits. Financing a purchase does not by itself prevent depreciation, and paying cash does not establish eligibility.

For a personally owned warehouse leased to the business, review self-rental and grouping rules. A loss does not automatically offset operating income. Basis, at-risk, passive-loss and excess-business-loss limits, state conformity and future recapture can change the result. See commercial study scope before commissioning a report.

Model owner pay and retirement after the operating records reconcile

There is no universal profit threshold or salary band that makes an S election optimal. Compare reasonable compensation for actual services, payroll and state costs, eligibility, retirement contributions, QBI effects and the cash needed for stock and property. S corporation distributions do not substitute for reasonable wages for services.

A spouse needs genuine eligible work and compensation; adding a spouse does not automatically double contribution capacity. Staff and related-employer rules can affect whether an owner-only plan is available. Cash-balance contributions require actuarial design and funding analysis, not a promised age-based deduction. Use the S corporation retirement handoff and cash-balance feasibility guide rather than adding plan maximums together.

Retail activity may produce QBI, but the deduction still depends on qualified income, taxable income and applicable wage/property limits. Changing wages also changes profit, payroll taxes and retirement capacity. Model the combined result, not only one deduction.

Illustrative review: growth into a second fulfillment location

An owner has marketplace sales, direct website sales and a proposed warehouse acquisition. The bookkeeper reconciles settlements and inventory first. A state-tax specialist reviews actual storage and sales locations. The preparer compares entity and property alternatives using those records, and any study provider documents asset classifications. The owner receives a responsibility list for elections, filings and implementation. No savings amount is assumed in this example.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a Form 1099-K show taxable profit?

No. Reconcile reported gross payments with sales, returns, fees and other records. The reporting threshold does not exempt business income from tax.

Can a small seller deduct all inventory purchases immediately?

Not automatically. The permitted method, eligibility, timing and records control. Section 471(c) is not a blanket year-end stock-purchase deduction.

Does marketplace sales-tax collection resolve every state obligation?

No. Review direct sales, fulfillment locations, registration and filing rules, and income or franchise taxes under each relevant state authority.

Does an S election guarantee savings after a strong sales year?

No. Model profit, reasonable compensation, payroll costs, state treatment, retirement contributions and administrative work before deciding.
