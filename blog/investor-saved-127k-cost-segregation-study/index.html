Cost Segregation Planning Example: Deduction vs Tax Savings

By AE Tax Advisors · Published 2026-05-31

Updated November 8, 2026.

This is a planning illustration, not a verified client result. A cost segregation deduction is not itself tax savings, and a published savings number cannot substitute for the owner's actual return calculation.

Set the basis and allocation assumptions

Assume an income-producing property has $500,000 of supported depreciable basis after excluding land. Suppose a supportable study identifies $100,000 as eligible shorter-lived property. That assumed allocation illustrates the calculation; it is not a promised percentage for other properties.

Determine the correct classes and dates first. The building balance, improvements and separately purchased furnishings must reconcile without double counting. A study completed now does not grant current bonus treatment to every older asset.

Compare a deduction with the alternative

For a simplified $100,000 five-year asset, assume the half-year convention and 200% declining balance would produce $20,000 of first-year regular depreciation. If the same asset instead qualifies for a 100% bonus allowance, the first-year deduction would be $100,000. The additional first-year deduction in this comparison is $80,000.

If that entire additional amount is currently deductible at an assumed constant 32% rate, the simplified tax difference is $25,600. Changing the rate or loss usability changes the result. This comparison excludes the building's depreciation, other expenses, state differences and fees.

Test whether the owner can use it now

A passive rental loss may be suspended. A qualifying nonpassive activity still faces basis, at-risk and other limitations. Real estate professional status and rental material participation are separate questions; a spouse's job title or a new election does not establish both.

Model the exit and costs

Earlier depreciation can change adjusted basis and later gain. Section 1245 recapture can apply to personal-property components, while real-property gain has different rules. Compare the current benefit, study and filing cost, expected holding period and later tax rather than treating acceleration as permanent exclusion.

Request a property-specific model with the closing records, existing depreciation and owner tax returns. Use the study decision guide before commissioning work.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the example a documented client result?

No. It is an illustration using stated assumptions, not a representation of verified client savings.

Is a $100,000 deduction a $100,000 refund?

No. Tax rates, other income, limitations and prior payments determine the tax and refund effect.

Is the benefit permanently tax-free?

Not necessarily. Accelerated deductions can affect later basis and recapture.
