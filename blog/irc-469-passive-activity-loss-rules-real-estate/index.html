IRC 469 for Real Estate: Classify Losses Before Deducting Them

By AE Tax Advisors · Published 2026-07-15

Updated November 8, 2026.

Section 469 determines whether losses are passive and when passive losses can be deducted. It is one layer in the return calculation, not a promise that an accounting loss reduces wages. Start with the taxpayer, the activity and the current-year facts, then reconcile prior suspended amounts.

1. Identify the activity and the applicable taxpayer rules

Rental activity is generally passive even when the owner works in it. The regulations contain exceptions, including qualifying short customer-use periods. For a nonrental trade or business, material participation is central. Special rules for corporations, publicly traded partnerships, self-rentals and recharacterized income mean that an individual-rental example cannot be applied to every entity.

Review grouping history before combining results. A grouping changes the unit tested and can affect a future disposition; it is not simply a spreadsheet preference. The special all-rental-real-estate election for a qualifying real estate professional is distinct from the general grouping rules.

2. Check the limited rental-real-estate allowance

An eligible individual who actively participates can qualify for an allowance of up to $25,000 of rental-real-estate loss against nonpassive income. Ownership, filing status and participation conditions matter. Under the general rule, the allowance phases out between $100,000 and $150,000 of modified adjusted gross income.

Married-filing-separately taxpayers have different limits and living-apart conditions; the general $25,000 figures cannot be applied indiscriminately. Use the specific MAGI calculation, not wages alone, when testing the phaseout. A taxpayer above the allowance range may still use passive income or another applicable rule; the phaseout does not erase every potential deduction.

3. Evaluate real estate professional status where relevant

For an individual to qualify, more than half of personal services in trades or businesses must be in real-property trades or businesses in which the taxpayer materially participates, and those services must exceed 750 hours. Employee work generally does not count unless the statutory ownership exception is met. One spouse must independently satisfy these qualification tests on a joint return.

Qualification removes the automatic rental-passive rule for rental real estate; it does not prove material participation in each activity. Spousal hours can count for material participation, which is a separate test. An all-rental election can affect that analysis but is not mandatory in the first qualifying year: review the regulation, election timing and any available late-election relief.

4. Distinguish current losses from old suspended losses

Ordinary passive losses generally carry forward and may offset passive income under the applicable rules. Interest, dividends and other portfolio income usually are not passive income for this purpose. Becoming nonpassive does not automatically release all prior passive losses. Former-passive-activity rules may allow old losses against current income from that same activity, with remaining losses continuing under the rules.

A fully taxable disposition of the entire activity to an unrelated person can trigger the Section 469(g) release rules. An installment sale, related-party transfer, gift, death or sale of one property from a grouped activity can produce different results. Do not treat every closing as an unrestricted release of all carryforwards.

5. Reconcile the other limitation schedules

Basis and at-risk restrictions generally must be considered before passive-loss limits. Excess-business-loss limits and state differences can also affect the usable result. Retain separate schedules so an amount suspended under one rule is not mistaken for an amount available under another.

Participation can be established by reasonable means under the regulations. Keep credible records of tasks, dates, time, properties and supporting communications; there is no universal required daily-log format and no guarantee that a reconstructed estimate will be accepted.

Continue with the participation tests, STR classification and investor loss-planning guide. Bring the prior returns and carryforward schedules for review.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does working 750 hours make rental losses deductible?

No. REPS requires more than 750 qualifying hours and the more-than-half test; rental participation and other loss limitations must also be satisfied.

Does becoming nonpassive release all old losses?

No. Former-passive-activity rules and separate carryforward calculations apply.

Does any property sale release suspended losses?

No. The entire-activity, taxable-disposition and unrelated-party requirements matter, along with special rules for particular transfers.
