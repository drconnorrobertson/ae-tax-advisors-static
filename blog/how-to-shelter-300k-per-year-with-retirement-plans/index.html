Can a Retirement Plan Support $300,000 of Contributions?

By AE Tax Advisors · Published 2026-08-20

Updated November 8, 2026.

A business owner may be able to fund a large retirement contribution through coordinated defined contribution and defined benefit plans. A $300,000 target is a design question, not a standard entitlement based on income or age.

Ask for a proposal using the actual census

Provide compensation, ages, service, ownership, existing plans and asset balances for the owner and staff. The actuary needs the promised benefits and funding assumptions; the administrator needs coverage and nondiscrimination facts. An estimate based only on the owner's age can omit substantial obligations.

Apply each limit, then the combined deduction rules

For 2026, the regular 401(k) elective-deferral limit is $24,500 and annual additions generally cannot exceed $72,000 before eligible catch-up. Contributions must also fit compensation and plan terms. The $290,000 defined benefit annual benefit limit is not an annual contribution cap.

For illustration, a proposal might target $72,000 of defined contribution additions and $228,000 of defined benefit funding. Adding those numbers gives $300,000, but the arithmetic does not establish that the design is permitted or fully deductible. The actuary and tax preparer must apply Section 404 combined-plan rules, PBGC-related treatment where relevant, compensation limits and employee requirements.

Distinguish contributions from current deductions

Roth deferrals and Roth catch-up do not create current income-tax deductions. For affected participants, 2026 catch-up treatment depends on prior-year employer wages and the applicable Roth rules. Employer deductions also depend on funding and statutory limits. Do not multiply every proposed contribution by a marginal rate without classifying it.

Stress-test future funding

Request a range for required and permitted contributions, not only a first-year target. Model lower revenue, investment underperformance, compensation changes and staff growth. A defined benefit promise can create funding obligations even when the current business year is difficult.

Use a written implementation calendar

Plan adoption, employee elections, deposits, employer funding and annual filings follow different deadlines. Ask the plan professionals to identify each applicable date. Do not assume that a December transfer alone establishes every deduction or that all plan choices remain available after year end.

Compare defined benefit and 401(k) structures and bring the written proposal to a tax review. The decision should show the owner's benefit, staff costs, fees, funding commitments and return treatment.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can every high-income owner contribute $300,000?

No. Compensation, age, service, plan design, assets, staff and funding rules determine what is permitted.

Is a $300,000 contribution always a $300,000 current deduction?

No. Roth treatment and employer deduction limits can produce a different current deduction.

Do the separate plan limits eliminate combined deduction restrictions?

No. The employer must also apply the relevant Section 404 rules.
