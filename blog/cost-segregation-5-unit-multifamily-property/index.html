Cost Segregation for a 5-Unit Multifamily Property

Published on August 10, 2026 | Cost Segregation | AE Tax Advisors Team

Compare a five-unit rental cost segregation study using supported basis, current loss usability, the study fee and expected resale timing.

When does a five-unit property justify a study?

Compare supported depreciable basis, the value of any accelerated deductions you can actually use, the full study and filing cost, and the expected holding period. The number of units or a minimum basis by itself does not determine the result.

Build a component and ownership inventory

Separate land, the building, separately purchased equipment and improvements. Gather the closing statement, business-use and rental records, invoices, photographs and any prior depreciation schedules. The report must support each classification and reconcile total costs to the recorded basis.

Worked example: first-year fee comparison

Suppose a five-unit rental has $900,000 of supported depreciable basis. Assume January placement in service, 27.5-year GDS treatment for the remaining residential building, $180,000 of independently supported bonus-eligible components, and 100% bonus eligibility. Without the study, the modeled January mid-month deduction is about $31,364. With the study, it is about $205,091. The additional first-year deduction is about $173,727.

Test deduction use and the sale separately

If 20% of that incremental deduction can be used at a hypothetical 32% federal marginal rate, the first-year federal tax reduction is about $11,119. Against a $5,000 assumed study and filing fee, that is about $6,119 of current federal cash benefit. If none is usable, the same fee is a first-year cash cost. Neither scenario includes state taxes, later depreciation differences, or sale and recapture effects. The example is illustrative.

For a property already on the return

Gather the original asset schedule, prior elections, improvements and disposition records. Ask the preparer which correction method applies, what documentation supports it, and when any adjustment is usable. A cost segregation report alone does not establish eligibility for Form 3115.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a five-unit rental too small for cost segregation?

There is no universal cutoff. Compare supported asset basis and classifications with study costs, the amount of deduction usable and expected sale timing.

How much of a five-unit building can be reclassified?

The amount depends on the property and its documented costs and functions. Do not apply a generic percentage or assume every listed component qualifies.

Can I use the deduction against salary?

It depends on the activity classification, participation, personal use and other loss rules. Identify which losses can be used currently before relying on a cash-tax estimate.

Discuss study scope and pricing

Bring a property summary and a list of the records available. Use the discovery call to discuss study requirements and the written scope before providing detailed records.
