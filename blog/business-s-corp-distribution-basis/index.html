My S-corp distribution exceeded stock basis after filing. What should I correct?

Published September 22, 2026 | Business Tax | AE Tax Advisors

First determine whether the corporation reported the distribution incorrectly or the shareholder calculated stock basis incorrectly. If the Form 1120-S and Schedule K-1 are right but your filed Form 7203 or Form 1040 omitted gain from a nondividend distribution above stock basis, the shareholder return may need correction; an entity amendment is not automatic. If the corporation omitted or misstated the distribution or another K-1 item, correct the entity return and affected K-1 first, then evaluate the shareholder return. An S-corp loan balance or debt basis does not make an excess distribution tax-free.

If you found this after filing, bring the corporation's distribution ledger, all affected K-1s, your stock-and-debt-basis rollforward, and both filed returns. AE can identify which filing is wrong before a second amendment creates a mismatch.

Rebuild stock basis before calling the payment taxable

The shareholder, not the corporation, is responsible for calculating stock and debt basis. The K-1 reports distributions but does not tell you how much is taxable. Under the IRS stock-and-debt-basis guidance, stock basis generally increases for income before it decreases for distributions, nondeductible expenses, and losses. Check the opening balance against the preceding year's ending balance and account for contributions, tax-exempt income, prior suspended losses, and any prior correction. Debt basis can matter for loss deductions, but the IRS expressly excludes it from the distribution taxability test.

For an S corporation without accumulated C-corporation earnings and profits, a nondividend distribution generally reduces stock basis to zero; the excess is gain reported on Form 8949 and Schedule D under the Form 7203 instructions. The stock's holding period and capital-asset status affect the rate. If the corporation has accumulated earnings and profits from a C-corporation period, Section 1368's AAA/dividend ordering can change the answer; use AE's earnings-and-profits ordering guide before treating the entire excess as capital gain. A property distribution has separate entity and shareholder consequences, addressed in the appreciated-property transfer guide.

If one shareholder received more cash than another, basis is not the only question. The unequal-distributions and S-election review separates the payment's tax character from any preferential rights in the governing documents before the company signs Form 1120-S.

When a corrected K-1 arrives after the personal return was filed, follow the separate corrected-K-1 filing sequence. If the S corporation's K-1 remains wrong and the shareholder cannot obtain a correction, the shareholder K-1 instructions discuss notifying the corporation and Form 8082 for inconsistent treatment. This is a professional-review path, not a default substitute for asking the corporation to fix its return.

Worked case: cash exceeded stock basis, but debt basis existed

Assume a shareholder began the year with $20,000 stock basis, was allocated $10,000 of current-year income, and received $50,000 of genuine nondividend cash distributions. With no other adjustments or accumulated E&P, stock basis before distributions is $30,000. The first $30,000 reduces stock basis to zero; the remaining $20,000 is potentially reportable gain on Form 8949 and Schedule D. A separate $100,000 basis in a direct shareholder loan to the company does not shelter that $20,000 distribution. If the filed K-1 correctly showed $50,000 in box 16, code D but the shareholder left the gain off Form 1040, review the shareholder's Form 7203, Form 1040-X, and state filing; do not amend the corporation solely to make the excess disappear.

Now change one fact: suppose the corporation's filed K-1 said $35,000 because it omitted a $15,000 cash transfer. The entity's records and actual payment must first be reconciled. If it was truly another distribution, the corporation may need an amended 1120-S and K-1 showing the corrected amount, and the shareholder then recomputes Form 7203 and the personal return. The answer differs if that transfer was a documented loan repayment or an accountable-plan reimbursement. The Form 1040-X instructions govern the individual amendment.

Document packet and failure points

  • Filed Form 1120-S and every affected K-1, including box 16 codes; filed Forms 1040, 7203, 8949, and Schedule D; prior-year amended returns and carryforward schedules.
  • Corporate bank statements and general ledger for each owner payment; stock purchase and contribution records; shareholder-loan notes and repayment schedules; any accumulated E&P and AAA history.
  • A year-by-year stock-basis rollforward showing income, distributions, nondeductible expenses, losses, and the point where basis reached zero.

Do not use cash in the corporation as proof of stock basis; do not use debt basis to absorb a distribution; do not fix a shareholder-only error by inventing a new K-1; and do not reflexively classify every withdrawal as a distribution. An omission can affect a later year's opening basis and loss carryovers even when the original year's tax change appears small. If an amendment is needed, check filing deadlines, state conformity, interest, and downstream years with the S-corporation amendment guide.
