Veterinary Practice Tax Planning: Owner Pay, Property & Exit

AE Tax Advisors Team · Published August 10, 2026 · Updated November 6, 2026

For an owner of a profitable veterinary practice and its clinic building, tax planning should connect four decisions: how operating income is reported, how employees and owner compensation are handled, how property deductions are used and what happens at sale. Choosing each strategy independently can create a large deduction in one entity that the owner cannot currently use.

For engagement scope and the first conversation, see tax advisory for veterinary practice owners. This guide covers decisions to resolve with the practice CPA, property preparer, attorney and plan administrator.

Entity selection begins with compensation and the eventual transaction

An S corporation may be suitable, but it is not the default answer for every practice. Model reasonable compensation for shareholder services, payroll and compliance costs, benefits, state rules, ownership eligibility and the exit. Distributions do not replace required reasonable wages. The IRS shareholder-compensation guidance explains the service-business considerations.

For a C corporation, compare the possible corporation-level tax on an asset sale and owner-level consequences of distributing proceeds. A conversion to S status does not instantly erase built-in gains exposure; the five-year recognition-period rules and other conversion consequences require specific review. Asset versus stock structure also depends on the buyer and transaction, not a blanket rule that every consolidator buys the same way.

Financed equipment: tax ownership and timing still matter

A purchase financed with debt may create depreciable basis beyond the down payment when the practice is the tax owner. Financing does not establish that the entire cost is deductible immediately. Confirm business use, placed-in-service timing, recovery period, acquisition rules, elections and eligibility for bonus depreciation or Section 179. Section 179 has separate limits, including a business-income limitation.

A contract called a lease may be a true lease or a purchase for tax purposes; financial-statement classification alone does not decide the tax result. Ask for the agreement and apply the tax ownership rules. Equipment bought to obtain a deduction still requires a business case and cash-flow forecast. See IRS Publication 946.

Retirement plans must account for staff and related businesses

For 2026, the regular employee 401(k) deferral limit is $24,500 and the defined-contribution annual additions limit is $72,000, subject to applicable compensation and plan rules; eligible catch-up contributions are separate. Those figures are ceilings, not guaranteed owner deductions. Confirm the year using IRS retirement limits.

Before choosing a safe harbor, profit-sharing or cash balance arrangement, provide employee ages, compensation, service and ownership across related businesses. A defined-benefit design needs actuarial calculations and funding review. It cannot promise every owner the same contribution based only on age. Model staff costs, administrator fees, ongoing funding, changes in practice profit and an anticipated sale. See controlled-group and affiliated-service-group rules and Publication 560.

Coordinate the clinic building with operating income

A study may identify shorter-life assets in a qualifying building or renovation, but there is no universal reclassification percentage. Review clinic asset documentation and QIP before applying a deduction estimate.

When a property LLC rents to the practice, self-rental rules can treat net rent as nonpassive without making losses nonpassive. Basis, at-risk, passive-loss and excess-business-loss rules may limit current use. A grouping election is not an automatic cure. The same practice-and-building structure can produce different results for owners with different participation, ownership and prior elections.

Selling the practice while retaining the building

Keeping the property can provide rental income, but it also leaves tenant-credit, vacancy, maintenance, refinancing and concentration risk. Compare a property sale with retaining it under the proposed lease. Review actual guarantees, rent escalations, capital expenditure obligations and expected after-tax cash flow rather than assuming a consolidator lease is risk-free or always the better asset.

After the owner stops participating in the operating practice, rental treatment must be evaluated under the new facts. Passive income may absorb suspended passive losses, but a practice sale does not by itself release all losses attached to a retained building. Grouped activities, retained interests and disposition rules can change the answer. Publication 925 explains the general passive-activity framework.

Negotiate purchase price allocation before the documents are final

In an applicable asset acquisition, buyer and seller generally report the allocation using Form 8594. Asset classes can have different consequences: equipment can trigger depreciation recapture, goodwill treatment depends on ownership and facts, and a covenant not to compete can create ordinary-income consequences for the seller. Allocation must reflect the applicable rules and supportable values, not an arbitrary shift designed solely to reduce tax.

Coordinate the letter of intent, allocation schedule, earnout, consulting or employment agreement and any retained property lease. See IRS Form 8594 instructions and obtain transaction-specific advice before signing. Removing appreciated real estate from an existing corporation can itself trigger tax; separating it is not a routine last-minute step.

A practical planning sequence

  1. Reconcile practice books, owner payroll, property rent and fixed assets.
  2. Map entity ownership, existing elections, employees and current advisers.
  3. Model the next purchase or renovation with owner-level deduction limits.
  4. Compare retirement designs with staff funding and business cash needs.
  5. Model practice and property exit alternatives before committing to transaction terms.
  6. Assign each election, return, payroll change and legal document to the responsible professional.

Questions before engaging AE

Is an S corporation always best for a veterinary practice?

No. Compare reasonable compensation, employees, benefits, compliance, state rules, ownership and eventual sale consequences before selecting or changing tax status.

Can financed clinic equipment be deducted immediately?

Financing may support depreciable basis beyond the down payment, but immediate deduction depends on tax ownership, business use, timing, eligibility, elections and deduction limits.

Should I retain the clinic building when selling the practice?

Compare the actual lease and tenant risk, financing, sale proceeds and after-tax cash flow. Retaining real estate is a separate investment decision, not a universal tax recommendation.

Will a study and grouping election guarantee usable losses?

No. Study classifications, participation, grouping eligibility, prior elections and multiple owner-level limitations need review. A projected deduction is not a guaranteed refund.
