Section 469 Passive Activity Loss Rules for Real Estate Investors

Published on August 25, 2026 | Real Estate Tax Strategy | AE Tax Advisors

Updated November 8, 2026.

A rental tax loss does not automatically offset wages or business income. Section 469 generally limits passive losses to passive income, with specific exceptions and carryforward rules. First calculate the allowable expense and apply any basis and at-risk restrictions; then test passive treatment and other owner-level limits.

Rental loss, passive income and portfolio income

Rental activities are generally passive even when the owner spends substantial time managing them. A qualifying exception can change the result. Interest, dividends and most investment gains are portfolio income, not a general source of passive income against which rental losses may be used.

For example, assume a rental has a $30,000 loss after calculating its allowed expenses, and the owner has sufficient basis and at-risk amount. If the activity is passive and there is no passive income or applicable exception, that loss is generally suspended. The existence of $200,000 in wages does not make the rental deduction currently usable.

The $25,000 active-participation allowance

An eligible owner who actively participates in rental real estate may use a limited special allowance against nonpassive income. Active participation is a lower standard than material participation and generally requires at least a 10% ownership interest. The usual $25,000 allowance phases out as modified AGI rises from $100,000 to $150,000. Married-filing-separately rules differ; see the allowance eligibility guide before assuming the full amount.

Real estate professional status has two stages

First, the taxpayer must satisfy the more-than-750-hours and more-than-half-of-personal-services tests for qualifying real property trades or businesses. Second, the taxpayer must materially participate in the relevant rental activities. Status alone does not make every rental loss deductible.

For a joint return, one spouse must independently meet the real estate professional thresholds; spouses' participation can be combined for material participation. A valid election to treat rental real estate interests as one activity may help the participation analysis, but has continuing consequences. Review the rental grouping election and prior returns first.

Short-term rentals use a separate test

An activity with average customer use of seven days or less generally falls outside the rental-activity definition for Section 469. Other regulatory exceptions may apply. That does not automatically make a loss nonpassive: the owner must still establish material participation. Personal use, basis, at-risk and other loss rules remain relevant.

Keep booking records and evidence of actual services, including other people's participation where the test requires comparison. Use the short-term rental tax guide for that decision. The Section 469 customer-use test does not itself decide the building's depreciation recovery period or whether Schedule C applies.

Track suspended losses by activity

Maintain separate carryforward records and reconcile them to Form 8582 and the underlying return schedules. Becoming nonpassive in a later year does not automatically free all old losses: former-passive-activity rules can allow prior losses against current income from that activity. Keep basis-limited losses separate from at-risk and passive carryforwards.

Sale, gift and death have different results

  • Qualifying sale: disposing of the entire activity interest in a fully taxable transaction to an unrelated buyer generally releases suspended passive losses, subject to the applicable rules.
  • Partial sale or installment sale: special rules affect the amount and timing. A property sale is not automatically a disposition of an entire grouped activity.
  • Gift: suspended passive losses are not transferred as the recipient's deductible carryforward. Instead, the transferred interest's basis is generally increased by those losses.
  • Death: the final-return deduction is limited by the applicable increase in the recipient's basis. A basis adjustment can absorb some or all of the suspended loss.

A Section 1031 exchange generally does not qualify as the fully taxable disposition needed for full loss release. Model the transaction and activity grouping before relying on a deduction.

NIIT is a separate calculation

Real estate professional status and material participation do not automatically exempt rent from the 3.8% net investment income tax. Whether the income is derived in the ordinary course of a trade or business also matters. The Form 8960 rules include a separate real-estate-professional safe harbor. Evaluate that test instead of assuming a Section 469 result settles NIIT.

Before claiming a cost-segregation loss

A study can change depreciation timing, but it does not override owner-level loss restrictions. Gather the purchase and debt documents, depreciation schedules, participation evidence, prior elections and all suspended-loss workpapers. Ask the preparer to show where the proposed deduction stops at each limitation. AE can review those records before you assume the study produces a current refund.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can passive rental losses offset W-2 wages?

Generally not, unless a specific exception applies. Real estate professional status with material participation or a qualifying special allowance can change the result; other loss limits still apply.

Are all short-term rental losses nonpassive?

No. A rental-definition exception and material participation are separate tests. Personal-use and owner-level loss limits must also be evaluated.

Do suspended losses pass to someone who receives a property gift?

They do not simply become the recipient’s deductible passive carryforward. The transferred interest’s basis generally increases by the unused passive losses.

Does real estate professional status eliminate NIIT?

Not automatically. The ordinary-course trade-or-business requirement and applicable Form 8960 rules need separate analysis.

Does a cost segregation study guarantee a current deduction?

No. Depreciation classification and owner-level deduction usability are different questions. Apply the relevant basis, at-risk, passive and excess-business-loss rules.
