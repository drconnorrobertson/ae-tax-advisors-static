1031 Exchange Rules 2026: Deadlines, Property and Tax Planning

By AE Tax Advisors · Updated November 13, 2026.

A 1031 exchange can defer eligible gain when business or investment real estate is exchanged for qualifying replacement real estate. Start planning before the sale closes. The identification deadline, receipt deadline, taxpayer identity and control of proceeds each need their own review.

  • Like-kind property
  • Boot, basis and recapture

The 45-day and 180-day exchange deadlines

Standard deferred-exchange planning calendar
MilestoneRequired action
Before the sale closesReview the exchange agreement, qualified intermediary, ownership and closing instructions.
Within 45 days after transferDeliver a signed written identification that clearly describes the replacement property to a permitted exchange party.
By the receipt deadlineReceive replacement property by the earlier of 180 days after transfer or the return due date, including extensions.

The clocks overlap; there are not 180 additional days after identification. A failed purchase does not restart them. Ordinary weekends and holidays do not provide an automatic extension. Check any applicable IRS disaster postponement separately. See IRS Publication 544.

Identification limits

The usual options are up to three properties regardless of value, or any number whose total fair market value does not exceed 200% of the transferred property's value. The alternative 95% rule requires acquisition of at least 95% of the total value identified. Ask the intermediary to check the list and valuation before delivery.

A late-year sale example

For an illustrative December 1, 2026 transfer, day 45 is January 15, 2027 and day 180 is May 30, 2027. If the taxpayer's return deadline otherwise comes first, a timely extension may be needed to preserve the full receipt period. Confirm the taxpayer's actual filing deadline and any relief; this example does not establish eligibility.

Which real estate is like-kind?

Business or investment real estate can qualify even when the replacement has a different use: investment land and a rental building need not look alike. A personal residence or property held primarily for sale requires a different analysis. U.S. and foreign real estate are not like-kind to each other. Read the IRS exchange guidance.

For mixed personal and investment use, collect occupancy records and ask the preparer to allocate the transaction. Do not assume an Airbnb listing establishes investment intent. Identify furniture and other assets separately; federal Section 1031 treatment is limited to qualifying real property.

Intermediary, title and related-party review

A qualified intermediary is a commonly used safe harbor for deferred exchanges. Arrange the agreement and restrictions on access to proceeds before closing. Receiving the full sale proceeds and buying another property later is generally a sale and purchase, rather than a qualifying deferred exchange. Partial receipt can create a partially taxable transaction.

Compare seller and buyer federal tax identities before forming a new LLC or changing ownership. A disregarded LLC and a partnership are different tax situations. Related parties and recent agents can raise disqualified-person issues; family or commonly owned entities also require separate related-party analysis. Have the intermediary and preparer review the structure, rather than relying on the name on a deed alone.

Cash, debt relief, replacement basis and depreciation recapture

Cash or other non-like-kind property received, and net debt relief after applicable offsets, can produce taxable boot. Buying at a similar price does not settle that calculation. Reconcile the closing statements, liabilities, cash added and exchange expenses using Form 8824 instructions.

Simplified cash-boot example: assume no debt, expenses or recapture adjustments, $500,000 of exchange value and $300,000 of adjusted basis. If the investor receives $450,000 of replacement real estate and $50,000 cash, realized gain is $200,000. The simplified result is $50,000 recognized and $150,000 deferred, leaving $300,000 of replacement basis. Actual transactions require a complete calculation.

Depreciation does not disappear. Section 1245 components can create ordinary-income recapture even where real property receives exchange treatment. Keep asset-level depreciation and any prior cost segregation report. Review both recognized gain and replacement basis before projecting future deductions.

State-specific considerations for an interstate exchange

List the relinquished-property state, replacement-property state and owner's residence. Ask the preparer to document conformity, state basis differences, deferred-gain tracking, withholding and future filing obligations. A federal deferral is not a complete state filing plan.

California example: exchanging California real estate for out-of-state property can require Form FTB 3840 and continuing annual reporting of California-sourced deferred gain until recognition. Moving away does not by itself eliminate that tracking obligation. Consult the FTB's Form 3840 instructions and check the applicable filing-year version.

When a standard deferred exchange needs a different plan

If you must buy first or fund improvements before receiving replacement property, review reverse or improvement-exchange arrangements before signing. Title parking, financing, construction delivery and safe-harbor requirements need specialist coordination. Work completed after you receive title does not simply become exchange value because exchange cash paid the bill.

Compare an exchange with a taxable sale using the same property assumptions. Include liquidity, financing, reinvestment quality, current tax, future basis, study costs and expected sale timing. A cost segregation study does not automatically offset sale tax: asset eligibility and usable losses matter. A replacement property's carryover basis also needs separate treatment from new investment.

Bring a complete transaction file to the review

  1. Before listing: ownership chart, tax returns, original purchase closing statement and current depreciation schedule.
  2. Before sale closing: intermediary agreement, planned buyer and seller identities, related-party details and instructions restricting proceeds.
  3. During identification: signed property list, valuation support, delivery evidence and financing contingencies.
  4. Before replacement closing: debt comparison, cash movement ledger, expense allocation and asset classifications.
  5. For filing: both closing statements, dates, Form 8824 workpapers, replacement basis and state reporting schedule.

Frequently asked questions

Does a 1031 exchange defer depreciation recapture?

A qualifying exchange can defer gain associated with prior depreciation, but it does not automatically defer every recapture amount. Cash, debt relief, asset classifications and the replacement property can affect current recognition. Reconcile the depreciation schedule and Form 8824, including any Section 1245 components identified through cost segregation.

Can I 1031 exchange a short-term rental?

Yes, provided it is genuinely held for investment or business use rather than personal enjoyment. Significant personal use undermines the position, and the exchange must be structured before closing on the sale, not afterward.

What are the 1031 exchange deadlines?

Two deadlines run from the closing of the relinquished property: 45 calendar days to identify replacement property in writing, and 180 calendar days to close on it. The 180-day period also cannot extend past the due date of your return including extensions, so filing an extension is often essential.

What property qualifies for a 1031 exchange?

Real property held for investment or productive use in a trade or business can qualify. A rental house can potentially be exchanged for a warehouse or investment land. Personal residences and property held primarily for sale do not qualify. U.S. real property is not like-kind to foreign real property; foreign property is not categorically excluded from every exchange.

1031 exchange tax planning guide: planning questions

Updated November 13, 2026. Use these answers to prepare a review of your records with AE Tax Advisors.

What are the 1031 exchange rules for 2026?

Identify replacement real estate in writing within 45 days. Receive it within 180 days or your return due date including extensions, whichever comes first. Arrange the intermediary before closing; review related-party and ownership issues.

Can I defer capital gains on real estate?

A qualifying exchange of business or investment real property can postpone gain. A cash sale followed by a purchase generally does not qualify. Personal residences and property held primarily for sale need different treatment.

How does a 1031 exchange work to avoid taxes?

It generally defers eligible gain rather than erasing it. Replacement-property basis preserves deferred gain. Cash received or net debt relief can create taxable boot; model closing costs, financing and basis before committing.

What happens to depreciation recapture in a 1031 exchange?

Do not assume every depreciation-related amount disappears. Recapture and recognized gain depend on exchanged assets and boot. Reconcile the old depreciation schedule, replacement assets and Form 8824 calculation, particularly after cost segregation.

What counts as like-kind exchange real estate?

Qualifying business or investment real estate may differ in use or improvements. Review the real-property definition for separately classified components. U.S. real estate is not like-kind to foreign real estate.

Bring the latest filed returns, ownership details and documents supporting the transaction or deduction. Identify deadlines and prior elections before implementing a change. Discuss your planning questions with AE Tax Advisors.
