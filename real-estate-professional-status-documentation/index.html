Can I Claim Real Estate Professional Status If I Use a Property Manager?

AE Tax Advisors · Tax Planning & Strategy

Yes, hiring a property manager does not automatically disqualify you. But the manager's hours are not your hours. To use rental losses as nonpassive, one taxpayer must meet the real-estate-professional tests with that person's own qualifying services and also materially participate in the rental activity or a validly grouped rental activity. A manager's work may make the comparison-based participation tests harder to meet, so reconcile both people's roles before claiming a W-2 offset.

Before filing, AE can compare the owner's service log, property manager's scope and hours, any grouping election, and the return's loss calculation. Bring the manager agreement, dated owner records, and draft Schedule E or entity K-1.

Why Documentation Determines REPS Audit Outcomes

The outcome depends on the actual services, activity grouping, and material-participation test—not on a job title or a target hour total alone. The IRS passive-activity guidance requires more than 750 hours and more than half of the taxpayer's personal services in real property trades or businesses in which that taxpayer materially participates. It separately requires material participation for rental losses to be nonpassive. Good evidence lets the preparer apply these separate gates rather than simply adding every real-estate-related calendar entry.

Contemporaneous vs. Reconstructed Documentation

The IRS does not require a contemporaneous daily log as the only proof. Form 8582 instructions allow participation to be established by any reasonable means, including appointment books, calendars, and narrative summaries identifying the work and approximate time. A dated log is still a strong practical choice because it can be matched to independent evidence. An unsupported estimate prepared later is weaker than a specific account backed by emails, invoices, or site records.

Useful corroboration can include daily calendar entries, emails about actual tenant or contractor decisions, timestamped site photographs, invoices showing work supervised, lease negotiations, and inspection checklists. A bank statement or passive review of a property report does not by itself establish hours of qualifying participation; the task and the owner's operational role matter.

What changes when a manager handles the rentals?

  1. REPS qualification: Count the owner's own personal services in qualifying real property trades or businesses in which the owner materially participates. Do not add a third-party manager's hours or combine spouses' hours to reach the owner's 750-hour and more-than-half tests. Form 8582 instructions state the spouse rule and describe a valid election to treat rental real estate interests as one activity for the material-participation test.
  2. Rental material participation: Test the rental activity separately. More than 500 owner hours in the activity is one route. The more-than-100-hours-and-no-other-individual-more test compares the owner with the manager and all other individuals. The facts-and-circumstances test restricts management time where another person is paid to manage or spends more management hours. A manager does not prevent every possible test, but it changes which tests can be supported.
  3. Investment versus operating work: Merely reading financial statements, monitoring returns, or preparing analyses for the owner's own investment is generally not participation unless the owner is directly involved in day-to-day management or operations. Record actual decisions and services, not just a total number of hours.

Worked example. An owner reports 780 hours of hands-on work spread across three rentals and 200 hours in another business. The property manager reports 900 hours. The manager's 900 hours cannot be added to the owner's 780, and the owner cannot pass a “more than anyone else” test based on the grouped total if another individual spent more. If the owner made a valid election to treat the three rentals as one activity and the 780 hours truly qualify as participation in it, the more-than-500-hours material-participation test may be available even with the manager. Without a valid grouping election, each rental generally needs its own analysis; 260 owner hours per property would not satisfy the 500-hour test by itself. The owner must still verify the REPS more-than-half test, other material-participation conditions, and loss limitations before deducting losses against nonpassive income.

Building Audit-Ready Documentation Systems

Systematic Calendar Entries

Review calendar each evening noting time on real property activities. Five-minute daily task creates continuous trail. Example: "Property B renovation: 3 hours, Tenant interview Property C: 1.5 hours, Contractor approval Property D: 1 hour." Brief but contemporaneous.

Email Organization

Create email folders per property. Archive all communications related to management, acquisitions, maintenance, tenants, renovations. Over a year, 30-100+ emails per property demonstrate regular involvement. Email trail of approvals and decisions shows active management.

Timestamped Photographs

Smartphones can timestamp site photographs. Use photos to corroborate a dated inspection or repair record, not as a substitute for a service description and reasonable time estimate. Twelve visits or 120 photos do not, by themselves, prove 750 qualifying hours or material participation.

Contractor Approval Trail

Email written approvals rather than verbal approval. Creates documentation trail showing your review and decision-making. A five-email trail on single renovation documents involvement in quotes, approval, modifications, and final payment.

Property Inspection System

Conduct quarterly property inspections (four annually per property). Create inspection checklists covering exterior, roof, systems, grounds, units. Document with dates and notes. Four annual inspections demonstrate regular oversight.

What IRS Auditors Examine

During audits, IRS agents evaluate:

  • Contemporaneity: Were records created during activity or reconstructed?
  • Specificity: Are activities linked to properties or vague?
  • Consistency: Does documentation support claimed hours throughout year?
  • Materiality: Is involvement regular and continuous or sporadic?
  • Reasonableness: Do claimed hours align with properties and income?
  • Return consistency: Does documentation align with tax return claims?

Common Documentation Failures

  • Vague calendar entries ("property management: 20 hours")
  • Time logs created in December for entire prior year (reconstructed)
  • Claimed hours exceeding available hours in period
  • No documentation of prior participation for historical tests
  • Inconsistency between documentation and return claims
  • Treating real-estate-professional qualification as an election or assuming it automatically makes every rental nonpassive
  • Failure to retain the rental-real-estate grouping election statement if one was actually made
  • Adding a manager's hours to the owner's hours or ignoring the manager in tests that compare individuals
  • Counting investment-only review of financial reports as day-to-day operating work

Documentation Retention

Keep support for as long as the related return years, suspended-loss carryforwards, grouping election, and property basis may need substantiation. A fixed seven-year discard rule can be too short when losses carry into later years. Organize records by property, activity, taxpayer, and tax year so both the qualification and rental-level participation tests can be reconstructed.

Next Steps

If you currently claim REPS while a manager operates the property, compare your own dated work with the manager's service records and identify the exact material-participation test used on the return. If a grouping election or suspended loss is involved, review the election statement and prior returns before changing a filing position. AE can reconcile that file to the current-year return.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the requirements for real estate professional status?

Two tests must be met in the same year under IRC Section 469(c)(7): more than half of all personal services performed in all trades or businesses must be in real property trades or businesses in which you materially participate, and you must perform more than 750 hours in those businesses.

What documentation does the IRS accept for hours?

The IRS allows participation to be proved by any reasonable means. Daily contemporaneous logs are not legally required if other reasonable evidence establishes the work and approximate time. Specific calendars, emails, invoices, and dated task notes make a later summary easier to verify.

What happens to suspended passive losses?

They carry forward indefinitely and become deductible when the activity produces income, when other passive income is available, or when the activity's character changes. They are also released in full on a qualifying disposition of the entire interest.

Can spouses combine hours to qualify?

No. The qualification tests apply to each spouse individually and cannot be combined, even on a joint return. One spouse must satisfy both tests alone. Once one spouse qualifies, spousal participation may then be combined when testing material participation in the rental activities.

Do my property manager's hours count toward my 750-hour REPS test?

No. The manager's work is not your personal service. Your own qualifying hours must satisfy the more-than-750-hour and more-than-half tests, and you must separately establish material participation in the rental activity or a validly grouped rental activity. The manager's hours can matter when comparing your participation with other individuals.
