S-Corp Reasonable Compensation: Document Your Owner Salary

A defensible owner salary starts with the services you perform and the evidence supporting comparable pay. Document the decision before treating distributions as a substitute for wages.

Book a Call

What reasonable compensation means for an S-corp owner

The IRS requires S corporations to pay reasonable compensation to shareholder-employees for their services before non-wage distributions. The analysis depends on the work performed and the source of gross receipts. The IRS can reclassify payments as wages. See the IRS compensation guidance.

A universal salary percentage cannot capture different owner roles, hours, staffing and businesses. A tax-saving target or an online benchmark alone is not a compensation analysis.

Build an owner role inventory

  • List the professional, production, management, sales and administrative work you actually perform.
  • Estimate time by role and identify changes during the year.
  • Record qualifications, relevant experience and responsibilities.
  • Describe what employees, contractors and other owners do.
  • Identify revenue supported by your services versus staff or other business resources.

For multiple businesses, keep the work and compensation records for each entity consistent. Use the related-company owner-pay guide when responsibilities cross entity boundaries.

Compare pay evidence with the actual job

Use comparable roles and document the source, date, geography, hours and responsibilities behind the comparison. Explain adjustments when the comparison differs from the owner’s work. Preserve the analysis rather than only recording the final number.

EvidenceWhat to document
Role and time recordThe services provided, hours and responsibilities during the year.
Pay comparisonWhy the selected jobs and geographic market are comparable.
Business operationsHow other workers and business assets contribute to receipts.
Implementation recordApproved compensation, payroll reporting and any later changes.

Revisit the file when hours, staff, duties or business operations materially change.

Coordinate salary, distributions and benefits

The planning model should show payroll administration, distributions, benefits and the owner’s broader return. A lower salary is not automatically the best overall result. Before changing payroll, identify who approves the change and who handles reporting.

Discuss business-owner tax advisory if salary decisions need to be coordinated with entity structure, retirement plans or rental holdings. Review owner health-insurance reporting when the W-2 and benefit records need reconciliation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the 60/40 salary rule an IRS safe harbor?

No universal salary-to-distribution ratio establishes reasonable compensation. Evaluate the services and supporting evidence.

Can the salary be based only on profit?

Profit alone does not establish the value of the owner’s work. Consider duties, time, responsibilities and comparable pay evidence.

Does a compensation analysis prevent an audit?

No. It documents the reasoning and evidence; it cannot guarantee an audit outcome.

Should I revisit salary every year?

Review whether duties, time, staffing, compensation evidence or business operations changed and update the file when needed.
