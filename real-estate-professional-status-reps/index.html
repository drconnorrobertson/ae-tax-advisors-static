Real Estate Professional Status REPS

AE Tax Advisors · Tax Planning & Strategy

Can a Business Owner Qualify for Real Estate Professional Status?

Real Estate Professional Status (REPS) under Section 469(c)(7) can remove the rule that treats rental real estate as passive regardless of participation. It does not itself make every property nonpassive or guarantee a current deduction. A profitable operating-business owner should first test the available working hours, then evaluate material participation and loss limits before paying for a depreciation study.

Two Annual Qualification Tests, Then a Rental Activity Test

For the same tax year, one individual must perform more than 750 hours of services in real property trades or businesses in which that individual materially participates, and those services must exceed half of all personal services that individual performs in trades or businesses. Exactly 750 hours or exactly half does not qualify.

Real property trades or businesses include development, redevelopment, construction, reconstruction, acquisition, conversion, rental, operation, management, leasing, and brokerage. The work must satisfy the applicable trade-or-business and participation requirements; simply owning property is not enough. Hours spent running a separate operating business belong in the total-personal-services denominator.

On a joint return, one spouse must independently meet both REPS qualification tests. Spouses cannot combine their hours to meet the more-than-750-hour or more-than-half requirements. A spouse's participation does count when testing material participation in an activity, even if the spouse has no ownership interest or the couple does not file jointly.

Real property employee services count toward the qualifying REPS hours only if the employee is a more-than-5% owner of the employer. Employment hours still count in total personal services for the more-than-half comparison even when they do not qualify for the real property numerator. Investor work, such as reviewing financial statements or monitoring an investment, generally does not count as participation unless directly involved in the activity's day-to-day management or operations. A task label alone does not establish qualifying work.

Material Participation Is a Separate Requirement

After establishing REPS, test material participation for each rental real estate activity unless a valid election treats all rental real estate interests as one activity. More than 500 hours is Test 1. More than 100 hours and at least as much participation as every other individual is Test 3. Five of the preceding ten years is Test 5. The personal service activity test is not a rental-specific three-of-five-years rule. Review all seven tests and their restrictions in the material participation documentation guide.

A property manager does not automatically disqualify an owner, but the manager's work can matter under Tests 2, 3, and 7. Approving leases or major expenditures may show active participation for the limited rental allowance without proving material participation. An owner's regular involvement must satisfy an actual test.

The Optional Rental Real Estate Election

Under Regulation 1.469-9(g), a qualifying taxpayer may elect to treat all interests in rental real estate as one activity. Without that election, each interest is generally a separate activity. This is an optional election, not a condition for REPS and not permission to combine any business or short-term rental with long-term rentals.

The election can be made in a qualifying year by attaching the required statement to the original return. It generally remains binding in future qualifying years, including after intervening years without REPS. Revocation requires a material change in facts and circumstances and the prescribed statement; an unfavorable tax result alone does not qualify. Review existing elections before changing an activity boundary or planning a sale.

Exactly 750 hours across four properties would fail the REPS hours test even with an election. More than 750 hours still does not establish the more-than-half test, and rental material participation must be evaluated using the appropriate activity boundaries.

Real Application Example

An engineer earning $450,000 owns five single-family rentals generating $80,000 combined annual loss. Documented activities: acquisition evaluation (120 hours), renovation oversight (240 hours), tenant selection (80 hours), property management meetings (60 hours), financial analysis (140 hours), contractor coordination (200 hours), inspections (75 hours), board meetings (50 hours). Total: 965 hours. As percentage of combined hours: 965 / (2,200 + 965) = 30.5 percent, exceeding 50 percent threshold. He qualifies for REPS and deducts full $80,000 loss, saving $29,600 federal tax plus state tax.

Records That Support the Qualification

Publication 925 permits any reasonable method that establishes the services performed and approximate hours, including calendars, appointment books, or a supported narrative. Contemporaneous daily time reports are not mandatory, but timely records backed by booking messages, work orders, invoices, and emails are stronger than unsupported year-end estimates. Do not double-count overlapping time.

Separate each spouse's work, each activity, employee services, investor tasks, and other business hours. Record the task and duration, not just a round weekly estimate. For an owner with a full-time operating role, evidence for the non-real-estate working hours is as important as evidence for the rental work. Support qualification with the work performed, rather than assuming ownership or a particular number of properties is enough.

The $25,000 Allowance Is Not a High-Earner Entitlement

The special allowance can permit up to $25,000 of eligible rental real estate losses for taxpayers who actively participate. For the usual eligible individual, it phases out between $100,000 and $150,000 of modified adjusted gross income; special married-filing-separately rules apply. It is not a general cap on all passive losses. Passive income can support deductions separately. REPS plus material participation can make a rental loss nonpassive, but does not create an unlimited write-off.

A nonpassive classification is not an unlimited deduction. Check applicable entity basis limits first, then at-risk limits and passive activity rules, followed by the excess business loss limitation under Section 461(l). Personal-use restrictions and other deduction-specific rules can also limit the result.

Depreciation Is Not Automatically a Tax Saving

Illustrative arithmetic: if $1.5 million is the depreciable building basis after excluding land, and the property qualifies for a 27.5-year residential rental recovery period, dividing basis by 27.5 gives approximately $54,545 before first-year or final-year conventions. A $1.5 million purchase price is not necessarily $1.5 million of depreciable basis. Rent, other expenses, placed-in-service timing, property classification, and loss limitations determine the actual deduction and tax effect. No assumed 40% federal tax rate or cumulative savings promise follows from that division.

Suspended Losses and the Next Review

REPS does not automatically release prior suspended passive losses. If an activity becomes nonpassive, its prior suspended losses can offset current net income from that same activity, with any balance remaining subject to passive loss rules. A qualifying fully taxable disposition of the entire interest to an unrelated person follows separate rules.

For a focused review, bring both spouses' work calendars, property and entity schedules, prior Forms 8582 and 6198, basis records, existing grouping elections, management agreements, and depreciation schedules. Ask the advisor to identify which year and activity qualify, which losses are usable now, and which remain carried forward. Read the REPS documentation guide before preparing the records.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the requirements for real estate professional status?

For the same tax year, one individual must perform more than 750 hours of services in real property trades or businesses in which that individual materially participates, and those services must exceed half of all personal services that individual performs in trades or businesses. Exactly 750 hours or exactly half does not qualify. Qualification alone does not make every rental nonpassive; material participation must also be established for each rental activity or valid elected rental group.

Can spouses combine their hours for REPS?

On a joint return, one spouse must independently meet both REPS qualification tests. Spouses cannot combine their hours to meet the more-than-750-hour or more-than-half requirements. A spouse's participation does count when testing material participation in an activity, even if the spouse has no ownership interest or the couple does not file jointly.

What happens to suspended passive losses?

Becoming nonpassive does not automatically release prior suspended passive losses. Prior losses from a former passive activity can offset current net income from that same activity; the remaining carryover is treated as a passive loss under the usual rules. A fully taxable disposition of the entire interest to an unrelated person has separate release rules. Other loss limits still apply.

What documentation does the IRS accept for hours?

Publication 925 permits any reasonable method that establishes the services performed and approximate hours, including calendars, appointment books, or a supported narrative. Contemporaneous daily time reports are not mandatory, but timely records backed by booking messages, work orders, invoices, and emails are stronger than unsupported year-end estimates. Do not double-count overlapping time.
