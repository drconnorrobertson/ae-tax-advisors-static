Will Distributing an Appreciated Partnership Building Trigger Gain?

AE Tax Advisors · Tax Planning & Strategy

Usually no—not merely because the building appreciated or its fair market value exceeds the receiving partner's outside basis. Under the general Section 731 rule, a partner normally recognizes gain on a distribution only when money distributed exceeds adjusted outside basis; gain on noncash property is generally deferred until a later sale or disposition. But an owner approving a deed must separately model cash, liability shifts treated as money, marketable securities, contributed-property lookbacks, and any disproportionate shift of hot assets. The receiving partner's basis in the building is a separate Section 732 calculation; it is not automatically fair market value.

This decision matters to a real-estate partnership owner or managing member moving a materially appreciated building before refinancing, an ownership exit, or filing Form 1065. AE can review the property and partner basis schedules, debt allocation, contribution history, proposed deed, and connected entity/owner returns before the transfer or a correction.

Start with the actual distribution, not the building's market value

Ask whether the owner remains a partner after the transfer (current distribution) or the property liquidates the entire interest. In either case, do not treat appreciation alone as automatic current gain. A partnership generally recognizes no gain or loss on a distribution to a partner, and the distributee partner generally defers gain on noncash property. That is different from an S corporation distributing appreciated real estate, where corporate-level gain rules can apply. Verify that the transaction really is a partnership distribution rather than a sale, disguised sale, or compensation arrangement.

Run the money and debt test before the deed

Compare money distributed with the recipient's adjusted outside basis immediately before the distribution. “Money” can include cash, certain marketable securities under Section 731(c), and a decrease in that partner's share of partnership liabilities under Section 752(b). The latter can occur without cash arriving in the owner's bank account. The debt allocation must be recomputed from the loan documents, guarantees, ownership and profit-sharing terms—not guessed from the mortgage balance. A partnership debt-basis review is especially important when a refinance or release of a guarantee accompanies the deed.

For a simple cash-only comparison, a partner with $300,000 of outside basis who receives $400,000 cash recognizes $100,000 of Section 731 gain, generally capital in character. An equivalent $400,000 decrease in the partner's share of liabilities can create a similar deemed-money issue, but only after all simultaneous liability increases and other basis changes are computed. Neither example means the building itself generated $100,000 of gain.

Worked property example: basis moves even when gain does not

Assume a partnership owns a rental building worth $2 million with $700,000 adjusted tax basis. It distributes the building to a continuing partner whose adjusted outside basis is $300,000 immediately before the transfer. Assume no cash, no debt shift, no marketable securities, no recent contributed property, and no other special rule. The building's $2 million value does not itself create immediate Section 731 gain. Under Section 732's current-distribution limitation, the partner's basis in the building is generally limited to $300,000 rather than the partnership's $700,000 basis or the $2 million value; the remaining outside basis is reduced accordingly. A later sale and depreciation must use that supported carryover basis, not the appraisal value. If the transfer liquidates the entire interest, Section 732 applies a different basis-allocation method; do not reuse the current-distribution worksheet.

Screen exceptions before calling the transfer tax-free

  • Recent contributions: A distribution of a partner's contributed appreciated property to another partner within seven years can make the original contributor recognize built-in gain under Section 704(c)(1)(B). A contributing partner receiving other property within the lookback period may have Section 737 gain.
  • Hot-asset shifts: A disproportionate distribution involving unrealized receivables or inventory can be treated as an exchange under Section 751(b); do not say every property distribution creates ordinary income. See AE's Section 751 guide for the character analysis.
  • Marketable securities: Section 731(c) can treat certain distributed securities as money; it is not the rule that creates deemed cash from liability relief.
  • Basis adjustments: A Section 754 election can affect partnership basis adjustments under Section 734(b), but it does not automatically set the recipient's building basis at fair market value. Compute recipient basis under Section 732 and any applicable partner-specific adjustments separately.

Documents and filing checks

Gather the operating agreement, deed and transfer documents, original contribution dates and values, property tax-basis and depreciation ledgers, a current appraisal, loan/guarantee documents, tax-basis capital and outside-basis rollforwards for every affected partner, any Section 754 election, and prior Forms 1065/K-1. Reconcile who bears each liability before and after closing and whether the transfer changes economic interests in receivables or inventory. The recipient should check the current Form 7217 instructions for property-distribution basis reporting; the partnership should coordinate the Form 1065 and K-1 disclosures with the owner's return. A book-entry distribution or a deed alone is not a tax-basis workpaper.

Sources and next step

The federal rules and examples are in IRS Publication 541, the Form 1065 instructions, and the partner K-1 instructions. State and local transfer taxes, loan covenants, depreciation recapture on a later disposition, and whether a special rule applies require transaction-specific review. Before filing a deed or return, have the transfer and both sides of the basis rollforward reviewed together.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is an appreciated building distribution taxable just because its value exceeds my outside basis?

Generally no. A distribution of noncash partnership property ordinarily does not trigger gain merely because fair market value exceeds outside basis. Cash, deemed money from reduced liability share, marketable securities, and special distribution rules must be tested separately.

Does a Section 754 election give the receiving partner fair-market-value basis?

No. The recipient generally computes basis in the distributed property under Section 732 using partnership inside basis and remaining outside basis. A Section 754 election may affect basis adjustments elsewhere, but it is not an automatic fair-market-value basis for the recipient.

Can a debt change make a property distribution taxable without cash?

Yes. A decrease in a partner's share of partnership liabilities is generally treated as a distribution of money. If total money distributed exceeds the partner's adjusted outside basis, Section 731 gain may result even when the partner receives no cash.
