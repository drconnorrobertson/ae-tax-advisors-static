IRS Audit Defense for Business Owners: Representation Scope

By AE Tax Advisors Team October 3, 2026 Updated November 6, 2026

An IRS examination creates two immediate business decisions: who is responsible for the response, and what work must be completed by the stated deadline. For owners with operating companies and real estate, the issues may cross personal returns, entity filings, payroll and depreciation schedules. Start with the actual notice rather than a generic list of supposed audit triggers.

Read the notice and confirm responsibility

The IRS generally initiates an audit by mail. Check the taxpayer, tax year, requested information, contact instructions and response date. A balance-due or matching notice is not necessarily an examination. Verify unexpected communications through official IRS channels. Do not assume booking a consultation, forwarding a letter or uploading records means a professional has accepted responsibility for the deadline.

Ask the prospective representative to confirm acceptance, scope and immediate next steps in writing. If more time is needed, discuss the applicable procedure promptly; requesting help does not automatically extend a deadline.

Define the representation engagement

QuestionWhat to put in writing
Which taxpayer and matters?Individual or entity, return type, years, notices and issues included.
Which stage?Records review, correspondence examination, office or field examination, Appeals, collection or court work.
Who communicates?Named representative, taxpayer responsibilities and approval of submissions.
What documents are needed?Record requests, reconciliation work, specialist reports and a process for missing evidence.
What does it cost?Agreed fee basis, additional work, outside counsel or specialist costs and the end of support.

Correspondence audits are handled through mail; other examinations involve an IRS office or a field meeting. The notice and later requests determine what needs review. Scope can change. Agree how additional issues or years will be handled before assuming they are included.

Choose the professional for the issues

Enrolled agents, CPAs and attorneys have unlimited representation rights before the IRS. EAs qualify through the examination or qualifying former IRS employment; CPAs and attorneys are licensed under their respective jurisdictions. A credential does not establish experience with every business or property issue. Ask about relevant examination work and who will handle technical questions.

Confirm the assigned professional and scope. IRS representation rights do not automatically authorize practice before the U.S. Tax Court. For litigation, criminal exposure or questions of legal privilege, discuss qualified counsel and applicable admission requirements. Do not assume every conversation with a tax preparer has attorney-client privilege.

Authorize the right person without confusing authorization with scope

Form 2848 authorizes an eligible individual to represent the taxpayer for specified tax matters and periods. Confirm the appropriate taxpayer and authorization when personal and entity returns are involved. The form does not itself establish the fee, deliverables or engagement acceptance. An information-access authorization alone is not equivalent to representation authority.

Build a usable business and property evidence file

  • Keep the full notice and all correspondence, including submission and delivery records.
  • Reconcile the filed return to books, bank records and supporting schedules.
  • For property issues, gather closing statements, land allocations, fixed-asset schedules, study reports and placed-in-service evidence.
  • For owner compensation, provide payroll filings, compensation analysis and the underlying work facts.
  • For rental participation positions, collect contemporaneous activity records and source documents; identify gaps honestly.
  • Track requested items, responsible people and deadlines. Retain copies of what was submitted.

Answer requests accurately and discuss the relevance and scope of requested records with the representative. Documentation supports a position; it does not make a strategy immune from examination. A cost-segregation report provider's study support may not include defense of the owner's eligibility, return preparation or representation.

Understand proposed changes and next-stage decisions

An examination can end with no change, agreed changes or disagreement. Ask the representative to explain the proposed tax, penalties, interest and supporting analysis before signing an agreement. Appeal and court rights depend on the notice and procedure; follow the actual deadline and obtain advice before it expires. Penalty relief has specific requirements and is not automatic.

Assessment limitation periods are separate from the date an audit begins. The general period is often three years, with exceptions and possible extensions; the taxpayer, return, filing date, payment history and issue matter. Have the representative calculate the applicable periods rather than relying on a generic website deadline.

Discuss availability and the accepted scope

Use a free discovery call to discuss the notice and whether AE can accept the work. Review published pricing and confirm representation fees separately where applicable. An introductory call does not guarantee acceptance or suspend an IRS deadline. Share only a general description initially; send confidential records through the agreed secure workflow.

Questions to ask before hiring

Does hiring a representative guarantee a favorable audit result?

No. The result depends on the law, facts, records and issues examined. Representation can help organize the response and explain the position, but it cannot guarantee no adjustment, penalty relief or acceptance.

Is audit support included in a tax-planning fee?

Check the signed agreement. Document support, examination representation, Appeals, collection work and litigation can have different scopes, professionals and fees. Do not assume a planning engagement covers every stage.

What should I provide before the first representation meeting?

Provide the complete notice, its date and deadline, the affected returns and schedules, prior correspondence and a summary of disputed items through the agreed secure process. Identify missing records without inventing or backdating them.

Does Form 2848 cover every entity and year?

No. The authorization identifies the taxpayer, representative, tax matters and periods covered. Confirm the correct authorization for each relevant taxpayer and matter; it does not replace the service agreement.
