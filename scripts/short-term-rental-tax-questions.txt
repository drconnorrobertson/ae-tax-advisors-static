# Guest stays and services
average-customer-use-multiple-property-classes|How do you calculate average customer use when an activity rents more than one class of property?|Compute the average for each class, then weight each result by that class’s share of gross rental income for the activity. Confirm the applicable activity and property classifications first.|Assume the activity has two supported classes. One averages four days and generates 75% of gross rental income. The other averages 12 days and generates 25%. The weighted result is six days.|Should the owner average the two class averages equally?|Only if their gross rental income shares are equal. Retain both the use-period calculations and the gross-income reconciliation for each class.|material-participation-short-term-rental-7-day-rule|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p925
significant-services-routine-versus-guest-specific|Does hiring a cleaner qualify a short-term rental for the 30-day personal-services exception?|The exception requires average customer use of 30 days or less and significant personal services. Common-area cleaning and routine repairs similar to those of long-term rentals are excluded from the analysis.|A property averages 18-day stays. Compare routine corridor cleaning with separately documented guest-specific services. Review frequency, labor and relative service value before deciding whether the services are significant.|Does the fact that a contractor performs a service settle the question?|No. Document who performs it, its scope and frequency, and the facts that support its value relative to the rental charge.|str-vs-ltr-tax-treatment|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p925

# Material participation and reporting records
manager-hours-individual-comparison|Do you add everyone’s hours together for the more-than-100-hours material participation test?|The comparison is to the participation of each other individual. Your participation must exceed 100 hours and be at least as much as that of any other individual under this particular test.|An owner has 130 qualifying hours. Two other people each have 90. Their combined 180 hours do not defeat this test by themselves. But one property manager with 140 hours would change the comparison.|What if the management company gives only a company-wide total?|Ask for records identifying the individuals who actually worked on the activity and the relevant time period. If you cannot substantiate this test, review whether another participation test applies.|str-material-participation|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p925

Log the tasks separately so the reviewer can evaluate the type of work and involvement.|Does creating a detailed time log establish that the tasks count?|A time log helps substantiate dates and duration.
