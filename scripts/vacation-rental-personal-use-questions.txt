# Counting guest and personal days
vacation-rental-fourteen-day-ten-percent-threshold|How does the 14-day or 10% personal-use test work for a vacation rental?|A dwelling unit is used as a home for this rule if personal days exceed the greater of 14 days or 10% of the days rented at a fair price. Use the actual rental calendar and classify overlapping days under the home-use test.|A cabin has 180 fair rental days and 18 personal days. The threshold is 18 days. Exactly 18 does not exceed the threshold. With 19 personal days and the same rental days, the cabin meets the home-use test.|Is this the same as the rule for renting a home fewer than 15 days?|The personal-day test first determines whether the property was used as a home. The separate under-15-day rule then looks at actual rental days for a dwelling used as a home.|short-term-rental-tax-strategy|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527
vacant-bookable-days-vacation-rental-expense-split|Do vacant nights count toward the rental share of mixed-use vacation home expenses?|Under the IRS day-based allocation, days available for booking but never rented are excluded from rental-use days. Compare days actually rented at a fair price with the days the unit was used.|A cottage has 72 fair rental days, 18 personal days and 30 vacant days offered for rent. The rental share of a $900 shared utility bill is 72 divided by 90, or $720, before applying any deduction limits.|Should I remove the open dates from my records?|Keep them in the calendar with a vacant status. owner-stay-during-paid-vacation-rental-overlap|What if I use my vacation rental on days a guest also pays fair rent?|Under the IRS approach, a fair-rent day with personal use counts as rental when dividing expenses. Count that same day as personal when testing whether you used the dwelling as a home.|A cottage has 90 fair-rent days, including 3 when the owner also uses the unit, plus 12 other personal days. The expense allocation uses 90 rental days out of 102 days. The home-use test uses 87 rental days and 15 personal days, so the property exceeds the 14-day threshold.|Why record overlapping use separately?|A single total can lose the information needed for one of the calculations. Keep separate columns for fair-rent occupancy and owner use.|short-term-rental-tax-strategy|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527
below-market-friends-vacation-rental-days|Does a below-market stay by a friend count as personal use of my vacation rental?|Days rented at less than a fair rental price count as personal use for these rules, even when the guest has no family relationship to you. Assuming $80 is substantially below fair rent for those dates, those four days count as personal use.|Does a lower rate during the off-season automatically mean personal use?|Compare similar properties for the same season, location, size, condition and amenities. # Limiting and carrying over rental expenses

Carry the disallowed amount forward as expenses of the same property and apply the limits that govern those expenses.|After allocating shared costs and completing Worksheet 5-1, an owner has $1,600 of limited depreciation expenses. The owner carries the $1,600 for that property into the next year and tests it there.|Does all of the carryover become deductible if I stop using the property personally?|The limitation continues to apply to the carried expenses even if the dwelling is no longer used as a home. Keep the carryover by category and property.|short-term-rental-tax-strategy|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527
