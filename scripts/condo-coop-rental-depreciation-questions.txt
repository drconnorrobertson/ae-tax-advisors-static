# Rental condominiums
condo-rental-basis-share-common-land|Does a rental condo have a land allocation for depreciation?|A condo owner has a share of the common elements, including land. Allocate the purchase basis between land and depreciable property using support for their relative values before classifying the depreciable portion.|An investor buys a condo for $360,000. Supported values allocate 12% of the purchase basis to the interest in land. The allocation leaves $43,200 in land and $316,800 for depreciable property before any other adjustments.|What should I give a cost segregation analyst?|Supply the closing statement, declaration, unit and common-element rights, valuation records and a list of assets conveyed. Reconcile all allocations to the purchase basis.|real-estate-depreciation|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527
rental-condo-common-area-maintenance-dues|Can I deduct condo dues for maintaining the common areas of my rental?|For a condo rented to others, dues or assessments paid for maintenance of common elements can generally be rental expenses. Look at how the association used the funds if the statement combines operating expenses and improvements.|A fully rented condo has $4,200 in annual dues documented as common-area maintenance. The association separately bills $2,000 for an elevator modernization. Review the $4,200 as maintenance and track the $2,000 separately as an improvement assessment.|What if the billing statement only shows a total?|Ask the association for a breakdown supported by the budget, assessment notice or project records before classifying the payment.|rental-property-tax-planning|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527
depreciate-condo-special-assessment-improvement|Can I depreciate a special assessment for a rental condo improvement?|A special assessment for an improvement is a capital item. A rental condo owner may be able to recover their share of the qualifying improvement cost through depreciation.|An owner pays an $8,000 assessment for a common roof replacement in March. The association puts the completed roof into use in September. Record the share of project cost and the September service date for depreciation review.|What if one assessment funded multiple projects?|Ask for the association's project-by-project allocation and completion dates so each improvement can be evaluated on its own facts.|real-estate-depreciation|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527
# Rental cooperatives and municipal assessments
rented-coop-apartment-stock-depreciation|How do I calculate depreciation when I rent out a co-op apartment?|A tenant-stockholder depreciates the cooperative stock using an allocated share of the depreciation on qualifying property owned by the corporation. Exclude depreciation for rentable space that tenant-stockholders cannot live in, and apply the limit based on the rental share of adjusted stock basis.|If qualifying annual depreciation after excluding that space is $300,000 and the owner holds 60 of 6,000 outstanding shares, the preliminary stock depreciation is $3,000 before the stock basis limit.|What records support the computation?|Obtain the share count, corporate property and depreciation data, and your stock basis records. A stock purchase after the initial offering may require additional debt and basis information.|real-estate-depreciation|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527
coop-capital-assessment-stock-basis|How does a co-op capital assessment affect the basis of rental stock?|Amounts earmarked for a capital asset or charged to the cooperative's capital account generally increase stock basis. The capital amount is limited to the excess of payments to the cooperative over the owner's share of its mortgage interest and real estate taxes.|A tenant-stockholder pays $7,500 to the co-op, including $2,400 earmarked for a new roof. The owner's share of interest and taxes is $3,100, leaving a $4,400 limit. The documented roof amount of $2,400 is within the limit and is added to stock basis.|Do co-op mortgage principal assessments get the same treatment as interest?|Principal charged to the capital account is a capital item. Keep the corporation's allocation so principal and interest can be evaluated separately.|real-estate-depreciation|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527
municipal-sidewalk-assessment-rental-basis|How do I treat a city sidewalk assessment on my rental property's tax bill?|The portion assessed for a local improvement that tends to increase property value is added to basis and is neither deductible as tax nor depreciable. Separately stated maintenance, repair or related interest charges can generally be deductible if otherwise eligible.|The city bills a landlord $2,700 for new sidewalk construction and separately identifies $300 for sidewalk maintenance. Add $2,700 to property basis and review the $300 as a rental deduction.|How should I record the charge for a cost segregation study?|Keep the city assessment and a separate ledger entry for the nondepreciable local improvement. Match that entry to the overall property basis reconciliation.|real-estate-depreciation|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527
