# Refinance proceeds and interest
mixed-use-rental-refinance-interest|Can a rental refinance create both rental and personal interest?|Yes. Trace the borrowed funds to their use. Interest on the portion used for personal spending generally does not qualify as a rental expense simply because the loan is secured by the rental.|A new $180,000 loan replaces $150,000 of outstanding rental debt and provides $30,000 used for personal purchases. Track the two uses separately and apply the applicable allocation rules as interest and principal are paid.|What support should the owner keep?|Retain the old payoff statement, new settlement statement and disbursement records, then reconcile the outstanding balances by use.|rental-property-tax-planning|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527
rental-refinance-points-same-lender|What happens to remaining rental mortgage points when you refinance with the same lender?|Remaining points generally are not deductible immediately in a same-lender refinance. They may be deductible over the term of the new loan under the applicable rules.|The original schedule shows $800 of unamortized points at refinancing. If the refinance is with the same lender, reconcile the $800 to the new loan and review the correct timing separately from the treatment of new fees.|Does it matter if the loan was sold or serviced elsewhere?|Identify the actual lender for the old and new loans using the agreements and payoff documents. The name on a servicing statement alone may not settle the question.|rental-property-tax-planning|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527
personal-loan-used-for-rental-project|Can interest on a loan secured by your home relate to a rental project?|The use of the borrowed funds matters when allocating interest. If proceeds paid documented rental costs, evaluate that portion under the rental interest and capitalization rules rather than classifying it solely by the collateral.|An owner draws $15,000 from a line secured by their main home and pays a contractor for work on a separate rental. Trace the draw to the invoice and payment, then determine whether the interest is currently deductible or subject to a capitalization or other limitation.|What if the line also paid household expenses?|Maintain a distinct record of each draw and use. Review how the applicable allocation and repayment rules affect interest rather than charging the entire line to the rental.|rental-property-tax-planning|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527
# Separating mortgage payment components
rental-mortgage-payment-principal-interest|Can you deduct the full mortgage payment on a rental property?|Analyze eligible rental interest separately from principal repayment. Principal reduces the loan balance and generally is not a rental expense.|A $1,700 payment includes $900 interest, $500 principal and $300 escrow funding. Reconcile the interest to the lender records and determine the treatment of escrow items when disbursed. Keep the principal on the loan schedule.|Does principal repayment determine the building’s depreciation deduction?|Calculate depreciation from the applicable basis, placed-in-service date and recovery method. The monthly principal portion is tracked separately.|rental-property-tax-planning|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527
rental-lender-appraisal-fee-property-basis|Does an appraisal required by a lender increase a rental building’s depreciation basis?|A lender-required appraisal is generally a financing cost rather than a property acquisition cost. Identify why it was required and apply the applicable loan-cost treatment.|A closing statement includes a $600 lender appraisal and a $1,400 owner title policy. Review the appraisal in the financing workpaper and the title policy in the acquisition-basis workpaper, using the underlying invoices.|Does every appraisal fee receive the same treatment?|Document the purpose and who required the appraisal. A valuation commissioned for a different purpose must be evaluated on its own facts.|real-estate-depreciation|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p551
