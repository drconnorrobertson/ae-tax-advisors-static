# Availability and acquisition records
rental-listing-before-required-approval|Does a rental listing establish a placed-in-service date if a required approval is pending?|A listing can support availability, but the property must be ready and available for its intended rental use. If a required approval prevents that use, reconcile the listing and approval dates and document when the restriction was resolved.|An owner posts a December listing for a property that cannot accept renters until a required approval arrives in January. Keep the approval and listing history and determine when the property could actually be offered for its intended use.|What if a listing accepts only future inquiries?|Check the earliest date the owner could actually make the property available, along with any remaining restrictions or work.|real-estate-depreciation|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527
occupied-rental-purchase-service-date-records|What supports the buyer’s placed-in-service date when a tenant is already in a rental at closing?|Verify when the buyer acquired the property and it became available for the buyer’s income-producing use. An existing lease and uninterrupted occupancy can provide evidence of that use.|A buyer closes on an occupied duplex in August. Reconcile the closing statement, assigned leases and rent proration before recording the asset. # Construction and equipment evidence
phased-rental-renovation-separate-availability|Can completed rental units become available while another part of the property is still under renovation?|Potentially. Document which units were ready and available, which remained restricted, and what part of the depreciable costs is supportably tied to each component. Review the proper asset and basis treatment before applying different service dates.|Two units are habitable and available in October while renovation prevents the use of a third until January. Preserve unit-specific inspection, listing and cost records. Review which assets actually entered service in each year.|How should you document common costs?|Track shared improvements and document their readiness and the basis allocation used. Avoid assuming the first completed unit establishes the date for every component.|real-estate-depreciation|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p946
december-delivery-january-equipment-installation|Does December delivery establish the depreciation start date if required installation occurs in January?|Equipment is generally placed in service when it is ready and available for its specific use. Delivery alone does not establish that date when necessary installation or testing is still pending.|A rental operator receives a commercial laundry system in December but cannot use it until required installation is completed in January. Keep delivery, installation and commissioning records and determine when it was operationally available.|What if an item was ready but no tenant used it yet?|Actual first use can occur later. Document when the asset was ready and available for its intended function.|bonus-depreciation-rental-property|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p946
# Availability before the first tenant
rental-listing-ready-before-first-tenant-depreciation|Can rental building depreciation begin before the first tenant moves in?|A rental building generally enters service when it is ready and available for rent. The first lease or move-in may happen later. Document the earliest date you could actually rent the property.|A landlord completes repairs by June 12 and makes the home available to qualified tenants on June 15. The first tenant moves in August 1. Assuming the home was legally and practically available on June 15, June is the service month.|What if I accept bookings in advance for a future opening?|Document the date the property itself is ready and available. A future reservation alone does not establish current readiness.|rental-property-tax-planning|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527
