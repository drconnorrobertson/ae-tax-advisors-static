# Documenting a study
cost-segregation-missing-construction-invoices|Can a cost segregation study use estimates when original construction invoices are missing?|The IRS audit guide describes methods that use estimates. A supportable report explains the data, quantities, unit costs and assumptions and reconciles allocated costs to the taxpayer’s actual cost or supported basis.|A buyer lacks the original contractor invoices but has plans, photographs and a purchase allocation. Ask the provider to explain which details it verified and how estimated component costs were reconciled to the buyer’s basis.|What if estimates add up to more than the recorded cost?|Ask for a reconciliation before using the study. A construction-cost estimate can inform relative allocations, but does not independently increase the taxpayer’s purchase basis.|cost-segregation-study|https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p5653.pdf
cost-segregation-indirect-cost-allocation|How should a cost segregation study explain indirect construction costs?|A study should identify indirect costs, explain its allocation method and reconcile the totals. Review whether each cost is properly included in basis and how it relates to the assets classified.|A construction ledger includes design fees and general contractor overhead. Ask the provider to distinguish costs attributable to specific assets from shared project costs and explain the basis used to allocate the shared amounts.|What documents support the allocation?|Keep the general ledger, contracts and invoices, along with the study’s schedule showing direct costs, indirect costs and reconciliation to the total.|cost-segregation-study|https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p5653.pdf
cost-segregation-asset-description-function|Why should a cost segregation study describe what a component does?|An item’s function and relationship to the building can affect its classification. A useful report provides enough detail to understand the factual and legal basis for the classification.|A report lists wiring as one line item. Before other costs, record $13,000 of actual improvement costs.|How should I document my own work?|Keep the work log to document the project scope and dates. rental-architect-fee-remodel-asset-basis|Does an architect's fee belong in the basis of a rental remodel?|An architect's fee related to a capital remodel is part of the cost of that improvement. Tie the fee to the work it covered so the basis schedule reflects the full project cost.|A rental remodel costs $54,000 in construction and $6,000 in design. Assuming both amounts relate entirely to the capital remodel, the asset schedule accounts for $60,000 before any further adjustments.|What if the architect also worked on another project?|Request a fee breakdown by project or use a supported allocation that reconciles to the total invoice.|cost-segregation-study|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527
rental-fence-installation-survey-basis|Where does a survey fee go if I paid for it to install a rental fence?|A survey specifically required to install a fence is part of the fence cost. whole-building-demolition-land-basis|Where do demolition costs and the remaining basis of a demolished building go?|When the building demolition rules apply, capitalize demolition costs and the resulting loss to the underlying land. The capitalized demolition amounts remain in nondepreciable land basis.|real-estate-depreciation|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p551
post-service-rental-addition-separate-asset-record|How do I record an addition made after a rental is already in service?|Keep a separate account for the addition. # Rebates and credits in the asset ledger
manufacturer-rebate-rental-appliance-basis|How does a manufacturer rebate affect the basis of a rental appliance?|A purchase rebate from the manufacturer or dealer generally reduces the basis of the item instead of being included in income. Match the rebate to the asset to which it applies.|A landlord purchases an appliance for $2,400 and receives a $300 manufacturer rebate on the purchase. Before any other adjustments, the appliance basis is $2,100.|What if the rebate check arrives separately from the invoice?|Keep the invoice, rebate terms and payment confirmation. excluded-utility-energy-subsidy-rental-basis|Does a tax-excluded utility energy subsidy reduce rental improvement basis?|If a public utility subsidy for an energy conservation measure qualifies for exclusion from gross income, reduce the basis of the property by the excluded amount. Determine whether the particular payment qualifies before changing the schedule.|An owner incurs $10,000 of capital costs on an energy conservation improvement to a dwelling and receives a qualifying $1,500 excluded subsidy. Before other adjustments, the improvement accounts for $8,500 of basis.|Does a payment labeled a rebate always qualify as an excluded utility subsidy?|Review the payer, program rules and covered work. A vendor purchase rebate and a utility subsidy are supported by different records.|real-estate-depreciation|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p551
section-179d-deduction-building-basis-reduction|Does the Section 179D deduction reduce the basis of an energy-efficient commercial building improvement?|When the building owner claims a Section 179D deduction for qualifying property, reduce the basis of that property by the deduction. The remaining basis is $145,000 before other adjustments.|Does this calculation establish eligibility for the deduction?|Determine eligibility and amount separately. Keep the deduction support and the ledger entry identifying the property that received the basis adjustment.|real-estate-depreciation|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p551
# Allocating a single purchase price
rental-purchase-relative-land-building-values|How do I allocate a discounted rental purchase between land and building?|Allocate the total purchase basis using the land and building's relative fair market values at acquisition. Apply the resulting percentages to actual purchase basis rather than substituting the appraisal totals.|An investor pays $450,000. The supported acquisition-date values are $125,000 for land and $375,000 for the building. Land gets 25% of cost, or $112,500. The building gets 75%, or $337,500, before other basis adjustments.|What if I am unsure of the fair market values?|IRS guidance permits using the relative real estate tax assessed values if you are uncertain of fair market values. Retain the assessment and the calculation you used.|cost-segregation-study|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p551
furnished-rental-purchase-relative-furniture-values|How do I allocate a single purchase price for a furnished rental?|For a lump-sum purchase of assets outside a transaction governed by special allocation rules, use supported relative fair market values to allocate total basis among the purchased assets. Identify the transferred furniture separately from the land and building.|A buyer pays $360,000 for a furnished rental. Supported values are $80,000 for land, $300,000 for the building and $20,000 for furniture. The 20%, 75% and 5% shares produce basis amounts of $72,000, $270,000 and $18,000.|How do I support the furniture allocation?|Keep the purchase agreement, itemized inventory and acquisition-date valuation records. Reconcile their allocation to the closing statement and depreciation schedule.|cost-segregation-study|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p551

Retain the schedules and evidence needed to substantiate depreciation, adjusted basis and gain or loss.|An investor buys a rental in 2018 and sells it in 2026. The original valuation, cost segregation report and later asset changes still support the 2026 sale calculation, so the owner keeps them through the applicable limitation period for that return.|Which records should I preserve with the report?|Keep the acquisition and sale statements, the asset-level schedule, prior depreciation and records of improvements or dispositions.|cost-segregation-study|https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/how-long-should-i-keep-records
