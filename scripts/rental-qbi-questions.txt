# Rental enterprise eligibility
qbi-rental-safe-harbor-startup-hours|How many service hours does a new rental enterprise need for the QBI safe harbor?|For a rental real estate enterprise in existence for fewer than four years, the safe harbor generally requires at least 250 hours of rental services each year. Check the records and other safe harbor requirements separately.|A second-year enterprise has 235 hours of documented qualifying services. It does not meet the hours test under these facts. Review whether the rental otherwise qualifies under the Section 199A trade or business rules.|What if an older enterprise has a low-service year?|For an enterprise in existence at least four years, review the test requiring 250 or more hours in at least three of the five consecutive tax years ending with the current year.|qbi-deduction-guide|https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-19-38.pdf
qbi-rental-property-manager-hours|Can a property manager’s services count toward the rental QBI safe harbor?|Qualifying services can be performed by owners, employees, agents and independent contractors. Track what they actually did under the safe harbor rather than relying on a flat management fee to establish hours.|A manager documents 160 hours of leasing, rent collection and maintenance coordination, and the owner documents 100 hours of other qualifying services. The combined 260 hours can support the hours test if adequately documented and the other requirements are met.|Which dates and details should the record include?|Retain the date, service description, time spent and person performing the work for the rental enterprise.|qbi-deduction-guide|https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-19-38.pdf
qbi-rental-investment-work-excluded-hours|Does time spent arranging a refinance count toward rental QBI safe harbor service hours?|Financing and other specified investment-management activities are excluded from qualifying rental services under the safe harbor. Separate those hours from leasing and day-to-day operating work.|An owner’s 270-hour log includes 35 hours negotiating a refinance. With those hours removed and no other adjustments, the documented qualifying total is 235 hours.|What other work should you isolate?|Separately identify work such as reviewing financial statements, acquiring property, planning or constructing long-term capital improvements, and travel to or from the property.|qbi-deduction-guide|https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-19-38.pdf
# Lease terms and enterprise records
qbi-triple-net-lease-safe-harbor|Can triple-net rental property use the QBI rental real estate safe harbor?|Real estate rented under a triple-net lease as defined in the revenue procedure is excluded from this safe harbor. Review general Section 199A eligibility separately.|A commercial tenant pays rent plus taxes, insurance and maintenance under the lease. Compare those obligations to the revenue procedure before including the property in the safe harbor calculation.|Does the lease title settle the question?|Review the actual payment and maintenance obligations. Keep the lease, amendments and relevant expense records with the analysis.|qbi-deduction-guide|https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-19-38.pdf
qbi-residential-commercial-separate-enterprises|Can you combine separate residential and commercial properties in one rental QBI safe harbor enterprise?|The safe harbor generally groups properties of the same category. Separate residential properties and separate commercial properties generally cannot be combined into one enterprise. Mixed-use buildings have separate treatment.|An owner has two residential duplexes and a freestanding retail building. Review the duplexes as residential interests and the retail building separately rather than combining their service hours in one enterprise.|What if both uses are in one building?|The revenue procedure allows a mixed-use property to be treated as one enterprise or bifurcated into separate residential and commercial interests. Apply the stated restrictions before combining it with other properties.|qbi-deduction-guide|https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-19-38.pdf
# Computing the deduction
qbi-self-rental-common-control|Can rent from your own operating company qualify for QBI without the rental safe harbor?|Rent to a commonly controlled operating business can be treated as a trade or business for Section 199A if the regulatory conditions are met. Evaluate the control and operating entity rather than assuming the safe harbor is required.|An owner leases a building to an operating business held through a pass-through entity. Check direct and indirect ownership and the operating business’s classification before including the rental income in the computation.|Does this decide whether the rent is passive?|Review Section 469 separately. The Section 199A treatment does not itself establish passive or nonpassive status.|qbi-deduction-guide|https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8995a
qbi-rental-business-loss-next-year|Does a qualified rental business loss reduce the QBI deduction in a future year?|If the applicable net qualified business loss carries forward, it generally offsets QBI in the next year under the Section 199A rules. Record it separately from passive losses still suspended under Section 469.|Assume a $9,000 QBI loss carries into a year with $40,000 of otherwise qualifying business income. With no other adjustments, the resulting net QBI is $31,000 before the remaining deduction limits.|Where should the balance be tracked?|Reconcile the applicable Form 8995 or Form 8995-A schedules each year. Keep the Section 469 and QBI balances on separate workpapers.|qbi-deduction-guide|https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8995a
qbi-rental-no-wages-ubia-limit|Can qualified rental property support the QBI deduction when the rental has no W-2 wages?|When the wage and property limits apply, the calculation can use 2.5% of eligible qualified property basis as part of the alternative limit. Check qualified property status and the other deduction limits.|Assume the limits apply in full, the rental has no qualifying W-2 wages, and qualified property UBIA is $400,000. The property component of the alternative limit is $10,000 before comparing it with the other applicable limits.|Does the depreciation schedule’s ending book value supply UBIA?|Reconcile the unadjusted basis immediately after acquisition under the applicable rules. Accumulated depreciation does not turn ending book value into UBIA.|qbi-deduction-guide|https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8995a
qbi-rental-suspended-loss-when-allowed|When can a previously suspended rental loss enter QBI?|A prior loss suspended under an applicable loss limit generally enters QBI when allowed if it satisfies the Section 199A rules. Identify its original year and whether it is attributable to a qualified business.|A landlord uses a previously suspended rental loss in the current year. Reconcile the year of origin and the portion now allowed before changing current QBI. Check the rules for losses from tax years beginning before 2018.|Does a suspended loss always become QBI in full when released?|Determine the portion attributable to qualified business income and apply the applicable ordering and reporting rules.|passive-loss-carryforward-correction|https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8995a
qbi-rental-overall-taxable-income-limit|Can the overall taxable-income limit reduce a rental QBI deduction below 20% of rental QBI?|Yes. The overall limitation is generally 20% of the amount by which taxable income before the QBI deduction exceeds net capital gain as defined for this calculation. Apply it after computing the applicable QBI and other components.|Assume a $10,000 tentative deduction and $35,000 of taxable income after the required net capital gain adjustment. The simplified overall cap is $7,000.|Should you use the rental’s net income for the overall limit?|Use the qualifying taxable-income measure from the entire return. Net rental income is relevant to QBI but does not replace the overall limit calculation.|qbi-deduction-guide|https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8995a
