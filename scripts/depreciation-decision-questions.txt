# Acquisition basis and study scope
reconcile-study-to-owner-tax-basis|What if a cost segregation study total differs from the property’s tax basis?|Reconcile the study’s starting cost to the owner’s actual tax basis before using its classifications. Separate nondepreciable land, financing costs and scope exclusions so you can explain the difference.|An acquisition workpaper shows $720,000 before land and the study allocates $620,000 to assets. If supported land is $90,000, $10,000 remains to reconcile. Look for acquisition costs, excluded assets or a difference in the ownership or transaction assumptions. Do not spread the balance among short-life assets without support.|What should the study provider explain?|Request a bridge from the study’s total cost to amounts assigned to land, the building and separately classified components. Ask for a list of any amounts the study intentionally excludes.|cost-segregation-study|https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p5653.pdf
separate-tenant-and-landlord-asset-costs|Can both a landlord and a tenant depreciate the same buildout invoice?|Both parties should establish the tax treatment of their own costs and rights before listing the same improvement for depreciation. The lease, improvement allowance, payment records and applicable tax rules matter.|A tenant signs a $100,000 buildout contract and the landlord reimburses $60,000. Before entering any asset, reconcile the construction cost, allowance and contract rights for each party. A ledger showing $100,000 for each taxpayer does not explain who incurred the respective basis or how the allowance was treated.|Which documents resolve the overlap?|Give the preparer the lease, allowance clause, vendor invoices, approval and payment records, and the landlord’s and tenant’s respective asset schedules if available.|real-estate-depreciation|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p946
closing-costs-title-policy-versus-loan-points|Do an owner’s title policy and lender points both go into property basis?|An owner’s title insurance premium for buying property generally enters property basis. Loan points are financing costs and generally are deducted over the loan term for a rental, subject to applicable rules.|A buyer pays $2,200 for the owner’s title policy and $3,000 in loan points. Route the $2,200 to the property-basis schedule and the $3,000 to the financing schedule. Use the supported property allocation before treating any title cost as depreciable.|What if both charges are listed as title or settlement costs?|Check the detailed invoice and the party the policy protects. The closing statement’s category by itself does not establish the tax treatment of the fee.|cost-segregation-study|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p551
second-parcel-land-basis-reconciliation|How do you handle a vacant parcel bought together with a rental building?|Identify each parcel and allocate acquisition cost using supported relative fair market values. Land is not depreciable, including a separate vacant parcel with no depreciable improvements.|A $900,000 purchase covers an improved parcel with supported fair market value of $800,000 and a vacant parcel valued at $200,000. Before separate cost adjustments, the relative values allocate $720,000 to the improved parcel and $180,000 to the vacant parcel. Split the improved parcel further between its land and depreciable assets.|Can an appraisal showing only a combined value establish both allocations?|Request support for the separate parcel values and for the land and building allocation within the improved parcel. Retain the deeds, parcel map and valuation date.|real-estate-depreciation|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p551
# Renovation and depreciation schedules
split-availability-dates-furnished-rental|Can furniture and a rental building have different placed-in-service dates?|Depreciation starts when each asset is ready and available for its intended use. Separate dates can apply when the facts establish different service dates, even for assets within the same property.|A rental suite is already available unfurnished in June. In August the owner adds furniture and begins offering it as furnished. Track building and furniture availability separately. A furniture purchase receipt dated May does not establish that the pieces were already available for rental use.|What if the rental was advertised as furnished before the furniture arrived?|Keep the listing history, delivery and installation records and any restrictions on occupancy. Establish when the property and furniture were actually ready and available.|bonus-depreciation-rental-property|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p946
mixed-renovation-invoice-short-life-assets|How do you split a single renovation invoice for a cost segregation review?|Start with the contract scope and actual costs. Identify the separately supported assets and determine their classifications by function. A lump-sum invoice does not establish a common recovery period for everything in the project.|A $48,000 invoice covers cabinets, appliances and installation with a single total. Request the contractor’s pricing or comparable supporting records to identify the appliance costs and related labor before the preparer assigns any short-life basis.|Can an estimate be used when the contractor has no breakdown?|Have the study provider explain the costing method, supporting data and reconciliation to the actual total. Preserve the original invoice and separate unsupported assumptions.|depreciation-and-fixed-asset-review-for-rental-properties|https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p5653.pdf
midquarter-test-building-exclusion|Does a rental building count in the MACRS mid-quarter test for equipment?|Residential rental and nonresidential real property are generally excluded from the mid-quarter test. Compute the test using the applicable basis of the other covered MACRS property, subject to the prescribed exclusions and adjustments.|Assume $6,000 of covered equipment went into service in April and $9,000 in November. With no other covered property or adjustments, 60% of the covered basis was placed in service in the last three months. A $400,000 residential rental building does not enter that calculation.|Does the convention change the building’s depreciation?|The building uses its applicable real property convention. The test determines the convention for the covered assets, so keep the building and equipment calculations separate.|macrs-depreciation-schedule-2026|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p946
temporary-rental-idle-time-renovation|Does depreciation stop during a temporary vacancy for renovations?|Property temporarily idle generally continues to depreciate if it has not been retired from service. Establish whether it remains held for business or income-producing use and evaluate newly added assets separately.|A tenant leaves in February and the owner renovates with plans and records supporting a return to rental use in May. Review whether the existing building stayed in service during the interruption. The new improvements have their own date when ready and available.|What if the owner moved into the unit during that time?|Review whether the facts show a conversion to personal use. Preserve occupancy, listing and renovation records and give the preparer a dated sequence of events.|real-estate-depreciation|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p946
# Depreciation at sale and correction
sale-allocate-equipment-and-building-proceeds|Should a rental sale price be split between the building and depreciated equipment?|When a sale includes multiple assets, use supported allocations to compute the results by asset. The amount allocated to equipment can affect depreciation recapture and gain character.|A transaction includes appliances and the building. If supported allocation assigns $15,000 of proceeds to equipment with $3,000 adjusted basis, evaluate the $12,000 gain under the applicable recapture rules. Compute land and building results separately.|Should you reuse the cost segregation percentages from acquisition?|Use evidence relevant to the disposition and the assets actually conveyed. Old cost percentages do not by themselves establish current relative values.|rental-property-sale-tax-calculator|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p544
improvement-removed-before-property-sale|Should a removed improvement stay in the depreciation records when the rental is sold?|Identify assets included in the sale and explain any earlier removals or dispositions. Reconcile the dates and prior treatment before calculating sale basis, remaining depreciation or any recapture.|A washer was removed before a building sale and its replacement transferred to the buyer. Reconcile both serial numbers to the asset register, confirm how the removed washer was treated and assign proceeds only to the assets actually conveyed.|What if the removed asset appears on the seller’s current depreciation schedule?|Document the removal and check earlier returns for any reported disposition. Have the preparer determine the appropriate treatment instead of deleting it silently or treating it as sold with the building.|rental-property-sale-tax-calculator|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p544
catch-up-adjustment-current-depreciation-split|Why separate a Form 3115 catch-up amount from current-year depreciation?|The catch-up computation for an applicable method change generally reconciles differences through the start of the change year. Current-year depreciation under the applicable method is calculated separately.|A supported computation shows $12,000 old-method depreciation and $29,000 proposed-method depreciation through the prior year. The $17,000 historical difference is evaluated for the Section 481(a) adjustment. If the current-year depreciation is $6,000, do not include it in that opening comparison.|Which schedules should be linked?|Retain the old and proposed historical schedules, the starting adjustment reconciliation and current-year depreciation. Tie them to the same assets and basis assumptions.|catch-up-depreciation-section-481a|https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i3115
sale-reconcile-fully-depreciated-equipment|Can fully depreciated rental equipment still create taxable gain at sale?|Yes. If an asset with zero adjusted basis has sales proceeds allocated to it, those proceeds can produce gain. Review applicable depreciation recapture and the consideration received.|Appliances originally cost $8,000 and have zero adjusted basis. If a defensible sale allocation assigns $1,500 of net proceeds to them, evaluate $1,500 of gain and the applicable recapture rules. The assets’ zero book balance does not determine proceeds.|What if the appliances were discarded rather than sold?|Keep disposal records and reconcile the purchase agreement or inventory to what was conveyed. Do not assign sale proceeds to items that were not included in the transaction.|rental-property-sale-tax-calculator|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p544
# Alternative Depreciation System elections
ads-voluntary-election-individual-rental-building|Can I elect ADS for one rental building without electing it for every rental I bought that year?|The voluntary ADS election generally applies to the whole property class placed in service that year. Residential rental and nonresidential real property are exceptions for which you can elect separately for each property. A voluntary election is generally irrevocable.|An owner places two residential rental buildings in service in the same year. If neither is required to use ADS, the owner can elect ADS for one building and generally use GDS for the other.|Where do I record the election?|Follow the Form 4562 instructions for the acquisition year and keep a copy showing which property the election covers.|real-estate-depreciation|https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i4562
ads-voluntary-election-same-class-equipment|Can I elect ADS for just one of two assets in the same equipment class?|A voluntary ADS election for equipment generally covers all property in the elected class that you place in service in the election year. Confirm the class and service year for each asset.|An owner places two pieces of equipment in the same property class in service in September and December. A voluntary ADS election for the class generally applies to both.|Does the election apply to otherwise similar property first placed in service next year?|The election applies to the property in the class placed in service in the election year. Analyze the later year's assets separately.|real-estate-depreciation|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p946
electing-real-property-trade-business-older-rental-ads-period|Can a pre-2018 residential rental use 30-year ADS after a real property trade or business election?|A special rule can give pre-2018 residential rental property a 30-year ADS recovery period if it is held by an electing real property trade or business and none of the specified ADS requirements applied to it before 2018.|An electing business owns an apartment building placed in service in 2016. Its records show no specified earlier ADS requirement. The owner reviews the 30-year rule and reconciles the prior schedule before computing depreciation under the applicable transition rules.|Should I restart depreciation as if the building were new?|Preserve the original service date and depreciation history. Determine the required transition treatment before making any adjustment.|real-estate-depreciation|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p946
