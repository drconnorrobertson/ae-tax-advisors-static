# Choosing the deduction year
federal-disaster-rental-prior-year-election|Can I amend last year's tax return for a federally declared disaster loss on my rental?|A deductible loss from a federally declared disaster in an area identified for public or individual assistance may qualify for an election under section 165(i). The election treats the loss as sustained in the tax year immediately before the disaster year.|A landlord sustains a qualifying $25,000 disaster loss in Year 2. The owner can compare the Year 2 deduction with an election to take it in Year 1. The comparison uses the full returns for both years, including applicable loss limits.|Can I choose an older year with higher taxable income?|The election applies to the immediately preceding tax year. For a Year 2 loss, the elected year is Year 1.|amended-tax-returns|https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/faqs-for-disaster-victims
form-4684-election-amended-return-attachments|What goes on Form 4684 when I amend a prior return to elect a disaster loss?|Attach the applicable Form 4684 for the earlier tax year to that year's amended return. Complete the loss computation and Section D Part I to make the election. Follow the instructions for the disaster identification and property location fields.|An individual who elects to deduct a qualifying 2025 rental loss on the already filed 2024 return uses a 2024 Form 1040-X with the applicable 2024 Form 4684. The amended package includes schedules affected by the change.|What details belong in the property's disaster file?|Keep the designation and assistance records, property address, deductible loss calculation, reimbursement records, and a copy of the submitted election.|how-to-file-amended-tax-return-form-1040-x-guide|https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i4684
section-165i-election-amended-return-deadline|What is the deadline to amend a return for a prior-year disaster loss election?|The section 165(i) election deadline is generally six months after the regular due date for the disaster-year federal return, measured without extensions. Check any IRS relief specific to the location, event, and filing act.|The IRS example for a calendar-year taxpayer with a loss sustained in 2025 gives October 15, 2026 as the deadline to amend the 2024 return to elect the loss.|Does my filing extension start a new six-month period?|Calculate from the unextended regular due date. Keep the election deadline separately from the filing extensions for the returns involved.|amended-tax-returns|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p547
rental-disaster-claim-settlement-election-year|What if insurance settles my rental disaster claim in the year after the storm?|For any portion with a reasonable prospect of reimbursement, the loss is sustained once you can determine with reasonable certainty that the amount will remain unreimbursed. Use the year of the sustained loss to identify the preceding year for a potential election.|A qualifying storm damages a rental in Year 1. The insurer reviews the full measured $30,000 loss and closes the claim in Year 2 after paying $12,000. If Year 2 is when the remaining $18,000 first became reasonably certain to go unreimbursed, the preceding year for that amount is Year 1.|Could one event produce portions sustained in separate years?|Yes. A portion with no reasonable prospect of recovery may be sustained before a separately disputed portion. Document the amount and timing of each.|amended-tax-returns|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p547
# Correcting a filed loss
revoke-section-165i-disaster-election|How do I revoke an earlier-year disaster loss election after filing?|Amend the preceding-year return to remove the loss and complete Form 4684 Section D Part II for the revocation. File within 90 days after the election deadline and on or before filing any disaster-year return or amendment that includes the loss.|An owner deducted a qualifying loss on a Year 1 amendment and now wants the deduction in Year 2. The owner files the Year 1 revocation within the deadline before claiming it in Year 2 and makes any required payment.|What should I reconcile before sending the revocation?|Recalculate the preceding year's tax without the election, determine payment due, and reconcile any related changes on both returns.|how-to-file-amended-tax-return-form-1040-x-guide|https://www.irs.gov/irb/2019-45_IRB
unexpected-insurance-after-rental-disaster-deduction|Does unexpected insurance reimbursement require amending a rental disaster loss return?|If the original deduction was proper based on the facts at filing and reimbursement arrives unexpectedly later, generally include the recovered amount in the year received to the extent the original deduction reduced tax.|A landlord properly deducted $10,000 after a claim closed. An insurer unexpectedly pays $4,000 in a later year. If the entire original deduction reduced tax, the later $4,000 recovery is generally included in income in the year received.|What if the original deduction never reduced my tax?|Determine how much of the prior deduction actually produced a tax benefit, including any carryover effect. That tax-benefit calculation controls how much of the recovery is included.|amended-tax-returns|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p547
