# Qualification and employment
reps-750-hours-exact-threshold|Does exactly 750 hours qualify for real estate professional status?|The annual hours test requires more than 750 hours of services in real property trades or businesses in which you materially participate. You must also meet the separate more-than-half test.|An owner records exactly 750 qualifying hours and 400 personal-service hours in an unrelated business. The owner clears the more-than-half test on those facts but does not clear the more-than-750-hours test.|Do estimated hours count automatically?|Keep reasonable support for the amount and nature of the services. Review the qualification tests using the supported totals.|real-estate-professional-status|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p925
reps-more-than-half-outside-work|Can you work more than 750 real estate hours and still fail to qualify?|Yes. More than half of the personal services you perform in all trades or businesses must be in eligible real property trades or businesses in which you materially participate.|A taxpayer documents 820 qualifying hours and 1,100 service hours in another business. The real estate share is about 43%, below the more-than-half requirement, even though 820 is above 750.|What should you record for the comparison?|Track qualifying services and other trade or business services separately during the same tax year. Review exclusions before calculating either total.|real-estate-professional-status|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p925
reps-employee-more-than-five-percent-owner|Can employee hours count toward real estate professional status?|Employee services generally do not count as qualifying real property services unless you are more than a 5% owner of the employer. The work and participation must still satisfy the applicable requirements.|Two employees perform comparable real estate work. One owns exactly 5% of the employer and the other owns 6%. The first does not clear the ownership exception and the second should evaluate the remaining requirements.|What evidence supports the ownership threshold?|Retain ownership records and information on applicable attribution rules. Track the duties and dates separately from the ownership proof.|real-estate-professional-status-documentation|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p925
# Joint filing and rental activities
reps-spouse-hours-two-separate-tests|Can spouses combine hours to qualify as a real estate professional on a joint return?|One spouse must separately meet both qualification tests. For the separate material participation analysis, generally count a spouse’s participation in the activity.|One spouse performs 430 qualifying hours and the other 400. Neither exceeds 750 individually, even though the total exceeds it. Their participation in a specific activity is evaluated separately under the applicable rules.|What if one spouse individually meets both qualification tests?|Then assess participation in each rental activity or applicable grouping. Maintain records that identify the services performed by each spouse.|real-estate-professional-status-and-material-participation-support|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p925
reps-separate-rental-activity-participation|Can a real estate professional still have a passive rental activity?|Yes. Qualification and material participation are distinct tests. Rental interests generally are treated separately unless a valid election treats all rental real estate interests as a single activity.|A qualifying taxpayer performs 620 hours at one rental and 60 at a second. Without an applicable election, review each activity separately. The first property’s hours do not automatically establish material participation at the second.|What should you keep if you elected to combine rental interests?|Retain the filed election and year-by-year participation records. Review its effect before evaluating a sale or the use of suspended losses.|real-estate-professional-status-and-material-participation-support|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p925
