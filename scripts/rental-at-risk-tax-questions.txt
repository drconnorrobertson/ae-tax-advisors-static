# Determining the amount at risk
rental-loss-at-risk-before-passive|Which limit comes first when a rental loss is subject to both at-risk and passive rules?|Apply the at-risk rules before the passive activity rules. When an ownership basis limit also applies, such as for a partnership interest, evaluate that limit first.|Assume a directly owned rental has a $20,000 loss and the owner has $12,000 at risk. Track the $8,000 disallowed by the at-risk rules separately and evaluate the remaining $12,000 under the passive activity rules.|Why track the balances separately?|Each balance is subject to a different test. Maintaining separate schedules helps prevent the same loss from being carried forward twice.|passive-activity-loss-rules-real-estate|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p925
qualified-nonrecourse-rental-financing|Can a nonrecourse loan increase the amount at risk for a rental?|Qualified nonrecourse financing can count for an activity of holding real property if the financing satisfies the applicable lender, security and other requirements.|An investor compares two loans secured by rental real estate. One is a bank loan and the other comes from a related seller. Review each lender and loan agreement before including either balance in the amount at risk.|What records support the review?|Keep the note, security agreement, lender identity and ownership information. Determine whether there are guarantees or arrangements that protect against loss.|rental-property-tax-planning|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p925
rental-owner-protected-against-loss|How does a reimbursement agreement affect the amount at risk?|Amounts protected against loss generally are excluded. Review guarantees, reimbursement agreements and similar protections along with the financing documents.|An owner is personally liable on a loan but has a separate agreement under which another party reimburses some losses. Analyze the protection before including the entire personally guaranteed balance.|Which documents should be reviewed together?|Compare the note, guarantee, indemnity or reimbursement agreement, and any side letters. Document which amounts the owner would actually bear.|rental-property-tax-planning|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p925
# Tracking changes and reporting
rental-cash-withdrawal-at-risk-recapture|Can a cash withdrawal trigger recapture under the rental at-risk rules?|If the amount at risk falls below zero, prior deductions can be subject to recapture, generally limited by the applicable prior loss and recapture history.|Assume a withdrawal reduces the year-end amount at risk to negative $4,000 and sufficient prior deducted losses remain subject to recapture. Reconcile the $4,000 shortfall before computing the inclusion.|Does every withdrawal require recapture?|Track the year-end amount at risk and prior losses. A withdrawal that leaves the amount at risk positive does not by itself trigger the below-zero rule.|passive-activity-loss-rules-real-estate|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p925
rental-at-risk-loss-next-year|What happens to a rental loss disallowed by the at-risk limit?|A disallowed loss is generally treated as a deduction from the same activity in the next tax year, subject again to the applicable limits.|An owner carries forward $6,000 from an at-risk limitation and contributes additional cash the next year. Recompute the amount at risk for that year and then test any amount allowed under the passive rules.|Can you add this loss directly to the Form 8582 carryforward?|Keep it in the at-risk rollforward until it clears that limit. Reconcile the amount to the passive activity schedule when applicable.|passive-loss-carryforward-correction|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p925
