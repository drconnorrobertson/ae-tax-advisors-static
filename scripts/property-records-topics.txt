# Closing and acquisition
Closing statement credits|The settlement statement includes a seller credit described only as an adjustment.|Match the credit to the purchase agreement, settlement detail and closing agent’s ledger.|Identify the underlying obligation before the preparer assigns the credit to a property or expense account.
Furnished rental purchase inventory|A furnished purchase includes items that the prior owner grouped under one sale price.|Keep dated photos, the signed inventory and any separate valuation or purchase terms.|Mark missing or discarded items so their values do not migrate into the opening asset list.
Acquisition escrow holdback|The title company retains part of the proceeds for unfinished work.|Keep the escrow agreement, release instructions and final disbursement receipts together.|Compare amount withheld with amount released and show the reason for any difference.
Seller paid repair invoice|A contractor invoice predates closing and the parties disagree about who paid.|Match the invoice to the settlement credit and bank evidence from the party that settled it.|Flag any duplicated credit or expense before posting the opening activity.
Earnest money trail|Several deposits were paid before the final settlement and one was partly refunded.|Start with the escrow receipts and trace each deposit or refund to both the bank and final statement.|List unresolved transfers separately instead of plugging a difference into the property ledger.
Acquisition boundary survey|A single transaction includes a second parcel that the marketing materials barely mention.|Collect the legal descriptions, survey, appraisal and deed schedule by parcel.|Identify which bills and cost schedules refer to each parcel before combining records.
Post closing price adjustment|A final adjustment arrives after the purchase was recorded.|Keep the purchase contract clause, adjustment worksheet, amended statement and proof of payment.|Show the original amount and the revised amount side by side with an explanation of the change.
Prepaid expenses at acquisition|The settlement prorates service costs that the seller had paid in advance.|Get the original bills and place the covered dates next to the proration calculation.|Verify which periods had already been entered in the new owner’s books.
Acquisition invoice cutoff|A range of vendor bills straddles the date a rental changes owners.|Record service date, invoice date, payor and the contractual responsibility for each bill.|Investigate bills that the seller and buyer both recorded or that neither party entered.
Inherited appliance records|The sale includes appliances with mixed purchase histories and condition.|Save serial numbers, photographs and the best available source for purchase terms.|Use item identifiers to connect subsequent repair and replacement records to the original inventory.

Warranty replacement record|A new component failed and was replaced through the supplier warranty.|Save the original purchase, warranty claim, replacement serial number and any labor invoice.|Use the record to avoid carrying the failed and replacement components as one indistinguishable item.
Damaged material refund|Supplies were returned after part of an order was installed.|Keep the original itemized invoice, return authorization and credit memo.|Tie the credit to the correct purchase line and verify the final project total reflects it.
Permit closeout file|A finished renovation has several inspections with different dates and no single completion memo.|Save the inspection responses, correction notices and local closeout record as separate milestones.|Record which areas were unavailable and why; give the timeline to the adviser for review.
Construction contingency use|A project’s reserve was drawn for several unrelated needs.|Track each use against an approved change, invoice and available budget line.|Identify unsupported draws and remaining amounts before comparing budget to actual costs.

Seasonal direct booking deposit|Guests pay months before the planned stay and some deposits are refunded.|Maintain a reservation-level log of deposits, refund terms, actual cancellations and final payments.|Match balances through period end and preserve the contract terms for preparer review.
