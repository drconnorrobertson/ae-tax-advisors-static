# Limits and carryforwards
section-179-income-limit-year-end|What happens when business income is lower than your Section 179 election?|After applying the dollar limit, apply the taxable business income limit. An amount disallowed by the income limit generally carries forward to a later year, subject to that year’s rules.|Assume a $32,000 election after the dollar limit and $24,000 of income available under the business income test. The simplified result is a $24,000 deduction and an $8,000 carryforward.|What should you reconcile next year?|Track the disallowed amount by asset and reconcile it with any new elections and the new year’s available business income.|how-to-maximize-section-179-deduction|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p946
section-179-dollar-limit-multiple-businesses|Does each business give the same individual a separate Section 179 dollar limit?|The limit is generally applied per taxpayer. When one individual actively runs more than one business, review the combined election amounts before applying the individual limit.|An owner elects to expense equipment in two sole proprietorships. Combine the applicable amounts on the owner’s calculation rather than using a separate full dollar limit for each operation.|What if an amount comes through a partnership or S corporation?|Check the entity-level rules and reconcile the amount allocated to the owner with the owner’s own limits.|how-to-maximize-section-179-deduction|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p946
section-179-partial-election-and-bonus|Can you elect Section 179 for part of an asset’s cost and still evaluate the rest for bonus depreciation?|A Section 179 election can designate a portion of eligible cost. Reduce the asset’s basis by the amount applicable under the Section 179 rules before calculating any special depreciation allowance on the eligible remainder.|Assume a qualifying $30,000 asset and a fully deductible $10,000 Section 179 election. Review the remaining $20,000 under the applicable bonus and regular depreciation rules.|How should the preparer record the election?|Keep the asset description, cost and elected amount, then tie the remaining basis to the depreciation schedule and Form 4562.|section-179-vs-bonus-depreciation-2026|https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i4562
# Use changes and qualified real property
section-179-business-use-falls-half|Can you owe Section 179 recapture when equipment’s business use drops to half?|Business use dropping to 50% or less during the recovery period generally triggers recapture. Recompute the depreciation that would have applied without the Section 179 deduction under the recapture rules.|A taxpayer claims Section 179 on equipment initially used 90% for business. During its recovery period that share falls to 50%. Preserve the business-use records and calculate the difference between the deduction and allowable depreciation for recapture purposes.|How does recapture affect the asset record?|Record the amount included in income and the corresponding basis increase under the applicable rules. Retain the corrected depreciation schedule.|how-to-maximize-section-179-deduction|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p946
section-179-roof-existing-nonresidential-building|Can a roof on an existing nonresidential building qualify for Section 179?|Certain roof improvements to nonresidential real property can qualify if made after the property was first placed in service. Review the property eligibility and election limits before claiming the deduction.|A business replaces the roof on its existing warehouse. Document when the building was first used and when the new roof was ready, then evaluate the expenditure under the qualified real property rules.|Does the roof have to qualify as qualified improvement property?|The specified roof category is a separate route under the Section 179 qualified real property rules.
