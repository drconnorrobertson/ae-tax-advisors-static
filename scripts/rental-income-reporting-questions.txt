# Rent timing and lease changes
advance-rent-december-january|When do you report January rent received in December?|Advance rent is generally included in rental income in the year you receive it, even when it covers a later period.|A tenant pays $1,800 in December for January occupancy. Record the covered month and payment date separately so the amount is included in the proper year and not reported twice.|How does this differ from a refundable deposit?|Check the lease and refund terms. A refundable security deposit generally is not income when received if you plan to return it.|rental-property-tax-planning|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527
tenant-lease-cancellation-payment|Is a payment from a tenant to cancel a lease rental income?|A payment a tenant makes to cancel a lease is generally rental income in the year you receive it.|An owner agrees to end a lease early for $2,400, paid in December, with the tenant moving out in January. Reconcile the payment date and agreement separately from the occupancy dates.|What if the agreement also settles a damage claim?|Track each separately identified component and review the purpose and treatment of each amount. Keep the signed agreement and evidence of payment.|rental-property-tax-planning|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527
uncollected-rent-cash-method-landlord|Can a cash-method landlord deduct rent a tenant never paid?|Generally no. A cash-method landlord typically has not included the unpaid rent in income and therefore cannot deduct that amount as uncollected rent.|A tenant pays $14,000 of $16,000 billed. Under the ordinary cash-method facts, reconcile actual receipts and do not enter a $2,000 bad-debt expense merely to clear the billing ledger.|What if a deposit was applied to the unpaid rent?|Document when it stopped being refundable and was applied. Check the treatment of the amount retained separately from the remaining unpaid balance.|rental-property-tax-planning|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527
# Rent paid through expenses or services
tenant-pays-landlord-utility-offset|How do you report rent if a tenant pays your utility bill and subtracts it from rent?|Include qualifying landlord expenses the tenant pays as rental income. Determine separately whether the underlying cost is deductible.|Monthly rent is $1,500. The tenant pays the owner’s $120 utility bill as agreed and sends $1,380 in cash. Reconcile the full $1,500 of rent and review the $120 utility cost on its own merits.|What if the lease makes the bill the tenant’s own responsibility?|Review the lease and invoice to determine whose obligation was paid. The example assumes the obligation belonged to the owner.|rental-property-tax-planning|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527
tenant-services-instead-of-rent|How do you report services accepted instead of cash rent?|Include the fair market value of services received as rental income. Review the value and the tax treatment of the work separately.|An owner accepts $400 cash and agreed painting work worth $600 for a rental period. Record the $1,000 of consideration under those facts. Analyze whether the painting is a deductible repair, part of an improvement, or otherwise treated.|What records support the noncash amount?|Keep the agreement, description of work, date of performance and available pricing support. Reconcile the services to the rental ledger.|rental-property-tax-planning|https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527
