STR Material Participation: Tests and Limits

Answers to five connected questions about str material participation. Review IRS sources, practical facts, records and filing responsibilities.

Book a Call

The decision this guide resolves

Material participation is tested under the applicable annual rules after activity classification. More than 100 hours alone is not the same as passing a test.

A practical fact pattern

A host logs 140 hours but a manager works 300. The host cannot simply use the more-than-100-hour, no-one-more test without evaluating the other person’s work.

Records to gather before deciding

  • Dated task log and supporting messages
  • Manager, cleaner and contractor hours
  • Prior participation history and any grouping statements

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the 500-hour material participation test?

One test is participation for more than 500 hours during the year. Exactly 500 is not more than 500. Only qualifying participation counts; the test does not automatically permit all rental losses and does not substitute for the separate activity-classification analysis.

Is 100 hours enough for an STR?

Not by itself. One test requires more than 100 hours and participation at least as much as any other individual, including a nonowner. Exactly 100 fails the numerical threshold for that test. Other tests may apply, but each has separate conditions.

Can I qualify if I perform substantially all the work?

Possibly, under the substantially-all participation test. It is a facts-and-circumstances question; outsourcing material operations can affect the conclusion. Keep records of other workers rather than assuming owner involvement is substantially all because the owner makes final decisions.

Can prior years of participation help this year?

Certain tests consider participation in prior years, including five of the preceding ten years. They are not a blanket exemption for all former landlords. Review the exact activity, continuity and applicable test under Publication 925 before relying on historical hours.

Can I group all my rentals to meet the hours test?

Not automatically. General activity grouping, rental grouping restrictions and the real estate professional election are distinct rules. A valid grouping must be supported and consistent. Review mixed STR and LTR activities before adding all hours together; a spreadsheet total does not create an election.

How to turn the answers into a return decision

Write down the property owner, affected tax year, the factual test being used and the evidence supporting it. Reconcile the amount on the study or workpaper with the return schedule, identify any limitation and assign the filing task to a named preparer. If a fact is missing, resolve it before treating the estimated deduction as usable.
