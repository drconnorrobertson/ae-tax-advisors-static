Estimated Tax Payment Methods: Prior Year, Current Year and Annualized Income

By AE Tax Advisors · Updated November 8, 2026.

Choose an estimated-tax method by comparing the applicable required payments with income timing and withholding. Paying enough to avoid an underpayment penalty does not necessarily pay the year's entire tax bill.

Start with the individual estimated-tax rules

For 2026, the general individual rule considers whether at least $1,000 will remain due after withholding and refundable credits, and whether those amounts are below the required threshold. Exceptions and special rules apply. Corporations use separate rules; do not apply this individual worksheet to a corporate return.

Compare the three approaches

Individual estimated-tax planning approaches
MethodWhat to calculateMain limitation
Prior-year safe harborGenerally 100% of the applicable prior-year tax, or 110% for certain higher-income taxpayers.The prior return must qualify, payments must be credited on time, and a balance may still be due.
Current-year projectionGenerally 90% of the applicable current-year tax, using an updated forecast.A forecast that understates income or other tax can leave a shortfall.
Annualized income installmentsCalculate required installments using the applicable cumulative income periods and Form 2210 Schedule AI.Requires reliable period-by-period records and the proper tax recognition of income and deductions.

For the general 2026 higher-income rule, use 110% rather than 100% when 2025 AGI exceeds $150,000, or $75,000 if the 2026 filing status is married filing separately. Use the form instructions to calculate the relevant tax; a single return line is not always the entire calculation.

Worked example: safe harbor versus total tax

Assume the relevant prior-year tax is $180,000, the 110% rule applies, the prior return covers 12 months and projected current-year tax is $250,000. The prior-year target is $198,000, compared with a $225,000 current-year 90% target. If $40,000 of withholding is credited evenly and no other adjustments apply, the remaining prior-year target is $158,000, or $39,500 per equal installment.

Timely payments totaling $198,000 can meet that assumed safe harbor, but the projected $250,000 tax still leaves $52,000 due. These figures illustrate the difference between penalty protection and paying the final bill.

Check installment dates and actual income timing

The standard 2026 individual installment dates are April 15, June 15 and September 15, 2026, and January 15, 2027. Weekend, holiday, disaster-relief and special-taxpayer rules can change the applicable deadline.

Annualization uses cumulative periods rather than four equal three-month quarters. A late cash distribution from a partnership does not alone prove that the taxable income arose late; review the actual allocation and income-recognition rules.

Reconcile withholding and payments already made

Withholding is generally treated as paid ratably unless the applicable actual-date treatment is used. Additional withholding may help a late-year shortfall, but the amount and other facts must support the result. A late estimated payment does not automatically cure earlier missed installments.

Keep payment and filing evidence

Confirm taxpayer identity, tax year, payment type, amount and processing date. Use an IRS-authorized payment method suitable for the return and retain confirmation. State estimates and pass-through entity payments require their own analysis.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does meeting a safe harbor mean no balance is due?

No. It addresses estimated-tax penalty exposure under the applicable assumptions; the actual annual tax may be higher.

Does receiving a K-1 distribution in December make all partnership income fourth-quarter income?

No. Review the allocation and tax recognition of the income rather than the cash-distribution date alone.
