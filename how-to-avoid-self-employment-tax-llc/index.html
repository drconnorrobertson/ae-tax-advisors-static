Can an LLC Avoid Self-Employment Tax? S Election Decisions

By AE Tax Advisors

Updated November 8, 2026.

Forming an LLC does not, by itself, eliminate self-employment tax. LLC is a state-law legal structure; federal tax classification is a separate question. An eligible LLC may elect S corporation treatment, but an owner working in the business normally needs reasonable W-2 compensation before non-wage distributions.

Identify how your LLC is taxed now

A domestic single-member LLC is generally disregarded for federal income tax unless it elects otherwise. Its owner commonly reports an operating business on Schedule C and calculates self-employment tax on net earnings. A domestic LLC with multiple members generally defaults to partnership taxation. Active members may have self-employment income; do not assume that the words “limited liability” make an owner a limited partner for this purpose.

An LLC taxed as an S corporation files a corporate return and passes tax items through to shareholders. The legal LLC may continue under state law even though federal tax reporting changes. Ownership eligibility, one-class-of-stock requirements and shareholder consent need review before an election.

Make the election before changing your payment practice

Form 2553 is generally due no later than two months and 15 days after the beginning of the tax year for which the election should take effect, or during the preceding tax year. Late-election relief exists when its conditions are met; it is not automatic. Keep the IRS acceptance notice and establish payroll and bookkeeping appropriate to the effective date.

Separate wages from distributions

If you provide substantial services, labeling every withdrawal a distribution is not a compliant payroll strategy. Document your responsibilities, hours, comparable compensation and the contribution of employees or capital to profit. Run wages through payroll, deposit employment taxes and reconcile Forms W-2 and 941. Shareholder distributions also require a basis and reporting review; they are not automatically free of income tax.

Decide using a full-year comparison

Ask for a projection of the current LLC structure and the proposed S corporation. Include owner wages, employer payroll tax, state entity taxes, accounting fees, benefit treatment and the QBI deduction. If you already earn wages elsewhere, identify how the Social Security limits affect employee and employer amounts. The result can differ materially from a simple percentage of distributions.

For expense and retirement distinctions, see what actually lowers self-employment tax. For operational setup, review S corporation versus LLC taxation and discuss your records through our consultation process.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I stop paying self-employment tax as soon as I create an LLC?

No. A default LLC classification does not remove self-employment tax from operating business earnings.

Can all S corporation payments be distributions?

No. An owner who provides services generally must receive reasonable compensation through payroll before taking non-wage distributions.
