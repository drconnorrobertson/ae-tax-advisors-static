The Augusta Rule for Business Owners and Executives

AE Tax Advisors · Tax Planning & Strategy

The Augusta Rule under IRC Section 280A(g) can exclude rent received from a home used as a residence and rented for fewer than 15 days during the tax year. The homeowner cannot deduct expenses attributable to that rental use. If a separate business rents the home, its potential rent deduction is a different question.

How the Augusta Rule Works

There are two tests for the homeowner. Under Section 280A(d)(1), personal use must be more than the greater of 14 days or 10% of the days rented at a fair rental. Under Section 280A(g), actual rental use must total fewer than 15 days for the dwelling during the tax year. Count rentals to all renters, including unrelated guests and every related company, together.

When these tests are met, the owner excludes the rental income and takes no rental-expense deduction for depreciation, utilities, maintenance, insurance or other costs attributable to that rental use. Qualified mortgage interest and real estate taxes may remain deductible as personal itemized deductions, subject to their usual limits. The exclusion does not remove those limits.

Practical Example

Assume an owner personally uses a lake house for 30 days and rents it to their separately taxed company for 14 days, with no other rentals that year. At a hypothetical $5,000 per day, the payment is $70,000. That rate is an illustration, not a verified market quote: it needs evidence for the actual venue, space, dates and business use.

If Section 280A(g) applies, the owner excludes the $70,000 but cannot deduct the rental period's depreciation, utilities, insurance or maintenance. Even if those rental-use costs total $15,000, they do not create a $15,000 rental deduction. The company's rent deduction must independently qualify as an ordinary and necessary business expense under Section 162, with reasonable pricing and a documented business purpose. The amount paid is not the amount of tax saved.

Fair Market Rent Requirement

For a related-business deduction, support the amount an unrelated business would pay for the same use. A whole-house overnight listing may not support a short daytime meeting rate. Compare the space used, hours, attendee capacity, location, date, amenities and included services. Retain dated comparable quotes and explain adjustments rather than choosing rent to reach a tax target.

The 14-Day Threshold

Fewer than 15 days means no more than 14 actual rental days in the tax year, not 14 days per business. At 15 rental days, the entire Section 280A(g) exclusion is unavailable. Rental income must then be reported under the applicable rules; expense allocation, personal-use restrictions and other limitations determine which deductions are allowed. Day 15 does not make every property expense deductible.

Common Uses

A genuine board meeting or business planning retreat may support a business rental when the expense and price are justified. A client party or recreational event does not become deductible merely because the business calls it a meeting. Section 274 can disallow entertainment and entertainment-facility expenses; meals and other event costs need their own review.

The Strategy

A qualifying primary or vacation residence can be rented to unrelated guests without the owner having a company. Renting to your own business adds the separate payer-side analysis. A sole proprietorship, or a single-member LLC disregarded as separate from its owner for federal income tax, cannot create a rent deduction by paying the owner to use the owner's property. A separately taxed entity still needs a real business reason and reasonable rent. A pure investment rental or commercial facility does not qualify merely because it is rented for fewer than 15 days; the dwelling must satisfy the residence test.

Documentation

Keep a single annual calendar of personal use and all rental use, a written rental agreement, comparable rate evidence, business agendas and attendance, and payment records. Give both the business and individual return preparers the same file. The owner generally does not report qualifying excluded rent on Schedule E. Have the preparers separately determine the payer's information-return obligations for the applicable year and address any Form 1099 received.

The Caveats

Do not claim rental-expense deductions against excluded rent, split one home's annual day count among related entities, or assume documentation makes a personal event deductible. Federal exclusion, the business deduction, information reporting and state or local obligations are separate decisions.

Should You Use the Augusta Rule?

Start with the residence and rental calendars, then compare supported rent with the business's actual meeting needs and the cost of implementation. Ask AE to review both returns, not just a proposed deduction. Bring the entity's tax classification, ownership details, meeting plans, rate evidence and prior rental history so the review can identify whether the strategy fits.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does the Augusta Rule work?

IRC Section 280A(g) excludes rent from a dwelling used as a residence and rented for fewer than 15 days during the tax year, but allows no deductions attributable to that rental use. Personal use must exceed the greater of 14 days or 10% of fair-rental days. A separate business must independently support an ordinary and necessary rent deduction with reasonable pricing and a genuine business purpose.

Are business meals deductible?

Business meals with a clear business purpose are generally 50% deductible when not lavish and when the taxpayer or an employee is present. Entertainment remains nondeductible. Certain employee-related meals have different treatment, so the category matters.

What vehicle deductions are available?

Either the standard mileage rate or actual expenses including depreciation. Passenger automobiles are subject to the Section 280F luxury auto caps, while vehicles above 6,000 pounds gross vehicle weight rating fall outside that definition and have separate rules. Contemporaneous mileage records are essential.

What is an accountable plan?

An arrangement under Treasury Regulation 1.62-2 through which a business reimburses employees, including owner-employees, for business expenses without the reimbursement being treated as taxable wages. It requires a business connection, substantiation within a reasonable period, and return of excess amounts.

Support the business rental, not just the day count

The less-than-15-day home-rental exclusion and the business's deduction are separate analyses. The home must meet the relevant residence rules, while the business needs a legitimate expense and reasonable rent. Keep agreements, agendas, attendance, comparable pricing and payment records. Fourteen calendar entries alone do not establish a defensible business deduction.

Illustrative decision

A company holds one planning meeting at the owner's home. A proposed daily rent should reflect the actual space, use and market evidence rather than a target deduction. Multiple related companies also need coordination so the owner's total rental days are not overlooked.
