Keep Your CPA: Add Pre-Closing Tax Planning

Define the transaction advice you need while keeping continuity with your return preparer.

AE Tax Advisors Team · Updated November 6, 2026

You can retain your existing CPA while hiring a tax advisor for a business acquisition, property purchase or other transaction. The important question is whether the signed engagements cover the decisions you need before closing and the filings required afterward.

A CPA firm may provide both planning and preparation, or scope them separately. Credentials and per-return pricing do not establish what advice has already been provided. Start by asking your current firm what it can handle, what requires another specialist and when the work can be completed.

When an additional planning engagement makes sense

Consider a defined project when you are buying a business and its building, purchasing through related entities, bringing in a partner or selling a profitable operation. These decisions may require coordinated tax, legal, financing and accounting work before documents are finalized.

Ask for a written list of alternatives, assumptions, unresolved questions and deadlines. A useful proposal identifies the entities and owners covered, the transaction being modeled, the intended deliverables and who will implement each decision. It should also explain what happens if the transaction changes or does not close.

For a broader explanation, see tax planning versus tax preparation. This guide focuses on adding transaction advice without losing continuity with your preparer.

Safe-harbor estimates do not prove planning was absent

Prior-year safe-harbor payments are one method of addressing estimated-tax penalties. They can be used alongside a current-year projection. Their use alone does not show that your CPA failed to model income.

Ask for two separate numbers: the payment schedule intended to address underpayment penalties and the projected total tax liability, including the expected balance due. A transaction can change the second number substantially even when prior-year payments remain an appropriate approach. Payment timing and applicable exceptions still matter. See the IRS Form 2210 instructions.

Agree on responsibilities before signing or closing

The following is a starting point for a written responsibility checklist, not a statement that every provider includes these services.

  • Tax advisor: Compare proposed structures and tax outcomes, documenting assumptions, open issues and deadlines. Deliver written analysis and an implementation checklist to the owner, counsel and preparer.
  • Return preparer: Review proposed reporting, information needs and filing responsibilities before implementation. Confirm covered entities, returns, elections and supporting schedules; flag disagreements or missing records.
  • Transaction counsel: Address legal structure, agreements, title and contractual terms within the legal engagement. Confirm that executed documents reflect agreed decisions and identify changes from the modeled proposal.
  • Lender: Confirm financing conditions, borrower structure and required consents. Financing approval is not approval of a tax position.
  • Valuation or other specialist: Provide separately commissioned appraisal, allocation support or engineering work within the specialist's scope. Confirm the report, assumptions, asset detail and limitations can be used by the advisor and preparer.
  • Bookkeeper: Record the completed transaction using approved documents and instructions. Reconcile opening balances, asset records, debt schedules and supporting entries for return preparation.
  • Business owner: Approve decisions, provide complete facts and appoint the coordinator. Maintain one agreed deadline list, authorization for information sharing and confirmation that assigned work is complete.

Do not assume the advisory firm will draft legal documents, obtain lender consent, prepare a valuation, maintain the books or file returns. Name the responsible party and separately quoted fee where applicable.

Tax savings need a model, not a profit threshold

Profit above $500,000 does not automatically produce a particular retirement deduction or PTET benefit. A deduction is also not the same as a dollar-for-dollar tax saving.

Retirement-plan proposals need employee and ownership information, compensation data, funding capacity and the appropriate plan specialists. Defined-benefit arrangements require actuarial calculations and may create ongoing funding obligations. Adoption, employee-election and contribution deadlines are not interchangeable; some work may remain possible after year-end. See IRS Publication 560.

PTET analysis requires the applicable state's eligibility, election, payment and owner-credit rules, plus a comparison of the overall federal and state result. IRS Notice 2020-75 addresses federal treatment of specified entity-level state and local income-tax payments; it does not promise a fixed benefit for every owner.

For a building purchase or depreciation project, ask the advisor to evaluate whether deductions can actually be used and what separate study, reporting or implementation work is required. Do not treat an estimated deduction as guaranteed cash savings.

What to bring to a pre-closing consultation

Provide a short transaction summary, target closing date, current ownership chart, entities involved, current preparer's contact information and a list of unresolved decisions. Relevant records may include draft purchase agreements, financial statements, prior returns, depreciation schedules, financing terms and allocation or valuation materials already available.

Send returns, identification documents and other confidential records only through an agreed secure channel. Do not put them in an initial public inquiry form. Confirm permission and scope before information is shared among providers.

Compare the complete engagement, not just the headline fee

Use the tax-advisor engagement scorecard to compare covered entities, written deliverables, implementation responsibilities, preparer coordination, deadlines and separate costs.

Review what AE's $7,800 tax advisory engagement includes and obtain a written proposal for your transaction. Confirm whether returns, amendments, studies, legal work and other third-party services require separate engagements. Do not assume an advisory fee purchases every step in the checklist.

Frequently asked questions

Can I keep my CPA and hire a separate tax strategist?

Yes. Confirm that both firms agree to the division of work, information sharing, deadlines and reporting responsibilities. A planning recommendation does not obligate the preparer to file a position they cannot support.

Do prior-year safe-harbor payments mean my CPA did no planning?

No. Safe-harbor payments can coexist with current-year projections. Ask separately for the payment schedule, projected total liability and expected balance due.

Who should coordinate the tax advisor, attorney and return preparer?

Name the coordinator in writing. The owner should know who maintains the deadline list, resolves conflicting assumptions and confirms that the final documents and reporting match the approved decisions.

Does a $7,800 advisory engagement include every transaction service?

Do not assume that it does. Review AE's engagement scope and obtain a transaction-specific proposal identifying included work and any separately priced filings, studies, legal services or other implementation costs.
