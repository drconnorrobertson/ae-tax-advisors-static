How to Do a Backdoor Roth IRA

AE Tax Advisors Team · Published 2026-10-03

The Short Answer

High-income earners who exceed Roth IRA income limits can use the backdoor strategy: contribute to a traditional IRA (non-deductible), then immediately convert to a Roth IRA. The pro-rata rule applies if you have other pre-tax IRA balances.

But the full picture is more nuanced than a one-paragraph answer. The right approach depends on your specific income level, business structure, state of residence, and long-term financial goals. Below, we break down everything you need to know.

Retirement Plans as Tax Reduction Tools

Retirement plans are among the most powerful tax reduction strategies available to business owners. A Solo 401(k) allows total contributions of up to $69,000 in 2026 ($76,500 for those 50 and older). Adding a defined-benefit or cash balance plan can push total deductible contributions above $200,000 to $350,000+ annually, depending on age and income.

The tax savings are immediate: every dollar contributed to a qualified retirement plan reduces your taxable income dollar-for-dollar. For a business owner in the 37% federal bracket plus state taxes, a $100,000 contribution can save $42,000 to $50,000 in current-year taxes.

Choosing the Right Retirement Plan for Your Business

The optimal plan depends on your age, income, number of employees, and how much you want to contribute. Solo 401(k) plans work best for owner-only businesses. SEP-IRAs are simpler but less flexible for owner contributions. Defined-benefit and cash balance plans provide the highest deductions but require actuarial administration and minimum funding commitments.

Mega Backdoor Roth and Advanced Strategies

For business owners who have maxed out their pre-tax contributions, the mega backdoor Roth strategy allows additional after-tax contributions to a Solo 401(k) that are immediately converted to Roth. This can add $30,000 to $40,000+ in Roth savings annually beyond standard contribution limits, growing tax-free for life.
