Will My Basis-Suspended Partnership Losses Be Deductible When I Sell?

AE Tax Advisors · Tax Planning & Strategy

No—selling the entire partnership interest does not, by itself, release losses suspended for lack of outside basis under Section 704(d). Those losses can be used only to the extent outside basis becomes available while the partner still owns the interest. A different bucket, losses already allowed through basis and at-risk tests but suspended solely by the passive-activity rules, may be released on a qualifying fully taxable disposition of the entire activity to an unrelated person. Calling both buckets “suspended losses” can turn a projected deduction into a costly filing error. The IRS partner K-1 instructions require a separate outside-basis worksheet and carry Section 704(d) excess losses to a year in which basis is available; Publication 925 describes the different passive-loss disposition rule.

If a sale or syndication exit is pending, AE can reconcile the final K-1, debt allocation, outside-basis schedule, and four separate loss-limitation ledgers before the proceeds and deductions are modeled.

Which limitation suspended the K-1 loss?

Apply the owner-level tests in sequence: outside basis under Section 704(d), amount at risk under Section 465, passive-activity rules under Section 469, then any applicable excess-business-loss limit. A loss stopped at the first test has not yet become a passive-loss carryforward. The partner's return workpapers—not just the K-1's box 1 loss or capital account—must identify the amount and tax character in each bucket. The partner K-1 instructions state this ordering and place responsibility for maintaining outside basis on the partner.

Section 704(d) governs interests in partnerships, including a multi-member LLC taxed as a partnership. An S corporation shareholder has different stock-and-debt-basis rules; do not apply this partnership guide to an S corporation merely because both entities issue K-1s. For related issues see AE's partnership debt basis guide and passive-loss sale guide.

Build the outside-basis rollforward before the sale

Start with prior-year outside basis. Add contributions, allocated taxable income or gain, and increases in the partner's share of partnership liabilities; account for distributions, nondeductible expenses, allocated losses and deductions, and liability decreases under the applicable rules. The tax-basis capital account in K-1 item L is not the same number: it generally excludes the partner's share of liabilities and may omit partner-specific transactions. A debt-financed real-estate partnership can therefore show a low capital account and a much larger outside basis, or the reverse after a partner-specific adjustment. Publication 541 and the K-1 basis worksheet explain the components.

Debt is not allocated by a blanket “general partner gets all recourse, limited partners get none” rule. Section 752 recourse allocation depends on who bears economic risk of loss after considering guarantees, reimbursement rights, related persons, and the constructive-liquidation rules. Nonrecourse liabilities also can create outside basis under their separate allocation rules; they are not limited to a partner's equity in the collateral. A guarantee may change basis only after its actual terms are tested, and outside basis created by debt does not automatically create an amount at risk. Qualified nonrecourse financing for eligible real-estate activities is a separate at-risk exception, not a universal rule for every loan. Reconcile K-1 item K1 and the loan documents before claiming a deduction or signing an exit agreement.

Worked example: basis-suspended versus passive-suspended losses

Assume a partner carries $80,000 of prior Section 704(d) basis-suspended ordinary loss into a sale year with zero starting outside basis. Before the sale, the partnership allocates $30,000 of taxable income to that partner, with no other basis changes. Up to $30,000 of the old loss may now pass the basis test; the partner must still run the at-risk and passive tests. The remaining $50,000 does not become deductible simply because the partner later sells the interest. If the interest is sold for $100,000 cash with no share of debt at closing and no remaining outside basis after the allowable loss, the sale gain calculation is separate. Sale proceeds are not a contribution or partnership income allocation that retroactively creates outside basis for the suspended loss. Actual sale tax character may also be affected by Section 751 hot assets.

Contrast a separate $20,000 loss that already passed the basis and at-risk tests in a prior year but was suspended solely under Section 469. If the partner disposes of the entire interest in the passive activity to an unrelated buyer in a fully taxable transaction, Publication 925 generally allows that passive loss in the disposition year, subject to the precise activity and installment-sale rules. The $20,000 passive bucket is not interchangeable with the $50,000 basis bucket. A final K-1 does not prove that both are released.

At-risk and passive rules remain separate

After a loss clears basis, the individual partner may still be limited to the amount actually at risk in the activity. A recourse label on the partnership's loan is not enough by itself to prove the partner's economic exposure. Conversely, qualifying nonrecourse real-estate financing can count under the special Section 465 rules when its conditions are satisfied. IRS K-1 instructions discuss the exception and Form 6198.

After basis and at-risk, passive-activity treatment depends on the activity, ownership and participation. The $25,000 rental real-estate special allowance is not automatic for every limited partner or every partnership rental: active participation, ownership, modified AGI and phaseout rules matter. Real-estate-professional status alone also does not automatically turn every rental loss nonpassive; rental material participation must be tested. Any remaining passive carryforward belongs on its own Form 8582 rollforward and may have a different sale-year result.

Decide before closing, then reconcile the final K-1

  1. Reconstruct each year: keep the outside-basis worksheet, K-1s, contributions, distributions, debt schedules, and each category of disallowed loss by year.
  2. Trace pre-sale events: determine whether partnership income, a contribution, or a valid liability increase created basis before the transfer. Check guarantees and indemnities rather than using the debt balance on a lender statement.
  3. Model the sale: include cash, debt relief, transaction costs and any Section 751 ordinary-income component in the partnership-interest gain calculation. Do not net a basis-suspended operating loss against sale proceeds without basis support.
  4. Run the other limitation ledgers: distinguish at-risk suspended amounts from passive losses, interest limitations, and excess-business-loss carryovers; check whether the disposition actually meets Publication 925's full, taxable, unrelated-party test.
  5. Review downstream returns: coordinate the final entity K-1 with the partner's federal and state returns, Form 6198, Form 8582, and any corrected K-1 or amended filing.

Bring the purchase and sale agreements, all K-1s, debt and guarantee documents, outside-basis and at-risk worksheets, Form 8582 carryforwards, partnership agreements, and any prior correction work. The major failure points are using item L capital as outside basis, treating every “suspended” line as a passive loss, assuming a guarantee supplies all at-risk capacity, and claiming that a final K-1 automatically frees a Section 704(d) carryover. This is a federal framework, not a filing position for a specific partner or state.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a final K-1 release losses suspended under Section 704(d)?

No. A final K-1 or sale does not automatically release a basis-suspended loss. Reconstruct outside basis before the transfer and determine whether any pre-sale income, contribution, or qualifying liability increase provides basis for that loss.

Are basis-suspended losses the same as passive losses?

No. A partnership loss must first clear the outside-basis and at-risk tests before the passive-activity rules apply. A loss stopped by Section 704(d) is not a Section 469 passive-loss carryforward.

Can selling the partnership interest free passive losses instead?

Generally, yes, if the losses already passed the basis and at-risk tests and the owner disposes of the entire passive activity in a fully taxable transaction with an unrelated person. Grouped activities, installment sales, and other facts can change the timing.

What should a partner gather before signing a sale agreement?

Gather every K-1, the outside-basis and at-risk worksheets, debt and guarantee documents, Form 8582 carryforwards, and the draft sale terms. Reconcile the final allocations and debt relief before modeling the deduction.
