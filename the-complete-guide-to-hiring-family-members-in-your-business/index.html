The Complete Guide To Hiring Family Members In Your Business

AE Tax Advisors · Tax Planning & Strategy

Reviewed November 6, 2026 for 2026 planning.

Hiring family members can support a real business need and, in some cases, reduce the family's combined tax cost. Start with the work, the legal employer, and reasonable pay. A deduction on the business return is not the same as net family savings, because wages may be taxable to the recipient and create payroll taxes or benefit costs.

Start With the Employer and the Work

Identify who directs and pays the worker, the business's federal tax classification, and whether the family member is an employee or an owner. Partners generally are not employees of their partnership. Under Section 162, a business wage deduction requires reasonable compensation for services actually rendered. An allowance, household chore, or family gift does not become a business expense by running it through payroll.

Children: FICA Before 18, FUTA Before 21

For a child working in a parent's sole proprietorship or a partnership in which every partner is a parent of that child, the federal rules differ by age. IRS Publication 15, section 3 and IRS Family Employees distinguish the following:

  • Under age 18: qualifying business wages are exempt from Social Security and Medicare taxes, and from FUTA.
  • Age 18 through 20: Social Security and Medicare taxes apply, but the qualifying FUTA exemption continues until age 21.
  • Age 21 and older: the child-specific family exceptions no longer apply.
  • Corporate employer or a partnership with a nonparent partner: the child-specific exceptions do not apply, regardless of the child's age. S corporations and C corporations follow ordinary payroll rules.

These are operating-business rules. Domestic work in a parent's home is a different category and should not be used to justify the business-child exception.

A single-member LLC disregarded for income tax generally reports employment taxes under its own name and EIN. The family-employment regulations nevertheless look to its owner for the specific FICA and FUTA family exceptions, if all requirements are met. An LLC electing S-corporation or C-corporation taxation is different. Confirm the actual employer, owner, election, and services; an LLC label or a new management company does not create an exemption. See the IRS LLC reporting guidance and the FICA and FUTA family-employment regulations.

Income Tax and Withholding Are Separate

Federal income-tax withholding is a separate question from FICA or FUTA. Business wages paid to a child are generally subject to withholding regardless of age. An employee claiming exemption on Form W-4 generally must have had no federal income-tax liability in the prior year and expect none in the current year. Being young, dependent, or paid below a particular amount does not by itself establish that exemption. Retain a valid W-4, renew an exempt certificate as required, and check state rules separately.

For 2026, a dependent's basic standard deduction is generally the greater of $1,350 or earned income plus $450, capped at the applicable basic standard deduction, ordinarily $16,100 for a single filer. Other income, filing status, age or blindness, and eligibility to use the standard deduction can change the result. A zero federal income-tax calculation does not remove payroll reporting or applicable state tax obligations.

Illustrative 2026 wage example: A 16-year-old does documented administrative work worth $12,000 for a parent's qualifying sole proprietorship. If the business has $250,000 of profit before the wage deduction, that deduction alone reduces it to $238,000. Assuming the child is a single dependent, has no other income, and can use the standard deduction, the $12,450 dependent deduction covers the wages. The qualifying FICA and FUTA exceptions apply. This does not establish a particular net family saving: the owner's self-employment tax, QBI, other income, state taxes, and payroll costs still need to be modeled.

Spouse Employment and Retirement Plans

An actual spouse-employee of a sole proprietor is generally subject to income-tax withholding and Social Security and Medicare taxes, but not FUTA. A spouse employed by a corporation or partnership generally does not receive that FUTA exception. A spouse who co-owns and jointly operates the business may instead be a partner or qualify for a different reporting arrangement. See IRS Married Couples in Business.

For spouses filing jointly, income is combined on one return. Paying wages to a spouse does not put those wages into a separate lower spouse tax bracket. Compare the household's payroll taxes, retirement contributions, QBI, and benefits rather than multiplying wages by the difference between two supposed spouse brackets.

Illustrative 2026 retirement calculation: A spouse-employee under age 50 with $50,000 of qualifying W-2 compensation could defer $24,500 and receive an employer contribution of $12,500, totaling $37,000, if the plan permits, no other deferrals use the personal limit, and all applicable limits are met. The employer amount here is 25% of employee compensation, not a self-employed-owner earnings adjustment. The employer contribution is additional business funding, not entirely a deduction from the spouse's paycheck. A one-participant 401(k) generally covers an owner alone or the owner and spouse; other eligible employees change the coverage and testing analysis.

Employing a Parent

A parent employed in their child's sole proprietorship generally has wages subject to federal income-tax withholding and Social Security and Medicare taxes, but not FUTA. Corporate employment and partnership employment require their own analysis and generally do not receive the sole-proprietor FUTA exception. Reasonable compensation, genuine duties, benefit eligibility, and the parent's own tax situation still matter.

Reasonable Pay and Records Before the First Paycheck

Set a job description, supervisor, schedule, and market-supported pay rate before work starts. For example, 10 documented hours each week for 52 weeks at $20 per hour supports $10,400 in wages, if the rate and work are reasonable. It does not justify an arbitrary $50,000 salary. Pay for work completed, keep records of actual payment, and retain work samples, timesheets, and compensation comparisons.

  1. Confirm the employer, tax election, ownership, family relationship, and worker classification.
  2. Check age-appropriate duties, working hours, required permits, and federal and state child-labor restrictions.
  3. Document actual services, the pay rate, compensation approval, and pay dates.
  4. Set up Form W-4, payroll reporting, deposits, and Forms W-2/W-3 and applicable Forms 941, 944, or 940 as required.
  5. Recheck birthdays, ownership or entity changes, other income, and retirement-plan eligibility each year.

Do Not Treat FUTA as a Universal 0.6% Tax

FUTA is an employer tax, not an employee withholding. The federal rate is 6% on the first $7,000 of covered wages per employee. A credit of up to 5.4% can reduce the effective rate to 0.6%, but eligibility, state payments, and credit-reduction states can change the result. For covered FICA wages, Social Security is generally 6.2% for each side up to the annual wage base; Medicare is generally 1.45% for each side, with Additional Medicare withholding rules for higher wages. Apply any family exception first, then the applicable reporting and tax rules.

What to Bring to a Family Payroll Review

Bring the entity's tax classification, ownership summary, proposed duties and hours, pay comparisons, existing payroll reports, and retirement-plan terms. Use a secure document channel for personal records. AE can help compare the proposed hire with the existing arrangement before the first payroll. For a focused child-employment checklist, read hiring children in a family business.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do all child payroll exemptions end at age 18?

No. In a qualifying parent sole proprietorship or partnership where every partner is a parent of the child, the business-child Social Security and Medicare exemption ends at 18, while the FUTA exemption continues until 21. Corporate employment does not qualify for these child-specific exceptions.

Can I skip withholding because my child owes no income tax?

Not automatically. Business wages generally follow income-tax withholding rules at every age. A valid Form W-4 exemption generally requires no federal income-tax liability in the prior year and none expected in the current year. FICA and FUTA exemptions are separate.

Can a disregarded single-member LLC use the family exceptions?

The LLC generally files employment taxes under its own name and EIN, but the specific family-employment regulations look to its owner when testing FICA and FUTA exceptions. All relationship, age, service, and ownership conditions must be met. Corporate tax elections have different consequences.

What should be documented before hiring a family member?

Record the actual employer and tax classification, employee duties, age-appropriate hours, reasonable wage comparisons, work performed, payments, and payroll filings. Review child-labor rules and retirement-plan coverage. A family relationship alone does not create a deductible wage.
