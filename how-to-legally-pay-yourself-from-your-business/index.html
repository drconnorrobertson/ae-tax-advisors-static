How to Pay Yourself: Draws, Wages and Distributions by Entity

By AE Tax Advisors · Published 2023-07-21 · Updated November 8, 2026.

The correct way to pay an owner depends on the business's tax classification and the reason for the payment. A bank transfer's memo does not determine whether it is a draw, wage, partnership payment, dividend or loan.

Sole proprietor or disregarded LLC

Owner draws generally transfer business cash to the owner; they are not wages or a deduction for the owner's labor. Taxable business profit is calculated independently of how much cash the owner withdraws.

Partnership or LLC taxed as a partnership

Partners generally are not employees of the partnership for federal employment-tax purposes. Allocated income, guaranteed payments and distributions are different items. Cash paid does not necessarily equal the partner's taxable income.

Review the agreement, outside basis, liabilities and self-employment-tax rules for the actual partner and activity. Do not assume every partnership distribution is taxable or that every allocated dollar avoids employment tax.

S corporation

A working shareholder must receive reasonable compensation for services before payments are treated as non-wage distributions. Determine wages from duties, time and relevant evidence rather than a standard percentage of profit.

Track stock and debt basis, payroll and distributions separately. Employment-tax effects depend on applicable wage bases and the complete circumstances, not a flat 15.3% of every distribution.

C corporation

Wages, dividends and bona fide loans follow different rules. Compensation must be supportable; dividends generally are not deductible by the corporation. Retaining earnings can defer shareholder taxation but raises separate accumulated-earnings and other considerations.

A payment labeled a loan needs a real debt arrangement. A corporate payment of personal expenses can require another classification rather than creating a business deduction.

Coordinate benefits and tax payments

Retirement contributions and owner health insurance depend on compensation, ownership and plan rules. Use current limits and coordinate other employers and related businesses. Set aside cash for the owner's tax liability rather than treating all available cash as spendable.

Request an owner-payment review with entity elections, payroll, agreements, basis schedules and recent transfers. The output should reconcile business deductions, owner income and the bookkeeping treatment.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are owner draws a business expense?

Generally not for a sole proprietor. Taxable profit is separate from cash withdrawn.

Can an S corporation pay a working owner only distributions?

Reasonable compensation for services must be addressed before treating payments as non-wage distributions.
