How to Plan a Section 179 Deduction in 2026

By AE Tax Advisors

Updated November 8, 2026.

For tax years beginning in 2026, the federal Section 179 dollar limit is $2,560,000. The limit is reduced dollar for dollar when qualifying property placed in service exceeds $4,090,000. Those ceilings are only the starting point: asset eligibility, business use and taxable business income can limit the deduction.

Buy an asset the business needs

A deduction reduces taxable income, not the purchase price dollar for dollar. For illustration, a deductible $40,000 asset at a 30% marginal income-tax rate produces $12,000 of income-tax reduction before other interactions; it still requires $40,000 of funding and leaves an economic cost. Compare the equipment's operating value, financing cost and expected resale proceeds before prioritizing the tax result.

Confirm eligibility and placed-in-service timing

Qualifying purchased business equipment, eligible off-the-shelf software and certain improvements to nonresidential real property may qualify. Land and ordinary building acquisition costs do not become Section 179 property merely because a business owns them. Related-party purchases and property leased to others require special scrutiny.

The asset must be ready and available for its intended use during the tax year. A December deposit or delivery of machinery that cannot operate until January may not establish the desired placed-in-service date. Save installation, acceptance and operational records, not just the invoice.

Apply all three limits

  1. Calculate eligible cost and the business-use percentage. Section 179 generally requires business use above 50%.
  2. Apply the annual dollar limit and purchase phaseout across the relevant businesses and entities.
  3. Apply the taxable-income limit from active trades or businesses. Disallowed amounts may carry forward under the applicable rules.

Pass-through businesses may face limits at both entity and owner levels. SUVs have a separate 2026 Section 179 limit of $32,000, with vehicle definitions and other depreciation limitations still relevant. Do not apply that amount to every vehicle without classification.

Coordinate with bonus depreciation and state rules

Section 179 is elective by asset and has a business-income restriction. Bonus depreciation follows different eligibility, timing and election rules and can produce a loss subject to other limitations. Model their ordering, QBI effects and state adjustments together. See Section 179 versus bonus depreciation.

Keep a schedule showing original cost, eligible basis, business use, election amount and remaining depreciation. A later reduction in business use or disposition can trigger recapture. Bring that schedule to a business tax planning review.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the 2026 Section 179 limit $1.25 million?

No. For tax years beginning in 2026, the federal limit is $2,560,000, reduced when qualifying purchases placed in service exceed $4,090,000.

Does ordering equipment before December 31 establish the deduction?

Not by itself. It must be placed in service, meaning ready and available for its intended business use, during the relevant tax year.
