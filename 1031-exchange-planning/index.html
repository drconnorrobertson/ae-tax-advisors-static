1031 Exchange Planning

Real Estate Tax Planning | Updated November 6, 2026 | AE Tax Advisors

A properly structured 1031 exchange can defer gain on qualifying investment real estate, but cash or other non-like-kind value received and asset-level depreciation rules can cause current tax. The exchange should be modeled before the sale, not assumed to defer every dollar.

Failures can arise from the qualified intermediary arrangement, identification paperwork, missed deadlines, or decisions made before the property is listed.

Exchange Deadlines and Limited Relief

Under IRC Sec. 1031(a)(3), replacement property must be identified within 45 days of the transfer of the relinquished property, and the exchange must be completed within 180 days or by the due date of the return including extensions, whichever is earlier.

A failed acquisition, financing problem, or seller withdrawal does not ordinarily extend either exchange period. Specific federally declared disaster relief can postpone a deadline when the taxpayer and transaction meet the applicable IRS guidance; check the actual notice rather than assuming relief.

The filing deadline can shorten the 180-day exchange period, especially for a late-year transfer. Calculate the actual transfer date, return due date and any valid extension; a November start does not automatically produce a shortened period for every taxpayer.

Identification Strategy

Three rules govern identification. The three-property rule permits identifying up to three properties regardless of value. The 200% rule permits identifying any number so long as their combined fair market value does not exceed 200% of the relinquished property's value. The 95% rule permits identifying more than that only if you acquire at least 95% of the identified value.

Most investors use the three-property rule, and most identify only the property they intend to buy. That is the mistake.

A valid backup identification may preserve options if the primary deal collapses, but each property must be described and delivered correctly before Day 45. A Delaware statutory trust interest may be one candidate only after reviewing eligibility, sponsor terms, financing, liquidity, fees, and the actual identification limit; it is not a guaranteed closing or debt-replacement solution.

If the primary target collapses on Day 42, there may still be time to revoke or revise the identification by the deadline through the required signed writing and delivery. After Day 45, a newly found property ordinarily cannot be added; a timely identified backup may still work. See the failed-replacement decision and 45/180-day timeline before instructing the qualified intermediary or assuming the sale is fully taxable.

Avoiding Boot

Cash, other non-like-kind property and net debt relief can cause recognized gain, generally limited by realized gain and subject to asset-level recapture rules. Reconcile both closing statements, debt paid off, debt assumed, new borrowing, cash contributed or retained and qualifying exchange expenses. Do not infer taxable boot just from a lower replacement mortgage.

Additional cash can offset debt relief in the exchange calculation. Additional borrowing does not erase cash actually received. A lower-debt replacement may still support full deferral when the owner contributes enough cash and all other requirements are met.

Model the character of recognized gain separately. Section 1245 or ordinary Section 1250 recapture can require current recognition under their exchange rules. Unrecaptured Section 1250 gain is different and is subject to a maximum 25% federal rate for individuals, not an automatic flat 25% tax.

Coordinating With Cost Segregation

Replacement tax basis is not replacement market value minus the old adjusted basis. In a simple exchange with no debt, boot, recognized gain or transaction costs, the replacement basis equals the old adjusted basis plus additional cash paid. The deferred gain remains embedded in the replacement property.

Illustration: relinquished property has a $1,000,000 fair market value and $180,000 adjusted basis. The owner adds $400,000 cash for a $1,400,000 replacement, with no debt, boot or costs. Replacement tax basis is $580,000 ($180,000 + $400,000), consisting of $180,000 carryover basis and $400,000 excess basis before allocation to land and depreciable components. The $820,000 deferred gain explains the difference between the $1,400,000 value and $580,000 tax basis.

Carryover basis generally continues under the exchange depreciation rules; qualifying excess basis is analyzed separately. Neither the full replacement value nor all excess basis automatically qualifies for bonus depreciation. Review asset class, land allocation, acquisition and placed-in-service dates, original-use or used-property requirements, applicable elections and the special exchange rules. A study classifies supported components; it does not create tax basis or guarantee a currently usable deduction.

Bring both closing statements, the complete depreciation schedule, old and new debt detail, exchange expenses and support for land and component allocations. Reconcile Form 8824 and the replacement depreciation schedule before authorizing a cost segregation study.

A fully deferred exchange generally does not release all suspended passive losses under Section 469(g). Track those losses by activity. A partially taxable exchange requires separate analysis of recognized passive income; do not assume every suspended dollar becomes deductible.

Sources: IRS Publication 551: exchange basis and allocations, IRS Publication 946: depreciation of exchanged property, IRS Publication 544: like-kind exchanges and recapture.

Partnership and Co-Ownership Problems

An interest in an LLC taxed as a partnership is not qualifying real property for Section 1031. The partnership may exchange qualifying real estate it owns, but a partner-level interest sale is different. A disregarded single-member LLC requires a different ownership analysis.

A proposed “drop and swap” is not a standard tax-free fix. Distributions of tenancy-in-common interests can raise debt-shift gain, disguised-sale, basis, holding-purpose and step-transaction issues. Moving title in an earlier tax year is not a safe harbor. Obtain transaction-specific tax and legal advice before changing ownership or signing a sale agreement.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the 1031 exchange deadlines?

Identify replacement property within 45 days after transferring the relinquished property. Complete the exchange by the earlier of 180 days or the return due date, including extensions. Check applicable IRS relief and the actual dates; an ordinary financing or acquisition problem does not extend them.

What is mortgage boot?

Net debt relief can contribute to taxable boot, but compare debt paid off, debt assumed, cash contributed, cash received and exchange costs. Additional cash can offset debt relief; additional borrowing does not erase cash actually received.

Can I do a cost segregation study on a property I exchanged into?

Potentially, after reconciling tax basis. With $180,000 old basis, $1,000,000 old value, $400,000 additional cash and a $1,400,000 replacement, with no debt, boot or costs, total replacement basis is $580,000 and excess basis is $400,000 before land and component allocations. Bonus eligibility and deduction usability require separate review.

Do my suspended passive losses release in an exchange?

A fully deferred exchange generally is not the fully taxable disposition of the entire interest to an unrelated person required for full release under Section 469(g). Track the suspended losses by activity and separately analyze any recognized passive income.

Can I exchange my LLC interest?

An LLC interest taxed as a partnership interest is not qualifying real property. The partnership itself may exchange qualifying real estate. A drop and swap raises separate distribution, debt, basis and holding-purpose risks; an earlier transfer date is not a safe harbor.
