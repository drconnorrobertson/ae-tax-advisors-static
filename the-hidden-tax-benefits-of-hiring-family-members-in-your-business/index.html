Tax Benefits of Hiring Family Members

AE Tax Advisors · Tax Planning & Strategy

Reviewed November 6, 2026 for 2026 planning.

The potential benefits of hiring family include filling a real business role, building experience, supporting eligible retirement contributions, and using family-employment tax exceptions where they actually apply. Start with reasonable compensation for actual services. Neither a family relationship nor a wage deduction proves the family's total taxes will fall.

Spouse Wages: Use One Joint-Return Calculation

For married filing jointly, both spouses' income and allowable deductions enter the same return. Paying $100,000 to a spouse does not give that spouse an independent 24% bracket while the business owner remains in a 35% or 37% bracket. The business deduction and taxable wage must be modeled together. See IRS Publication 501.

A spouse's wages also do not automatically reduce unrelated investment income or eliminate Net Investment Income Tax. Income classification, net investment income, and the household's modified adjusted gross income need separate analysis under the IRS NIIT rules. Consider the actual services, payroll taxes, retirement eligibility, QBI, and state taxes before claiming a benefit.

Self-Employment Tax Is Not a Gross-Wage Savings Formula

Paying a family employee may reduce self-employment profit, but can introduce or increase employer and employee payroll taxes. An actual spouse-employee of a sole proprietor generally has wages subject to income-tax withholding and Social Security and Medicare taxes, with a FUTA exception. Corporate or partnership employers have different rules. Partners generally are not W-2 employees of their own partnership. See Publication 15 and IRS Paying Yourself.

Do not present $100,000 of wages multiplied by 15.3% as net savings. Recompute the owner's self-employment tax, including the net-earnings adjustment and Social Security wage base; then include both payroll tax shares, other earnings, Additional Medicare Tax where applicable, income-tax effects, and administration costs. A spouse who co-owns and operates the business may require partnership or qualified-joint-venture analysis instead of employee treatment.

Child Payroll Exceptions Require the Right Employer

For a child working in a parent's sole proprietorship or a partnership where every partner is a parent of the child, qualifying business wages are exempt from Social Security and Medicare before age 18 and from FUTA before age 21. Ages 18 through 20 therefore require a different payroll treatment from under-18 wages. Corporate employers, including S corporations and C corporations, and partnerships with a nonparent partner do not receive these child-specific exceptions.

A single-member LLC disregarded for income tax generally reports employment taxes under its own name and EIN. The family-employment regulations nevertheless look to its owner for the specific FICA and FUTA family exceptions, if all requirements are met. An LLC electing S-corporation or C-corporation taxation is different. Confirm the actual employer, owner, election, and services; an LLC label or a new management company does not create an exemption. See the IRS LLC reporting guidance and the FICA and FUTA family-employment regulations.

Federal income-tax withholding is a separate question from FICA or FUTA. Business wages paid to a child are generally subject to withholding regardless of age. An employee claiming exemption on Form W-4 generally must have had no federal income-tax liability in the prior year and expect none in the current year. Being young, dependent, or paid below a particular amount does not by itself establish that exemption. Retain a valid W-4, renew an exempt certificate as required, and check state rules separately.

2026 Child Income and the Limits of the Tax Benefit

For 2026, a dependent's basic standard deduction is generally the greater of $1,350 or earned income plus $450, capped at the applicable basic standard deduction, ordinarily $16,100 for a single filer. Other income, filing status, age or blindness, and eligibility to use the standard deduction can change the result. A zero federal income-tax calculation does not remove payroll reporting or applicable state tax obligations.

Illustrative wage deduction: If a single dependent with no other income earns $14,600 for genuine business work in 2026 and is eligible for the standard deduction, the basic dependent deduction is $15,050. That can leave no federal taxable income from those wages. The employer's $14,600 wage deduction is not a measured net saving of $5,110: employer eligibility, payroll taxes, QBI, the parent's other tax facts, and state taxes still determine the family result.

Wages for actual work are earned income. In 2026, unearned income over $2,700 may trigger the kiddie-tax calculation if the age, support, filing, and other conditions apply. The rule can reach certain age-18 children and full-time students ages 19 through 23. Employment does not shield a child's separate investment income from these rules. See IRS Topic 553.

QBI: Payroll Is Not a Dollar-for-Dollar Deduction Increase

Qualifying W-2 wages can affect a Section 199A wage limitation when that limitation applies, but deductible wages also reduce business profit and potentially QBI. The wage limitation generally considers 50% of qualifying W-2 wages or an alternative based on 25% of wages plus 2.5% of the unadjusted basis immediately after acquisition of qualified property, subject to the applicable rules. Paying $200,000 of wages does not increase the deduction or wage limit by $200,000. Wages are not the employee's QBI. See the IRS QBI guidance and Form 8995-A instructions for the wage and property limitation.

Retirement and Experience Can Matter Without a Tax Windfall

Eligible compensation can support retirement contributions subject to plan terms, coverage, income, and annual limits. For 2026, a person under age 50 generally has a combined traditional and Roth IRA limit of $7,500, limited further by eligible compensation and applicable rules. Pre-tax contributions generally defer tax; Roth contributions are after-tax and qualified distributions may be tax-free. A hire should be useful to the business even before projected savings. See the 2026 IRS retirement limits.

Make the Decision With Documentation

Record duties, work performed, hours, reasonable pay comparisons, approvals, and actual payments. Check the correct W-4, payroll return, deposit, and W-2 requirements, plus state unemployment, workers' compensation, and child-labor rules. Evaluate covered Social Security earnings separately from wages excluded under a family exception.

Before starting payroll, ask for a comparison of the current arrangement and the proposed hire across the business, worker, and household returns. Bring the ownership and tax classification, proposed duties and compensation, payroll summaries, and benefit-plan details through a secure document channel. The family-employment planning guide explains the full comparison, and the child-employment guide covers hiring checks.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does an S-Corp election reduce income tax?

An S election can reduce employment taxes on business profit remaining after reasonable compensation. Income-tax results can also change, including through the Section 199A qualified business income deduction, so compare the combined tax effect rather than assuming income tax is unchanged.

Does paying a lower-earning spouse create separate income-tax brackets?

No, not when the spouses file jointly. Their income and allowable deductions are combined on one return. A wage deduction and the recipient's taxable wage must be considered together with payroll, QBI, retirement, and state-tax effects.

Are all family employees exempt from payroll taxes?

No. The employer's tax classification, ownership, relationship, age, and services determine the rules. Qualifying child business wages have a FICA exception before 18 and a FUTA exception before 21; corporate employment does not receive those child-specific exceptions. Income-tax withholding is separate.

Does adding family payroll increase QBI dollar for dollar?

No. Qualifying wages can affect a wage limitation when it applies, but the wage deduction also reduces profit and may reduce QBI. The limitation is a formula, not a dollar-for-dollar credit for wages, and employee wages are not the recipient's QBI.
