Family Management Company Strategy

AE Tax Advisors · Tax Planning & Strategy

Reviewed November 6, 2026 for 2026 planning.

A family management company can centralize real property management, bookkeeping, administration, or operational support. A second entity is useful only when its business purpose, services, ownership, and costs support the arrangement. An invoice does not automatically create a deduction, and moving income between family-owned entities does not establish net family tax savings.

Begin With the Operational Need

Identify the work now being performed, which business benefits, who supervises staff, and whether a separate company would improve delivery. Compare the structure with keeping employees in the operating business or hiring an unrelated provider. Include formation and annual state fees, bookkeeping, payroll, insurance, additional returns, and contract administration in the decision.

An LLC is a legal form, not a single federal tax result. A management business might be disregarded, a partnership, an S corporation, or a C corporation depending on ownership and elections. A fee between two activities of the same disregarded owner generally is not a separate income-tax deduction and receipt simply because two invoices or bank accounts exist. Corporate tax and later distributions require a different analysis.

Real Services, Reasonable Charges, and Deduction Timing

Use a written service agreement defining tasks, staff, properties or operations served, pricing, and payment terms. Retain time records, deliverables, invoices, evidence of payment, and comparable third-party quotes. For an operating business, Section 162 requires an ordinary and necessary business expense; amounts tied to an acquisition, construction, or improvement may need capitalization rather than an immediate deduction. Related-party rules can also affect the timing of unpaid fees. The recipient must report the corresponding income under its applicable rules.

The 3-10% of gross rental income range sometimes used in a preliminary property-management model is an illustrative assumption, not a published AE price or evidence that a particular fee is reasonable. Test the actual location, property type, tenant workload, included services, and unrelated provider quotes. A round percentage selected to reach a desired tax result is not a pricing study.

Joint-Return Spouses Do Not Have Separate Brackets

A spouse who owns the management company does not independently occupy a 24% bracket while the other spouse occupies a 37% bracket when they file jointly. A joint return combines their income and allowable deductions. Moving $100,000 of taxable income from one spouse's activity to the other's therefore does not by itself establish $13,000 of federal savings. Payroll, QBI, retirement contributions, state taxes, and the genuine costs of the services can still affect the household result.

Ownership by an adult child or a trust also needs substantive review. Compensation must follow actual services, income allocations must follow valid ownership and applicable tax rules, and trust income is not automatically taxed at a lower rate. Grantor-trust rules may attribute income back to the grantor. Do not treat a family name on formation documents as sufficient to move taxable income.

Pass-Through Allocations and Working Owners

An S corporation generally allocates tax items under its share-ownership rules, while a partnership follows its agreement subject to tax allocation requirements. A 50-50 ownership label does not establish the same result for every entity or item. Working shareholders may need reasonable W-2 compensation; partners generally are not employees of their partnership. See Publication 541 and IRS Paying Yourself.

A Management Company Does Not Manufacture Child Payroll Exemptions

Follow the real employer, worker, and entity classification. For a child in a qualifying parent sole proprietorship or a partnership where every partner is a parent of the child, Social Security and Medicare exemptions end at 18 and the FUTA exemption ends at 21. A corporate employer does not get these child-specific exceptions. Business-child wages generally remain subject to federal income-tax withholding rules unless a separate valid exemption applies. See Publication 15.

A single-member LLC disregarded for income tax generally reports employment taxes under its own name and EIN. The family-employment regulations nevertheless look to its owner for the specific FICA and FUTA family exceptions, if all requirements are met. An LLC electing S-corporation or C-corporation taxation is different. Confirm the actual employer, owner, election, and services; an LLC label or a new management company does not create an exemption. See the IRS LLC reporting guidance and the FICA and FUTA family-employment regulations.

Routing a corporation's payroll through a newly named family entity is not enough. Determine which entity actually employs and directs the worker, what services each business provides, who bears payroll obligations, and whether the contracts and records match the work. Retirement controlled-group and affiliated-service-group rules can also require combined review; an extra entity is not an automatic way to exclude employees from benefit plans.

Illustrative Real Estate Management Calculation

Assume a family partnership owns 10 rentals with $200,000 of annual gross income and purchases genuine management services for $18,000. That fee equals 9% of gross income. The percentage is an assumption to test against the actual services and market, not a finding that the rate is appropriate. If the full fee is currently deductible, the payer has an $18,000 expense and the management company has $18,000 of gross service revenue before its own expenses.

Those two entries alone do not establish $6,300 of family tax savings. Reconcile the recipient's expenses, taxable profit, wages or self-employment tax, owner allocations, passive-activity consequences, QBI, state taxes, and added compliance costs. Where the same taxpayers report both sides, income may largely move between returns or schedules rather than disappear. A deduction can also have a different timing or usability from the recipient's income.

Before Forming or Transferring an Entity

  • List each business, its tax classification, owners, staff, and services needed.
  • Obtain comparable fees and prepare the service agreement and recordkeeping process.
  • Model both sides of the fee, employment taxes, income-tax effects, annual costs, and any benefit-plan aggregation.
  • Separate operational succession from transfers of ownership. Have counsel review governance, valuation, gift and estate consequences before transferring interests.
  • Compare a second entity with a simpler arrangement, then decide whether the operational value and net financial effect justify it.

Bring an ownership chart, current management contracts, proposed duties and fees, payroll summaries, and the existing accounting treatment to a review. Use a secure document channel for personal records. For additional context, read the management company structure guide if you are comparing a second entity with your current operations.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a management company owned by my spouse create a lower tax bracket?

Not on a joint return simply by moving the income. Spouses filing jointly combine their income and allowable deductions. Model the actual payroll, QBI, retirement, state-tax, and cost differences rather than assuming separate spouse brackets.

Is a management-company invoice automatically deductible?

No. Verify the actual services, business purpose, reasonable charge, payment and accounting method, related-party timing, and any capitalization requirement. The recipient must account for the fee as well; a payer deduction alone is not net family savings.

Can a management company make corporate child wages payroll-tax exempt?

Not merely by routing the payments through another entity. Determine the real employer, ownership, tax classification, and services. The qualifying parent-business exceptions use different age limits for FICA and FUTA, and corporate employment does not qualify for those child-specific exceptions.

When is a second management entity worth considering?

When genuine centralized services or an operational need justify the additional entity. Compare actual costs, both sides of the fees, payroll and income taxes, benefit-plan rules, governance, and simpler alternatives before forming it.
