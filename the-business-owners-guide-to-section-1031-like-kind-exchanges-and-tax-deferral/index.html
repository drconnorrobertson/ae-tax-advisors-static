The Business Owner's Guide to Section 1031 Like-Kind Exchanges and Tax Deferral

AE Tax Advisors · Tax Planning & Strategy

IRC Section 1031 can defer gain when qualifying investment or business real estate is exchanged for like-kind real property. It does not make every sale and reinvestment tax-free. Before closing, compare a taxable sale with an exchange using adjusted basis, debt, cash needed outside the deal, replacement financing, and both federal and state tax treatment. For example, a $3 million sale with a $1.5 million adjusted basis has $1.5 million of realized gain before selling costs; the tax cannot be determined from appreciation alone. The benefit is generally deferral, with the deferred gain reflected in replacement-property basis.

The Section 1031 Mechanics: Exchange vs. Sale

Under IRC Section 1031(a), qualifying real property must be held for investment or productive use in a trade or business, and replacement property must be acquired for a qualifying purpose. An ordinary sale followed by a purchase is not enough. In a deferred exchange, arrange the exchange and restrictions on access to proceeds before transferring the relinquished property.

The process typically involves:

  • Identification of relinquished property (the property being sold)
  • Engagement of a qualified intermediary for the usual deferred-exchange safe harbor before closing
  • Sale of relinquished property; proceeds held by qualified intermediary
  • Identification of replacement property within 45 calendar days
  • Acquisition by the earlier of 180 calendar days or the transfer-year federal return due date, including extensions
  • Closing of replacement property acquisition; title transfer to exchangor

Choose the Intermediary Before Closing

A qualified intermediary (QI) is the usual safe harbor under Treasury Regulation 1.1031(k)-1(g)(4) for a delayed exchange. A written exchange agreement must meet the acquisition, transfer, and access-restriction rules. A QI is not required for every possible exchange, including a properly structured direct simultaneous exchange. Review the provider's fund controls, insurance, financial stability, and applicable state requirements before sending proceeds.

Actual or constructive receipt of all sale proceeds before receiving replacement property generally makes the transaction a sale rather than a deferred exchange. A permissible receipt of cash in an otherwise qualifying exchange can instead create partial taxable boot. A disqualified person cannot serve as QI: the rules generally cover recent agents and specified related persons, with exceptions for prior exchange services and routine financial, title-insurance, escrow, or trust services described in IRS Publication 544.

The 45-Day Identification Period

Identify replacement property in a signed written document within 45 calendar days after the relinquished property is transferred. Deliver it to the QI or another permitted exchange participant, not to yourself or a disqualified person, and describe the property unambiguously. Do not rely on an unsigned email or a private list you have not delivered.

The "Three-Property Rule": The investor can identify up to three replacement properties without limit. OR the investor can identify more than three properties, provided the total value of identified properties does not exceed 200% of the relinquished property value.

Example: A $1 million property is sold. The investor can identify:

  • Up to 3 properties of any value, OR
  • More than 3 properties as long as their combined value does not exceed $2 million (200% of $1 million)

The 180-Day Exchange Period

Receive the replacement property by the earlier of 180 calendar days after the relinquished-property transfer or the due date of the federal return for the transfer year, including extensions. The identification and exchange periods run concurrently. There are at most 135 days after day 45 under the ordinary 180-day rule, and fewer if an earlier return due date controls. Arrange a timely return extension when needed; an extension does not restart the 180-day clock.

Ordinary illness, travel, weekends, holidays, financing delays, or a lack of suitable property do not extend these deadlines. Applicable IRS relief, including qualifying disaster relief under Revenue Procedure 2018-58, can postpone them. Verify the specific IRS notice, covered taxpayer or transaction, and affected dates rather than assuming a disaster declaration automatically grants more time.

Reinvestment, Cash Boot, and Recognized Gain

Trading equal or up in value and reinvesting exchange equity are useful planning checks, but the tax result depends on cash received, non-like-kind property, liabilities, eligible exchange expenses, and any separate recapture. Boot generally causes recognition up to realized gain. Buying a larger replacement property or borrowing more does not by itself eliminate cash retained outside the exchange.

Simplified example: sell qualifying real estate for $1 million with a $600,000 adjusted basis and acquire a $950,000 replacement, keeping $50,000 cash. Assuming no debt, exchange expenses, or separate recapture, realized gain is $400,000, recognized gain is $50,000, and deferred gain is $350,000. Replacement basis is $600,000: the $950,000 value less $350,000 deferred gain.

Real Property vs. Personal Property

For current exchanges, Section 1031 is limited to qualifying real property. The historical transition rule for certain exchanges begun before 2018 is not an exception for equipment merely because it was purchased before 2018. Identify each asset separately: some permanently affixed structures and structural components can be real property under the regulations even when depreciation classifications differ.

Allowed Property Types: Land, buildings, commercial real estate, residential rental property, and other real property used in investment or business.

Generally excluded: Standalone equipment, vehicles, artwork, inventory or other property held primarily for sale, and ordinary partnership interests and securities. Limited statutory or regulatory exceptions require separate review.

Reverse Exchanges and Simultaneous Closings

A forward exchange transfers the relinquished property first. For a reverse exchange, Revenue Procedure 2000-37, as modified by Revenue Procedure 2004-51, provides a safe harbor using an exchange accommodation titleholder (EAT) and a qualified exchange accommodation arrangement (QEAA). The EAT holds qualified indications of ownership of the parked property and is treated as its beneficial owner for federal tax purposes. This is a separate role from a QI; simply buying the replacement yourself first is not the safe harbor.

The written QEAA must be signed within 5 business days after ownership is transferred to the EAT. When replacement property is parked, identify the relinquished property within 45 days. Complete the required transfer within 180 days, and keep combined parking within 180 days, subject to applicable relief. The safe harbor does not cover replacement property you owned during the 180 days before transfer to the EAT. Transactions outside the safe harbor need specialist analysis, not an assumption that they automatically qualify or fail.

Delayed Exchanges and Parking Arrangements

A parking arrangement can let an EAT hold the replacement or relinquished property while the exchange is completed. For an improvement exchange, coordinate which qualifying improvements will actually be received before the deadline and transfer to you. Work completed after you receive the property generally is not replacement property received in the exchange.

Destination State/Entity Considerations

The replacement property need not be located in the same state or jurisdiction as the relinquished property. A real estate investor can sell a residential rental property in New York and acquire a commercial property in Florida as the replacement, provided both are real property held for investment or business.

Like-kind concerns the nature or character of the real-property interest, not identical use: qualifying land can be exchanged for a building. U.S. real property is not like-kind to foreign real property. Foreign-to-foreign exchanges may qualify, but local law, U.S. tax reporting, and the exact interests exchanged need review.

Debt Assumption and Mortgage Planning

Liability relief must be netted against liabilities assumed and additional cash or other qualifying offsets paid in the exchange. A smaller replacement mortgage does not automatically create mortgage boot. Review Form 8824 using both closing statements, not only the two loan balances.

Simplified example: the relinquished property sells for $1 million with a $400,000 mortgage, leaving $600,000 exchange equity. The $1 million replacement uses a $200,000 mortgage, all $600,000 of exchange equity, and $200,000 of additional cash. Ignoring expenses and other property, the $200,000 debt reduction is offset by the additional cash, so there is no net debt-relief boot. If the replacement instead costs $800,000 and uses only $600,000 exchange equity plus $200,000 debt, the unoffset $200,000 debt relief may be boot, limited by realized gain.

Delaware Statutory Trusts (DSTs) and Tenancy in Common (TIC) Structures

A properly structured tenancy-in-common (TIC) interest may represent direct co-ownership of real property. Certain Delaware statutory trusts (DSTs) qualify under the facts and restrictions of Revenue Ruling 2004-86 because the investor is treated as owning an interest in the underlying real estate. Neither structure qualifies merely because of its label, and ordinary interests in real estate partnerships generally are not qualifying replacement property.

Before selecting a DST or TIC investment, review the ownership and tax classification, sponsor documents, fees, debt allocation, transfer restrictions, and investment risk. Passive management is an investment feature, not a substitute for meeting the exchange rules.

Depreciation Recapture and Section 1250 Considerations

A qualifying real-property exchange can defer gain attributable to depreciation, subject to separate Section 1245 and Section 1250 recapture rules. For individuals, unrecaptured Section 1250 gain has a maximum 25% federal rate; ordinary-income recapture is a different category. Boot or a taxable sale can require current gain recognition, but one blanket rate cannot calculate the tax. Review asset classifications, prior cost-segregation deductions, carryover basis, NIIT applicability, and state treatment.

Key Takeaways for Real Estate Investors

  • Section 1031 can defer qualifying gain; deferred gain generally remains embedded in replacement basis
  • Arrange the usual QI safe harbor and restricted access to proceeds before the relinquished-property closing
  • Identify within 45 days and receive by the earlier of 180 days or the return due date, including extensions; check applicable IRS relief
  • Model cash, liabilities, exchange expenses, and recapture instead of relying only on replacement price
  • Real property only (2018+); personal property exchanges no longer qualify
  • Additional cash can offset debt reduction; cash retained is not erased simply by buying a larger replacement
  • DST and TIC interests require review of the actual ownership structure and qualification
  • Coordinate replacement basis and depreciation with the exchange; the full purchase price is not automatically a new depreciable basis

The Bottom Line

Before you commit to an exchange, obtain a side-by-side projection of a taxable sale, full exchange, and partial exchange with cash retained. Bring the adjusted-basis and depreciation schedules, debt payoff, proposed replacement financing, signed identification, QI agreement, ownership records, and closing dates. A deferred tax bill is valuable only if the replacement investment and liquidity plan also fit your goals.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a 1031 exchange defer depreciation recapture?

A qualifying real-property exchange can defer gain attributable to depreciation, but full deferral is not automatic. Boot and the separate Section 1245 and Section 1250 recapture rules can require current recognition. Unrecaptured Section 1250 gain for individuals has a maximum 25% federal rate; it is not a flat 25% ordinary-income tax on all depreciation. Track deferred gain and carryover basis for a later taxable disposition.

What property qualifies for a 1031 exchange?

Both properties must be qualifying real property held for investment or business use. A rental house may be like-kind to a warehouse or raw land. Personal-use residences and property held primarily for sale do not qualify. U.S. real property is not like-kind to foreign real property, but foreign-to-foreign exchanges may qualify if the other requirements are met.

What is boot in a 1031 exchange?

Boot includes cash, non-like-kind property, and net debt relief after permitted offsets. Additional cash paid can replace debt, so equal replacement borrowing is not mandatory. A more expensive replacement or extra borrowing does not by itself erase cash retained. Recognized gain is generally limited to the lesser of realized gain or boot after eligible exchange expenses, with recapture rules reviewed separately.

Can I 1031 exchange a short-term rental?

Yes, provided it is genuinely held for investment or business use rather than personal enjoyment. Significant personal use undermines the position, and the exchange must be structured before closing on the sale, not afterward.
