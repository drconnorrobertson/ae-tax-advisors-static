Real Estate Developer Tax Planning: Projects, Entities and Exit Decisions

AE Tax Advisors · Updated November 6, 2026

A developer building property for sale, an owner developing a long-term rental and a contractor building for a customer can have very different tax results. Start with each project's purpose and contracts. A proposed tax strategy should explain which activity it applies to, when a decision is required and who will implement it.

Classify the project before choosing the tax strategy

Property held primarily for sale to customers in the ordinary course of a trade or business generally produces ordinary business income. Holding it for more than a year does not by itself turn inventory profit into long-term capital gain. Rental or investment property requires a separate factual analysis, including acquisition purpose, development and sales activity, actual use and any change in intent.

Keep a project-by-project schedule rather than treating every LLC as proof of investment status. An entity name or a lease signed shortly before a sale does not independently establish the desired tax treatment. See IRS Publication 544. If classification could change the planned sale or exchange, resolve it with the tax advisor and counsel before committing to the transaction.

When can a developer use installment reporting or a 1031 exchange?

Installment reporting is not a general deferral option for developer inventory. Dealer dispositions generally are excluded, with narrow statutory exceptions and special rules that require separate review. Even an eligible installment sale can have current depreciation recapture, interest, related-party and other consequences. Seller financing changes collection timing; it does not automatically change ordinary income into capital gain. Consult Publication 537.

Section 1031 applies to qualifying real property held for investment or productive use in a trade or business, not property held primarily for sale. Repeatedly buying, developing and selling projects does not automatically qualify as a series of tax-deferred exchanges. For an eligible deferred exchange, identification and completion deadlines, ownership continuity, receipt of proceeds and intermediary arrangements still matter. See the Form 8824 instructions before signing or closing.

Construction accounting is not a free choice between methods

First distinguish construction performed under a customer contract from development for the owner's own account. Section 460 generally requires percentage-of-completion reporting for covered long-term contracts unless an exception applies. A project lasting several tax years does not by itself establish which rules govern it.

The small-contractor exception includes an expected completion period of no more than two years and an applicable gross-receipts test. Related-business aggregation and tax-shelter restrictions need review. Residential-construction exceptions and contract-date changes also matter. Review the Form 8697 instructions, project contracts and the applicable year's rules before assuming completed-contract treatment is available.

A permissible method can affect timing, look-back calculations and alternative minimum tax. Changing an established method may require consent and Form 3115; it is not simply a choice made when preparing the next return. Keep lender and bonding reports distinct from the tax-method analysis. Our construction-owner guide describes the records to assemble.

Capitalization, inventory and the move into rental use

Land and buildings developed for customer sale are not depreciated as rental assets merely because construction is finished. Costs may remain in inventory until sale. A property held for rental or another income-producing use needs a supported basis allocation and a placed-in-service date before depreciation begins. Land remains nondepreciable.

Construction interest and other direct or indirect production costs can require capitalization under Section 263A and other rules. Small-business exceptions, aggregation and the character of each cost need review; an exception to one provision does not establish that every cost is immediately deductible. See Section 263A and Publication 551.

For a completed rental or operating property, cost segregation may identify eligible shorter-life components. Asset function, ownership, acquisition and service dates, elections and depreciation-system rules govern treatment. A study does not establish that all build-out costs receive bonus depreciation or that an owner can use the resulting loss immediately. Review Publication 946 and the commercial study scope.

Entity and investor terms should fit funding and exit plans

Compare investor eligibility, financing, distributions, compensation, state taxes, legal protections and the cost of unwinding a structure. An LLC is a legal form, not a single federal tax classification. An S corporation has shareholder and stock restrictions and reasonable-compensation requirements; it is not limited to one owner, and it is not automatically suitable for appreciated real estate.

A partnership's allocation of depreciation, income or gain cannot be chosen solely to produce the best tax result for each investor. The partnership agreement, economic arrangement, capital accounts and Section 704 rules must support it. See Publication 541. Review guarantees, debt allocations and planned distributions with counsel and the preparer before accepting capital.

Passing a loss through an entity does not guarantee a current personal deduction. Basis, at-risk, passive-activity and excess-business-loss limits may apply in sequence. Self-rental rules generally recharacterize net rental income from a business in which the owner materially participates; they do not automatically make rental losses nonpassive. A permissible grouping needs its own factual and consistency analysis. See Publication 925.

Opportunity-zone analysis needs the investment date and gain type

Do not apply a historical opportunity-zone illustration to a new investment without checking the governing regime. Legacy deferred gains generally face inclusion no later than December 31, 2026. A new 2026 investment cannot earn a seven-year holding-period benefit before that date. Post-2026 rules require a separate analysis of effective dates, qualifying gains, fund and business requirements, and the investment's economics.

Dealer-sale profit is not automatically an eligible capital gain, and a ten-year holding period does not make every fund distribution tax-free. Request current advice using the IRS opportunity-zone guidance and applicable law rather than relying on an old marketing example.

What to request in a developer tax-planning engagement

Ask for a written project classification, permitted accounting-method analysis, entity and investor-term questions, a financing and exit comparison, and an implementation calendar. Assign legal drafting, valuation, engineering studies, bookkeeping, elections and return filing to named professionals. A planning proposal should state which services are included and how the existing CPA receives the work.

For the first discussion, describe whether you build for sale, hold rentals or perform customer contracts; the project stage; approximate budget; ownership; and the next transaction deadline. Before detailed modeling, arrange secure delivery of contracts, cost ledgers, financing documents, operating agreements and relevant returns.

An illustrative review might compare selling a completed project with retaining it as a rental. The first questions are classification, actual use, financing and investor rights, not a promised savings percentage. The tax result follows the supported facts and available methods.

See entity-planning services and published engagement fees. Confirm the signed scope and outside-provider costs before work starts.

Frequently asked questions

Can a developer use a 1031 exchange when selling a completed project?

Only if the property and transaction qualify. Section 1031 excludes property held primarily for sale. Development for customer sale does not automatically become investment property because it is held in an LLC or retained for more than a year.

Can a builder choose completed-contract accounting?

Not freely. Determine whether Section 460 applies and whether a specific exception is available, using contract type and date, expected duration, gross receipts and related-business rules. An established method change can require consent.

Can I depreciate a completed development before it is sold?

Development inventory is not depreciated merely because construction is finished. Property held for rental or another qualifying income-producing use requires a supported basis and placed-in-service date. Land is not depreciable.

Will partnership losses offset my other business income?

Not automatically. Partner basis, at-risk, passive-activity and excess-business-loss limitations can restrict current deductions. Allocations also must be supported by the partnership agreement and applicable tax rules.
