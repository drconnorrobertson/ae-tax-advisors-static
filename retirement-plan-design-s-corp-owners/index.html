Retirement Plan Design for S Corporation Owners: Payroll and Advisor Handoff

A coordination checklist for the owner, payroll provider, plan specialist and tax preparer.

AE Tax Advisors Team · Updated November 6, 2026

S corporation distributions do not create retirement-plan contribution capacity. Eligible compensation paid to the shareholder-employee matters, but wages are not the only input to plan design. Employee eligibility, related businesses, existing plans, plan terms and available funding can change the result.

The useful next step is a coordinated payroll, plan-administrator and tax review. It is not a blanket instruction to increase salary or adopt a plan because profit crossed a threshold.

Which S corporation income counts?

The IRS S corporation contribution guidance distinguishes compensation from shareholder distributions. Contributions depend on eligible compensation under the applicable plan and tax rules; pass-through income does not become compensation simply because it is reported on the owner's return.

A sole proprietor or partner uses a different calculation based on adjusted net earnings from self-employment, not a simple percentage of all accounting profit. See Publication 560. Rental cash flow and S corporation distributions should not be added to the contribution base without identifying why they qualify.

Reasonable compensation still needs its own analysis

Document the owner's services, responsibilities, hours and comparable compensation. An S corporation must pay reasonable compensation for shareholder services before treating payments as nonwage distributions. Compensation deductions also have reasonableness requirements; calling the analysis a minimum floor does not mean an unlimited salary deduction is automatically permissible.

Do not compare a single payroll-tax percentage with a headline income-tax rate and conclude that increasing wages must save money. Social Security and Medicare thresholds, other wages, employer deductions, QBI, retirement limits, state rules and cash flow all affect the calculation. See IRS compensation guidance and Publication 334.

Give the plan specialist a complete employee and ownership census

An owner-only 401(k) cannot be used to exclude otherwise eligible employees. Ask the administrator to review employee ages, service, compensation and ownership, including businesses related through controlled-group or affiliated-service-group rules. A separate LLC or payroll account does not necessarily create a separate employer for plan testing.

List other employer plans and employee deferrals made elsewhere. Individual deferral limits and employer-plan limits are different constraints. The IRS one-participant 401(k) guidance explains the owner/spouse framework and employee considerations. See our related-business retirement guide before modeling each entity independently.

Compare the complete cost before changing payroll

  1. Compensation: Establish a supported wage range for the actual role and identify payroll-tax and state effects.
  2. Plan design: Ask the administrator or actuary for eligible compensation, employee obligations, permitted contributions and any combined-plan constraints.
  3. Tax model: Have the tax preparer compare the proposed structure with the current one, including QBI, employer deductions, owner tax and future taxable distributions where applicable.
  4. Cash commitment: Include employee funding, administration, investment costs and money needed for the business or properties.
  5. Timing: Assign plan adoption, payroll elections, contribution deposits and employer funding separately. A filing extension does not extend every deadline.

A contribution is money committed to a retirement arrangement, not an equal amount of tax saved. Traditional tax-deferred funding and Roth treatment have different current and future consequences.

Adding a cash balance plan requires more than higher wages

A cash balance plan is a defined benefit arrangement. Its benefit formula, participant ages, service, compensation, plan assets and actuarial assumptions affect funding. An age-and-profit illustration is not a contribution quote. Minimum funding, employee coverage and ongoing administration can make a large projected deduction unsuitable for a business with uncertain cash flow.

Request a written feasibility analysis with a funding range, employee costs and lower-profit scenarios. Coordinate any 401(k) or profit-sharing plan rather than adding each headline maximum. See cash balance feasibility and funding questions.

When a spouse works in the business

Document genuine services, reasonable compensation, actual payroll and plan eligibility. A spouse may have separate contribution capacity, but that does not automatically double the household's available amount. Individual limits, other plans, compensation, ownership attribution and testing still apply. Do not create wages for services that were not performed.

Illustrative handoff: an owner with an operating company and rentals

An owner receives S corporation wages and distributions and holds rental interests through another entity. Before changing payroll, the advisor needs the current wage analysis, year-to-date payroll, employee census, ownership map, existing plan documents, other deferrals and funding forecast. Rental income may affect cash availability without being eligible plan compensation.

The deliverable should show alternatives and responsibilities, not merely the largest contribution number. The payroll provider implements approved payroll instructions; the plan administrator or actuary confirms plan requirements; the preparer handles the agreed tax reporting; the owner approves the funding commitment.

Define the engagement and implementation boundaries

Ask who provides compensation support, plan design, actuarial work, payroll changes, annual administration and return preparation. Confirm whether each task is included or separately priced. Use reasonable-compensation services, the engagement scorecard and published pricing to frame the discussion.

For the initial call, describe the entity, employee count, related businesses and decision deadline. Arrange secure intake before sending payroll or employee records.

Frequently asked questions

Can S corporation distributions count toward retirement contributions?

No. Shareholder distributions are not earned compensation for this purpose. Eligible compensation and the applicable plan rules determine contribution capacity; pass-through profit does not substitute for wages.

Should I raise my salary to contribute more to retirement?

Not without a combined compensation, plan and tax analysis. Compare reasonable pay, employee obligations, payroll and state taxes, QBI, funding needs and contribution limits before changing payroll.

Is paying myself more than reasonable compensation never a problem?

No. Reasonableness is relevant to the compensation deduction as well as avoiding underpayment for shareholder services. A proposed wage should be supported by the role and facts, not selected solely to generate a retirement contribution.

Can I pay my spouse to increase household contributions?

A spouse who performs genuine services may receive reasonable compensation and participate if eligible. The available amount depends on compensation, plan rules, other plans and testing; it does not automatically double household capacity.
