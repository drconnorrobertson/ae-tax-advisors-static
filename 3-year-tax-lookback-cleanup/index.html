Three-Year Tax Lookback and Records Cleanup

Turn incomplete business and property records into an organized package your tax reviewer and return preparer can evaluate.

AE Tax Advisors Team · Published January 6, 2026 · Updated November 6, 2026

A prior-year review is difficult when filed returns, bookkeeping balances and property schedules tell different stories. The first job is to identify which records exist, reconcile supported differences and document what remains unresolved. Finding a discrepancy does not establish a deduction, refund or filing remedy.

This service is designed for individuals and businesses earning $500,000 or more annually, particularly owners with multiple businesses, practices or real estate holdings. That amount describes the intended client fit; it is not an IRS threshold for claiming a deduction or correcting a return.

Start with an entity-by-year inventory

Agree on the review period and covered entities before work begins. A three-year intake window is a practical starting scope, not a reason to discard older records. Acquisition documents, basis calculations, depreciation histories and carryforwards may require earlier information.

  • Entities and ownership: Identify operating businesses, property entities, ownership changes and owners whose returns may be affected.
  • Filed returns: Inventory federal and state returns, amendments, partnership adjustments, schedules and filing confirmations for each covered year.
  • Bookkeeping: Locate year-end trial balances, general ledgers, bank and credit-card statements, loan statements and prior adjusting entries.
  • Property and equipment: Assemble closing statements, improvement invoices, asset purchases and disposals, depreciation schedules and prior cost segregation reports.
  • Owner and intercompany activity: Identify contributions, distributions, reimbursements, loans and transfers needing support or reconciliation.
  • Open matters: List missing documents, unfiled returns, notices, examinations, unresolved preparer questions and deadlines.

Keep taxpayer identifiers and detailed financial records in the agreed secure document channel, not an initial consultation form.

Separate bookkeeping cleanup from tax conclusions

A bank reconciliation supports the cash balance; it does not by itself establish whether an expense is deductible. A ledger entry also does not establish tax basis, ownership or the correct depreciation method.

The cleanup scope should specify which balances will be reconciled, who will obtain missing records and how proposed adjustments will be reviewed. Preserve original records and an explanation of each approved change rather than silently replacing historical figures.

  • Reconcile cash, debt and material balance-sheet accounts to available documents.
  • Compare year-end books with filed returns and identify known book-tax differences.
  • Match related-party transfers without assuming every transfer is income or an expense.
  • Separate supported corrections from unresolved questions and estimates requiring evidence.
  • Identify differences affecting later years, owner returns or state filings.

If ongoing bookkeeping is needed, define it separately from this historical project. See bookkeeping services.

Define the handoff deliverables before hiring

Ask for a written proposal describing the package your preparer will receive. Depending on the agreed work, it may include:

  1. Coverage list: Entities and years reviewed, exclusions and available records.
  2. Reconciliation package: Supported balances, proposed or approved adjusting entries and source-document references.
  3. Missing-record register: Outstanding items, the responsible person and their effect on the review.
  4. Tax issue list: Each discrepancy, affected years and entities, supporting facts and questions requiring a tax conclusion.
  5. Preparer handoff: Next steps, filing responsibilities, unresolved disagreements and deadlines requiring attention.

A useful completed package can conclude that no filing change is warranted or that more evidence is needed. The project should not be sold on an assumption that every review produces a refund.

Name the reviewer, bookkeeper and filing preparer

You can retain your existing CPA. Confirm that the receiving preparer is willing to review the package and discuss reporting questions before purchasing work that may not meet their needs.

  • Owner: Supplies complete records, approves access and information sharing, and identifies known decisions or deadlines.
  • Cleanup specialist: Reconciles agreed accounts, documents proposed entries and preserves the audit trail.
  • Tax reviewer: Evaluates issues and documents tax analysis, limitations and potential next steps within the engagement.
  • Filing preparer: Confirms the returns and procedures they will handle, supporting schedules, signatures, deadlines and delivery of filing evidence.

One provider may perform several roles, but each responsibility should remain explicit. Another advisor's recommendation does not require the preparer to sign a position they cannot support. Decide who resolves questions and what further work requires a separate engagement.

Three years is not a universal refund deadline

For individual refund claims, the general rule considers the original filing date and the tax payment date. Separate rules limit how much tax can be refunded, and exceptions may apply. The review window alone cannot establish that a claim is timely. Ask the responsible preparer to evaluate actual filing, payment and extension records, applicable relief and state deadlines.

See the IRS Form 1040-X instructions and Publication 556. Tell the advisor promptly about a possible deadline rather than waiting for every bookkeeping issue to be resolved.

A finding does not determine the filing procedure

Correcting books and correcting tax reporting are separate decisions. The right procedure depends on the taxpayer, year, issue and applicable rules.

  • An individual return correction may involve Form 1040-X, subject to the relevant requirements.
  • Partnerships subject to the BBA regime generally use an administrative adjustment request for partnership-related items rather than an ordinary amended return. See IRS BBA adjustment guidance.
  • Some issues involve an accounting-method change. The Form 3115 instructions and applicable procedures require separate analysis; a bookkeeping discrepancy does not automatically qualify.

For depreciation issues, see the Form 3115 cost segregation lookback guide. This service focuses on preparing supported records for that decision, not promising a particular remedy.

Confirm scope, fees and completion criteria

Confirm covered entities and years, expected record condition, reconciliation work, review deliverables and preparer coordination. Ask how additional years, missing records or newly discovered entities affect the quote and timeline.

Confirm separately whether the engagement includes bookkeeping entries, returns, amendments, partnership adjustments, accounting-method applications, specialist studies or notice responses. Support duration and any audit-defense coverage depend on the written agreement. Historical cleanup does not promise unlimited future planning or free annual filing.

Use the engagement scorecard and review pricing and engagement options when comparing proposals.

Frequently asked questions

Does a three-year lookback mean all three years can be amended for a refund?

No. Three years describes the initial review scope. Refund eligibility depends on filing and payment dates, refund limits, applicable exceptions and the procedure required for the taxpayer and issue.

Can I keep my current CPA for the tax filings?

Yes, if the firms agree on the handoff. Confirm who reviews the findings, resolves questions, prepares any required filings and tracks deadlines. Your preparer must be able to support the positions they sign.

What happens if records are missing?

Document the missing items, who will obtain them and which conclusions remain unresolved. Agree on any additional reconstruction work and fees before proceeding. Missing evidence should not be replaced with an assumed deduction.

Does records cleanup include amendments or Form 3115?

Only if the written engagement includes that work. Cleanup identifies and supports issues for review; the appropriate amendment, partnership adjustment, accounting-method procedure or other response requires separate analysis and assigned filing responsibility.

Discuss Your Records Cleanup Project

Tell us how many entities and years are involved, whether you have an existing preparer and whether a filing or notice deadline is approaching. A general description is enough initially; arrange a secure channel before sharing confidential records.
