Section 163(j) Real Property Election: ADS and Self-Rental

AE Tax Advisors · Tax Planning & Strategy · Updated November 6, 2026

A real property trade or business election can remove interest allocated to an eligible real estate business from the Section 163(j) limitation. The price is required alternative depreciation system (ADS) treatment for specified real property. For an owner with an operating company and a separate building entity, eligibility comes before the tax-savings calculation: leasing to a commonly controlled company can restrict the election.

Start with whether Section 163(j) applies

For tax years beginning in 2026, the small-business exception generally uses average annual gross receipts of $32 million or less for the preceding three tax years. Apply related-business aggregation and the tax-shelter restriction before concluding that a property LLC is exempt. If the exception already applies, an election may create a depreciation cost without a current interest benefit. A protective election is permitted in qualifying circumstances, but it is still a substantive decision.

Do I need real estate professional status to elect?

No. The election concerns an eligible real property trade or business, not an individual's real estate professional status (REPS). Do not confuse the business-activity definition referenced in Section 163(j) with the separate hours and participation tests in Section 469. The election does not itself make rental losses nonpassive, and passive ownership does not by itself prohibit the business from electing.

After the interest calculation, an owner may still face basis, at-risk, passive-activity and excess-business-loss limitations. A current deduction at the entity level is not a promise of a usable deduction on the owner's return. Review the rental-loss usability sequence separately.

My LLC rents the building to my own business: can it elect?

Not automatically. Treasury Regulation 1.163(j)-9(j) contains a special restriction when at least 80% of the real property's fair market rental value is leased to a commonly controlled business. There are defined exceptions and look-through rules, so square footage or the name on the lease alone does not settle the result.

Owner-occupied example: A building company leases its entire factory to a commonly controlled manufacturing company that uses it in its own non-excepted business. Creating the separate lessor does not by itself make the building business eligible to elect. The regulation includes this manufacturing pattern as an example. Obtain an ownership chart, actual use and sublease information before modeling an election. See also self-rental rules, which answer a different tax question.

Which depreciation benefits change?

An electing real property trade or business must use ADS for its nonresidential real property, residential rental property and qualified improvement property. Those affected assets are ineligible for Section 168(k) bonus depreciation. This does not mean every asset identified in a cost-segregation study loses bonus eligibility: analyze properly classified personal property and land improvements under their applicable rules.

A review should reconcile each asset's classification, placed-in-service date, prior method and remaining basis. A building acquisition, renovation and cost-segregation catch-up can create different consequences. Do not apply a single slower-life assumption to the entire study or assume Form 3115 automatically makes an election reversible.

Compare two scenarios over the holding period

  1. No election: calculate annual interest capacity, current deductions, entity-specific carryforwards and depreciation under otherwise applicable methods.
  2. Election, if eligible: remove properly allocated interest from this limitation, recalculate ADS for affected assets, and retain other deduction limits.
  3. Owner result: compare usable deductions and cash taxes under both scenarios, including refinancing, projected income, sale timing and state treatment.

For tax years beginning after 2024, depreciation, amortization and depletion deductions are added back when computing ATI. A model built for 2022–2024 can therefore overstate today's need for an election. Accelerating depreciation does not automatically create extra ATI when the deduction is simply added back.

How is the election made, and can it be reversed?

The taxpayer generally attaches the required statement identifying the taxpayer and electing business to a timely filed original federal return, including extensions. A partnership makes the election for its own business on its return; a partner cannot elect for that business merely by attaching a statement to an individual return. S-corporation status is not a blanket disqualification. Identify the actual taxpayer and eligible trade or business rather than treating every LLC as a separate federal taxpayer.

The election is generally irrevocable. Revenue Procedure 2026-17 provided limited withdrawal procedures for qualifying elections made for tax years beginning in 2022, 2023 or 2024, with a general outside deadline of October 15, 2026 and potentially earlier limitation deadlines. As of this page's November 6, 2026 update, that ordinary window has passed. Do not read the relief as a continuing option to undo any election; confirm whether any separately applicable relief affects your facts.

Records to bring before filing

  • Three-year aggregated gross-receipts calculation and ownership chart.
  • Prior returns, election statements, Forms 8990 and interest carryforward schedules.
  • Loan schedules and an allocation workpaper for excepted and non-excepted activities; collateral alone does not control the allocation.
  • Lease, sublease and actual-use records, including related-party use.
  • Fixed-asset ledger, cost-segregation classifications and projected acquisition, renovation and sale dates.

Use the interest-capacity and carryforward guide for the calculation, or the 2026 rules update for year-specific changes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a passive rental owner make a Section 163(j) real property election?

Passive status alone does not bar an eligible real property trade or business from electing. Election eligibility and the owner’s Section 469 loss treatment are separate analyses.

Can my building LLC elect if it rents to my own company?

Not automatically. Related-party leasing restrictions can prevent or limit the election, depending on common control, rental value, actual use and the regulatory exceptions.

Does the election eliminate bonus depreciation on every cost-seg asset?

The election requires ADS for specified real property and qualified improvement property. Analyze other correctly classified assets separately; do not treat every study component as affected.

Is the real property election reversible?

It is generally irrevocable. Limited relief under Revenue Procedure 2026-17 had an October 15, 2026 outside deadline, subject to earlier limits. That ordinary window has passed as of this page’s update.
