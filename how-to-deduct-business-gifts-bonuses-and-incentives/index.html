Business Gifts, Bonuses and Awards: Deduction and Payroll Rules

By AE Tax Advisors · Published 2025-07-06

Updated November 8, 2026.

Business gifts, employee bonuses and achievement awards follow different tax rules. Classify the payment before choosing a budget or recording the expense. A deductible employer payment may still be taxable to its recipient.

Business gifts generally have a $25 annual limit

The ordinary business-gift deduction limit is generally $25 per recipient per tax year under Section 274(b), with specific exceptions and incidental-cost rules. Giving several gifts to the same person does not restart the annual limit.

Keep the recipient, date, cost and business relationship. A $100 gift does not generally produce a $100 deduction merely because it includes a company logo. Review qualifying low-cost promotional items and indirect gifts under the actual rules.

Entertainment is generally nondeductible

A golf outing or entertainment ticket is not a broadly deductible substitute for a gift. Business entertainment generally is nondeductible even when clients attend. Qualifying meals can have different treatment, including requirements for separate purchase or separately stated food and beverage charges.

A business meal generally faces a 50% limitation when its conditions are met, but specific categories and exceptions differ. Employee meals and employer facilities require current-year review; do not assume every food expense is deductible at the same percentage.

Employee bonuses ordinarily are compensation

Cash bonuses and cash-equivalent gifts such as general-purpose gift cards are generally wages. Apply payroll withholding, employment taxes and reporting. Calling a payment a holiday gift does not remove it from payroll.

The employer deduction depends on the compensation rules, reasonableness, accounting method and payment timing. Accrued bonuses can involve fixed-liability, economic-performance, deferred-compensation and related-party restrictions. An announcement alone does not establish a current deduction.

Achievement awards have narrow requirements

Qualifying employee achievement awards generally concern length of service or safety, must meet the applicable presentation and other requirements, and generally must be tangible personal property. Cash, most gift cards and many other items do not qualify. A sales-performance bonus is not automatically an excludable achievement award.

The employer's deduction limit generally is $400 for nonqualified-plan awards, with a $1,600 aggregate ceiling for qualifying plan awards when the statutory requirements apply. The plan's nondiscrimination and average-cost conditions must also be checked. These are not a $400 qualified-plan limit and a $100 nonqualified limit.

The employee exclusion and employer deduction must each be evaluated. A written label or policy does not make a nonqualifying item tax-free.

Sales commissions need correct worker treatment

Paying a commission instead of a salary does not determine whether a worker is an employee or contractor. The arrangement needs correct classification, payroll or information reporting, contractual support and expense recognition.

Use accurate arithmetic when setting incentives. For example, a commission of 10% of sales is 50% of gross profit when gross profit is 20% of sales, before other expenses. It is not 2% of gross profit.

Prepare the records before year-end

  • Gift recipient and cumulative annual cost.
  • Meal and entertainment categories, receipts and business purpose.
  • Bonus approval, payment dates and payroll records.
  • Award eligibility, plan terms and tangible-property description.
  • Related-party status and the employer's accounting method.

Request a compensation and expense review before relying on the deduction. Match the books, payroll and tax return to the actual payment.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are client entertainment expenses 50% deductible?

Entertainment generally is nondeductible. Qualifying food and beverages need a separate analysis.

Can I give an employee a tax-free cash bonus?

Cash bonuses ordinarily are wages with payroll reporting, even when described as gifts.

Does every employee award qualify for exclusion?

No. The award type, purpose, plan and statutory requirements determine the treatment.
