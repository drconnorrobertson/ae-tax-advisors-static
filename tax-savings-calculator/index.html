2026 federal ordinary-income scenario

Use a reviewed income projection, not business gross receipts, property value or company revenue. In taxable-income mode, do not subtract deductions a second time.

Enter the allowed total after applicable limits, not raw spending. Do not include amounts already subtracted in AGI or the additional change below. This tool does not compute itemized-deduction limits, QBI or special deductions.

Leave zero if unknown. Enter only the net taxable-income reduction after eligibility, deduction limits and interactions have been reviewed. A retirement contribution, study deduction or expense is not automatically the same amount.

What this calculation means

The model applies the progressive 2026 federal ordinary-income rate schedules separately to baseline and proposed taxable income. It does not multiply the entire deduction by the top marginal rate when the reduction crosses brackets. Taxable income is floored at zero; any excess entered reduction is not treated as an automatic loss carryover or refund.

Basic 2026 standard deductions are $16,100 for single or married filing separately, $32,200 for married filing jointly or a qualifying surviving spouse, and $24,150 for head of household. Use that mode only if eligible for the basic amount. Additional age/blindness amounts, dependent rules and restrictions when a separately filing spouse itemizes are not determined here. Use reviewed taxable income or an allowed deduction total when those rules apply.

Limits: This is not a Form 1040 computation. It excludes preferential capital-gain and qualified-dividend rates, AMT, tax credits, payroll and self-employment taxes, Additional Medicare Tax, NIIT, state taxes, withholding and estimated payments. It does not calculate QBI, itemized-deduction limitations, special senior deductions or other interactions. A reduction that changes those items needs a full return projection; use a reviewed net taxable-income change, and do not interpret this result as the final tax difference.

For example, single taxable ordinary income of $100,000 produces $16,712 of rate-schedule tax. Reducing it by $10,000 produces $14,512, a $2,200 difference before excluded items. These are hypothetical inputs, not a client result, eligible strategy or promise of savings.

IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-32, sections 4.01 and 4.14 supplies the 2026 brackets and basic standard deductions. Reviewed November 6, 2026. The rate formula is an illustration; actual filing may use required tax tables and other worksheets.

From an illustration to an implementation decision

Ask the preparer what changes between the two scenarios, which tax year it affects, what supports eligibility and whether it reduces tax now or shifts deductions between years. Separate the cost of making a contribution or buying an asset from the tax difference. A current-year projection is not evidence that earlier returns can be amended for the same amount.

Keep the baseline projection, proposed change, deduction-limit workpapers and implementation dates together. Identify who will prepare the return and what remains to be verified before acting.

Share only a general description in the inquiry. Arrange approved secure delivery before sending tax returns, account details or other financial records. Discuss the review and preparer handoff.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does this calculate a tax refund or amendment recovery?

No. It compares two ordinary-income rate-schedule amounts for 2026. It does not use payments, withholding or prior returns and cannot establish a refund or amendment claim.

Does the calculator decide which deductions I qualify for?

No. Unknown additional deductions start at zero. Use a net taxable-income reduction only after eligibility, limits and interactions have been reviewed. It does not assume retirement capacity, rental-loss usability, an accountable plan or an Augusta Rule deduction.

Can I use it for an S corporation or C corporation?

This uses individual federal income-tax schedules, not a corporate return or payroll model. A pass-through owner needs a reviewed owner-level income projection. C corporation revenue or profit is not a substitute for an individual’s taxable income.

Why might my final tax difference be different?

Capital gains, QBI, deduction limitations, AMT, credits, payroll taxes, state taxes and other interactions can change the result. The tool excludes those items and is not a complete return calculation.

Are return preparation and amendments included in the advisory fee?

Only if expressly included in the written scope. Standard advisory pricing is $7,800 in two $3,900 installments 30 days apart. Returns, amendments, cost segregation and additional services are separately scoped and priced.
