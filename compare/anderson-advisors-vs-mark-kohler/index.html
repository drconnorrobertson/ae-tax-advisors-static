Both of these providers are known for entity structuring and asset protection, and investors researching them are usually deciding how many entities they actually need.

That is the right question, and it deserves a more careful answer than either the maximalist or minimalist position gives.

Both Emphasize Structure, With Different Emphases

Anderson Business Advisors is known for multi-entity asset protection structures for real estate investors, combining land trusts, LLCs, and holding entities, typically paired with ongoing platinum-style membership services.

Mark Kohler is a CPA and attorney known for education, books, and content on entity structuring, the trifecta framework, and small business tax strategy, alongside a firm providing legal and tax services.

Both are legitimate and both have substantial followings. The practical question for an investor is not which is better in the abstract but how much structure your situation actually warrants.

What Entities Actually Do

An LLC provides liability isolation, protecting your other assets from claims arising from a property, provided you maintain separateness with distinct accounts, adequate capitalization, and no personal expenses run through the entity.

Charging order protection works in the other direction, limiting a personal creditor to a charging order rather than reaching entity assets. This is strong in Wyoming, Nevada, Delaware, South Dakota, and Alaska, and weaker elsewhere, particularly for single-member LLCs.

A land trust provides privacy in the public record. It provides no liability protection and no tax effect, since it is generally a grantor trust with everything flowing to the beneficiary.

None of these change your income tax. A single-member LLC is disregarded, a multi-member LLC is a partnership, and the property is taxed the same either way. Structure is bought for protection, not for tax savings.

The Cost Side Nobody Emphasizes

Every entity carries annual cost: state filing fees, registered agent fees, bookkeeping, and often a separate tax return.

California charges $800 minimum franchise tax per LLC annually plus a gross receipts fee, and an out-of-state LLC owning California property owes the same. An investor with eight California LLCs pays $6,400 before any tax on income, on properties that may be losing money.

Multi-member LLCs file Form 1065 and issue K-1s, adding preparation cost and capital account tracking that did not exist before.

A structure that costs $9,000 annually to maintain on a portfolio generating $60,000 of cash flow is a real decision, not an administrative detail. It may still be correct, but the cost belongs in the analysis.

What Actually Determines How Much Structure You Need

Equity at risk. Structure protects equity. A portfolio with $200,000 of total equity across four leveraged properties does not warrant the same architecture as one with $4,000,000 of equity.

Liability profile. Short-term rentals with guests on site, properties with pools, and multifamily with common areas carry different exposure than a single-family rental on a long lease.

Insurance. Adequate primary coverage plus an umbrella policy is the first line and is far cheaper than entity architecture. Structure is what handles claims exceeding coverage, not what replaces it.

Your other assets. An investor whose only meaningful asset is the real estate has less to protect than a surgeon with a practice, retirement accounts, and personal net worth.

Financing constraints. Most residential lenders will not lend to an LLC at conventional terms, which is why land trusts appear so often, and transfers after closing carry due-on-sale exposure.

Questions to Ask Any Structuring Provider

What is the total annual cost of this structure, including filing fees, registered agent fees, bookkeeping, and additional tax returns?

What specific claim does this structure protect against that adequate insurance would not?

Does this change my income tax at all, and if so how? The honest answer for most real estate structures is that it does not.

Which state's law governs my charging order protection, and does that state protect single-member LLCs?

What happens to my existing financing when I transfer these properties?

A provider who cannot answer the cost question specifically is selling a product rather than analyzing your situation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do multiple LLCs reduce my taxes?

No. A single-member LLC is disregarded and a multi-member LLC is a partnership, so the property is taxed the same either way. Entity structure is purchased for liability protection and privacy, not for income tax savings.

How many entities do I actually need?

It depends on equity at risk, liability profile, your other assets, and financing constraints. A portfolio with $200,000 of equity does not warrant the same architecture as one with $4,000,000. Adequate insurance plus an umbrella policy is the first line, not entity count.

Does a land trust protect me from lawsuits?

No. A land trust provides privacy in the public record only. It gives no liability protection and no tax effect, since it is generally a grantor trust with all income and deductions flowing to the beneficiary. Protection requires an entity holding the beneficial interest.

What does a multi-entity structure cost annually?

Filing fees, registered agent fees, bookkeeping, and often separate returns per entity. California alone charges $800 minimum franchise tax per LLC plus a gross receipts fee. Ask any provider for the total annual carrying cost before adopting the structure.

Can entity structure hurt my tax result?

Yes. Real estate in an S corporation caps your deductible loss under IRC Sec. 1366(d) and makes distributions of appreciated property taxable under IRC Sec. 311(b). Self-rental arrangements without a grouping election can suspend large depreciation deductions.
