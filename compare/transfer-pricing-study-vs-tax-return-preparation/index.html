Transfer Pricing Study vs Tax Return Preparation: Different Deliverables

Compare a transfer-pricing documentation engagement with annual tax preparation by transaction analysis, assumptions, updates and handoff.

Book a Call

The decision to make

A transfer-pricing study and an annual tax return are different work products. A company may need coordinated help with both, but should not assume a return-preparation fee includes a documentation project for related-party transactions. Define the decisions, evidence and update process before engaging a specialist. This page does not determine an arm’s-length price or promise penalty protection.

Define the transactions to be studied

IRS transfer-pricing documentation guidance emphasizes meaningful analysis and the facts supporting the method used. Ask the prospective specialist which related-party flows are covered, which entities and years are included and what assumptions require management confirmation. A document’s length or a label such as benchmarking study does not establish its suitability for every transaction. The business needs an explanation it can connect to its actual operations and records.

Use a service-charge arrangement as the interview case

For a fictional group where one company performs services for another, provide the intercompany agreement, staff functions, cost records and invoicing process. Ask the specialist how the engagement would examine those facts and what additional information would be requested. Ask the return preparer how the resulting position and figures would enter the annual filings. The two proposals should identify gaps between an economic analysis, accounting entries and reporting implementation.

Specify maintenance and limitations

Request a list of the final report, supporting schedules, management representations and any implementation assistance. Clarify whether the fee covers a single year, updates, changed transactions or responses to later questions. A documentation engagement does not automatically include representation in an examination, and a filed return does not by itself establish that documentation requirements were met. Ask the professionals to identify applicable requirements and allocate responsibility for retaining the evidence.

Build a written selection brief

  • Specified transactions, entities and tax periods.
  • Facts and assumptions management must confirm.
  • Report, supporting analysis and accounting implementation outputs.
  • Annual update triggers and responsibilities.
  • Separate scope for return preparation and examination support.

Agree on a change trigger

Use a proposed change in services or pricing as a test of the maintenance arrangement. Ask who notifies the specialist, what records are needed and whether the change falls within the current fee. The contract should make it possible to distinguish an ordinary update from a new transaction requiring additional analysis before anyone changes the accounting process.
