Purchase Price Allocation Valuation vs Form 8594 Preparation

Define valuation, allocation agreement and tax-reporting responsibilities separately when hiring help for a business asset transaction.

Book a Call

The decision to make

Valuing acquired assets, agreeing a purchase-price allocation and preparing Form 8594 are connected but different tasks. A business should ask who performs each job and how the conclusions are reconciled. This hiring guide does not determine whether Form 8594 applies or assign values to a transaction.

Compare the documented scope

Documented scope comparison
DecisionFirst optionSecond option
Valuation workSupported values for specified assets under the agreed purpose.Reporting work: determine and prepare applicable tax reporting.
Coordination pointAssumptions, transaction documents and final consideration.Allocation agreement, tax categories and return information.
Review evidenceValuation support and limitations.Reconciled schedules and approved filing inputs.

Establish applicability and purpose first

IRS Form 8594 instructions describe the transactions for which the form is required and the role of an allocation between assets. Ask the tax adviser to evaluate the actual transaction before commissioning a form or assuming every business sale follows the same process. Separately ask the valuation specialist what purpose, assets, assumptions and standard of value the engagement covers. A report prepared for another purpose may not answer the tax team’s question.

Use an asset sale with changing consideration

For a fictional transaction with equipment, customer-related assets and later price adjustments, ask how each team receives the final agreement and consideration schedule. The valuation team should identify its evidence and limitations; the tax team should explain what it needs for the reporting decision. Ask who reconciles the numbers used by the parties and who addresses a difference before filings are prepared.

Plan for later changes and the return handoff

Form 8594 instructions address changes in allocated amounts after the sale year. Have the adviser identify the relevant process if that situation occurs; do not assume the initial report ends every future reporting task. Request a final schedule with clear ownership, assumptions and approvals. Keep valuation, tax analysis, form preparation and any dispute or amendment work separate in the written proposal.

Build a written selection brief

  • Tax adviser’s applicability review for the actual transaction.
  • Valuation purpose, assets, assumptions and limitations.
  • Shared final agreement and consideration schedule.
  • Responsibility for reconciling buyer/seller reporting inputs.
  • Later-adjustment process and separate filing fees.

Request a reconciliation at handoff

Ask the teams to reconcile the final consideration, asset values, agreed allocation and reporting schedule before the preparer uses the numbers. Record each approved adjustment and the person authorizing it. A single final packet reduces the risk that different professionals use different versions of the closing documents without realizing the discrepancy.
