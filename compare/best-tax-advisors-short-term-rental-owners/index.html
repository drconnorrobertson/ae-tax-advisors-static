Separate a first purchase from an operating rental

A buyer still under contract needs a document list and a calendar: closing, furnishing, availability for rental, bookkeeping setup and the preparer’s review date. An owner with two operating seasons needs a reconciliation of existing depreciation and carryforwards. Send the advisor the stage of your property alongside the address. A proposal that treats both owners identically may miss the work you actually need.

Ask who reviews the operating facts

Give each firm the same stay-history export, management agreement, ownership details and description of the work performed by each spouse. Ask what participation records it expects and when it reviews them. An engineer’s report cannot resolve all owner-level questions. Request a written list of unresolved facts before approving a projection or a study purchase.

Compare a managed STR with a self-managed STR

Consider two hypothetical owners with similar purchase prices. One handles guest messages, maintenance coordination and turnover scheduling; the other delegates almost everything to a manager. Their comparison brief should show that difference. Ask each advisor how the service scope changes, which records it needs and whether the initial projection remains provisional. Neither purchase price nor a website calculator establishes the final return treatment.

Cost Segregation, Bonus Depreciation and Section 179

Cost segregation classifies the components of a property for depreciation. Bonus depreciation may accelerate deductions for eligible property. Section 179 is a separate election with business-use, income and other eligibility requirements; it is not another name for a cost segregation study. The cost of a residential rental building is generally recovered under its applicable building depreciation rules, while eligible components require their own analysis.

A deduction reduces taxable income; it is not a dollar-for-dollar tax saving. Loss usability, state treatment, financing, fees and later disposition can change the economic result. Review AE’s cost segregation service and compare Section 179 with bonus depreciation.

Sources: IRS Publication 527, IRS Publication 946 and IRS Publication 925.

Choosing an STR Tax Company: Common Questions

How do I choose the best short-term rental tax company?

Compare review of operating facts, participation documentation, study coordination, return preparation, total fees and implementation responsibility. Obtain a written proposal tailored to your property and household; a high advertised deduction does not establish the best fit.

Can an Airbnb tax accountant handle lodging taxes too?

Ask explicitly. Federal and state income-tax preparation, local lodging-tax registration, occupancy-tax filings and marketplace collection reconciliation are separate responsibilities. Confirm which provider handles each obligation.

Is cost segregation the same as Section 179?

No. Cost segregation identifies property components and their applicable recovery periods. Bonus depreciation and Section 179 are separate deduction provisions with their own eligibility rules. Do not assume an STR building qualifies for Section 179 or that a study makes a loss deductible against wages.

Does a seven-day average stay automatically make STR losses deductible?

No. An average customer-use period of seven days or less can affect the rental-activity classification under the passive-activity rules. Material participation and other limitations must still be evaluated. Return reporting and self-employment tax require a separate analysis of services and facts.

What records should I bring to an STR tax advisor?

Bring closing documents, furnishing invoices, placed-in-service evidence, stay-history exports, personal-use records, owner and spouse work logs, manager agreements, prior returns and depreciation schedules. Ask which missing facts must be resolved before changing estimated payments.
