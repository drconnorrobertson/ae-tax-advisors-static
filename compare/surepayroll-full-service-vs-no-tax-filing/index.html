SurePayroll Full Service vs No Tax Filing: Assign the Filing Work

Compare SurePayroll service scopes through payroll-tax responsibility, year-end forms and optional integrations.

Book a Call

The decision to make

SurePayroll distinguishes Full Service from a No Tax Filing option. Both descriptions concern payroll operations, but the No Tax Filing option leaves the business responsible for filing and depositing payroll taxes. That responsibility should determine the decision before a monthly price comparison. The public page contains multiple price presentations, so confirm the current applicable offer directly.

Make the responsibility transfer explicit

For a fictional owner running a small professional office, list each tax return, deposit and year-end form and name the person responsible. If the company chooses No Tax Filing, verify that a qualified internal or outside resource will complete those tasks on time. Payroll calculations alone are not proof that a deposit was made. For Full Service, ask which federal, state and local accounts are supported and how the provider confirms a completed filing or payment.

Read the entire fee schedule

The reviewed page displays more than one Full Service starting-price block, so a single universal base-price claim would be misleading. Ask which offer applies and obtain the base, per-employee and promotional terms in writing. The page separately describes year-end W-2 and 1099-NEC charges of $50 annually plus $5 per form. Accounting integration is listed at $4.99 monthly, and timeclock options have their own pricing. Use the complete service-year schedule rather than multiplying an introductory headline by twelve.

Test the close and exception process

Ask how an approved pay run reaches the accounting file and how a reversal or corrected employee record is handled. Confirm direct-deposit eligibility and cutoff requirements rather than assuming every run qualifies for the advertised timing. During a provider change, reconcile opening year-to-date totals and assign filing responsibility for periods spanning the transition. Keep access to payroll reports and submission evidence so the employer can respond to later questions.

Build a written selection brief

  • Named filing and deposit responsibility for each account.
  • Applicable offer, ordinary rate and annual year-end charges.
  • Required accounting or time integrations and their fees.
  • Payment terms and reversal or correction process.
  • Opening reconciliation and transition-period filing ownership.

Plan for a missing confirmation

Agree on what the employer will do if an expected filing or payment confirmation is absent. Record the support contact, escalation deadline and source documents needed. This is especially important when the business keeps filing duties itself, because a completed payroll run is not a substitute for the separate filing record.
