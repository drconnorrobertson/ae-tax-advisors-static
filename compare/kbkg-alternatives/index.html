Start with the service you need

KBKG is described here as specialty tax services. It may be a good starting point for owners and CPA firms needing cost segregation, credits, or fixed asset expertise. Before switching or signing, compare the study method, report, property fit, and handoff to the tax return. A familiar brand or a lower quote says little about whether the specific work you need is included.

This guide is published by AE Tax Advisors, one of the options below. It is an editorial shortlist, not an independent market ranking or a guarantee of current service availability.

Build a shortlist around the work, not the brand

  1. A study for one property: compare the study method, asset schedule, basis reconciliation, inspection or remote-review process and technical support. Confirm that your existing preparer can implement the report.
  2. Existing asset or accounting-method problems: ask for review of prior depreciation, repairs, dispositions and the appropriate correction process. A new study alone may not resolve the historical records.
  3. Multiple specialty incentives: identify each needed analysis and its responsible professional. A broad service menu does not mean every incentive is included or applicable.
  4. Owner-wide tax planning: compare advisory work across the business, household and rental portfolio separately from the technical study. Confirm who reviews loss limitations and coordinates filings.

Shortlist two or three providers for the same defined work. Keep KBKG in the comparison if its proposal meets that need; an alternatives search is not evidence that switching is necessary. The options below are a research shortlist, not a ranking of quality or a recommendation based on verified customer outcomes.

For a residential property, use the cost segregation company checklist. For an older property, review prior-year depreciation and Form 3115 questions. Use KBKG pricing for fee evidence and the direct KBKG versus AE comparison only when those are your two finalists.

KBKG alternatives to compare

1. AE Tax Advisors

Consider AE when the decision extends beyond a property study to business-owner or investor planning. Ask for the written plan, implementation assignments and separately scoped study or filing work. An advisory engagement is not a substitute for every specialty-tax service.

2. Engineered Tax Services

Include Engineered Tax Services in the proposal review when researching specialty project options. Ask which building analysis or incentive work it would recommend for your facts, what the technical deliverables are, and who coordinates with your preparer. Confirm its current scope directly rather than treating this listing as an equivalent offer.

3. Capstan Tax Strategies

Capstan's official service menu includes cost segregation, energy incentives, unit-of-property and tangible-property analysis. Consider these workstreams when the issue involves building components or repairs as well as depreciation. Ask which analyses your quote includes and how its team will coordinate with your accountant.

4. ICS Tax

ICS Tax's official menu includes cost segregation, accounting methods and comprehensive fixed-asset review, alongside tax incentives. Ask whether your project needs a study, an accounting-method review or both, which records are required, and who handles return implementation.

The order is for navigation, not a score. For study providers, ask for a sample report; for advisory firms, ask for a sample work plan and complete fee schedule.

Where KBKG may still be the right choice

KBKG may fit especially well when your priority is owners and CPA firms needing cost segregation, credits, or fixed asset expertise. Keep the current provider on the shortlist if it can show a clear scope, a qualified delivery team, and a fee that makes sense for the outcome. If its offer is specialty tax services, do not assume a different type of firm supplies an identical service.

Decide whether you need a specialty study alone or a tax advisor who will coordinate the study with filings.

Compare proposals on the same basis

  • Work product: Ask for the exact report, plan, return, or program access you will receive.
  • People: Identify the lead advisor, engineer, preparer, or coach, and who signs any tax return.
  • Coordination: Confirm who implements recommendations and who supports questions after delivery.
  • Fees: Ask for first-year, renewal, amendment, and project costs in writing.
  • Fit: Share your entities, states, property types, and deadlines before accepting a proposal.
