ShareBuilder Solo Saver vs Plus: Self-Managed or Administered?

Compare ShareBuilder Solo Saver and Solo Plus by retained administrative work, included services and product-specific fee questions.

Book a Call

Compare the work retained by the owner

ShareBuilder describes Solo Saver as self-managed and Solo Plus as fully administered. Its feature table also distinguishes supported owner counts, loan availability and document-related services. This creates a practical choice about the work the owner wants to perform and the service the provider will accept. It is not a comparison of solo eligibility against an employee plan. An appropriate professional should first confirm whether an owner-only arrangement fits the business and related employment facts.

Write the annual operating checklist

List the records the owner expects to maintain, the statements needed for review and the person who will coordinate any required filing. Ask the provider which documents it prepares, which are signature-ready and which steps remain with the owner. A listed form service does not determine whether the owner has a filing requirement or whether all conditions are met. Keep IRS guidance and qualified review separate from the provider’s product feature description. Do not copy simplified contribution or employee-eligibility claims into the decision file.

A fictional owner comparing effort

A fictional consultant is comfortable retaining investment statements but wants help maintaining the annual plan file. The consultant asks ShareBuilder to explain the practical difference between Saver and Plus using the same expected activity. The request includes whether another owner may participate and whether any loan feature is actually available in the selected product. A written response reveals which tasks the consultant must perform, which the provider handles and what information must be supplied on time.

Compare product-specific charges

ShareBuilder publishes setup and account-service pricing that can vary with the product and asset level. It separately publishes a custom-services schedule. Ask which entries apply to Saver or Plus, whether an advertised refund or discount has conditions and what investment-related expenses remain. Do not assume a general transaction schedule overrides a product-specific feature table or that every listed service is included. The useful cost comparison matches the selected product, the owner’s anticipated events and a complete fee disclosure.

Preserve flexibility without assuming it

Ask how the service arrangement would change if ownership, staffing or plan activity changes. Record the requirements for moving between products or to a different plan, without assuming the process is automatic. The provider’s explanation should include the records and responsibilities at transition. An owner choosing self-management should understand the tasks retained; an owner buying administration should understand its limits. Neither choice guarantees tax savings, relieves every obligation or establishes the suitability of the investments available through the plan. If a requested feature is described as coming soon, ask for its present availability in writing. Do not treat a roadmap item as a usable service when deciding how the plan will operate immediately after opening.
