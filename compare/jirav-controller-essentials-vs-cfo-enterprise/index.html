Jirav Controller Essentials vs CFO Enterprise: Advisory Planning Scope

Compare Jirav’s advisory-practice products by planning approach, scenarios, forecast horizon and the work promised to clients.

Book a Call

Start with the work to be done

Jirav’s reviewed pricing page addresses accounting and CFO-advisory practices. Controller Essentials and CFO Enterprise are positioned around different levels of advisory planning. They are not quotes for an owner purchasing a complete outsourced CFO engagement. A practice should first define the client deliverables it intends to provide and the professional work it will perform around the software.

Separate a software tier from a client service

For a fictional advisory practice, list whether a client will receive historical reports, budget comparisons, a hiring model or ongoing scenario discussions. Identify who validates the starting data and reviews recommendations. The platform’s feature list does not determine what the practice should promise, price or sign off for the client.

Test the modeling depth actually required

A simple annual budget and a detailed staffing model ask different questions. Use one anonymized client scenario to examine drivers, assumptions, revisions and report outputs. Avoid selecting a longer forecast horizon merely because it is available. More projected periods do not make uncertain inputs more reliable or remove the need to explain assumptions.

Confirm the commercial and implementation details

The page displays starting prices; obtain the applicable account, client-company, user and billing terms directly. Discuss integration setup, training and the process for maintaining client models. Keep software subscription costs separate from the practice’s professional time and any independent services it purchases.

Prepare a decision brief

Create a service-design brief before discussing tiers. The most useful comparison connects the promised client deliverable to a repeatable professional review process, rather than to feature count alone.

  • Client deliverables and the frequency of advisory conversations.
  • Planning drivers and scenarios that those deliverables require.
  • Source accounting, staffing and operational information.
  • Review responsibility before reports or recommendations reach the client.
  • The actual subscription and implementation quote for the practice.

Check the final handoff

Have the practice define its review standard before packaging a client service around either tier. Record the supporting documents and assumptions needed, how an exception is escalated and what happens when information is incomplete. A tool’s planning horizon should not become a promise about the reliability of a client’s distant results.
