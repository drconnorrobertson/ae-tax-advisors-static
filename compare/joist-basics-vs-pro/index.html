Joist Basics vs Pro: Document Volume and Work-Order Records

Compare Joist Basics and Pro by document allowance, revised estimates, job photos, client activity and work orders.

Count the documents your process creates

Joist Basics and Pro differ in document allowance and the detail available around the estimate-to-invoice process. Count the documents a representative job actually creates before comparing the monthly limit. A quote, a revision and an invoice may matter differently under the provider’s counting rules, so confirm those rules directly.

A business with only occasional work may have a different need from a contractor preparing documents every day. Avoid using revenue or client count alone as a proxy. The useful question is whether the selected plan can support the normal record trail without forcing staff to omit revisions or maintain a second unofficial copy.

A fictional repair estimate

A contractor inspects a repair, prepares an estimate and later changes one line item after the customer clarifies the requested work. Use fictional details to test how the original and revised documents are identified. In Pro, include a photograph and show the work order passed to the person doing the job.

After the job, generate the invoice and ask the office user to locate the accepted version and client activity. The test should make clear what is a proposal, what is an instruction to perform work and what is a bill. A document that looks professional does not by itself establish customer acceptance or completion.

Review the records after export

Pro lists unlimited documents and clients, branding, photos, activity tracking and work orders. Confirm the exact output and access your team needs. Ask whether an exported document retains enough references for the bookkeeper and for a later question from the customer.

Compare current payment-processing terms and any additional services separately from the subscription. The plan choice does not guarantee faster payment or resolve a disputed charge. Select the document allowance and handoff functions that match the business’s actual work, with a named owner for keeping the record current.

Ask whether duplicate drafts and revised documents consume separate allowance entries.

Decision checklist

  • Confirm how drafts, revisions and invoices count toward the document allowance.
  • Test one changed estimate and identify the version the customer accepted.
  • Inspect the work order and exported invoice for clear job and client references.
  • Review ongoing subscription and payment terms without assuming that unlimited documents means unlimited services.
