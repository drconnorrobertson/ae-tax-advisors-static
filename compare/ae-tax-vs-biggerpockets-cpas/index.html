The BiggerPockets Community and CPA Recommendations

BiggerPockets has built the largest and most active online community for real estate investors. With millions of members exchanging ideas, deal structures, and operational advice, the platform is a genuine resource for investors at every level. One of the most common questions posted in BiggerPockets forums is some variation of "Who is a good CPA for real estate investors?" The responses that follow are typically well-intentioned, drawn from individual members' personal experiences with their own tax professionals.

The challenge is that these recommendations carry no formal vetting. A CPA who performs well for one investor's straightforward rental portfolio may be entirely unprepared for another investor's complex short-term rental strategy, multi-entity structure, or cost segregation needs. Forum endorsements are valuable data points, but they should be the starting point of your evaluation -- not the end of it.

This page offers a framework for evaluating any CPA you find through BiggerPockets (or any community forum), and explains where AE Tax Advisors fits for investors who want a dedicated, full-spectrum real estate tax advisory relationship.

Why Forum Recommendations Vary So Widely

Not all BiggerPockets CPA recommendations are created equal. There are several reasons quality varies significantly across forum suggestions:

  • No verification process. BiggerPockets does not vet, certify, or endorse the CPAs recommended in its forums. Any member can suggest a CPA based on their own experience, and there is no mechanism for confirming the recommended professional's specialization, credentials, or track record with real estate investors.
  • Experience context is often missing. A recommendation that says "my CPA is great" does not tell you whether that investor owns two single-family rentals or a 200-unit portfolio. What works for a simple situation may fall short for a complex one.
  • Generalists can appear specialized. Some CPAs who receive community recommendations are general practitioners who handle a handful of real estate clients. They may understand the basics of rental income reporting but lack deep expertise in cost segregation, bonus depreciation strategy, real estate professional status documentation, or entity structuring for liability protection and tax efficiency.
  • Popularity is not the same as quality. Some CPAs are frequently recommended because they are active in the BiggerPockets community themselves, not necessarily because they deliver the most sophisticated tax outcomes. Visibility and expertise are two different things.

None of this means that BiggerPockets recommendations are unreliable. Many investors have found excellent tax professionals through the community. The point is that every recommendation deserves independent evaluation before you entrust someone with your tax strategy.

How to Properly Vet a BiggerPockets CPA Recommendation

If you are evaluating a CPA you found through BiggerPockets forums, here is what to look for before making a commitment. These criteria apply whether you are considering a community-recommended CPA or any other tax professional.

1. Ask About Their Real Estate Client Base

A CPA who works with five real estate investors out of 500 total clients is not a real estate specialist. Ask what percentage of their practice is dedicated to real estate investors. A strong answer would indicate that real estate investors and business owners constitute the majority of their client base, not a small side category.

2. Understand Their Cost Segregation Capability

Cost segregation is one of the most powerful tax strategies available to property owners, yet many CPAs do not offer it at all -- or outsource it to third-party engineering firms at marked-up prices. Ask whether the firm performs cost segregation in-house and what their pricing structure looks like. AE Tax Advisors maintains a dedicated in-house cost segregation team and charges $1 per square foot, among the lowest rates in the industry.

3. Evaluate Their Bonus Depreciation Knowledge

Under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA), 100% bonus depreciation is available and represents a significant tax planning opportunity for property owners. Ask your prospective CPA how they are advising clients on bonus depreciation, whether they understand the current rules and phase-out schedules, and how they integrate bonus depreciation into a comprehensive tax strategy. A vague answer here is a warning sign.

4. Confirm Their Pricing Model

Many CPAs recommended on BiggerPockets charge by the hour, which can lead to unpredictable costs and a disincentive for the CPA to spend time on proactive tax planning. Ask whether they offer a fixed-fee engagement model. AE Tax Advisors offers a transparent $7,800 annual advisory engagement that includes ongoing strategy, compliance support, and access to the full advisory team throughout the year.

5. Ask About Amendment Recovery

One of the most overlooked areas in real estate tax planning is prior-year amendment recovery. Many investors have filed returns that missed available deductions -- particularly cost segregation, bonus depreciation, and entity-level elections. A qualified real estate tax advisor should proactively review your prior three years of returns and identify recoverable savings. If a prospective CPA does not mention this as part of their onboarding process, they may be leaving significant money on the table.

6. Look for Full-Spectrum Advisory

Tax compliance -- preparing and filing returns -- is only one piece of the puzzle. True tax advisory includes proactive strategy around entity structuring, retirement plan design, real estate professional status qualification, and long-term wealth building. A CPA who only handles compliance is not providing the strategic depth that real estate investors need to minimize their lifetime tax burden.
