How to read this list

AE Tax Advisors publishes this page and ranks itself first, which you should factor in. In exchange, we name the firms that beat us in specific situations and say exactly when to hire them instead.

Most "best tax advisor" lists rank on brand size. That is the wrong metric. For a real estate investor, four capabilities determine the outcome, and a firm can be famous while being weak at two of them.

Cost segregation. Is it performed in house, referred, or merely discussed? Depreciation timing is the largest single lever most investors have.

Passive activity planning. Section 469 decides whether your losses reduce this year's tax or carry forward. Real estate professional status, the short-term rental exception, and grouping elections are the tools.

Entity structuring. How property is held, and how it relates to any operating business, affects flow-through treatment, self-employment tax, basis, and your exit.

Execution. Whether the firm that wrote the plan files the return. A remarkable amount of good tax strategy dies in the handoff.

Which One Should You Choose?

If you own a business and real estate

You want one firm holding both sets of numbers. The largest plans we build move income out of an operating entity and absorb it with real estate depreciation, and that only works when a single advisor controls both sides.

If real estate is your entire tax picture

Hall CPA and Keystone CPA are strong specialists, and Hall CPA in particular is the right call for syndication and fund exposure. We will tell prospective clients this on the call.

If your problem is legal, not tax

Anderson Business Advisors or KKOS. Land trusts, multi-state layering, anonymity, and asset protection are legal work that a tax firm cannot perform. Hire the attorney, then hire the tax firm to operate inside the structure.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who is the best tax advisor for real estate investors?

It depends on the shape of your income. If you own an operating business alongside real estate, a firm that handles both, AE Tax Advisors is built for exactly that, avoids the coordination gap. If real estate is your whole picture, Hall CPA and Keystone CPA are strong specialists. If you need legal structuring, Anderson Business Advisors or KKOS.

Do I need a real estate CPA or will a regular CPA do?

For a single rental, a competent general preparer is usually adequate. Once you have multiple properties, short-term rentals, or a cost segregation strategy, the gap widens fast. Form 3115, Section 469 grouping elections, real estate professional status documentation, and depreciation recapture planning are specialist territory, and mistakes there are expensive.

How much does a real estate tax advisor cost?

Most firms quote per engagement and do not publish. Among those that do, AE Tax Advisors charges $7,800 for strategic tax advisory with a split-pay option, $1,500 per entity return, $1,000 per personal return, $2,500 per amended return, and $1 per square foot for cost segregation with a $2,000 minimum. WCG CPAs also publishes flat-fee packages.

When is a tax advisor worth it for a real estate investor?

Generally once your income clears roughly $250,000 or your portfolio reaches a few properties, whichever comes first. Below that, a good preparer plus a handful of targeted moves usually beats paying for full advisory. Any honest firm will tell you when you are under the threshold.

What is the most important thing a real estate tax advisor should do?

Make sure your losses are usable. A cost segregation study producing a $200,000 deduction is worth nothing this year if the loss is passive and suspends under Section 469. Real estate professional status, the short-term rental exception, grouping elections, and passive income planning are what turn deductions into refunds.

Can I switch tax advisors mid-year?

Yes, and mid-year is usually the better time. Switching before year-end means the strategy can still affect the current tax year. A good firm will request your prior three years, review them for missed deductions, and handle the transition.

Should my rental properties be in an S-Corp?

Almost never. Appreciating real estate inside an S-Corp creates problems on distribution and exit, because you cannot pull property out without triggering gain. Rentals generally belong in an LLC taxed as a partnership or disregarded entity. The S-Corp question belongs to the operating business.
