Select a provider for the property and implementation needs

A residential rental owner should compare relevant property experience, methodology, documented deliverables and how the report reaches the tax return. National scale, software convenience and bundled advisory are different characteristics. There is no universal best provider for every property. Compare residential fit and implementation

Give each provider the same address, property type, acquisition year, improvement history and existing depreciation information. Ask whether it accepts the project and which deliverables are included. Confirm separately who prepares any Form 3115, reviews loss usability and files the return. Do not infer that a provider lacks a service because a particular page does not describe it.

Use a proposal-based final decision

Compare the current written proposals and the total work remaining after delivery. Do not rely on old online prices, promised universal savings or unverified turnaround claims. For AE, cost segregation is separately priced from advisory. Ask about the specific property and scope on a call; the engagement should identify any additional preparation or implementation fees.

Illustrative decision

An investor with complete records and an experienced preparer may need a different scope from an owner missing five years of depreciation history. Use the same factual property brief when requesting information from providers. A provider's public website is a starting point; confirm current terms in its proposal.

Records and decisions to prepare

  • Confirm experience with the actual property type
  • Request current primary-source scope information
  • Distinguish software output from professional deliverables

Provider models and pages to check

Different proposals may describe an engineering study, a remote residential report, software-generated calculations, or a tax advisory engagement. Ask for the actual asset schedule, property photographs or site methodology, reconciliation to depreciable basis, and the scope of audit support. A study changes depreciation timing; it does not automatically make a passive loss usable against wages or business income.

How to choose a cost segregation company for a residential rental

The best fit is the provider whose written scope matches your property, records and filing needs. Use the checklist below with each proposal rather than comparing a software price with a full-service study or assuming a larger deduction is immediately usable.

Use the same property facts to compare proposals

Two owners can reasonably choose different providers

An owner with a current depreciation schedule and an experienced preparer may primarily need a study and technical handoff. An owner missing prior-year schedules may also need reconstruction, a correction-method review and separately scoped return work. Neither situation establishes that one provider is universally better.

Prepare a same-scope request

Send the same property type, square footage, purchase and placed-in-service dates, closing statement, improvements and available depreciation schedules to each shortlisted provider. Ask for the deliverable, responsible professional, expected timing, required client records, exclusions and total quoted work.

Before ordering, review whether a study is worthwhile, study and implementation costs, prior-year depreciation and Form 3115, and sale-timing considerations.

A shortlist test for cost segregation companies

Send every provider the same one-page property brief and request a written proposal and redacted report. Check whether the report ties asset totals to the building basis, shows evidence for key classifications and defines the handoff to your tax preparer.

Ask which work is included for your scenario. A warehouse with detailed construction records, a small rental with limited records and a portfolio with prior-year depreciation each require a different mix of site work, estimates and implementation. Compare firms against that scope before comparing headline fees.
