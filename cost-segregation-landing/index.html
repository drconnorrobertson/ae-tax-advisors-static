Cost Segregation Estimate: Start With Your Property Records

By AE Tax Advisors · Published 2026-01-06 · Updated November 8, 2026.

A preliminary cost segregation estimate helps decide whether a detailed study is worth considering. It is a screening calculation based on stated assumptions, not a completed asset classification or a promise of tax savings.

Bring the facts that change the answer

  • Property type, ownership entity and location.
  • Purchase price, land allocation, closing statement and acquisition date.
  • Original placed-in-service date and later renovation dates.
  • Existing depreciation schedule, including separately recorded furniture or equipment.
  • Current use, rental arrangement, owner participation and planned sale or exchange.

Understand what an estimate can establish

The initial conversation can identify missing records, discuss a reasonable study scope and assess whether further analysis is useful. A supported final allocation requires the relevant property evidence. A short call cannot establish the exact deduction or the tax benefit on a return.

The study fee and timing depend on the agreed work. Ask for a written scope stating deliverables, responsibilities and any separate return-preparation or historical-correction fees.

Separate an accelerated deduction from tax saved

Eligibility and owner-level limits determine whether a deduction can be used now. A property can generate depreciation while the owner carries a loss forward. State rules and future disposition taxes also belong in the decision.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will the first call tell me my exact deduction?

No. A preliminary discussion uses assumptions and identifies records needed for the study and tax review.

Can I review a property I already own?

Yes. Bring the original acquisition and service dates and existing depreciation schedule. A new study does not reset those dates.
