C Corporation Net Operating Losses: Carryforwards and the 80% Limit

By AE Tax Advisors

Updated November 8, 2026.

A C corporation's net operating loss can reduce taxable income in another year, subject to the loss's vintage and applicable limitations. It does not reduce tax dollar for dollar, pass automatically to shareholders, or guarantee a refund. Keep a year-by-year rollforward before assigning value to a loss carryforward.

Start with a tax loss, not a book loss

The tax calculation adjusts financial-statement income for items treated differently on the return. A book loss, negative bank balance or operating cash deficit is not by itself an NOL. Reconcile the filed Form 1120 and supporting schedules before carrying an amount into the next year.

Capital losses, charitable contributions, business-interest carryforwards and credits follow separate rules. Do not combine them into one unrestricted loss balance. A financial-statement deferred tax asset also requires its own accounting analysis; it is not cash available to the business.

Which carryforward rules apply?

For an ordinary C corporation, post-2017 NOLs generally carry forward indefinitely. In a post-2020 deduction year, the post-2017 component is generally subject to an 80% taxable-income limitation, using the statutory computation. Pre-2018 carryforwards follow older rules and ordinarily have a 20-year carryforward period. Mixed-vintage balances require ordering and separate calculations.

Carrybacks are not generally available for a newly generated ordinary corporate NOL. Farming losses and non-life insurance companies have special rules. Losses from 2018–2020 were subject to temporary CARES Act provisions. Review the actual loss year, elections and prior usage rather than applying a current-year shortcut to every historical balance.

A simplified 80% limitation example

Assume a corporation has $1,000,000 of taxable income before its NOL deduction, only a $1,200,000 post-2017 NOL carryforward, no Section 382 restriction and no other relevant adjustments or special regime. The 80% limit permits an $800,000 deduction. That leaves $200,000 of taxable income and $400,000 of unused NOL.

At the regular 21% corporate rate, the illustration produces $42,000 of federal tax before credits and other taxes. The NOL reduced this year's regular tax by $168,000, not $800,000. Different loss vintages, credits, alternative minimum tax or other limitations can change the result.

Ownership changes can restrict use under Section 382

A loss corporation needs an ownership analysis before significant stock transfers, new investment, redemptions or a reorganization. Section 382 generally tests whether specified 5% shareholders increased their ownership by more than 50 percentage points over their lowest positions during the testing period, generally three years. Attribution, public groups, options and other detailed rules make a cap-table snapshot insufficient.

After a qualifying ownership change, the annual limitation generally begins with the old loss corporation's value multiplied by the applicable long-term tax-exempt rate, subject to adjustments and exceptions. Built-in gains and losses, continuity, consolidated groups and successive changes can matter. A transaction described as staged funding or a reverse merger does not automatically avoid the rules.

Corporate losses are different from owner losses

A C corporation's NOL belongs to the corporation. Shareholders do not simply deduct it on their personal returns. An S corporation or partnership instead passes through items that encounter owner-level basis and other limitations; changing entity status is not a way to move an existing corporate NOL freely to owners.

It is also incorrect to say passive-activity rules never apply to C corporations. They can apply to personal service corporations and closely held C corporations, with special corporate rules. At-risk restrictions may also matter. Test these provisions before treating a loss as available.

Build a usable loss schedule

  • List the original loss amount, tax year, return version and supporting reconciliation.
  • Record carrybacks, waivers, carryforwards used, corrections and remaining balance by vintage.
  • Keep capital loss, interest and credit carryforwards separate.
  • Maintain dated ownership records and previous Section 382 studies.
  • Project taxable income, annual limitations and state differences over several years.
  • Reconcile the opening balance on each return to the prior year's closing workpaper.

Plan transactions using after-tax cash flow

An NOL may reduce the marginal tax cost of future income, but recognition must follow the corporation's accounting method and substantive tax rules. Artificially invoicing early or delaying a deduction does not create permission to report items in a convenient year. Compare lawful alternatives, operating needs and the cost of delay.

Before accepting a purchase price that assumes full value for tax losses, ask who calculated their expected use and what happens if the forecast fails. Frequently Asked Questions

Does a corporate NOL reduce taxes dollar for dollar?

No. An NOL deduction reduces taxable income. The tax effect depends on the allowed deduction, tax rate, credits and other applicable rules.

Can a C corporation offset all future profit with NOLs?

Not automatically. Post-2017 NOLs generally face an 80% taxable-income limit in post-2020 years, while loss vintage and other restrictions can change the computation.

Can shareholders deduct a C corporation loss?

The corporate NOL generally stays with the corporation. It does not pass through to shareholders like an S-corporation or partnership item.

Do new investors affect corporate loss carryforwards?

They can. Review Section 382 and related rules before stock transactions or financing; the ownership analysis can limit future use.
