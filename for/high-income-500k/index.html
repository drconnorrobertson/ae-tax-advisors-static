Tax Planning for Business Owners Earning $500K or More

By AE Tax Advisors · Published 2026-01-06 · Updated November 8, 2026.

At $500,000 or more of income, several decisions can interact, but gross income alone does not determine the best tax plan. Identify the income sources, owners, states, cash needs and upcoming transactions before recommending an entity election or investment.

Reconcile the current position

Start with business and household returns, year-to-date financials, payroll, investments and estimated payments. Separate operating profit, wages, rental income, capital gains and one-time events. A projected effective rate needs a defined income base and the full computation.

Evaluate compensation and entity choices

Compare the current arrangement with eligible alternatives using supportable owner compensation and the same cash assumptions. Include payroll taxes, QBI, benefits, state entity taxes and compliance costs. A second company or C corporation does not automatically reduce the combined owner tax.

Match retirement funding to the business

Review ages, compensation, employee census, existing plans and related employers. Defined-contribution limits and defined-benefit funding are different concepts. An advertised maximum is not the amount every owner can contribute or deduct.

Include staff contributions, required future funding and business cash flow before adopting an additional plan.

Test property deductions before buying

Cost segregation can accelerate eligible depreciation, but a property purchase does not guarantee an offset against wages or business income. Review activity classification, participation, basis, at-risk and excess-business-loss rules.

Evaluate the property itself, financing, study fees, state adjustments and sale consequences. A tax projection should identify currently usable deductions separately from carryforwards.

Coordinate investments and giving

Realized gains, equity compensation and charitable gifts require asset- and year-specific planning. Appreciated-property donations, donor-advised funds and IRA distributions have different eligibility and limitation rules. Compare the economic gift and the allowable deduction rather than promising a fixed tax saving.

Address state and transaction timing

Residency, work locations, business nexus and income sourcing can continue to matter after a move. Before a business sale, evaluate the proposed transaction while terms can still change. After signing, some choices may no longer be available.

Ask for an implementation plan

A useful engagement identifies the recommended actions, assumptions, responsible people, deadlines, fees and required follow-up. Discuss a coordinated owner tax review with a summary of the businesses, properties, states and expected transactions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does earning $500K establish the right entity?

No. Ownership, services, cash needs, benefits, state taxes and exit plans all matter.

Will buying an STR automatically reduce my current tax?

No. The deduction must be eligible and usable under the owner’s applicable limits.
